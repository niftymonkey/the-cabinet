# Before the branch

The human work comes first. Before a branch starts, Mark does his part. Its record is the contract for the time he is away.

Terms, the ladder named "Who answers a question", and the branch charter are defined in [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md).

1. Grill Mark one question at a time. Each answer becomes a numbered decision in the branch's decision log, with its prior art beside it. A decision enters the decision log only after Mark approves its exact wording. Until then, what he said is an open question. Explore how his idea can work, and bring back what you find.
2. Close the grill with two sweeps, and turn each gap into a grill question. First, read the plan against itself: every scope edge (what is inside this branch, what is separate work) and every place where one part contradicts another. Second, walk each mechanic to its edge: what it means at its lowest, at its highest, and where it meets its neighbor. Done when every plan step and every named mechanic has been through both sweeps.
3. Sort every open question by who answers it (the ladder in `SKILL.md`). Only Mark's questions stay in the grill. A call the agent makes itself during the grill goes in the decision log, marked as the agent's call and open to Mark's overrule. Instrument work the sort turns up goes into the step order.
4. Draft the step order and the branch charter for his review. Place each instrument ahead of the first work it checks. Create no branch, file, or ticket before his yes.

## The step order

The step order names every slice before the build starts, so Mark sees how many commits and reviews the branch will make. Size a slice as the most one coder can finish well inside its context. Fewer, fuller slices beat many thin ones.

The step order is the first version of the follow-along list. Its row form is in [`during-the-branch.md`](during-the-branch.md), under "The follow-along list".
