# Research: a boss hit tell is a short colour beat carried by a shake, never a flash, and a big body that resists knockback takes about a third of an ordinary one's push

Gathered 2026-09-15 for round two of step 4, serving Mark's ruling of the same date: the Waking's source gets pushed a little by a toll, less than a mob, and bosses stay unpushed and get a visible hit tell per toll landed. The ruling is not reopened here; the research only sizes it.

Labels: **DOCUMENTED** = a developer statement, shipped source or shipped data file, a decompile of a shipped binary, or a measurement of this repository. **COMMUNITY-MEASURED** = a wiki or datamine transcription of values the game does not publish. **INFERRED** = a reading across sources, marked wherever it appears.

It sits beside [`watched-pushback-duration.md`](watched-pushback-duration.md), which sized the shove itself, and [`push-feel-precedent.md`](push-feel-precedent.md), which sized how a push reads. Nothing in either is re-fetched, and where this record contradicts one it says so.

## 0. The field's own rulers, and one shipped constraint the ruling has to pay for

Read from the tree on 2026-09-15. All **DOCUMENTED (this repository)**.

A boss is 120 by 80 field units (`BOSS_HALF_WIDTH` 60, `BOSS_HALF_HEIGHT` 40, `bosses/phases.ts`), which is **2.3% of the 540 by 760 field** and **86 by 58 CSS pixels** on a 390-wide phone at the 0.72 pixels per unit `tuning.ts` states. The set piece's source is 90 by 60 units, **1.3% of the field**, 65 by 43 CSS pixels. A shambler is 22 wide with 8 health; the grave is 27 wide at its start size and moves at `BASE_SPEED`, 270 units a second, while the field scrolls at 38.

The bell's rows are `reach` / `damageReach` / `push` by rung: 160/112/55, 183/128/63, 207/145/71, 231/162/80, 261/183/90 (`lines/bell.ts`). Push at a body is `row.push * (1 - distance / row.reach)`, spent over `SHOVE_TICKS` 30 ticks with a linear fall (`shove.ts`). A toll fires every `BELL_PERIOD` 180 ticks, three seconds.

The Waking's source opens at a quarter of the field's depth, y 190, and rides down at exactly the scroll, 38 units a second (`stage/setPiece.ts`, `DRIFT_PER_TICK = SCROLL_SPEED`). **Its pour is 75 bodies at one per 0.1 seconds, so the pour lasts 7.5 seconds and the pour point falls 285 units in that time**, 37% of the field's depth, and two or three tolls land inside it. Its health is 2400, so it survives every toll of the fight.

**The shipped constraint, said plainly before any option leans on it.** `stormTargets.ts` answers `pushable` false for a boss and for the set piece's source, and that is the decision. But both `moveStormTarget` and `shoveStormTarget` also guard on `slot.mob !== null`, and the module's own comment says the two "agree today, and only the first of them is a decision: a target the seam has no way to move is a target nothing can push either way." **So the ruling needs the seam widened as well as the flag flipped**: a set piece has no move path through `moveMobInsideBounds` at all. A second consequence rides with it: a shove stands a mob's own walk down (`shoveInFlight`, and `push-feel-precedent.md` section 1 found every source that specifies a mechanism does the same), so an impulse applied to the source on a mob's terms would also stop its 38-a-second drift for the 30 ticks it runs, which is 19 further units of ground the toll buys without asking. Whether the drift keeps running under a shove is an authoring call the slice makes, not a figure this record hands over.

**What a boss draws today, and what it does not.** `BossRenderer` is a dumb view with no per-tick state of its own: it reads `run.boss`, rebuilds the sprite only when `bossLook` changes, and sets the position. The only change of appearance in the file is the phase-break flash, which swaps the boss's declared bright and dark palette pair every `FLASH_HALF_PERIOD` 12 ticks for `PHASE_FLASH_TICKS` 30 (`bossSprite.ts`, `bosses/phases.ts`), with a comment recording that the figure was raised from 5 because six flashes a second breaches WCAG SC 2.3.1. **There is no boss health readout anywhere**: `RunHud.ts` carries the score and the per-line pips and nothing about a boss. And `StormRenderer.ts` refuses hitstop by name, because a sim pause changes the tick count and ADR 0015 makes the tick count the run.

## 1. Cave, Touhou, and what a shmup does about a boss that will not move

**Cave's bosses do have a health bar, and the per-hit tell on the boss body is an animation swap rather than a colour one.** The deep source is `Mixup` (<https://github.com/Fabulu/Mixup>), a hand translation of the *DoDonPachi DaiOuJou Black Label* ROM into JavaScript in which every routine carries the 68000 address it was read from; its own limit is stated in its files, that the HP bar routine is transcribed from the ROM rather than observed running. All **DOCUMENTED at the transcription's own standard**, which is a decompile of a shipped binary rather than a wiki. The bar exists: the routine at `$284A3E` is labelled "THE BOSS HP BAR", follows a pointer at `$81B62A` to the boss record's HP longword and draws it, gated on `btst #$0,$8130F9`, "a boss is up, show his bar instead of the chain popup" (`games/ddpdoj/src/hud.js`). **This corrects a prior this record set out with**, that Cave hides boss health; it is true for no game we could check and false for the one we could.

Three mechanisms sit under it and each is worth having on its own terms. **Ordinary enemies flash by a palette XOR against a per-type mask**, so the sprite alternates every frame for as long as fire keeps landing and stops the frame it stops, and the code deliberately flashes from the base colour when the body is already wearing its low-HP palette, so the flash toggles around the damage tint instead of cancelling it (`src/handlers.js`). **The boss does not use that path: `$294AD8` XORs animation bytes instead**, `anim0 ^= $0C` and `anim3 ^= $09` on a hit frame, writing the rest-state bytes back on a non-hit frame, so the boss body alternates animation frames under fire while trash alternates colour (`src/boss.js`). **And the hit spark is its own pool**, pool E, "the little flash where a bullet CONNECTS", 60 records, freed when its cursor borrows, at most 36 frames, about 608 milliseconds at the IGS PGM's 59.19 Hz (`src/spark.js`, the refresh rate from MAME's `igs/pgm.cpp`, "framerate verified"). **DOCUMENTED.**

**The finding under all three is that there is no flash timer anywhere in DaiOuJou**, in any damage arm transcribed, because the mechanism is a per-frame XOR with nothing to time. That is the cleanest statement in the record of a tell that is a **state rather than a pulse**: it is on while fire lands and off when it stops, and it needs no duration. Cave's per-threshold tells are separate and are real: a permanent low-HP palette below a per-type threshold (three are tabulated, `$140`, `$380`, `$980`), a boss critical state at `cmp.l #$48CC` that writes the low-HP value into both animation bytes, a one-shot near-death audio latch at HP `<= $6FB8`, and destructible parts with their own health at boss record `+$16`, `+$1A` and `+$1E`, each worth `$1000` and a dropped item and a `$6E`-frame hit stop. **No source gives a flash duration for any Cave game**, and no Cave interview found discusses hit feedback at all; seven Ikeda translations on shmuplations were read and none of them touches it.

**Touhou's first game is the one shipped shmup whose boss hit tell is readable line by line, and it is a palette swap rather than a flash.** ReC98's decompile of *Highly Responsive to Prayers* (TH01, 1996) holds it in one function, `boss_hit_update_and_render` (<https://github.com/nmlgc/ReC98/blob/master/th01/main/boss/hit.cpp>, header at `th01/main/boss/boss.hpp`). All **DOCUMENTED**. On a hit the game calls `z_palette_flash_colors(invincibility_flash_colors, count, i)`, and that macro is defined as "Sets the given specific hardware colors to #FFF" (<https://github.com/nmlgc/ReC98/blob/master/th01/hardware/palette.h>). **The list is per boss and it is tiny: SinGyoku passes `const vc_t flash_colors[1] = { 13 }`, one palette index** (`th01/main/boss/b05.cpp`). So the tell is not a white-out of the body; it is one declared colour of the body going white and back, which is a tint swap on a named subset. Beside it the same function plays a sound, `mdrv2_se_play(3)`, adds `hit_score` (3000 for SinGyoku), and calls `hud_hp_decrement(hp)`, so **the hit announces on three channels at once: the boss's own colour, a sound, and one discrete unit leaving a HUD gauge.**

Its cadence is stated too, and it is fast. `BOSS_HIT_INVINCIBILITY_FRAMES = 40`, and inside that window the function flashes on `(invincibility_frame % 4) == 2` and restores on `% 4 == 0`, so the boss alternates two frames white and two frames normal for the whole window. **DOCUMENTED.** ReC98's contributing guide gives the PC-98's native rate as 56.423 FPS (<https://github.com/nmlgc/ReC98/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md>), **DOCUMENTED**, so **INFERRED**: the window is 0.71 seconds and the alternation runs at about 14 a second. That is far past WCAG's three in one second, and it is only survivable because one palette index on one sprite is a small area. Said plainly because it cuts against this game's own rules rather than for them: **the shipped shmup precedent for a boss hit tell is a fast repeating flash, and this game cannot have it.**

**And the one that matters most here: in Touhou 4 and 5 the main boss does not flash at all.** ReC98's disassembly renders the white silhouette from `enemy_t.E_damaged_this_frame` in `th04/main/enemy/render.asm` for ordinary enemies, and `midboss_put_generic` does the same for a midboss, but `boss.damage_this_frame` is written in `boss_hittest_shots` and read in exactly one place, a Shinki-specific health cap, and **nothing renders from it**. ReC98 is a complete disassembly, so that negative is meaningful. **DOCUMENTED.** The boss's per-hit tell is therefore **the sound plus the HUD bar and nothing on the body**: `th04/main/boss/boss.hpp` says the hit test "plays the given sound effect if that amount is nonzero", SE 4 for a hit and SE 10 for a hit on an invincible boss. And the per-threshold tell lives on the bar rather than the boss: `HUD_HP_COLORS` is red, magenta, cyan, yellow, white, indexed by `BAR_MAX / (HUD_HP_COLOR_COUNT - 1)`, so **32 bar units per colour band** over a `BAR_MAX` of 128, and the bar drops instantly on a hit but refills one unit a frame (`th04/main/hud/hp.cpp`, `hp[data].asm`). **DOCUMENTED.** ZUN shipped a boss with no body tell whatever, in the games immediately after the one that flashed its palette fourteen times a second.

**Windows Touhou flashes for exactly one frame and then locks the flash out for four.** From the matching decompilation at <https://github.com/GensokyoClub/th06>, `src/EnemyManager.cpp`: on a frame where life fell, the game plays a damage sound, sets `primaryVm.flags.colorOp = AnmVmColorOp_Add` and sets `unk_e41 = 4`; on any frame where `unk_e41` is nonzero it decrements it and sets `colorOp = Modulate` instead. `AnmVmColorOp_Add` maps to `D3DTOP_ADD` and the enemy colour defaults to `0xffffffff`, so the sprite renders as a blown-out white silhouette. All **DOCUMENTED**. **INFERRED** from those constants and the file's own `FRAME_TIME (1000. / 60.)`: **the additive draw lasts one frame, 16.7 milliseconds, and cannot re-arm for five, so at most twelve a second**, with the hit sound throttled on the same branch. Two details ride with it: there is **no `isBoss` guard**, so a boss flashes exactly as a trash enemy does, and the whole colour-op change is skipped under two config bits, so **the flash can be configured off**.

**Touhou's boss gauge eases rather than snaps, and by TH08 it is genuinely segmented.** `src/Gui.cpp` in the same decompile chases the true value with `bossHealthBar2 += 0.01f` when filling and `-= 0.02f` when draining, so **INFERRED** at 60 Hz, a hit never snaps the bar: it drains at most 2% of full a frame, 50 frames from full to empty, and refills at 1% a frame. Segmentation in TH06 is not in the bar at all; it comes from the script opcode `ENEMYLIFESET` re-basing the boss's life to full for each attack. TH08 makes it explicit: `src/Gui.hpp` declares `MAX_BOSS_LIFEBAR_SEGMENTS 8` with per-segment start, stop and colour arrays, drawn as up to eight coloured spans over a white base and clipped to the bar's current length (<https://github.com/GensokyoClub/th08>). All **DOCUMENTED**.

## 2. Three action games whose hit tell is readable as shipped code

These three are the strongest evidence in the record because the constants are in the shipped build rather than in a wiki.

**Enter the Gungeon: a 40-millisecond white override, a 200-millisecond lockout, and a brightness clamp deliberately lifted for the duration.** All from `HealthHaver.cs` in the `fedes1to/EtG-source` decompile (<https://github.com/fedes1to/EtG-source/blob/main/ExportedProject/Assets/MonoScript/Assembly-CSharp/HealthHaver.cs>), **DOCUMENTED**. `c_flashTime = 0.04f`, `c_flashDowntime = 0.2f`, and `flashesOnDamage` defaults true. `FlashOnHit` sets `_OverrideColor` to opaque white on every body sprite **and on its outline sprites**, then over 0.04 seconds lerps each one back from white to the actor's own colour, then waits `0.2f` before clearing `m_isFlashing`. A new flash cannot start while `m_isFlashing`, so **the tell is hard-limited to one per 240 milliseconds however fast the player shoots**, about 4.2 a second, and the hurt sound `Play_ENM_hurt` is posted inside the same coroutine so **the sound is rate-limited by the flash rather than by the hit**.

Two further facts from the same file matter more here than the duration. First, **the flash is a declared exception to a shipped brightness ceiling**: before setting the override the coroutine walks each material's `shaderKeywords`, and where it finds `"BRIGHTNESS_CLAMP_ON"` it disables that keyword, enables `BRIGHTNESS_CLAMP_OFF`, and re-enables the clamp when the flash ends. The keyword is real and on shipped materials: `ExportedProject/Assets/Material/shadow.mat` carries `m_ShaderKeywords: BRIGHTNESS_CLAMP_ON ...` and the glow materials carry the OFF form. **DOCUMENTED** that the clamp exists and that the hit flash lifts it; what the clamp actually clamps to is **not sourceable**, because the exported shaders in that repository carry no readable body. Second, **a boss can flash only the part that was struck**: `RegisterBodySprite(tk2dBaseSprite sprite, bool flashIndependentlyOnDamage = false, int flashPixelCollider = 0)` keys a sprite to one pixel collider, and `FlashOnHit` narrows `spritesToFlash` to that one sprite when the hit names that collider. **DOCUMENTED.** The same file also holds a boss-only damage rule, `c_bossDpsCapWindow = 3f` with a per-level `bossDpsCap` applied when `IsBoss && !IsSubboss`, which is shipped evidence that a boss gets its own damage arithmetic rather than the mob's.

**Nuclear Throne: the tell is a whole-body white-out sized per boss between 67 and 200 milliseconds, and the invulnerability window caps it at five a second.** The open rebuild from the decompiled Update 19 source is at <https://github.com/toarch7/nt-recreated-public>, and the game runs at 30 frames a second (`objects/UberCont/Step_0.gml`, `game_set_speed(30, gamespeed_fps)`). All **DOCUMENTED**. `scripts/scr_hit/scr_hit.gml` is the whole of the ordinary tell: on any damaging hit it sets `nexthurt = current_frame + 6`, swaps `sprite_index = spr_hurt` with `image_index = 0`, and plays `snd_hurt`. Six frames is **200 milliseconds**, and `scr_can_hit` refuses a hit while `current_frame < nexthurt`, so nothing can be struck more than five times a second.

The bosses have no hurt sprite, so they draw the white a different way and **each one picks its own length**, which is the useful part:

| boss | shipped condition | frames white | milliseconds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technomancer | `nexthurt > current_frame` | 6 | 200 |
| Nothing (final boss) | `(nexthurt - current_frame) > 2` | 4 | 133 |
| Dog Guardian | `(nexthurt - current_frame) > 4` | 2 | 67 |
| Last | `sprite_index == spr_hurt && image_index < 1.5` | 3.75 at `image_speed` 0.4 | 125 |

All four are **DOCUMENTED**, read from `objects/<boss>/Draw_0.gml` in that repository; the millisecond column is **INFERRED** from the 30 FPS above. The mechanism is `gpu_set_fog(true, c_white, 0, 0)` around the draw, which in GameMaker paints the whole sprite one flat colour, so **on a boss it is a full silhouette white-out and not a tint**. Nothing draws eight sprites under one fog call, so its several body parts whiten together rather than per part, which is the opposite of Gungeon's choice.

**Hades: a 30-millisecond red tint plus a 150-millisecond shake, and the armoured variant swaps the colour and triples the length.** Hades ships its Lua uncompiled; the mirror read here is <https://github.com/zhang-wen-guang/Hades>, file `CombatPresentation.lua`. All **DOCUMENTED**. `DoUnitHitFlash` is one line of effect: `Flash({ Id = args.triggeredById, Speed = 0.85, MinFraction = 1.0, MaxFraction = 0.0, Color = Color.Red, Duration = 0.03, ExpireAfterCycle = true })`. **The tell is red rather than white, it falls from full to nothing, and it runs 30 milliseconds**, two ticks at this game's rate. `DoUnitShake` runs beside it on every ordinary hit: `Shake({ Distance = 6, Speed = 550, Duration = 0.15 })`, **150 milliseconds of positional jitter that is not brightness at all**. An armoured enemy takes `DoUnitArmorShake` instead: `Shake({ Distance = 10, Speed = 650, Duration = 0.15 })`, a white flash on the armour icon at `Duration = 0.15`, and a `Color.ArmorFlashGold` flash on the body at `Duration = 0.24`, plus `DoUnitArmorSpark`. So **the armoured body's tell is 1.67 times the shake distance and eight times the tint's length**, which is a shipped answer to "how do you say a hit landed on something that shrugged it off". One caution about that colour, found by reading the palette rather than the name: `Color.lua` defines `ArmorFlashGold = { 255, 255, 255, 255 }`, **pure white**, while `Red = { 220, 20, 20, 255 }`. **DOCUMENTED.** The armoured flash is named gold and is shipped white, so Hades is a hue shift on an ordinary hit and a white-out on an armoured one, and only the first half of it transfers here.

Hades' boss health readout carries a second reading of the same hit. `UIScripts.lua` builds `BossHealthBarFill` with a separate `BossHealthBarFillFalloff` in `Color.HealthFalloff` driven by the animation `EnemyHealthBarFillSlowBoss`, so **a hit shows as an instant step in the fill and a slower trailing chunk behind it**. **DOCUMENTED.**

## 3. The bullet heavens, where one game publishes everything and the others publish almost nothing

**Vampire Survivors gives an enemy no health bar at all, and makes both of the tells it does have optional.** The wiki's Options page transcribes the shipped menu (<https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Options>), **DOCUMENTED as a transcription of the shipped UI**: the Audio and Video sections carry **Damage Numbers**, "Enable or disable displaying damage numbers", and **Flashing VFX**, "Enable or disable the flashing effects", and the Gameplay section carries Damage Numbers again. The same page quotes two of poncle's own patch notes: patch 0.9.0's "Fixed option to hide damage numbers also affects recovery numbers", and a later one, "Pentagram flash now obeys Flashing VFX in the options". **The shipped position is therefore explicit: the damage number and the flash are both things a player may turn off, so neither is load-bearing.** Health bars in that game belong to the player and to two named exceptions; searching the wiki's whole text for "health bar" returns character and boss-script pages, never an enemy readout (**INFERRED** from the search, <https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Special:Search>).

**Brotato is the best-documented hit tell in the whole record, because its shipped GDScript is readable and its numbers are all in it.** The decompile read is <https://github.com/Yeet195/BrotatoDecompiled>, whose own export log records GDRE Tools run against a retail `Brotato.pck`, at `VERSION = "1.1.14.5"`. All **DOCUMENTED**. Six facts, and the shape they make together is the point:

- **The flash is a full white replacement, not a tint, and it runs 100 milliseconds.** `resources/shaders/flash.gdshader` does `color.rgb = mix(color.rgb, flash_color.rgb, flash_modifier)`, and `unit.tscn` ships `flash_color = Color(1,1,1,1)` with `flash_modifier = 1.0` and a `FlashTimer` at `wait_time = 0.1`. At Godot 3's default 60 Hz that is **6 frames**.
- **It is per hit, on any damage above zero, and it does not stack: it extends.** `unit.gd` calls `flash()` whenever `full_dmg_value > 0`, and `flash()` checks `is_already_flashing` and only restarts the timer. **Under sustained fire the body is simply white rather than flashing**, which is the same answer Cave's XOR reaches from the other direction: the tell is a state, and a state has no flicker rate.
- **The spark is 150 milliseconds and is the thing that gets budgeted away, never the flash.** `hit_effect.tscn` is three frames at `speed = 20.0`, and `effects_manager.gd` plays it only `if randf() < effect_scale`, a per-weapon `export` that ships at **0.1 on the minigun's top tiers, the flamethrower and the top gatling laser**, 0.2 and 0.5 on the SMG and Shredder, and 1.0 on the other 275 entries, under a global ceiling of 100 concurrent effects. **The cheap per-hit confirmation stays reliable at any fire rate and only the expensive decoration is thinned.**
- **The spark is placed by the body's own size**, at `unit.global_position + knockback_direction * (sprite.texture.get_width() * 0.25)`, so a big enemy's spark is thrown out toward its own edge on the incoming side rather than sitting at its centre.
- **Only bosses get a health bar, it is a single continuous `TextureProgress` with no ticks, and the bar itself flashes white for 200 milliseconds on every damage event.** `enemy.tscn` has no bar node at all; `boss.gd` instances one 64 by 16 pixels sitting above the body, and `ui_progress_bar.gd` sets `tint_progress = Color.white` on a change that is not healing, for a `one_shot` timer at `wait_time = 0.2`. **So a Brotato boss carries two synchronised per-hit tells, a 100-millisecond white body and a 200-millisecond white bar**, and the bar's is twice as long. Steam corroborates the arrival: "Bosses now have HP bars", patch 0.5.10, 2022-09-29 (<https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1942280/view/4588629064111041737>), and the toggle to switch them off in 0.6.0.7.
- **The phase change fires on a health threshold or a timer, whichever comes first**, `current_stats.health <= max_stats.health * hp_start or elapsed_time >= timer_start`, shipped at 0.75 then 0.40 with 30 and 60 second backstops for the Invoker and 0.50 for the Predator, announced by a voice line and a floating "MUTATION" label. **Nothing in Brotato ties any tell to a hit count, and nothing sheds parts.**

**Halls of Torment ships its flash intensity as a slider with an off position, and publishes nothing else.** The developer's notes say "It's possible to adjust the flash effect on enemies from the settings" (<https://steamcommunity.com/app/2218750/announcements/detail/5151600576606544664>), that it is one global value other effects scale against, "Radiant Aura flash intensity now scales with the enemy flash intensity" (<https://steamcommunity.com/app/2218750/announcements/detail/6300039510119857905>), and that it can be taken to zero: "Turning off flash intensity also deactivates the flashes when the player is hit by enemies" (<https://steamcommunity.com/app/2218750/announcements/detail/6931694080291548684>). All **DOCUMENTED as developer statements**. **No duration, no colour and no per-hit rule is published anywhere**, across all 111 of that game's Steam news items, and there is no readable resource dump.

**The one thing Halls of Torment does publish is the most interesting single answer in this section, and it is not a visual at all.** Its monster scenes carry a `KillAfterNumberOfHits` property beside `StartHealth` and `ApplyDamage`, and two entities set it: the Possessed Effigy at 20 and a gold slime at 25 paired with a health value of 999,999,999 (<https://github.com/barnden/HoT/blob/master/output/monsters.tsv>, a derived transcription, **COMMUNITY-MEASURED**). The community wiki supplies the reason, verbatim: the Possessed Effigy has "a Damage Factor of 0.1%, which means that all damage is reduced to 0.1% against them", it has "a Base Health of 4 400, but will die after 21 hits", and it "doesn't move when looked at by the character" (<https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Possessed_Effigy>), **COMMUNITY-MEASURED and community-run, not the developer's wiki**. **So a shipped survivors-like has exactly this game's case, a large body that does not move, and it answered the tell by making the damage number meaningless on purpose and counting hits instead.** Note the off-by-one before anyone copies it: the shipped figure is 20 and the wiki says it dies on the twenty-first hit, so the number is a threshold the next hit crosses.

## 4. Partial knockback: the shipped ratios, big body to small

This is the better-sourced of the two questions, because three of these games ship the number.

**Enter the Gungeon ships a per-enemy weight, its reference value, and the same enemy at two sizes, so the ratio is read rather than estimated.** `KnockbackDoer.cs` (<https://github.com/fedes1to/EtG-source/blob/main/ExportedProject/Assets/MonoScript/Assembly-CSharp/KnockbackDoer.cs>) declares `public float weight = 10f` and applies every knockback as `direction.normalized * (force / (weight / 10f))`, on all four `ApplyKnockback` overloads and on the continuous ones. **So displacement is inversely proportional to weight, and weight 10 is unity.** The same file caps an AI actor's knockback velocity at `MAX_ENEMY_KNOCKBACK_MAGNITUDE = 30f` after summing, which is a shipped cap sitting on top of the ratio, and `SetImmobile(bool, string reason)` is a named-override stack that a dozen behaviours use to switch knockback off for the length of a scripted move (`TetherBehavior`, `DisplaceBehavior`, `SummonEnemyBehavior`, `DeflectBulletsBehavior`, `BuffEnemiesBehavior` and others). All **DOCUMENTED**. One item makes the size-to-weight link explicit: `ShrinkEnemiesInRoomItem.cs` does `target.knockbackDoer.weight /= 3f`, so **shrinking an enemy makes it exactly three times easier to push**. **DOCUMENTED.**

The weights are on the shipped prefabs and read straight out of them (`ExportedProject/Assets/Asset_Bundles/enemies_base_001/data/enemies/`), all **DOCUMENTED**:

| body | weight | push against a Bullat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bullat (the small flier) | 10 | 1.00 |
| Blobulin, Poisbulin (small blob) | 18 | 0.56 |
| Blobuloid, Poisbulon (mid blob) | 25 | 0.40 |
| Bullat_Giant, Cubulon, Cubulead | 30 | 0.33 |
| BulletMan (the basic mook), GripMaster | 35 | 0.29 |
| Blobulon (big blob), BulletShark, Poisbuloid | 40 | 0.25 |
| BulletManTank | 45 | 0.22 |
| MushroomGuy, Killithid | 80 | 0.13 |
| **Bullet King, Blobulord, Mineflayer, Lich (bosses)** | **100** | **0.10** |
| BulletmanTitan | 120 | 0.08 |
| LeadMaiden | 130 | 0.08 |
| Shelleton | 200 | 0.05 |
| **Demon Wall (boss)** | **10000** | **0.001** |
| **Beholster, Gatling Gull (bosses)** | **100000** | **0.0001** |

Three readings matter. **The cleanest big-to-small ratio in this whole record is Bullat 10 against Bullat_Giant 30: one body and its giant variant, by the prefab's own name, and the giant takes exactly one third of the small one's push.** The blob family gives a second reading across three sizes, 18 to 25 to 40, so the largest takes 0.45 of the smallest. And **Gungeon does not treat "boss" as one answer**: four of its bosses sit at weight 100, which is a real 0.29 of the basic mook's push rather than nothing, while three sit at 10000 and 100000, which is a refusal written as a number. **INFERRED**, and it is the finding that matters for Mark's ruling: a shipped roguelite ships both a partial resistance and a practical immunity, chosen per body, out of one mechanism.

**Vampire Survivors ships a taken-multiplier and a separate cap, and the ordinary values are 1 and 3.** The game's `knockback` and `knockback-max` fields per enemy are transcribed on the wiki and the wiki's own module states the thresholds it sorts by: `Module:Statbox Enemy` adds an enemy to "Knockback resistant enemies" when `knockback < 1` or `knockback_max < 3` (<https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Module:Statbox_Enemy>). **COMMUNITY-MEASURED for the values, DOCUMENTED that 1 and 3 are the unresisted pair**, since that is what the sorting rule treats as the baseline. The mechanic is stated on <https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Knockback>: "the product of the damaging weapon's knockback dealt multiplier and knockback taken multiplier of the target enemy", for 120 milliseconds, and Garlic and Soul Eater raise the taken multiplier by 0.3 per hit "until its maximum knockback value is reached", which is the cap doing work. Transcribed values, all **COMMUNITY-MEASURED**:

| enemy | knockback taken | cap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skeleton (ordinary trash) | 1 | 3 |
| Zombie | 0.8 | 3 |
| Boar Infantry | 0.6 | - |
| Skeleton Ape | 0.4 | 2 |
| Kamaitachi, The Creature, Njuggles | 0.3 | 1 |
| Akmodan II | 0.3 | 1 |
| Disc Armor | 0.25 | 1.25 |
| Beelzebub | 0 | 1 |
| Bat Dragon | 0 (listed as a resistance) | - |

**So Vampire Survivors' resisting-but-not-immune band is 0.25 to 0.8 of an ordinary body, clustered at 0.3, and the cap is usually cut from 3 to about 1 at the same time.** **INFERRED** from the table. ADR 0007 cites this game for "bosses and elites resist or ignore knockback"; the data says both halves of that sentence are true of different bodies, and that the resisting half is a fraction near a third rather than a token.

**Risk of Rain 2 divides by mass, publishes one pair of masses, and lets a caller bypass the divisor entirely.** The decompiled `RoR2/CharacterMotor.cs` (read at <https://github.com/WarmBuns/PoP2/blob/main/RoR2/CharacterMotor.cs>) declares `public float mass = 1f` and `ApplyForceImpulse` does `if (!forceInfo.massIsOne) { force *= 1f / mass; } ... velocity += force;`. **DOCUMENTED.** The `massIsOne` flag is a shipped escape hatch: a caller that wants a fixed displacement regardless of the body asks for one. The wiki's Damage article states the rule and the one published ratio: "The final knockback applied to the victim is the quotient of the attack's force vector and the victim's mass", and "Acrid only suffers half as much knockback as Loader, since Acrid has 300 mass compared to Loader's 150" (<https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Damage>). **COMMUNITY-MEASURED**, and the same page states a shipped floor worth noting: if the y component of the result is under 6 and the victim is grounded, it is zeroed, so **the push is flattened to purely horizontal below a threshold**. Per-monster masses are not published anywhere found; see section 7.

**Hades has no fraction at all: its answer is a boolean toggled around the authored move.** `ImmuneToForce` is a Thing property set true and back through `SetThingProperty` in `EnemyAI.lua`, saved and restored around a leap (`local immuneToForceReset = GetThingDataValue(...)`, lines 2089 to 2121) and set for the length of other scripted states; `Powers.lua` and `ShoutScripts.lua` do the same to the player. **DOCUMENTED** (<https://github.com/zhang-wen-guang/Hades/blob/main/EnemyAI.lua>). There is no weight, mass or resistance field in the shipped Lua: `Weight` in `EnemyData.lua` is a spawn-cap weight (`ActiveCapWeight`), and the only per-enemy force figure is `DeathForce`, which launches the corpse and runs 900 to 1400 by enemy. **So Hades' precedent is not a ratio; it is the exact shape ADR 0007 already chose, immunity scoped to the window in which a pattern would smear.**

**Nuclear Throne has no mass term whatever, and its size variable does the opposite job.** `scr_projectile_hit` applies knockback as a bare `motion_add(_kb_direction, _kb_amount)` on the victim with nothing per body in the path, and `scr_explosion_generic_hit` pushes every victim by a flat 6 and clamps the result with `if (speed > 16) speed = 16`, a cap applied to everything alike. **DOCUMENTED** (<https://github.com/toarch7/nt-recreated-public/blob/main/scripts/scr_hit/scr_hit.gml>). The `size` field, which runs 0 for a Maggot through 1 for most trash, 2 and 3 for heavies and mid-bosses, 5 to 8 for the big bosses (`objects/*/Create_0.gml`), never divides an incoming push. It decides collision precedence (`objects/enemy/Collision_enemy.gml`, `objects/enemy/Collision_prop.gml`) and how hard the body pushes **the player**: `objects/Player/Collision_enemy.gml` does `motion_add(point_direction(...), other.size * 0.5)`. **DOCUMENTED.** So Nuclear Throne's big bodies are not hard to push; they are hard to stand next to, and the only brake on knockback in the game is the shared 16-speed cap. That is a real counterexample to the mass model and worth having.

**Brotato ships the formula, the whole ladder, and a documented decision to spread that ladder rather than keep a binary. It is the closest precedent in the record to the ruling being sized.** From the same decompile, `entities/units/unit/unit.gd`, all **DOCUMENTED**:

```gdscript
func get_knockback_value() -> Vector2:
	return (knockback_vector * (100 - (stats.knockback_resistance * 100)))
```

`knockback_resistance` is an `export (float, 0.0, 1.0, 0.05)` defaulting to 0, so **push scales exactly as `1 - resistance`**, and the push decays by 10% a physics frame (`linear_interpolate(Vector2.ZERO, 0.1)`), about 12% left after 20 frames. The shipped ladder, read from the `.tres` files:

| resistance | push against an ordinary enemy | who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.00 | 1.00 | chaser, pursuer, fly, healer, looter and the rest of the trash |
| 0.20 to 0.25 | 0.80, 0.75 | anglerfish, lobster |
| 0.50 | 0.50 | helmet alien, junkie, crab, hermit, stargazer |
| 0.60 to 0.75 | 0.40 to 0.25 | anemone, spawner, tentacle, pufferfish, colossal squid |
| 0.80 | 0.20 | charger, horned charger |
| 0.90 | 0.10 | bruiser, horned bruiser, slasher, walrus |
| 0.95 | 0.05 | spitter, lamprey, buffer |
| 1.00 | 0.00 | **every elite and every boss** |

**And the decision behind it is published.** Update 1.1's notes read "Junkies: 0% Knockback resistance => 50%", "Small Chargers: 100% => 80%", "Buffers, Lampreys, Shooters: 100% => 95%", "Slashers: 100% => 90%" (<https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1942280/view/6212245217911850965>), and every one of those matches the shipped file. **DOCUMENTED.** Before that patch the non-boss heavies were all at total immunity and the developer deliberately moved them down into a graded band while moving a small enemy up from zero, **and kept the binary only for elites and bosses.** That is the exact division Mark's ruling draws, reached independently by a shipped game: the boss stays at nothing and the big non-boss body gets a fraction.

Three further Brotato facts are worth carrying. **Immunity cannot be pierced but resistance can**: `get_increased_piercing_knockback_amount` carries an explicit `if resistance == 1: return amount` guard ahead of its arithmetic, so a knockback-immune body is immune to the stat that beats resistance too. **There is a mass term and it deliberately does not touch hit knockback**: the entity is a `RigidBody2D` and a boss ships `mass = 100.0` against Godot's default 1.0, but `unit.gd` writes `state.linear_velocity` directly inside `_integrate_forces`, so **mass governs only body-against-body shoving in a crowd and never the response to a hit**, two dials for two jobs. And the one nobody would predict: `enemy.gd` disables the enemy's own hitbox while `get_knockback_value().length_squared() > get_move_input().length_squared()`, so **being knocked back switches an enemy's contact damage off**, which makes a knockback-immune boss not merely unmoved but still dangerous while everything around it has stopped hurting you.

**Halls of Torment's knockback is a gate rather than a fraction, so its ratio is 0 or 1 and never in between.** The community wiki's mechanics page states the four stats verbatim: Knockback Power on the attack, Knockback Force for the magnitude, Knockback Resistance which "determines the minimum threshold for the enemy to be affected by Knockback", and Movement Force which "determines how quickly the enemy recovers", with the rule "If an attack's Knockback Power is at least equal to the enemy's Knockback Resistance, the Knockback Force is applied to them" (<https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics>). **COMMUNITY-MEASURED**, on a community-run wiki, and its worked example checks out arithmetically against its own stated conversion of 18 pixels to the metre, which is why it is here at all. **So a big enemy in Halls of Torment is either not pushed or pushed exactly as far as a small one, and the gradation lives in a second stat, how fast it walks the push off.** No per-enemy resistance value is published for any enemy, so no ratio can be derived; see section 7. What the developer does publish is the shape of its neighbouring resistance, for slow rather than knockback: "Resistance: champion (25%), elite (50%), boss (66%), lord (80%). Limit: champion (30%), elite, boss, lord (50%)" (<https://steamcommunity.com/app/2218750/announcements/detail/5229301621590571094>). **DOCUMENTED.** Two terms rather than one, a percentage that climbs by tier and **a separate absolute ceiling that stops differentiating at the top**, elite and boss and lord all sharing one cap. Whether knockback takes the same shape is unsourced.

**Diablo 2 holds the distance fixed and scales the chance, and its size class is a flag in the shipped data.** The Amazon Basin's mechanics article states the distance rule outright: a target "can be knocked back a distance of up to three sub-tiles when unobstructed", and "This distance cannot be increased", the chance being "fixed by the target" and checked once per successful attack (<https://www.theamazonbasin.com/wiki/index.php?title=Knockback&action=raw>, whose TLS certificate has expired so it needs `curl -k`). The Phrozen Keep file guide for `MonStats2.txt` documents the two shipped booleans: `small` is "whether the unit can be knocked back with 100% efficiency", `large` is "whether the unit can be knocked back with only 25% efficiency", and `mKB` decides whether the unit can be knocked back at all (<https://d2mods.info/forum/kb/viewarticle?a=359>). **DOCUMENTED as a transcription of the shipped file.** The file itself bears this out: parsing the 1.13 `MonStats2.txt` (<https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fabd/diablo2/master/code/d2_113_data/MonStats2.txt>) partitions 610 rows into 75 small, 361 medium and 174 large, and **every act boss is `large=1` with `mKB` blank**, so the boss immunity is the animation flag rather than a separate boss rule. **DOCUMENTED.** The 50% middle tier is named only by the Basin and by Maxroll (<https://maxroll.gg/d2/resources/crowd-control>), never by the file guide, so it is **COMMUNITY-MEASURED** and weaker than the ends. **INFERRED**: with distance fixed, expected displacement scales as the chance alone, so **the big body takes 0.25 of the small body's push and 0.5 of the medium one's**. Blizzard itself never published any of this: the Arreat Summit has no knockback mechanics page (<https://classic.battle.net/diablo2exp/monsters/basics.shtml>) and no Resurrected patch note changes the mechanic.

**Diablo 3 refuses the fraction entirely and ships a distance floor instead, which is the strongest single piece of design evidence in this section.** Blizzard's own Game Guide, archived 2014-08-11, says it in one sentence: "If you attempt to knockback that same boss and can't hit it over 21 yards, it won't budge an inch", and its Monster Resistance Values table gives a **Knockback Minimum of 10 for a Champion or Minion and 21 for a Rare or a Boss** (<https://web.archive.org/web/20140811043908/http://us.battle.net/d3/en/game/guide/gameplay/game-difficulty>). **DOCUMENTED, and it is Blizzard's own words.** Normal monsters are absent from the table, so **INFERRED** their floor is zero, and **INFERRED** a rare or boss needs 2.1 times the push a champion needs before it moves at all. The neighbouring crowd-control types on the same table **are** fractional, which is the tell that the binary treatment of knockback was a choice: move slow resistance 25%, attack speed 65%, stun 65% for champion and rare and 70% for a boss. By the 2015 rework the knockback minimum column is gone and the rule became "When a monster's Hard CC resistance reaches 65% or greater, Knockback will no longer have any affect" (<https://web.archive.org/web/20150905061909/http://us.battle.net/d3/en/game/guide/gameplay/game-difficulty>), which is the same all-or-nothing shape reached a second way. Wyatt Cheng's crowd-control developer journal of 2012-09-05 never mentions knockback at all (<https://web.archive.org/web/20130601000000/http://us.battle.net/d3/en/blog/7100052/>), because that system is about duration and a knockback has none. **So Diablo 3 is a counterexample worth naming: a shipped game that priced a fractional push for a big body and shipped a threshold instead.**

**Minecraft is the cleanest published fraction, and it is the one game in the record that switched from Diablo's model to this one on purpose.** The attribute `knockback_resistance` has default 0, minimum -2, maximum 1, and "determines the proportion of horizontal knockback from attacks and projectiles an entity resists" (<https://minecraft.wiki/w/Attribute>, read as `?action=raw`). The formula is stated: with velocity `v` and resistance `r%`, "the mob's actual velocity is determined by the formula `A = v x (1 - r/100)`" (<https://minecraft.wiki/w/Knockback_(mechanic)>). Shipped base values: Iron Golem and Warden 1, Ravager 0.75 (the mechanic page says 70% in Java and 75% in Bedrock, an unreconciled disagreement between two wiki pages), Hoglin and Zoglin 0.6, Squid 0.85, and the zombie family 0 to 5% rolled at spawn. **DOCUMENTED as a transcription of the game's own attribute data.** **INFERRED** from a stated base melee knockback of 1.552 blocks: a Ravager travels 0.388 blocks where an ordinary mob travels 1.552. And the history entries are the argument rather than the number: version 1.16 made the attribute "decrease the knockback taken from attacks, in addition to its existing effect of giving a random chance to ignore all knockback", and 1.18.2 removed the chance roll outright. **Mojang shipped Diablo's chance model, then replaced it with a velocity multiplier.** That is the clearest statement anywhere in this record of which of the two models a live team preferred once it had both.

## 5. Which of these run under a brightness constraint, and the three shipped answers

Only one game in the record has a technical ceiling like this one's, and it lifted the ceiling rather than lowering the flash. **Enter the Gungeon runs a brightness clamp and makes the hit flash its declared exception**: `BRIGHTNESS_CLAMP_ON` is a shader keyword on shipped materials, and `FlashOnHit` walks each material, disables it for the 40 milliseconds of the flash and re-enables it after. **DOCUMENTED.** This game cannot take that escape, because ADR 0014's ceiling binds "every colour drawn while the field is live" and the exemption it grants covers only colours that never draw while the field is live.

What the rest of the record offers instead is **three shipped answers to the same pressure**, and what each one costs here.

**One, make the tell a state rather than a pulse, so it has no flicker rate to breach.** DoDonPachi DaiOuJou's XOR is on while fire lands and off when it stops and has no timer anywhere; Brotato's 100-millisecond flash does not stack but restarts, so **under sustained fire a Brotato enemy is simply white rather than flashing**. Both **DOCUMENTED**. The limit here is that a toll's contact is one tick by construction, the cone's leading edge marking each body in `toll.struck` exactly once, so there is no sustained contact to hold a state through; the state answer belongs to the always-on lines rather than to the bell.

**Two, move the tell off the body and onto a readout.** Touhou 4 and 5 do it exclusively, the boss carrying no body tell at all and announcing on a sound plus a HUD bar, with the per-threshold reading carried by the bar's five colour bands rather than by the boss. Brotato does it additionally, its boss bar flashing white for 200 milliseconds against the body's 100. Both **DOCUMENTED.** ADR 0054 explicitly permits a readout that announces "by count, by shape or by subtraction", so this is the one shipped answer the rules already bless, and the only reason it is not an option below is that **this game has no boss readout at all today** and adding one is a larger decision than a tell.

**Three, ship the off switch.** Vampire Survivors' "Flashing VFX: Enable or disable the flashing effects"; Halls of Torment's flash intensity slider, which the developer says other effects scale against and which can be taken to zero; and Touhou 6's colour-op change, skipped entirely under either of two config bits. All **DOCUMENTED.** **No game found treats its hit flash as mandatory**, which is worth knowing before this one treats its tell as load-bearing.

**Two figures close the section.** First, **no shipped game found rate-limits its hit tell below three a second**: Touhou 6 allows twelve, Touhou 1 runs at about fourteen, Nuclear Throne five, Gungeon 4.2, and Cave alternates at the frame rate. This game's toll fires once every 180 ticks, **0.33 a second**, so it can afford a longer and quieter tell than any of them and never approaches the threshold that forced ADR 0040's rule. Second, **the shipped tell is never one channel**: Gungeon pairs colour with sound, Nuclear Throne pairs a sprite swap with sound, Touhou 1 pairs a palette swap with a sound and a HUD decrement, Touhou 4 drops the colour and keeps the other two, Hades adds a shake, and Brotato runs a flash, a spark, a damage number, a randomised sound from an eight-clip bank and a damage-scaled screen shake at once. **Not one game in the record announces a hit on one channel alone.**

## 6. Options

Every figure below is a first-pass data row for the tuning step, not a compiled constant, and the arithmetic that converts each one is stated beside it.

### Question 1: the tell on a toll landing on a boss

The boss already sheds pieces at phase breaks and the shed armour is ruled (decision log entry 4.5, and Mark's ruling of 2026-09-15), so the per-toll tell only has to carry the tolls that shed nothing. **The fourth shipped answer, a boss readout that reacts per hit, is left out of the options on purpose**: two games in the record use it and one of them uses nothing else, ADR 0054 already permits a readout that announces by subtraction, and this game has no boss readout, so taking that answer means deciding to have one rather than deciding a tell.

**Option A, the inversion at a hit's length: five ticks.** A toll landing sets the boss's existing inverted read for a short count, the body taking the pair's dark half and the detail and rim taking the bright one, which is exactly what `bossFlashInverted` already does, at a hit's length rather than a phase's. **Five ticks, 83 milliseconds, is the median of the eight shipped per-hit colour changes in this record**, which run 1 tick (Touhou 6's single additive frame), 1.8 (Hades), 2.4 (Gungeon), 4 (Nuclear Throne's Dog Guardian), 6 (Brotato), 7.5 (Nuclear Throne's Last), 8 (Nuclear Throne's Nothing) and 12 (Nuclear Throne's Technomancer) when each is restated at 60 a second. The long half of that band is affordable here in a way it is not there, because a toll lands 0.33 times a second against Nuclear Throne's five and Gungeon's 4.2. It introduces no colour, so ADR 0014's ceiling is untouched by construction, and it keeps the bright rim, which matters: `bossSprite.ts` says in its own comment that "a dark body needs a bright edge, so the rim goes to the pair's own body colour on the inverted beat rather than staying near-black on near-black", **so a plain darkening of the body without the rim swap is a shape this file has already refused**. ADR 0040 is the analogy here rather than the authority, since it rules how a hit on the player announces; what binds a hit on a boss is ADR 0014's ceiling and ADR 0054's no-brightness rule. The cost is real: **the phase break owns this same lever**, inverting the same pair for `PHASE_FLASH_TICKS` 30 at `FLASH_HALF_PERIOD` 12, so the toll's tell and the phase's would differ only in length, and five ticks against thirty is a thin discriminator at a glance.

**Option B, the shake: one rim width for nine ticks.** The drawn body jitters around its own position by up to `BOSS_STROKE`, 4 field units, 2.9 CSS pixels on a phone, for 9 ticks, 150 milliseconds. **The duration is Hades' own**, `Shake({ Distance = 6, Speed = 550, Duration = 0.15 })` on an ordinary hit and `Distance = 10` on an armoured one, and 0.15 seconds is 9 ticks here, taken unchanged; Brotato shakes for the same 0.1 to 0.15 band and scales it by the damage number under a cap of 3. **The amplitude is not Hades'**, because that game publishes no unit scale, so it comes from this repository's own ruler instead: `BOSS_STROKE` is the thinnest line deliberately drawn on a boss, so one rim width is the smallest displacement certain to be seen on the body that moves it. Brightness rules do not bind it at all, and ADR 0007 is untouched because the sim position never changes: `BossRenderer.sync` reads `boss.x` and `boss.y` and the pattern's emitters keep reading the same place. It is also the only candidate that **adds** a channel rather than sharing one, since the boss's colour is already spoken for. Three costs are real. The drawn body leaves its hitbox by up to 4 units, 3% of its width, for 150 milliseconds. The renderer is a dumb view with no per-tick state, so the countdown has to live on the sim's `Boss` record beside `flash`, or a replay redrawing a pinned tape draws a boss that is not shaking (ADR 0015). And Hades and Brotato are the only sources for a shake at all, so its magnitude is the least-evidenced figure in this record.

**Option C, both, which is what every shipped game does.** A two-tick inversion on the tick the cone reaches the body, where Hades' 0.03 seconds and Gungeon's 0.04 both land, carried by the nine-tick shake. Two channels, the short colour beat marking the instant of contact and the longer motion carrying it far enough to be seen. Its cost is two rows to tune instead of one, and it spends the boss's colour channel on the toll as well as on the phase break.

**The evidence favours option C**, because not one game in the record announces a hit on a single channel and the channel the others lean on is unavailable here: this game ships five clips and none is a hit landing on a body, `swallow`, `treasure`, `toll`, `hit` (the grave taking a hit, not dealing one) and `eruption` (`src/app/sound.ts`, **DOCUMENTED (this repository)**), and a sixth clip on a toll landing would fire on the same tick as the toll's own clip and read as one noise rather than two. If the phase break's discriminator turns out to matter more than the colour beat, **option B alone is the safer single**, because the shake is the one channel nothing else on a boss is using.

### Question 2: the fraction of a mob's push the Waking's source takes

The push at a target is `row.push * (1 - distance / row.reach)`, so every figure below is the straight-below case, the grave directly under the source, where the whole of the push is up-field; a grave standing off to one side splits the same figure between up-field and sideways. **The reversal line is 19 units.** Over the 30 ticks a shove runs, the source drifts half a second at 38 a second, so **a push under 19 units slows the source's descent and a push over it makes the source visibly go backwards**. That line assumes the drift keeps running under a shove; if the source's drift is stood down the way a mob's walk is, every push reverses and the line disappears.

| fraction | rung 1 | rung 5 | rung 5 on a phone | rung 5 against the source's own 60-unit height | three rung-5 tolls against the pour's 285-unit descent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1/5** | 11.0 units, slows | 18.0 units, **still only slows** | 13 px | 0.30 | 54 units, 19% |
| **1/3** | 18.3 units, slows | 30.0 units, reverses by 11 | 22 px | 0.50 | 90 units, 32% |
| **0.45** | 24.8 units, reverses by 6 | 40.5 units, reverses by 21 | 29 px | 0.68 | 122 units, 43% |

**Option 1, one third.** **Derived from Enter the Gungeon's Bullat at weight 10 against Bullat_Giant at weight 30**, one body and its giant variant with push as `force / (weight / 10)`, so the giant takes exactly a third. Corroborated three ways: Vampire Survivors' resisting-but-not-immune band clusters at 0.3 and cuts the knockback cap from 3 to 1 at the same time, which is a third again; Minecraft's Hoglin sits at 0.4 and its Ravager at 0.25; and Brotato's own mid band, anemone through colossal squid, runs 0.4 down to 0.25. At this field's scale a rung-1 toll gives 18.3 units, four percent under the reversal line, so **the bottom rung holds the source still and does not push it back**; a rung-5 toll gives 30 units of gross impulse against the 19 the source drifts down in the same half second, so it **nets 11 units backwards**, 8 CSS pixels, a fifth of the source's own 60-unit height. Over the pour's 7.5 seconds the pour point falls 285 units and two or three tolls land, so a maxed bell gives back about a third of the descent.

**Option 2, 0.45.** **Derived from Gungeon's blob family across three sizes**, Blobulin 18 to Blobuloid 25 to Blobulon 40, the widest same-creature span the shipped prefabs offer, and corroborated by the 0.5 tier that three separate games ship: Diablo 2's medium size class, Risk of Rain 2's Acrid against Loader, and Brotato's helmet alien, junkie, crab, hermit and stargazer. It is the only option where **every rung reverses the source**, rung 1 included at 24.8 against the 19-unit line, so the ladder reads as more rather than as different in kind. Its cost is that rung 5 is 40.5 gross, netting **21.5 units backwards** after the drift, a third of the source's own 60-unit height in half a second; the gross figure is most of the way to a mob's own 90, which starts to argue against the ruling's "less than a mob".

**Option 3, one fifth.** **Derived from Brotato's chargers at a resistance of 0.80**, which is the tier the developer deliberately moved down from total immunity in Update 1.1, so it is a shipped statement of how little a heavy body should be pushed once someone decided it should be pushed at all. Its cost is decisive and arithmetic rather than aesthetic: **18.0 units at rung 5 is under the 19-unit reversal line, so at one fifth the source never goes backwards at any rung of the ladder**, and the toll only slows a descent the player would have to be watching closely to notice changed.

**The evidence favours option 1.** It is where the shipped ratios cluster, Gungeon's own same-enemy pair landing on exactly a third and three other games putting their resisting-but-not-immune bodies in the 0.25 to 0.4 band, and it is the only one of the three that puts the reversal line **inside** the ladder, so rung 1 holds the source and rung 5 pushes it back, which is what "a high bell rung lets the player stay under the pour" looks like as a figure.

**One corroboration of the ruling itself, recorded because it arrived unasked.** Brotato's Update 1.1 moved every non-boss heavy off total immunity into a graded band of 0.05 to 0.2 while leaving every elite and every boss at exactly zero (<https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1942280/view/6212245217911850965>, matched against the shipped `.tres` files). **That is the same division Mark drew on 2026-09-15**, the boss binary and the big non-boss body fractional, reached by a live team with a shipped game in front of them. Enter the Gungeon reached it differently and is worth naming beside it: four of its bosses sit at weight 100, a real 0.29 of the basic mook's push rather than nothing, while three sit at 10000 and 100000, which is a refusal written as a number. **So the two best-sourced games in the record disagree about whether a boss should be pushed at all, and both agree that a big non-boss body should be pushed less rather than not at all.**

## 7. What no source answers

**No game found changes a boss's colour continuously as its health falls; the two that change it at all step it at a named threshold.** This was looked for directly rather than assumed absent. DoDonPachi DaiOuJou has a permanent low-HP palette below a per-type threshold and a boss critical state at its own, and Touhou 4 puts five colour bands on the HUD bar at 32 units each, but **both are steps at a line rather than a gradient**, and Hades' `LowHealthPresentation` chain belongs to the hero alone while Nuclear Throne's boss draw branches on `nexthurt` and never on `hp`. So the answer to the brief's "tint shift as health falls" is that **shipped practice is a threshold step, on the body in Cave's case and on the readout in Touhou's, and a continuous tint has no precedent anywhere in this record.**

**No per-enemy knockback resistance value is published for Halls of Torment, so no ratio can be derived from it.** Its structure is sourced, a threshold gate whose answer is 0 or 1 with a separate recovery stat doing the grading, but not one enemy's number is published and there is no readable resource dump. The wiki's monster infobox has no knockback or mass field and its Lua data modules cover abilities, items, artifacts, blessings, champions, marks, quests and vault modifiers but no monsters. **And the widely repeated claim that Halls of Torment enemies have a "mass" that dampens knockback traces to one Steam forum post by a user with no developer badge and should not be cited.**

**No per-monster mass is published for Risk of Rain 2.** The formula is stated and one pair of survivor masses is stated (Acrid 300, Loader 150, a ratio of 0.5); the monster masses live on Unity prefabs and no dump of them was found on the wiki, in the modding documentation, or in the decompiled assembly, which carries the code and not the prefab values. **So that game's own big-to-small ratio is unknown**, and the 0.5 above is between two player characters rather than between a mob and a boss.

**Cave publishes almost nothing outside DaiOuJou.** Whether DoDonPachi, Ketsui, Mushihimesama or ESP Ra.De. draw a boss HP bar rests on fan FAQs, one forum post, an uncited wiki line and an uncited Japanese Wikipedia sentence; no flash or spark duration exists for any Cave game but DaiOuJou; no CV1000-era disassembly of comparable depth is public, so whether Mushihimesama uses the same palette XOR is unknown; and Cave's documentation survives only as image scans. Beyond that, **no Ikeda or ZUN statement on hit feedback, hitsparks, damage numbers or hiding boss health exists at all**, across seven Cave interview translations and a December 2024 Ikeda-and-ZUN dialogue that was pulled raw and searched (the quote about a health bar existing "solely to express the boss" that search engines attribute to it **is not in the article**, and is not reported here).

**Three smaller holes, each named so a later reader does not re-dig it.** Touhou 6's impact spark lives in `data/bullet4.anm` rather than in code, so its visible length is unpublished. What writes Touhou 8's boss lifebar segment arrays is not yet decompiled. And Touhou 7 and everything from Touhou 9 on have no decompilation at all.

**What Enter the Gungeon's brightness clamp clamps to is unreadable.** The keyword is on shipped materials and `FlashOnHit` lifts it by name, but the shaders in that decompile carry no readable body, so the ceiling's value and the clamp's arithmetic are unknown. Only the fact of the lift is sourced.

**Hades publishes no unit scale for its shake**, so `Distance = 6` and `Distance = 10` could not be transposed and option B derives its amplitude from this repository's `BOSS_STROKE` instead. The duration transfers; the magnitude does not, and Hades and Brotato are the only two sources for a shake in the whole record.

**Nothing anywhere describes what a shed piece looks like leaving a boss.** Not this record, not the two before it, and not any source found: Brotato's bosses change behaviour state and lose no geometry, Halls of Torment's two-health-bar bosses turn out to be two separate entities swapped in sequence rather than one body losing parts, and only DaiOuJou has destructible boss parts at all, with their own health and a score and an item and a 110-frame hit stop but no description of how they read. **How a shed part is drawn, how many a fight sheds, and whether the shedding is timed to the hit that caused it are all unauthored**, and the decision log rules only that bosses shed.

**Four published sources contradict each other and none was resolved.** Minecraft's Attribute page gives the Ravager a `knockback_resistance` of 0.75 while its Knockback mechanic page gives 70% in Java and 75% in Bedrock. Blizzard's 2012 crowd-control journal says monster resistance decays 10% a second while its own 2015 game guide says 5%. Diablo 2's shipped `Missiles.txt` carries a documented 0-to-100 `KnockBack` percentage whose only non-zero values are 1, 33 and 75, which cannot be a literal percentage and cannot be reconciled with the stated 50% for Molten Boulder. And Halls of Torment's shipped `KillAfterNumberOfHits` is 20 where its wiki says the Effigy dies on the twenty-first hit. None of the four touches a figure this record leans on, and all four are recorded so a later reader does not have to find them again.

**Two sources fought back and the workaround is worth writing down.** `hallsoftorment.wiki.gg` returns HTTP 401 on every route including its MediaWiki API and is probably not public; the reachable Halls of Torment wiki is `hot.fandom.com`, which is **community-run rather than the developer's**, so nothing taken from it is a developer statement. And `theamazonbasin.com`, which holds the fullest Diablo 2 knockback tables, has an expired TLS certificate, so it answers only to `curl -k` or to its raw action.
