# What feeds a score besides kills

Research for Hungry Grave path step 5, slice M7, gathered 2026-09-16 for [`../design/show-what-you-have.md`](../design/show-what-you-have.md) ruling R4. Labels: **DOCUMENTED** = a primary source read directly, an official or operator manual, an in-game scoring table, or a developer statement. **SECONDARY** = an established wiki, a well-regarded community scoring guide, or a detailed forum analysis of shipped behaviour. **WEAK** = a single forum comment, a video description, or an inference.

It sits beside [`portrait-hud-and-catchable-loss.md`](portrait-hud-and-catchable-loss.md), which is step 5's own record and is about where a readout sits rather than what feeds it, and beside [`floor-ladder-precedent.md`](floor-ladder-precedent.md), which holds Sonic's rings and what a counter beside the score does. It is its own file rather than a section of either because the question is a different one: **Mark's ruling of 2026-09-16 opened the score to several inputs, and this is the evidence for what each input is worth against a trash kill.** Anything in those two files is cited and never repeated.

**The question, stated once.** A kill pays `TRASH_KILL_SCORE` today. Mark named three more inputs: boss damage, the Waking's source killed, and large food taken after full power. Each needs a weight, and the weight that matters is the **ratio to a trash kill**, because that is what says whether an input is felt at all and whether it swamps the others.

## 0. What the tree already pays, read before anything was searched

Read at `43ed76c9df`, by content and never by line.

**The unit.** `TRASH_KILL_SCORE` is 100 (`src/game/tuning.ts`), and every mob row states its `scorePayout` as a multiple of it (`src/game/mobs.ts`). The multiple is the whole number of mow bodies the row's health is worth, floored: the shambler is 8 health and pays 1, the ghoul is 20 and pays 2 with the half discarded, the revenant is 64 and pays 8. **The cairn is the one deliberate exception**, 2206 health paying 1, because the rule would make grinding the curtain down the best-paying play in the game and retire the belch as its key (ADR 0042, and the row's own comment says so).

**So the tree already has a stated rate: one trash kill per 8 points of health taken, floored.** Every ratio below is read against that rate, because an input that pays at a different rate from the rate the mob table already states is a second scoring rule wearing the same number.

**What a run scores today, so a weight can be set against something.** Slice M1's batch, twelve runs on the birthright rig at `11483ecf31` (`step-5-progress.md` section 7): `run.score` at the end ran 0 to 22,611 and `tuning.damageTaken.scoreBled` ran 11,900 to 54,201, so **a run makes roughly 12,000 to 55,000 points from kills and overflow before the ladder takes any of it**. That is the swamping problem in one number, and it is why the composition question below is not decoration.

## 1. Boss damage and boss kills

**The headline, and it is a correction to the assumption the question was asked with: almost nothing ships boss damage as a per-hit trickle of score.** Five of the six games checked pay the fight as a lump computed from something the fight accumulated, or as a clock. **Ikaruga is the one genuine per-damage-tick payer in the set, and its own community calls that term the least important scoring element in the game.**

**DoDonPachi (Cave, 1997). The hits are counted during the fight and paid as one lump at the stage clear.** Lasering the boss builds a HIT counter that falls rapidly the moment the laser stops, and nothing is credited while the fight runs. The stage-clear bonus is `200,000 + (100,000 x stage) + hits x (2,000 + 1,000 x stage)`, so stage 1 is 300,000 plus 3,000 a hit and stage 6 is 800,000 plus 8,000 a hit; the loop does not raise the stage number. **Two independent sources derive the same figures**, the wiki's formula and the Bee Preying manual's own words, "the boss's point value divided by 100 and then multiplied by the hit count from the boss", against a table running 300,000 at area 1 to 800,000 at area 6. **The ratio has to be stated twice or it lies**: the stage-1 boss base alone is 3,000x a raw 100-point popcorn enemy, and about **30x** the same enemy killed inside a 100-hit chain. **No per-enemy point table is published for this game at all**, which is itself the finding in section 4. https://shmups.wiki/library/DoDonPachi and http://shmups.com/beepreying/old/strategy/basis/score.html, **SECONDARY** both, and corroborating each other closely enough to weigh near documented.

**DoDonPachi DaiOuJou. The same shape, at half the per-hit coefficient.** BOSS HIT BONUS sits inside the end-stage bonus: 300,000 + 1,500 x BOSS_HITS at stage 1 rising to 700,000 + 3,500 at stage 5, "always true, no matter how many deaths during stage section or boss fight", and the loop does not change it. The base is identical to DoDonPachi's and the per-hit term is exactly half of it. **The cleanest ratio anywhere in this research is here**: a bee item pays `1,000 x current chain hits`, so **one bee collected at a 300-hit chain is worth 300,000, which is the entire stage-1 boss base**. A boss in this game is worth about one well-timed pickup plus whatever the fight accumulated. https://shmups.wiki/library/DoDonPachi_DaiOuJou, **SECONDARY**, the table reading as extracted data.

**A correction worth carrying, because a later reader will hit the same thing.** An automated page summary reported DaiOuJou as paying boss damage "per hit, not as a lump sum upon destruction". **That is wrong**, and it was caught only by pulling the page source and seeing BOSS HIT BONUS sitting inside the End-stage Bonus section. Any per-hit claim in a secondary summary of a DoDonPachi-family game is suspect until the section heading around it has been read.

**Ikaruga (Treasure, 2001). The one genuine per-hit payer, and it is deliberately trivial.** Every HP removed is worth **10 points**, boss parts included: the wiki notes a player can chase the increment "during some Main Boss fights, by firing at supporting pods or protective covers" in idle moments, and calls it "by far the least important scoring element" because enemy HP is fixed and the player has almost no control over it. **The boss itself pays no kill lump at all.** Main Boss parts are the single stated exception to the rule that every destructible enemy has a polarity and can be chained, and what the fight pays is a time bonus, a countdown generally starting at 100 seconds at **10,000 points a remaining second**. Trash pays through chains of three same-polarity enemies at 100, 200, 400, 800, 1,600, 3,200, 6,400, 12,800, then 25,600 for the ninth chain and every one after, so a max-chain enemy is about 8,533. **The ratios**: a boss killed with 60 seconds left is 600,000, about **70 max-chain trash enemies**; and the per-HP term, at 10 points against a max-chain enemy's 8,533, pays about **1/850th of a trash kill per point of health removed**. **The number a designer should carry out of this game** is that breaking a max chain costs at least 204,900, about a third of a boss time bonus, so one dropped chain is worth roughly twenty seconds of boss fight: **the boss is a timer and not a prize**. https://shmups.wiki/library/Ikaruga, **SECONDARY**, unusually detailed and internally consistent.

**Sky Force (Infinite Dreams). The boss is not one kill at all.** Destructible objects "produce either Stars, Power-ups, aircraft parts or simply extra score when taken down", and the wiki is explicit that "bosses that take place at the end of most Stages are also counted as enemies": each boss armament drops stars and points and each feeds the chain bonus, so structurally a boss is a cluster of ordinary destructibles. The only boss-specific payment is a **Nightmare-difficulty-only 20,000-point bonus that appears when the fight starts and decays**, corroborated by community footage showing 18,048 mid-decay. **The ratio, from the anchors that do exist**: 20,000 is **20 survivor rescues** at 1,000 each, or **four perfected stage objectives** at 5,000 each, and it lands exactly on the "Godlike" chain tier. **So the maximum boss bonus is worth less than perfecting the stage's four medals**, which total the same 20,000 and then get multiplied along with everything else. Per-enemy values are **not published**. https://sky-force-reloaded-2016.fandom.com/wiki/Bosses, https://sky-force-reloaded-2016.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Play and https://sky-force-reloaded-2016.fandom.com/wiki/Enemies, **SECONDARY** for the mechanics and the 20,000, fetched through a text proxy because Fandom refuses automated requests.

**Bullet Hell Monday (Masayuki Ito). The currency is a multiplier and the boss is the largest single grant of it.** Score comes from destroying enemies and collecting items, items being "the primary source of score", everything scaled by a Multiplier shown as a percentage and capped at 9,999.9%. **The multiplier is a speed-kill mechanic**: "destroying an enemy quickly will reward the maximum amount of Multiplier from them, with the multiplier gain decreasing the longer the enemy is on screen", doubled on a bomb kill, 1.5x on Spark or on a homing laser or blade landing the kill, 3x on bomb plus Spark in Black. "Bosses and boss parts often give the largest multiplier increases from a single entity", and the stated strategy is to build the multiplier on the stage and then farm the boss's patterns for score items through large bullet cancels. **No lump sum for the kill is documented and no numeric enemy or boss values are published**, so no ratio is available. https://shmups.wiki/library/Bullet_Hell_Monday and https://www.shmups.wiki/library/Bullet_Hell_Monday_Black, **SECONDARY** for the mechanics, **not published** for any ratio.

**Danmaku Unlimited 2 (Doragon Entertainment). The extreme case: what you killed is made deliberately irrelevant.** A reverse-engineered formula, credited on the wiki to a named disassembler, gives the score for destroying an enemy as `(enemy value + points value) x proximity multiplier x stage multiplier`, where `points value = Graze x 16` multiplied by the Trance multiplier while Trance runs and capped at 9,999,999, the proximity multiplier maxes at 16 and rises the closer the ship is, and the stage multiplier is built from crystals, halved by a hit outside Trance and reset to 1 on death. **The enemy's own value is an additive term sitting beside a graze-derived term that reaches seven figures**, so once graze is meaningfully high a boss kill and a popcorn kill are worth approximately the same: **the ratio is about 1x by construction**, and what separates a good kill from a bad one is proximity and Trance rather than the target. The wiki itself marks "enemy value" and "damaging enemies" as needing more research, so whether damage ticks pay at all in that game is unresolved, and a Flawless Phase Bonus tied to boss phases exists with no published value. https://shmups.wiki/library/Danmaku_Unlimited_2, **SECONDARY (strong)**, credited to reverse engineering and carrying verified float-precision and 2^32 high-score bugs beside it.

**Danmaku Unlimited 3 is documented too thinly to use.** Scoring is a graze counter acting as a multiplier over a gauge that must be kept full, Trance turns bullets into gems, and in Spirit mode cancelled bullets leave souls that count as graze. Reviews mention a bonus for defeating a boss within a time limit or flawlessly, with no figure anywhere. **WEAK**, and no number here is built on. https://shmups.wiki/library/Danmaku_Unlimited_3

## 2. Sources, generators and cores

**The headline, and it is the most usable result in this file: the genre prices a source in two clearly separated tiers, and which tier a thing sits in depends on whether it is a spawner or an objective.**

**The spawner tier is 6x to 10x a trash kill, and it is remarkably tight across three games, though only two studios: two of the three are Williams.** Robotron 2084's Spheroid and Quark pay **1,000** against a Grunt's 100, **10x** (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Robotron:_2084/Gameplay, **SECONDARY**, a transcription of the cabinet's own scoring panel; the Williams operator manual carries no per-enemy values at all, only the 25,000 extra-life threshold, so the cabinet card is the origin and the wiki is the citable copy). Gradius's hatch, the enemy spawner, pays **1,000** against popcorn's 100, **10x**, and sits on exactly the same tier as the Mother, the Iron Maiden and the tentacle core, all 1,000, so **the hatch gets no premium at all for being a source** (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Gradius/Enemies, **SECONDARY**, and the Konami NES manual for the popcorn roster at 100 each, https://archive.org/details/Gradius-Manual-NES-US-1986, **DOCUMENTED** for the home port). Defender's Pod, which releases five to seven Swarmers, pays **1,000** against a Lander's 150, **6.7x**, the lowest source-to-trash ratio anywhere in this research (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Defender/Gameplay, **SECONDARY**).

**The structural-core tier is 30x to 133x, and it is where a thing that is the objective sits.** Bosconian's enemy base, which launches the fighter squadrons and is the stage objective, pays **1,500** against an I-Type's 50, **30x** (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Bosconian/Gameplay, **SECONDARY**). Gradius's Big Core pays **5,000** against popcorn's 100, **50x** in the arcade, and the NES manual documents the two-tier kill outright, "Destroy entire core: 10,000 points / Destroy center only: 5,000 points", which is 100x and 50x there. Xevious's Andor Genesis core pays **4,000** against a Toroid's 30, **133x**, with each of its four gun ports paying 1,000 beside it (https://www.atari7800.org/manuals/Xevious.htm, **DOCUMENTED**, the official manual of a licensed port carrying the arcade table verbatim; the gun-port split is https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/xevious/, **SECONDARY**).

**Almost every one of them is a single lump on the kill.** The accrual cases are few and every one is deliberate: Xevious's Sol Citadel pays 2,000 to raise it and another 2,000 to destroy it, Defender's smart-bombed Pod pays 1,150 through 2,050 depending on how many Swarmers were caught in the same blast, and Robotron's rescued humans escalate 1,000 through 5,000 within a wave.

**The axis a designer actually has to pick, stated as two shipped games pointing opposite ways.** Xevious's Andor Genesis pays 4,000 for the core and ends the encounter, while its four ports pay 1,000 each and can be stripped first, so **the game pays double for milking the source before denying it**. Robotron's Spheroid pays 1,000 while the several Enforcers it would have hatched pay 150 each, so **the raw points favour letting it hatch and the 1,000 is a safety premium for denying the spawn**. Defender is the sharpest counter-example of all: **the Lander is the real source**, it abducts humanoids and converting all of them explodes the planet, and Defender pays it the flat trash rate of 150, identical to the Mutant it creates. **Defender puts the whole of the source-denial pressure on the loss side and none of it on the score side.**

**The retinue multiplier is a different device and does not transfer here, but it is worth knowing it exists.** Galaga's Boss Galaga pays 150 in formation, 400 diving alone, 800 with one escort and 1,600 with two, a clean 2x an escort, and the multiplier is banked at dive initiation so shooting the escorts off first still pays the higher value: the game pays you for **leaving the retinue attached**. Galaxian's flagship pays 150 alone, 200 with one escort intact, 300 with two, and **800 for clearing both escorts first**: the same Namco lineage pointing the incentive the other way. https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Galaga/Gameplay and https://shmups.wiki/library/Galaxian, **SECONDARY** both. Neither leader spawns anything, and what the device rewards is how the kill was taken rather than what was killed.

**One negative result, recorded because it cost time.** **R-Type has no published point values at all**, for Dobkeratops or for any ordinary enemy: StrategyWiki's R-Type pages carry no scoring section and its Enemies page is a 404, Shmups Wiki's Enemy Overview is an empty template that says score values would go there if available, and the system11 threads have nothing. R-Type is not a scoring-community game the way the Williams and Namco titles are, and no number for it is invented here.

**One anomaly preserved rather than cleaned up.** Several community sources report that destroying Xevious's Andor Genesis core awards more than 4,000, because the game also pays the value of whatever last occupied slot 15 of its object table. It could not be verified against a disassembly and is **WEAK**; nothing here is built on it. Separately, **whether Bosconian's 1,500 base value also fires when the base is killed by destroying all six orbs is unresolved**: if it does, the slow path pays 2,700 and the game rewards patience, and if it does not, the snipe pays 1,500 against the orb path's 1,200. StrategyWiki does not say and the one source that addresses it is an inference. It is answerable by MAME instrumentation if it ever becomes load-bearing.

## 3. A pickup that can no longer help you

**This is the best-documented ground in the file, and the rule it yields is one sentence: a pickup the player can no longer use pays out only where there is already a scoring or economy channel for it to pay into, and the payout is sized to be a real but secondary income rather than a reason to hold yourself back.** Roughly twenty games were checked and the convention holds from Toaplan in 1988 to Cave in 2005 and straight through into the modern bullet heavens, where the payout changed from score to currency and nothing else about it did.

**[`reward-delivery-models.md`](reward-delivery-models.md) finding 5 already holds part of this and is cited rather than repeated**: Truxton 5,000, Raiden 5,000 for a surplus power-up or missile and 10,000 for a surplus P, Twin Cobra 2,000 against 100, Touhou escalating to 51,200, and Fire Shark's four-on-screen discard.

**The arcade convention, five publishers, one flat value per item class.** Weapon power-ups and missiles sit in a **1,000 to 5,000** band and a full bomb stock or a full-power item sits in the **10,000** band, against stage clear bonuses of a million. Toaplan's Tatsujin (1988) is the earliest found, "collecting excess items rewards the player with an extra 5,000 points". Raizing's Mahou Daisakusen (1993) pays 1,000 on each of its three shot types at max "and this does not apply to bombs as they never cap". Psikyo's Gunbird (1994) pays 2,000 at max power and 10,000 on a full bomb stock, and Strikers 1945 and its sequel pay 4,000 for a surplus P and 10,000 for a surplus bomb. Cave's Ibara and Pink Sweets pay a surplus value per item, Pink Sweets stating 1,000 outright. **Raiden II's wiki has a section headed "Surplus items" in those words**, listing 5,000 for a weapon power-up or missile, 10,000 for a P, and 5,000 for a bomb rising to **50,000 when all seven held bombs are the same colour**. All **SECONDARY**, Shmups Wiki from ROM analysis and community testing: https://shmups.wiki/library/Tatsujin, https://shmups.wiki/library/Mahou_Daisakusen, https://shmups.wiki/library/Gunbird, https://shmups.wiki/library/Strikers_1945, https://shmups.wiki/library/Strikers_1945_II, https://shmups.wiki/library/Pink_Sweets:_Ibara_Sorekara, https://shmups.wiki/library/Raiden_II.

**Battle Garegga states the rule more explicitly than anything else found, and prices the counterweight in the same breath.** "Normally, collecting shot power ups, weapon fragments, and option power up items awards no extra points. However, when at maximum shot level, weapon ammunition or options, picking up excess power ups will award bonus points. However, this is generally not recommended since the bonus points awarded does not outweigh the rank increase from picking up the items." Small Shot 100, Large Shot 5,000, Small Weapon Fragment 1,000, Large Weapon Fragment 10,000, Option 500. https://shmups.wiki/library/Battle_Garegga, **SECONDARY**.

**Three games in the pass get the sizing wrong in opposite directions, and their own communities say so, which is the most useful part of this section.** Great Mahou Daisakusen chains its surplus values to 10,000 and 50,000 caps and the wiki's verdict is that "these are extremely minuscule score gains and the player can safely ignore them". Battle Garegga's is real and is still not worth taking, per the quote above. Gunbird's is large enough that "a lot of players playing for score will deliberately suicide their two starting lives to get two free power-ups" and conserve ammo to farm the bonus. And DoDonPachi's MAXIMUM Bomb Bonus, taken by collecting a bomb at full stock, pays "220 pts x (MAXIMUM Multiplier) every frame" with the multiplier rising on every further bomb, worth about **260,000,000 points** over a perfect run and becoming the entire high-level game. **Too small to bother with and large enough to farm are both shipped failures with names attached**, and a weight set here is set between them. https://shmups.wiki/library/Great_Mahou_Daisakusen and https://shmups.wiki/library/DoDonPachi, **SECONDARY**.

**Two headline games do not convert at all, and both refusals have a reason worth carrying.** **Gradius pays its capsule unconditionally rather than at max**, 0 points in the 1985 original, 300 in Gradius III "except for stage 4", 800 in Gradius V, and the meter wraps to the first slot rather than refusing, so the surplus case never arises as a scoring question at all. **R-Type pays nothing for any item in the first place**, and this is the strongest negative in the file because the absence is evidenced rather than merely unfound: the official Master System manual carries a complete enemy and boss scoring table, Zipp 100 through Bydo 15,000, and then describes all six power-up types in a separate section with no numbers anywhere. A second bit when two are held "does nothing", and a same-colour laser crystal at max Force is a no-op. **Doom is the clean counter-example and its reason is the rule restated**: surplus ammo simply disappears and green armour cannot be collected at 100 percent, and classic Doom has no score at all, so there is nowhere for a surplus to go. https://gradius.fandom.com/wiki/Power_Capsule and https://shmups.wiki/library/Gradius_III:_Densetsu_kara_Shinwa_e, **SECONDARY**; https://www.smspower.org/seganotebook/rtype/manual/rtypem.html, **DOCUMENTED**; https://doomwiki.org/wiki/Ammo, **SECONDARY**.

**The modern bullet heavens inherited the rule and pay currency instead of score, and three of them reached the same two fallback objects independently: a coin and a health pickup.** **Vampire Survivors** offers a **Big Coin Bag worth 25 gold, multiplied by Greed**, or a Floor Chicken, once every weapon and passive held is maxed and Limit Break is off; the toggle is a real patch note, **0.8.0 "The Winged One", 7 July 2022**, "Added option to always pick Coin Bag or Floor Chicken when leveling up after maxing out all weapons", and the always-Chicken option pays gold instead when the player is already at full health. **DOCUMENTED** (https://vampire-survivors.fandom.com/wiki/Version_History), with the 25 from the official wiki, **SECONDARY** (https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/Big_Coin_Bag). **Halls of Torment** names its fallbacks in in-game text, **Alms** at **+50 Gold** ("You have learned all there is to learn... for now") and **Health** at +100, **SECONDARY** (https://hot.fandom.com/wiki/Trait). **HoloCure**'s level-up menu has four slots each with its own cascade, and the fourth bottoms out at **HoloCoins, +100**, the between-run currency, **DOCUMENTED** from the datamined generation algorithm (https://holocure.fandom.com/wiki/Level_Up).

**And the cheapest modern answer is to make the state unreachable, which three of them also do.** **Brotato has no version of this pattern by construction**: its level-up pool is sixteen primary stats at four magnitudes, all infinitely repeatable, so exhaustion never happens, and its only conversion is a voluntary lossy recycle at 25 to 85 percent of price. **Death Must Die** regenerates its three offer pools from what the player already holds. **HoloCure's first two slots** degrade to an unbounded stat pool and never reach a consumable at all. **Super Mario Bros. does the same one genre over**, never spawning a Super Mushroom while Mario is Super and spawning a Fire Flower instead. https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Upgrades and https://dmd.fandom.com/wiki/Blessing/Mechanics, **SECONDARY** and **DOCUMENTED** respectively.

**The best-numbered conversion found anywhere scales the payout with the pickup's tier, which is the shape this game's rich tier already has.** Bayonetta: "If the health bar is full the Laurel Leaf is turned into 50 halos, while the Green Laurel is turned into 100 halos", and the same 50 and 100 schedule is reused for the magic pickups. https://bayonetta.fandom.com/wiki/Items, **SECONDARY**. Mario reached the same answer from 2011 on, paying **ten coins** for a power-up taken with the reserve box full in Super Mario 3D Land and Captain Toad and **five coins** for a redundant Super Mushroom in Super Mario Run. https://www.mariowiki.com/Super_Mushroom, **SECONDARY**.

**Four answers exist and converting is only one of them**, which is worth stating because this game has already chosen and should know it chose: convert it, refuse it, store it (Super Mario World's reserve box, which pays nothing on release), or change the drop table rather than the pickup (Battle Garegga's Blast Branch turning every flying enemy's drop into medals once fully powered, and Ratchet & Clank giving ammo-crate priority to weapons below capacity). **`CONTEXT.md`'s Overflow entry already rules this game a converter**, "nothing swallowed is ever worthless", so what is open is the weight and never the shape.

**What none of this settles is the weight for a rich swallow at full power, because not one game in the pass scores food.** Every figure above is a weapon pickup, a bomb, a health orb or a level-up slot. **What transfers is the band and the two failure modes**: a surplus payout sits at a few thousand against a clear bonus of a million in the arcade games, at 25 gold against a run's thousands in Vampire Survivors, and the named ways to get it wrong are Great Mahou's ignorable and Gunbird's farmable. **The sizing argument that actually binds here is section 4's**, because the window is small by construction: full power means every rostered line at `MAX_LEVEL`, a run reaches that late if at all, and the items are few, at most five feasts a run (two from the Banshee, its phase break and its death, and three from the Undertaker) plus whatever revenant corpses the grave dives under inside that window.

## 4. How a single number is composed without one input swamping the rest

**The one rule that every game in sections 1 and 2 obeys, stated once: a lump is calibrated against a stretch of ordinary play, never against a single trash kill.** DoDonPachi's stage-1 boss base is 3,000x a raw hundred-point popcorn enemy and about **30x the same enemy killed inside a chain**, and the second figure is the one the game is built around, because a chained enemy is what ordinary play actually pays. Ikaruga's boss time bonus is about **70 max-chain enemies**, and its own community's headline number is that breaking a max chain costs at least 204,900, roughly a third of a boss, so one dropped chain is worth twenty seconds of boss fight. Sky Force's maximum boss bonus of 20,000 is exactly **20 survivor rescues** and exactly **four perfected stage objectives**, which is to say it is worth no more than doing the stage properly. **Not one game in the pass lets a single kill be worth more than a few tens of what ordinary play pays**, and the two that look like exceptions on paper, Gradius's 50x core and Xevious's 133x core, are measured against a raw popcorn enemy in games with no chain multiplier at all, so the stage's own total is the honest denominator there too.

**The second rule: the tiers are separated by roughly an order of magnitude each, and a thing's tier is decided by what it is to the player rather than by how much health it has.** Trash at 1x, a spawner at 6x to 10x, a structural core or an objective at 30x to 130x. **Health does not enter it anywhere.** Gradius's hatch and its Mother and its Iron Maiden all pay 1,000 on completely different amounts of health, and Defender pays its Lander, the game's actual strategic source, the flat trash rate of 150 while paying its Pod, a pure spawn-on-death container, 1,000.

**The third rule, and it is the one worth arguing with: a per-damage-tick term is either absent or deliberately trivial.** Ikaruga is the only genuine payer in section 1 and pays about **1/850th of a max-chain trash kill per point of health removed**. Danmaku Unlimited 2 goes furthest and makes the enemy's own value an additive term beside a graze term that reaches seven figures, so what you killed is worth approximately nothing and the ratio between a boss and a popcorn enemy is about **1x by construction**.

**What that says about this game, in its own numbers.** The mob table pays one trash kill per 8 points of health, floored. **Taken literally onto a boss, that rate pays 637 trash kills for the Undertaker and 275 for the Banshee** (section 0), and a run measured at slice M1's tip makes between 12,400 and 58,112 points from kills and overflow put together. **So the mob table's own rate, applied to a boss, pays more for one fight than the whole rest of the run pays**, which is the swamping failure the genre's tiers exist to avoid, and it is also what the cairn's row already refuses one tier down (ADR 0042).

**The band the precedent gives, converted.** A boss worth 20 to 70 trash kills is 2,000 to 7,000 points here, which is **4 to 40 percent of a measured run** at the two ends of that batch. **That is the shape of a felt input rather than a dominating one**, and it is the band a weight should be set inside. **The health rate it implies is roughly 100 points of boss health to one trash kill**, against the mow body's 8: the Banshee's 2,200 health pays 22 trash kills and the Undertaker's 5,100 pays 51, both inside the band, and the derivation is the table rather than a typed figure.

**A source's tier, decided rather than assumed.** The Waking's source is a spawner by behaviour and an objective by authoring, which puts it between the genre's two tiers rather than in either. **What settles it is #104**: killing the source does not stop the pour, the remaining budget keeps coming from the pour point, so **there is no source denial here at all** and none of the denial premium the Robotron tier is paying for applies. What is left is the commitment: ADR 0042 makes the Waking's property the commitment up the trail, and the bonus pays for having made it. **So it sits above the 6x to 10x spawner tier, because it is the section's objective and not roadside furniture, and below the 30x to 130x core tier, because a core kill in all three of those games ends or denies something and this one ends nothing.** At the same 100-health-per-trash-kill rate its 2,400 health is **24 trash kills**, which lands exactly in that gap and is derived rather than picked.

**Xevious against Robotron is the axis a designer has to choose on, and this game has already chosen.** Xevious pays 4,000 for the Andor Genesis core and ends the encounter while its four ports pay 1,000 each first, so **milking before denying pays double**. Robotron pays 1,000 for a Spheroid that would have hatched several 150-point Enforcers, so **the premium buys safety rather than score and the game pays you to be impatient**. Hungry Grave is on neither axis, because #104 already ruled that the kill denies nothing: **the Waking's bonus is a trophy for the commitment and not a lever on the pour**, and that is worth saying in the row's own comment so a later reader does not reach for either precedent by mistake.

## 5. Searched for and not found

**20 Minutes Till Dawn's pool-exhausted behaviour could not be closed and is deliberately not an instance in section 3.** Its pool is finite and small enough for exhaustion to matter, 25 trees and 100 upgrades with four or five shown, but no wiki, patch note or developer statement describes any fallback. The only source that addresses the state is a single July 2022 comment on a cheat-table thread about a pre-1.0 build, saying the game crashed when the pool ran out. **WEAK**, and nothing here rests on it. https://fearlessrevolution.com/viewtopic.php?t=20437

**Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor and Soulstone Survivors produced nothing citable either way**, and Deep Rock's community complaint runs the opposite direction, pool bloat rather than exhaustion. **Quake was not researched, only Doom**, so nothing is claimed about it.

**Three things nobody has published, each named so a later pass does not re-run them.** What pressing the power-up button does in an arcade Gradius with four Options already out. What a sixth Speed Up does in R-Type, which the official manual, the R-Type Wiki and Shmups Wiki all leave open. And what a 1-Up does at Mario's life cap, where the caps themselves are documented and the overflow behaviour is not.

**No published per-enemy point table exists for DoDonPachi, DoDonPachi DaiOuJou, Sky Force, Bullet Hell Monday or Danmaku Unlimited 2.** In every one of them the community has documented the formulas, the boss constants and the item values and has never extracted the raw enemy values. **That absence is itself the finding**: in all five games the enemy's own value is the least interesting term, swamped by a chain, a multiplier or a graze counter, and the communities do not consider the boss-to-trash base ratio a quantity worth measuring. A designer asking "what is a boss worth against a popcorn enemy" is asking a question those games' own players do not ask.

**R-Type has no published point values at all**, for Dobkeratops or for any ordinary enemy (section 2). It is not a scoring-community game the way the Williams and Namco titles are.

**Whether Bosconian's base pays its 1,500 when killed by the six-orb path is unresolved**, and **the reported Xevious core overpay bug could not be verified** (both in section 2). Neither is load-bearing here.

**The real Xevious arcade operator manual was located and could not be read.** https://www.arcade-museum.com/manuals-videogames/X/Xevious-Operators-Manual--TM230-1st-Printing.pdf, TM230 first printing, 72 pages, is a pure image scan with no text layer, and no OCR was available. The Atari 7800 manual's table is used instead and is believed to carry the arcade table verbatim. **If a later pass ever needs the arcade-native figures, that PDF is where they are.**
