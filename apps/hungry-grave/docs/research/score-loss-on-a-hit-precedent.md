# What a hit takes out of a standing score

Research for Hungry Grave path step 5, slice M1-fix, gathered 2026-09-16 for [`../design/show-what-you-have.md`](../design/show-what-you-have.md) ruling R4's closing amendment. Labels: **DOCUMENTED** = a primary source read directly, an official or operator manual, a developer wiki, a disassembly or decompilation, or an in-game scoring table. **SECONDARY** = an established wiki, a well-regarded community scoring guide, or a detailed forum analysis of shipped behaviour. **WEAK** = a single forum comment, a video description, or an inference.

It sits beside [`floor-ladder-precedent.md`](floor-ladder-precedent.md), which asks whether graduated loss before death is a shipped pattern at all and already holds Sonic's rings and Ghosts 'n Goblins; beside [`score-inputs-precedent.md`](score-inputs-precedent.md), which is about what feeds the number; and beside [`portrait-hud-and-catchable-loss.md`](portrait-hud-and-catchable-loss.md), which is about where the number sits and whether a lost power can be chased. Anything in those three is cited and never repeated. **The question here is a fourth one and it is narrow: when a hit takes something out of a standing total, how much does it take, and what shape does the amount have.**

**The question, stated once.** Mark ruled on 2026-09-16, in two words, that the floor ladder's score bleed is capped. The shape of a cap is not obvious: it could be a fraction of the standing score, a flat amount, or the larger or the smaller of the two. This file is the evidence for which, and for the band the figure sits in.

## 0. What the tree does today, read before anything was searched

Read at `d68114a1a8`, by content and never by line.

**The whole score, gone.** `bleedScore` (`src/game/grave.ts`) reads `state.score`, sets it to zero, and fires `scoreBled` carrying what it took. Its own comment says "The score tier is exactly one rung, so it never partly bleeds." `runFloorLadder` runs it on the first floor hit that finds the rung armed, and the rung is spent on any ladder run whether or not it found score to take (design record R4).

**What that costs, measured rather than argued.** Slice M1's batch, twelve runs on the birthright rig at `11483ecf31` ([`../push/step-5-progress.md`](../push/step-5-progress.md) section 7): `tuning.damageTaken.scoreBled` ran **11,900 to 54,201** while `run.score` at the end ran **0 to 22,611**. Adding the two, **a run makes 12,400 to 58,112 points gross and the ladder takes most of it**, and eleven of the twelve runs bled exactly once. **So the whole bleed is not a tax on a run, it is the run**, and which run wins a friends' list (#135) is decided first by whether a hit landed at the floor.

**The unit every score figure in this game is stated in.** `TRASH_KILL_SCORE` is 100 and every mob row's `scorePayout` is a multiple of it (`src/game/tuning.ts`, `src/game/mobs.ts`). The storm kills about **2.47 bodies a second** ([`../push/step-4-progress.md`](../push/step-4-progress.md)), so ordinary mowing pays roughly **247 points a second** and one trash kill is about four tenths of a second of play. Every figure below is converted into that unit, because a penalty is only legible against the income it is taken out of.

## 1. The banked score is sacred, and the genre puts the volatility somewhere else

**This is the strongest negative result in any of this project's research files, and it was reached three times independently by passes that shared no queries and no sources.** Not one shipped shmup takes a fraction, a flat amount, or a capped slice of the accumulated score on a hit or a death. What dies is always one of two other things: the **earning rate**, or **income that was accumulated but never banked**.

**Resogun (Housemarque, 2013). The score stands and the multiplier resets to 1x, with no step-down and no partial retention.** The multiplier caps at 5x on Recruit, 10x on Experienced, 15x on Veteran and 20x on Master, is built in fractional increments per kill (the trophy names read `(1+4)x`, `(5+5)x` and `(10+1.5+3.5)x`, so it is tracked in decimals rather than integer steps), and it also resets on a kill timeout whose window shortens with difficulty. **SECONDARY**, aggregated across independent guides with no Housemarque statement found: https://www.hxchector.com/resogun-guide/ and https://www.ign.com/wikis/resogun/Gameplay_Basics.

**DoDonPachi (Cave, 1997). The death cost is enormous and every part of it is unbanked income.** The chain breaks totally and the hit counter resets to zero; the star counter, which is only cashed at the stage clear at 500 or 1,000 points a star, **resets to zero if the player loses a life**; and the bee ladder, which runs 100 through 100,000 within a stage and compounds toward a million a bee across a perfect game, collapses back to 100 on a single death, in the wiki's own words. The No Miss Bonus forfeited runs 200,000 at 1-1 to 8,000,000 at 2-7. **The accumulated score never decreases.** **SECONDARY**, https://shmups.wiki/library/DoDonPachi.

**Ikaruga (Treasure, 2001) quantifies its own break, and the number is pure opportunity cost.** Chain values run 100, 200, 400, 800, 1600, 3200, 6400, 12800 and 25600 from the ninth chain on, and the wiki states that "a max chain break costs a minimum of 204900 points", derived from the dropped chain plus the eight max chains thereafter. **The chain resets on a death and on every stage boundary, and the score itself never goes down.** **SECONDARY**, https://shmups.wiki/library/Ikaruga.

**Battle Garegga (Raizing, 1996) refuses the expectation outright, and it is worth carrying because this question was first asked assuming the opposite.** The medal ladder runs 100 to 900 in hundreds and then 1,000 to 10,000 in thousands, and **the reset trigger is a medal falling off the bottom of the screen, not a death**. Death costs a shot level and the options and *pays* twenty small weapon fragments; and because suiciding lowers rank, **death is a deliberate scoring tool there** for stages one through five. **SECONDARY**, https://www.shmups.wiki/library/Battle_Garegga.

**Crimzon Clover is the one modern shmup in the pass with a genuinely partial knock-down, and it lands on the multiplier rather than on the score.** A death empties the Break gauge and costs roughly **30 percent** of the Break rate. **SECONDARY**, https://shmups.system11.org/viewtopic.php?p=1426076.

**Crazy Taxi (Sega, 1999) states the same architecture outside the genre and draws the line precisely.** Contact with another vehicle **zeroes the Tip Multiplier** and leaves every tip already banked on the Fare Meter intact; hitting a box, a phone booth or a wall does not break it at all. **Total confiscation is reserved for a failed delivery**, where missing the customer's timer pays no money and no time bonus whatsoever. **SECONDARY**, http://crazytaxi.net/rules.html.

**Galaga and Pac-Man are the baseline and the convention is older than either.** Losing a life deducts nothing, play resumes, and a Pac-Man player can exploit a death to re-farm a scoring opportunity, which is the opposite of a penalty. The reason is the coin-op model itself: the score is the receipt for the money spent. **SECONDARY**, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-pac-man-dossier. **Galaga's own version of a hit that costs earning capacity is the fighter capture**, which takes the dual-fighter firepower and halves the kill rate: capacity, never score.

**So the architecture the genre actually ships is a banked-against-pending split rather than a slice**, and the reason offered for it is worth recording as an inference rather than as evidence: a score is a monotonic record of what the player did, and clawing back points already awarded reads as the game lying about the past, so designers put the volatility in a separate multiplier and let the headline number only ever rise. **WEAK**, offered as inference and not found stated by any source.

**ADR 0054 already recorded that this game's score bleed is ours rather than precedent** ("Nothing shipped subtracts score directly on a hit"), and this pass confirms that sentence rather than weakening it. **What the pass adds is that three genuine exceptions exist, and what shape they take.**

## 2. The three arcade games that genuinely subtract, and all three subtract a flat amount

**Astrosmash (Mattel, Intellivision, 1981) is the best find in the pass and the only one with a primary source.** Its cartridge manual carries a two-column scoring table under the headings "Your score goes UP when you hit..." and "Your score goes DOWN when...". Verbatim: Big Rock lands −5, Small Rock lands −10, **"Your Gun Is Hit, or a Spinner Lands = −100"**, against rewards of Big Rock 10, Small Rock 20, Big Spinner 40, Small Spinner 80, Guided Missile 50, UFO 100. **"Point values are multiplied by the level number"**, and the level multiplier runs 1x to 6x banded by the score itself: 1x under 1,000, 2x to 4,999, 3x to 19,999, 4x to 49,999, 5x to 99,999, 6x above. **DOCUMENTED**, https://www.digitpress.com/library/manuals/intv/astrosmash.txt.

**Astrosmash's real lesson is the one sentence this whole file turns on: the penalty rides the same scale the reward rides.** Being hit costs 100 at level 1 and 600 at level 6, and the reward for a UFO moves from 100 to 600 with it, so the hit is **worth one to ten of the game's own kills at every tier** and never more. It is the nearest shipped thing to a proportional slice, and what it is proportional to is the player's tier rather than the player's literal score. Its score can go negative. **SECONDARY** for the negative score, https://tasvideos.org/10242S.

**Star Hawk (Cinematronics, 1979) subtracts 800 and floors at zero.** An enemy Command Ship not killed in time "zaps 800 points off the player's score", and the same ship is worth 800 if killed, which is a clean symmetric bet: the penalty is exactly one kill of the thing that inflicted it. **SECONDARY**, https://www.arcade-history.com/game/2618/star-hawk and https://www.thelogbook.com/phosphor/starhawk/. **The floor is reported rather than documented**: a VAPS member who owns the hardware says the score-zapping enemy does not appear at all unless the score is at or above 800, so the total can never go negative. **WEAK**, https://forums.arcade-museum.com/threads/can-you-name-a-game-where-you-can-get-a-negative-score.489447/page-2. The Vectrex home port's manual lists the Command Ship at 800 points and says nothing about subtraction, so this may be arcade-only or simply undocumented. **DOCUMENTED** as an absence, http://www.digitpress.com/library/manuals/vectrex/star_hwk.txt.

**Barrier (Vectorbeam, 1979) subtracts for moving backwards, "BUT only down to zero, it won't go negative".** **WEAK**, same arcade-museum thread.

**All three take a flat amount and two of the three floor at zero.** The zero floor is the one part of a capped bleed with genuine arcade pedigree, and it is exactly the behaviour a lesser-of rule produces without being written down separately.

**Two claims a search summary served up as precedent are false, and both are recorded so a later pass does not re-find them.** **Hellsinker's "losing a life: −2" is not a score penalty**: the −2 sits in a table headed "Effect on NECESSARY", the extend counter, and an extend is earned when it falls below zero, so a death pushes the player *toward* a free life. **DOCUMENTED**, read in its section, https://www.shmups.wiki/library/Hellsinker. **Zero Wing's "up to 35000 points lost on death" is opportunity cost**: the wiki says in the same sentence that the loss happens "as the items that would have provided these points must go towards restoring the player's power and speed instead", so nothing is deducted. **SECONDARY**, https://shmups.wiki/library/Zero_Wing.

## 3. The shapes that actually ship, and the two that ship the shape this game needs

**The lesser of a flat amount and what the player is holding is a shipped rule, in two families, and it is the most mainstream shape in the whole pass.**

**Mario Kart takes a fixed three coins on a hit, floored by what is held.** Being struck by an item or driven off-course costs **3 coins** in Mario Kart 7, 8 and 8 Deluxe, and **1 coin** from a fireball specifically; the carrying cap is 10, or 20 in 8 Deluxe's Coin Runners, so the rule is exactly `min(3, held)` and the loosened coins land on the track where anyone including the player can take them back. Super Mario Kart used a variable **1, 2 or 4** by cause. **SECONDARY**, https://www.mariowiki.com/Coin.

**Shadow the Hedgehog takes ten rings a hit rather than all of them**, which is the one entry in that series to bound the loss instead of the recovery. **SECONDARY**, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_the_Hedgehog_(video_game); the contemporaneous community belief was 20 and the sourced figure is 10.

**Where a cap rides on a fraction instead, it is always the lesser of the two and never the larger.** Games reach for **MIN(fraction, ceiling)** constantly and for **MAX(fraction, floor)** essentially never.

**Minecraft is the textbook case and it is documented.** On death a player drops experience orbs "worth 7 * current level experience points, **up to a maximum of 100 points**, and all of the other experience vanishes". **DOCUMENTED**, https://minecraft.wiki/w/Experience. Two things in its shape transfer directly. The variable term is a linear function of a *different* quantity rather than a fraction of the standing pot, and **the ceiling is what does the real work**: past level 15 the loss is a flat 100 whatever the player has.

**Stardew Valley runs the shape twice at two magnitudes.** Passing out from exhaustion outside the house loses **10 percent of gold up to a maximum of 1,000g**; passing out from zero health loses a randomised **5 to 25 percent capped at 15,000g** (raised from 5,000g in 1.6) plus up to three inventory items. **DOCUMENTED**, https://www.stardewvalleywiki.com/Energy and https://stardewvalleywiki.com/The_Mines. The randomised fraction under a hard ceiling is a variant worth knowing exists.

**Diablo II caps the rate rather than the amount, which is a third shape, and bolts a protected floor underneath it.** On a multiplayer death the gold lost is **min(character level as a percent, 20 percent)** of gold carried plus gold in the stash, dropped at the death spot for recovery; the worked example on the wiki is a level 50 character holding a million dropping 200,000. In single player the stash is exempt and **500 gold per character level is exempt on top**, so a level 20 character holding 10,000 drops nothing. **DOCUMENTED**, Blizzard's own Arreat Summit, http://classic.battle.net/diablo2exp/basics/characters.shtml, with the worked examples at https://diablo2.diablowiki.net/Gold, **SECONDARY**.

**Poker's rake is the same shape outside games**, `min(percentage of pot, cap)`, with the cap scaling by stakes so the effective rate falls as the pot grows. **SECONDARY**, https://www.vip-grinders.com/poker-strategy/rake-explained/.

**The one clean MAX(fraction, flat floor) found anywhere is not a game at all**: a credit-card minimum payment is the greater of a flat floor and a percentage of the balance plus interest and fees, a shape US regulation constrains without setting the constants. **SECONDARY** for the formula, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1026.51. **Two dedicated searches for a game using a greater-of rule each returned an explicit negative.**

**The complementary shape protects a poor player rather than a rich one**: a fixed retention, `lost = max(0, standing − allowance)`. Diablo II's single-player exemption above is one. **Dead Cells** sells the same thing as an upgrade, Gold Reserves I to V "preserving up to 500 / 1000 / 1500 / 2000 / 2500 GOLD when you die", while cells themselves are a flat total loss with no rune, mutation or item retaining any of them. **DOCUMENTED**, https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Runes_and_upgrades. **Old School RuneScape** does it by count rather than by amount, keeping the three most valuable items, four with Protect Item, none when skulled. **SECONDARY**, https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Items_Kept_on_Death.

**Where no bound ships at all, the fraction is large and the resource is short-lived.** Rogue Legacy's Charon takes **100 percent of gold at the castle entrance every run**, reducible to 90, 80, 70, 60 and finally 50 percent by the five Haggle tiers and never to zero, which the wiki settles against guides claiming 6 percent by quoting Charon's own dialogue at each tier. **DOCUMENTED**, https://roguelegacy.wiki.gg/wiki/Charon. Terraria drops **50 percent of coins on Classic, 75 on Expert and 100 on Master, rounded up to the nearest whole coin**. **DOCUMENTED**, https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Coins. Sekiro takes a flat **50 percent** of held Sen and of unspent skill progress and, instead of shrinking the fraction, hands out a probabilistic waiver, Unseen Aid at a 30 percent base chance of losing nothing. **SECONDARY**, https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Unseen+Aid. Hades loses obols entirely with no talent, boon or keepsake retaining any of them. **SECONDARY**, https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Charon%27s_Obol. Risk of Rain 2 has no death tax on money because money is already stage-local, converting to experience at the teleporter. **DOCUMENTED**, https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Gold.

**The one shipped per-hit proportional tax on a standing pot is a cursed opt-in.** Risk of Rain 2's Brittle Crown, a lunar item, takes `(fraction of max health lost in that hit) × standing gold`, at 100 percent per stack. **DOCUMENTED**, https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Brittle_Crown. **That it is a lunar item is the finding**: the one game that prices a hit as a share of the player's purse sells it as a curse.

## 4. Sonic caps the mercy, not the loss, and that is the inverse of what is being built

**Sonic's rings are already this project's cited precedent for the floor ladder's first rung** ([`floor-ladder-precedent.md`](floor-ladder-precedent.md), corrected 2026-09-16 to say that the rings are a counter beside the score rather than the score). **What this pass adds is that the belief the loss is capped is backwards, and the disassembly settles it.**

**The loss is total and the recovery is what is bounded, at thirty-two.** Sonic 1's object 37 clamps the spawn count and then empties the counter regardless, verbatim from the Sonic Retro disassembly:

```
		moveq	#32,d0
		cmp.w	d0,d5		; do you have 32 or more?
		bcs.s	.belowmax	; if not, branch
		move.w	d0,d5		; if yes, set d5 to 32
```

followed by `move.w #0,(v_rings).w ; reset number of rings to zero`. **Every ring above 32 is never created as an object at all**: not rendered, not recoverable, deleted outright. **DOCUMENTED**, https://github.com/sonicretro/s1disasm. Sonic 2 carries the identical clamp with the in-source comment "if yes, limit to 32 rings" and Sonic 3 & Knuckles uses `moveq #$20,d0` for the same thirty-two. **DOCUMENTED**, https://github.com/sonicretro/s2disasm and https://github.com/sonicretro/skdisasm.

**The windows around the scatter, read off the same source.** Invulnerability is **120 frames**, two seconds, and ring pickup is gated while that counter is at or above 90, so **the first 30 frames are a hard re-collect lockout** with the rings visibly there and untouchable and the remaining 90 are invulnerable and collectible. The despawn timer is **255 frames**, and in all three Mega Drive games it is a single global byte rather than per-ring state, so a second hit refreshes the lifetime of the older nearly-expired rings: a shipped bug for three titles running, fixed in the modern disassemblies behind a flag. **DOCUMENTED** for the constants, **SECONDARY** for the bug's write-up, https://info.sonicretro.org/SCHG_How-to:Fix_Ring_Timers.

**Sonic Mania kept the thirty-two and spent the surplus on making a big loss look big.** Its decompiled `Ring_LoseRings` builds three groups of at most sixteen: the first two are real collectible rings and the third is `Ring_State_LostFX`, which has no collision check at all and deletes itself after 64 frames. **So a sixty-ring hit looks worse than a thirty-five-ring hit and is worth exactly the same to recover.** Mania also moved the lockout onto the ring's own timer, 63 frames, and fades the last 15 frames of the 255 to telegraph the expiry. **DOCUMENTED**, https://github.com/RSDKModding/Sonic-Mania-Decompilation.

**Hyper Sonic is the one variant that caps the token count while preserving the value, and it is the cleverest thing in the pass.** `Ring_LoseHyperRings` clamps to at most **8** pickups each carrying `rings / count`, so nothing is lost to the cap and the player has fewer things to chase. **DOCUMENTED**, same decompilation.

**Later entries diverge and two of them bound the loss itself.** Sonic 4 keeps the 32. Sonic Adventure 2 drops all rings and the widely repeated "caps at 20" is a cap on the sprites and not on the loss. **Sonic Frontiers genuinely changed the rule**: Sonic loses only a portion, scaling with his Defense stat, and the loss resolves on landing rather than on impact so a Dodge or Parry cancels it outright. The series' own wiki summarises the sprite budget across entries: about fifteen on Master System and Game Gear, thirty-two in the mainline Mega Drive games and Sonic 4, about fifty in Sonic Rush, about twenty in most 3D entries, and "it is impossible for the player to recover all their Rings when they drop them, unless they only carry a few of them". **SECONDARY**, https://sonic.fandom.com/wiki/Ring.

**So Sonic bounds the mercy and leaves the loss whole, and Mark's ruling bounds the loss.** Both are bounded and they are bounded at opposite ends, which is worth saying out loud because this project already cites Sonic for the rung and the citation does not carry over to the cap.

**The neighbours, for completeness, because each is a different answer to the same question.** **Tony Hawk's Pro Skater** is the cleanest banked-against-pending split: a bail forfeits 100 percent of the uncommitted combo, which can be the bulk of a run, empties the Special meter, and never touches the banked score. **SECONDARY**, https://support.activision.com/tony-hawks-pro-skater-1-2/articles/tony-hawks-pro-skater-1-2-scoring-and-combos. **Shovel Knight** drops **25 percent** of accumulated gold as winged Treasure Bags at the death spot, halved to 12.5 percent by four different pieces of defensive gear, permanently lost on a second death or on finishing the level without them. **SECONDARY**, https://shovelknight.fandom.com/wiki/Death. **Hollow Knight** is the contrast case at the other extreme: **100 percent** of held Geo on the Shade, no cap, no fraction, and dying again destroys it permanently. **SECONDARY**, https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Shade. **Kirby** ejects the whole Copy Ability as a bouncing Ability Star that can be re-inhaled and bursts on a timer or on magma, an all-or-nothing drop on one indivisible thing. **SECONDARY**, https://wikirby.com/wiki/Ability_Star. **Spelunky rules itself out**: ordinary damage costs no gold at all, and only Leprechauns and Monkeys pull nuggets out, which stay in the level. **SECONDARY**, the Spelunky wiki.

**Partial recovery of a slice, where it ships, is generous rather than token.** Diablo II's Lord of Destruction returns **75 percent** of lost experience for recovering the corpse where it fell; EverQuest's resurrection restores **90 percent**, 93 for a Necromancer and up to 96 for a Cleric. **DOCUMENTED** and **SECONDARY** respectively, the Arreat Summit and https://wiki.project1999.com/Experience.

## 5. The band, converted into this game's own numbers

**The shape the evidence supports is the lesser of the standing score and a flat amount.**

**It is the shipped shape twice over rather than a construction.** Mario Kart's `min(3, held)` is exactly it, on a scoreboard-adjacent currency, applied per hit, with the loosened coins re-collectible; Shadow the Hedgehog's ten rings a hit is the same rule on the other family's own resource. Every genuine arcade *score* subtraction in section 2 is a flat amount, and two of the three floor at zero, which is the behaviour a lesser-of rule produces without a second sentence. And where a fraction does ride under a bound, the bound is always the lesser of the two and never the larger: Minecraft, Stardew, Diablo II, poker.

**A fraction of the standing score is refused on the evidence.** Nobody ships one on a score at all; the one game that prices a hit as a share of the purse sells it as a lunar curse; and a fraction of a small score would make the first rung cost nothing in exactly the early runs where the ladder fires soonest.

**There is a second reason from this game rather than from the genre, and it is the stronger one.** The score rung is spent once per arming whatever it paid, including on a hit that finds no score at all (design record R4). A rung whose cost in rungs is constant and whose cost in points is not would be two rules wearing one name. **A flat cap makes the sentence a player can say out loud: a hit at the floor costs you so many kills.**

**The band is 10 to 40 trash kills, which at today's `TRASH_KILL_SCORE` of 100 is 1,000 to 4,000 points, and at the storm's measured 2.47 kills a second is 4 to 16 seconds of mowing.** The value inside it is the coder's craft call, stated as a multiple of `TRASH_KILL_SCORE` exactly as every mob row's own payout is, and annotated as a first figure.

**Both ends are argued rather than picked.**

**The lower end, 10 trash kills.** Below four seconds of mowing the loss is under one percent of a mid-sized run's gross and the rung reads as free, which is a named shipped failure rather than a worry: Great Mahou Daisakusen's own wiki calls its surplus values "extremely minuscule score gains and the player can safely ignore them" ([`score-inputs-precedent.md`](score-inputs-precedent.md) section 3), and a readout the player learns to stop reading is the thing R4 already refuses one step up.

**The upper end, 40 trash kills.** At 4,000 points a run that bleeds twice loses 8,000, which is two thirds of the leanest measured run's whole gross of 12,400 and about an eighth of the richest run's 58,112. Above that the ladder is again the first thing that decides which run wins, which is what Mark's ruling was taken against.

**Two shipped ratios were checked for a tighter derivation and neither converts, which is why the band is stated against this game's own measurements instead.** Astrosmash's gun hit costs one to ten of its own kills at every tier, which here would be 10 to 1,000 points; the band's floor sits at the top of that range because this game's runs are minutes rather than seconds long and its score is orders of magnitude larger by the end. Mario Kart's three coins is 30 percent of the ten-coin carrying cap, which looks like a large fraction and is not comparable at all: that stock is hard-capped and refills in seconds, while this score has no ceiling and accumulates over a whole run.

**What the band is read against once it exists**, and it is the same answer R4 already gives for `TRASH_KILL_SCORE` itself: the score a whole run ends on and the ladder's own cost, which is slice M6's reading. **Nothing here can measure it, because nothing has yet played a run where a hit at the floor left anything standing.**

## 6. Searched for and not found

**No shipped arcade or console game deducts a fraction of an accumulated score on a hit or a death.** Three passes reached this independently, with no shared queries and no shared sources, and it is a genuine negative rather than a search that ran out of time.

One pass ran a dedicated `answer` call for a game that subtracts points from the total on a hit or a life lost, which surfaced only Hellsinker and Zero Wing, both disproved in section 2; searches for a game costing a percentage of accumulated score, which returned itch.io game-jam entries and non-score analogues only; and both pages of the arcade-museum thread "Can you name a game where you can get a negative score?", read in full, whose entire yield is Barrier, Star Hawk, Astrosmash, Space Hawk, Golden Tee, Diggerbonk and Alpine Ski, **not one of them a shmup and not one of them fractional**. It also checked Sky Force Reloaded, Deathsmiles, Radiant Silvergun, Crimzon Clover, Geometry Wars, Tempest, Rally-X, Elevator Action, Sin & Punishment, Raiden and Dangun Feveron, every one of which resets a multiplier or a counter and none of which touches the total. A second pass ran four searches and two `answer` calls phrased to invite either a named game or an explicit "none known", across shmups.wiki, Hardcore Gaming 101, arcade-history and pinball rulesheets, and returned the same negative.

**Pinball is clean too.** Classic tables never decrease the game score. The two exceptions found are both mode-local: Drained (2023) starts a battle at 25,000 and counts down per switch hit, and Jack-Bot's Casino Run can erase points and awards earned inside that mode. **Neither touches the banked game score.** **SECONDARY**, https://www.pinballnews.com/site/2023/10/02/drained-bite-sized/ and https://pinball.org/rules/jackbot.html.

**One number could not be closed and nothing here rests on it.** EverQuest's experience loss is a percentage of the experience the previous level required, which is why its bite varies across hell levels, but the percentage itself is not stated on the Project 1999 wiki and the EQEmu `zone/exp.cpp` source could not be fetched. **The shape is what this file needed and the shape is settled**; the constant is left open and named here so a later pass does not re-run the search blind.

**Three things nobody has published, each named so a later pass does not re-run them.** Whether Star Hawk's 800-point subtraction is in the arcade ROM or only in community accounts, which the Vectrex manual's silence leaves open. Whether any game floors a proportional loss at a flat minimum, which is the MAX shape section 3 could find only outside games. And Sonic CD's own spawn clamp, which the series wiki groups with the mainline thirty-two but which was not read out of a disassembly here.

**Two beliefs this pass carried in and had to drop, recorded because both would have become fake precedent.** That Sonic caps the ring loss: it caps the recovery and empties the counter, section 4. And that Sonic Adventure 2 caps the drop at twenty: that is a sprite budget, not a rule, and the loss there is total.
