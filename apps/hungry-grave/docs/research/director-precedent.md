# Director precedent: what shipped directors read, what they may change, and how they were tuned

Research for Hungry Grave. Labels: **DOCUMENTED** = a developer statement (talk, commentary, interview, patch note), a shipped data or script file, decompiled code, or a disassembly write-up. **COMMUNITY-MEASURED** = wiki or player-derived, including convar dumps and speedrun records. **INFERRED** = my reading across sources.

This document sits beside [survivor-numbers.md](survivor-numbers.md), [reward-delivery-models.md](reward-delivery-models.md), [progression-tuning-precedent.md](progression-tuning-precedent.md) and [shmup-stage-design.md](shmup-stage-design.md). Those already hold Vampire Survivors' per-minute `Stage.json` spawn schedule for Mad Forest, 20 Minutes Till Dawn's authored spawn session table, Halls of Torment's Agony history through 2023-10-30, Risk of Rain's cut kill-speed scaling, the rank formulas for DoDonPachi, Touhou EoSD, Garegga, Gradius and Truxton, and Ikaruga's authored mountain-and-valley curve. Anything there is cited, never repeated. The new ground here is the director itself: what a shipped one reads, what it is allowed to change, what it never touches, how it was tuned and against how much play, and what broke.

The question this answers: should a five-minute single-stage shmup have a density director at all, and if so what does a shipped director read, what may it change, and how was it tuned? The alternative on the table is an authored stage whose per-phase density numbers are real knobs in data. Hungry Grave's current ruling is ADR 0047, directed density inside authored beats, which reads pressure and never power, adds over the authored rows as a floor, both raises and lowers, and is silent during boss phases, the drain-out and the Wall. It superseded ADR 0006's authored-rows ruling on 2026-09-01 and names Left 4 Dead as its origin. It is treated below as one option with that origin, never as a constraint. Two facts carried throughout: power now arrives from authored carrier mobs the director never spawns (ruled 2026-09-02), so the director cannot touch power; and the stage is five minutes, one stage, a miniboss, a drain-out, a final boss.

The strongest sources are Michael Booth's two GDC 2009 decks hosted by Valve, a 2009 transcription of the spoken talk, the shipped Left 4 Dead developer commentary, Gabe Newell's own December 2008 essay on the director, Valve's shipped director VScripts for Left 4 Dead 2, a 2026 disassembly of the L4D2 server binary that recovers the intensity coefficients, the decompiled Risk of Rain 2 C# and the decompiled Vermintide 2 and Darktide Lua, Chasing Carrots' four Agony patch notes across six weeks, and the Cave Shooting History and Gradius interviews at shmuplations.com. The gap, as in the siblings: no developer in any lineage reports a playtest count or a tuning duration for a director as a number.

## What is solid

These are the claims I would build on.

1. **Every shipped director in this record reads the same thing, and it is pressure in Hungry Grave's sense: damage taken, being downed, and enemies close by.** Left 4 Dead's per-survivor intensity rises when injured proportional to damage, on incapacitation, on being pulled off a ledge, and when a nearby infected dies inversely proportional to distance; it decays toward zero over time and does not decay while infected are actively engaging the survivor. Vermintide 2 ships the same four inputs with numbers: 1 point per 1% health lost, 50 on knockdown, 10 on being pounced, 1 per nearby kill scaled by 1/distance, decaying 2 per second after a 3 second hold. Darktide's "tension" reads knockdown, death, pounce, net, and per-hit damage. Barotrauma's reads crew health, hull integrity, flooding, fire and enemy danger. Gautam Babbar's words for it: "a system that tracks each Survivor's 'stress level' by watching for events like 'how much damage you are taking', 'how many zombies have you killed near you', and so on." DOCUMENTED throughout. No shipped director reads the player's inventory, level, or damage output.

2. **The Left 4 Dead signal is one float per survivor, and the exact increments are now on record.** Five severity categories add +0.0125, +0.05, +0.125, +0.25, or saturate to 1.0; damage under 20% of health is category 2, 20% to 50% category 3, 50% and over category 4; the first incapacitation saturates; a Witch or Tank dying within 150 units is category 4, within 500 units category 3. After an event the value holds for 5 seconds, then decays linearly from full to zero over 30 seconds. The tempo reads the maximum instantaneous intensity among living survivors, never a time average. DOCUMENTED (disassembly, 2026), consistent with Booth's 2009 description.

3. **The cycle is build up, sustain peak, peak fade, relax, and the thresholds and durations are known.** Build Up runs the full population until the maximum survivor intensity crosses 0.9. Sustain Peak holds the full population 3 to 5 seconds. Peak Fade switches to the minimal population and waits until the intensity falls back under 0.9 and the current fight resolves. Relax holds the minimal population 30 to 45 seconds, or until the survivors have travelled far enough toward the next safe room, then Build Up resumes. Wanderers return during Relax once intensity is under 0.3 on Normal, 0.5 on Advanced, 0.8 on Expert. DOCUMENTED (deck for the shape and durations, disassembly for the thresholds). Vermintide 2's cycle is the same four states with a mean instead of a max, a peak threshold of 45 of 100 on Normal rising to 90 on Cataclysm, a fade exit at 32.5, and a relax of 50 to 70 seconds. Darktide's is six states with a relax of 65 to 100 seconds. DOCUMENTED (decompiled Lua).

4. **What the Left 4 Dead director may change is the population between the beats, and it never touches the bosses, the set pieces, or where items can be.** The full population is wanderers, mobs (20 to 30 common infected every 90 to 180 seconds on Normal, 75% from behind) and special infected; the minimal population is none of those, though existing specials may still attack. Booth's slide reads: "Boss Encounters NOT affected by adaptive pacing. Overall pacing affected too much if they are missing. Boss encounters are intended to change up the pacing anyhow." Tank and Witch positions are dealt from a shuffled three-card deck (Tank, Witch, Nothing) at fixed intervals along the escape route, no repeats allowed. Weapon caches and scavenge items are placed by the map designer; the director only chooses which of the possible ones exist. During crescendo events, finales and Tank fights the director is quieted: no common or special spawns, though their timers keep counting. DOCUMENTED (deck, shipped scripts) and COMMUNITY-MEASURED (the quieting rule).

5. **Booth's own summary is that the director adjusts pacing, not difficulty, and that the intensity estimate is crude.** "Algorithm adjusts pacing, not difficulty. Amplitude (difficulty) is not changed, frequency (pacing) is." And: "Simple algorithms can generate compelling pacing schedules. Survivor Intensity estimation is crude, yet the resulting pacing works." Spoken: "We tried several things. As I'm sure you've found, the more clever you get the more complicated and worse things end up. What we've found is that the simplest worked really effectively." DOCUMENTED.

6. **The director was tuned by daily playtests for three years, several hours a day, with bots playing tens of thousands of games overnight; no count is published.** Booth: "in about a week we had a very rough, but very playable and fun prototype. We began our daily cycle of playtest/discuss/modify that continued for the next three years." Charlie Burgin on Left 4 Dead 2: playtesting "often takes several hours out of every day," every session recorded. Newell: "We can turn off the graphics and have the game play itself and then use much more statistical methods for analyzing outcomes. We can play tens of thousands of games every night and then spend some time looking at graphs rather than watching individual players play," and "I don't want to give anyone the impression that we've abandoned human play testing." DOCUMENTED. Nothing comparable is on record for any other director.

7. **The four-state machine was three states first, and the fourth was added because players used up the relax period still fighting.** Booth: "Sustain Peak which basically holds for 3-5 seconds to try to make sure that whatever got the survivors into this situation is resolving itself. That turned out not to be enough, so we added a Peak Fade... What we found with this early on is that in really bad situations the survivors would still be fighting and finishing up what got them in trouble in the first place, and use up this whole relax period. Next thing they know they're back in Build Up and another mob would come." DOCUMENTED (transcribed talk).

8. **Players game every shipped director, and every shipped director carries a counter for it in code.** Booth: "Players are pretty smart, they figure things out like 'Hey it seems pretty quiet, I think the director is in Relax mode. Let's run!' So we mark the spot where that happens, and if they go too far then we put them back into build-up anyway." That is the flow-travel exit on Relax, shipped as `RelaxMaxFlowTravel`. Vermintide 2 ships `are_players_rushing`, a `rush_intervention` that spawns a special on the loneliest player, and `handle_speed_runners`. Barotrauma's event threshold "gradually increases with time... even if the players are trying to abuse the system by intentionally keeping the intensity high by causing breaches, damaging themselves or such." Left 4 Dead speedrunners incapacitate the bot survivors in the safe room because "a team that is struggling may find additional health kits and less enemies." DOCUMENTED (Booth, the three code bases) and COMMUNITY-MEASURED (the runners).

9. **Risk of Rain 2's directors read nothing about the player except headcount and the clock, on purpose, and the reason is on record twice.** Credits per second are `creditMultiplier × (1 + 0.4 × coeff) × (playerCount + 1) / 2`, where the coefficient is the time-keyed formula in the sibling; a grep of the decompiled `CombatDirector.cs` for health, item count, kill or damage finds nothing. Drummond, on the cut player-state scaling: "If we did the scaling with you correctly, I think it'd make every round feel the same." DOCUMENTED. Its documented failure is the opposite of Left 4 Dead's: the director stalls when everything it can afford is "too cheap," patched at least three times over two years, and Hopoo's own note reads "things just... stop spawning."

10. **Arcade rank is the only adaptive difficulty on record that lives inside a two to four minute stage, and it reads survival time and power rather than pressure, drops only on death or a hit, and is capped.** What it changes is enemy HP at spawn (Garegga ×1.125 to ×2.75, applied to newly spawned enemies only), bullet count and speed (EoSD bosses "a factor of 4 or more" and speed doubled between minimum and maximum rank; Truxton bullet speed by formula), fire frequency, boss timing (DoDonPachi's boss "will wait more or less time between its attacks depending on your rank"), and in Garegga and Pink Sweets the count of a few named spawns (four to six mini Black Hearts). Nothing on record says rank changes stage layout, which enemies appear, or the item drop order. Yagawa on why it exists: "income at the arcades is equivalent with the amount of time one spends playing... being able to play longer and longer as you improve at the game is enjoyable." DOCUMENTED (disassembly transcriptions, interviews).

11. **The two survivors-likes that read difficulty from play both moved it to a clock, and Halls of Torment's record shows the tuning cost: six weeks and four patches, then removal.** Agony shipped 2023-08-31 kill-driven with living monsters decaying it; 2023-10-30 moved the gain to time for "overly dramatic rubber-banding" but kept the decay; 2023-11-24 multiplied the decay by five and delayed it 25% and claimed "Agony does not rubberband anymore"; 2023-11-27 "Decreased the effect of monsters on Agony in general"; 2023-12-14 "Agony decay has been entirely removed... Agony will increase by 1 level every 5 minutes." DOCUMENTED (Steam patch notes). Brotato, Vampire Survivors and 20 Minutes Till Dawn never had a director; the developer statements found explain nothing about why, and the shapes they shipped instead are per-wave and per-minute records whose field names are recorded below.

12. **No shipped director lives in a unit of play shorter than a Left 4 Dead chapter, and no shipped director is replayable from a seed and an input tape.** Left 4 Dead campaigns run 20 to 75 minutes over five chapters, so a chapter is roughly four to fifteen minutes; Risk of Rain 2 stages are four to ten minutes inside a 30 to 60 minute run; Vermintide and Darktide missions are 20 to 40 minutes; Barotrauma rounds are longer still. Devil Daggers (a five-minute-scale game) ships a "completely scripted" spawnset file, GTFO ships authored population-point waves, Deep Rock Galactic's swarms are hazard-keyed timers, and Killing Floor 2's squads read wave number and player count. COMMUNITY-MEASURED for the lengths, DOCUMENTED for the data shapes. Searched for and not found: any director whose state derives only from a seed and recorded inputs.

13. **Newell names the cost of a director that no other source does: the level designers lose their knobs.** "They don't place weapons. They don't place monsters. They don't control the number of monsters that show up. Those very simple knobs that they're used to turning just aren't there. They've gone from being the director of the experience to handing that off to a big chunk of AI code. That was an interesting thing for them to wrestle with - to have to learn to have faith that the in-game director would do something interesting with their spaces." DOCUMENTED. Left 4 Dead 2's answer was to give some of it back as authored finale scripts (a fixed list of PANIC, TANK and DELAY stages per map) and director option overrides per map section.

14. **The authored-table games tune by editing a row after reading player reports, and the patch notes show the edits.** Vampire Survivors: "changed enemies in Mad Forest minutes 25-30" (0.2.5), "a late-game issue that would occasionally spawn too many enemies off-screen" (0.2.13), "increased XP gain over the first 10 minutes in Stage 3" (0.3.0c), "Minor changes to spawn waves" (0.5.0). 20 Minutes Till Dawn's "Enemy spawn rate slightly reduced at the 8-10 minutes mark" and the 10 to 18 minute HP cut are in the sibling. DOCUMENTED (patch notes). No survivors-like developer describes a tool beyond play and the table.

## 1. Left 4 Dead: the primary source

### What the intensity signal reads (DOCUMENTED)

Booth's GDC 2009 slide "Estimating the 'emotional intensity' of each Survivor," verbatim from the deck at <https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf>: "Represent Survivor Intensity as a value. Increase Survivor Intensity: When injured by the Infected, proportional to damage taken. When the player becomes incapacitated. When player is pulled/pushed off of a ledge by the Infected. When nearby Infected dies, inversely proportional to distance. Decay Survivor Intensity towards zero over time. Do NOT decay Survivor Intensity if there are Infected actively engaging the Survivor." The companion deck adds "a single floating point value." Source: <https://cdn.cloudflare.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ReplayableCooperativeGameDesign_Left4Dead.pdf>.

Newell's essay lists what the team looked at before settling on that: "Are they moving together as a group or are they splitting up? Is their mouse jerking around a lot, or are they interacting smoothly? Are they agitated or are they relaxed? How much damage are they taking? How accurate is their shooting? Then, using all that data we tried to come up with a model to tell us how the player is faring in the game." Source: <https://web.archive.org/web/20090106130006/http://www.edge-online.com/blogs/gabe-newell-writes-edge>. Only the damage, incapacitation, ledge and nearby-death inputs survived into the shipped signal Booth describes; the community wiki adds that friendly fire and sniping distant infected add nothing. COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <https://left4deadwiki.com/wiki/The_Director>.

The exact numbers, from a 2026 clean-room disassembly of the June 2026 `server.dll` validated against 209 captured runtime updates (DOCUMENTED, disassembly, <https://github.com/zeljkovranjes/l4d2-director-system-research>):

| Category | Recovered use | Increment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | distant or low-threat enemy death | +0.0125 |
| 2 | damage under 20% of health, or a nearby ordinary enemy death | +0.05 |
| 3 | damage 20% to 50%, ledge grab, Witch or Tank death within 500 units | +0.125 |
| 4 | damage 50% and over, later incapacitation, Witch or Tank death within 150 units | +0.25 |
| 5 | first incapacitation, or defibrillator revival in coop on Easy or Normal | saturates to 1.0 |

After an event the instantaneous value holds five seconds, then `instant(t + dt) = max(0, instant(t) - dt / 30)`: a linear decay over 30 seconds. The convar `intensity_decay_time` is 30. A separate time-averaged value exists but the tempo does not use it; the tempo reads "the maximum instantaneous intensity among eligible living survivors."

### The cycle (DOCUMENTED)

From the deck, verbatim: "Build Up: Create full threat population until Survivor Intensity crosses peak threshold. Sustain Peak: Continue full threat population for 3-5 seconds after Survivor Intensity has peaked. Ensures minimum 'build up' duration. Peak Fade: Switch to minimal threat population ('Relax period') and monitor Survivor Intensity until it decays out of peak range. This state is needed so current combat engagement can play out without using up entire Relax period. Peak Fade won't allow the Relax period to start until a natural break in the action occurs. Relax: Maintain minimal threat population for 30-45 seconds, or until Survivors have traveled far enough toward the next safe room, then resume Build Up."

The compiled thresholds: `IntensityThreshold = 0.9` ends Build Up, `IntensityRelaxThreshold = 0.9` ends Peak Fade, and wanderers may return during Relax once the maximum intensity is under 0.3 (default), 0.5 (Hard) or 0.8 (Expert). Sustain Peak's timer is `director_sustain_peak_min_time 3` to `director_sustain_peak_max_time 5`. DOCUMENTED (disassembly) and COMMUNITY-MEASURED (convar dump, <https://www.gamerconfig.eu/command/left-4-dead-2/director_sustain_peak_min_time/>).

What each state permits, from the deck: "Full Threat Population (Build Up): Wanderers, Mobs, Special Infected. Minimal Threat Population (Relax): No Wanderers until team is 'calm', No Mobs, No Special Infected (although existing Specials may attack)." Wanderer counts stored per navigation area are zeroed "When an area becomes visible to any Survivor" and "When the Director is in Relax mode," so a relax does not merely stop adding, it erases the pending ambient population ahead. That is the director lowering, and it lowers what it would have spawned, never what the map placed.

### The population it directs, and the numbers (DOCUMENTED)

From the deck: wanderers at high frequency; mobs of 20 to 30 "at randomized interval between 90 and 180 seconds" on Normal, "75% of Mobs come from behind," with Boomer vomit forcing a mob and resetting the interval; "Mob size grows from minimum just after spawn to maximum after a duration to balance difficulty of successive, frequent Mobs"; specials "at individually randomized intervals," spawned in areas not visible to any survivor, with cleared status ignored. The disassembly recovers the special-class timers as 30 to 60 seconds initial delay, 180 maximum extra, 45 respawn, 20 ineligible, 5 after a failed spawn, and no weight table: "it chooses uniformly among due-and-eligible class IDs 1 through 6." The convar dump agrees: `director_special_respawn_interval 45`, `director_special_initial_spawn_delay_min 30`, `_max 60`.

The community wiki puts the shipped common cap at 30 spawned at once, mob intervals at "1-4 minutes (varying with difficulty)," and records that a mob the director cannot place is queued, "effectively punishing them with a larger Mob." COMMUNITY-MEASURED.

### What it never touches (DOCUMENTED, COMMUNITY-MEASURED)

Bosses: "Boss Population: Positioned every N units along 'escape route' +/- random amount at map (re)start. Three Boss events are shuffled and dealt out: Tank, Witch, and Nothing. Successive repeats are not allowed." And the pacing slide: "Boss Encounters NOT affected by adaptive pacing. Overall pacing affected too much if they are missing. Boss encounters are intended to change up the pacing anyhow." The disassembly confirms Tank and Witch placement is "first-valid after the recovered shuffle" with no weighted ranking, and that the finale routine "calls the script policy `ShouldAllowMobsWithTank`," so a Tank changes the concurrent mob policy rather than being one more spawn.

Items: "Map designer creates several possible weapon caches in each map, the AI Director chooses which will actually exist... Why designer-placed? Prediction of possible locations beneficial in this case. Allows visual storytelling/intention. Solves item placement issues." The one adaptive item behaviour is conversion: pain pills ahead of the team convert to first aid kits when health is critical, governed by `director_convert_pills_critical_health` (default 50), and only ahead of the farthest point reached, never behind, and never on Expert. COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <https://left4deadwiki.com/wiki/The_Director>, convar dump.

Set pieces: "Normally this only occurs in Crescendo Events, Finales and Tank fights. During this time no Common or Special Infected may spawn, although their timer still counts down, allowing them to instantly spawn when the Director returns to normal." COMMUNITY-MEASURED. The shipped script `director_quiet.nut` is exactly that: `ProhibitBosses = true`, `SpecialRespawnInterval = 999999`, `CommonLimit = 0`. DOCUMENTED: <https://github.com/Stabbath/L4D2-Decompiled/blob/master/Decompiled%20VScripts/director_quiet.nut>.

### The authored skeleton inside which it runs (DOCUMENTED, shipped scripts)

Left 4 Dead 2 hands the tempo back to a script wherever a map wants a beat. `director_onslaught.nut` overrides the cycle for a scripted rush: `ProhibitBosses = true, MobSpawnMinTime = 8, MobSpawnMaxTime = 8, MobMinSize = 20, MobMaxSize = 30, SustainPeakMinTime = 1, SustainPeakMaxTime = 3, IntensityRelaxThreshold = 0.90, RelaxMinInterval = 5, RelaxMaxInterval = 5, RelaxMaxFlowTravel = 600`. Every one of those is a knob the map author turns, in data, on top of the same reader. Source: <https://github.com/Stabbath/L4D2-Decompiled/blob/master/Decompiled%20VScripts/director_onslaught.nut>.

The finales are fixed stage lists. The Dark Carnival concert: `PANIC, PANIC, DELAY 15, TANK, DELAY 15, PANIC 2, DELAY 10, TANK, DELAY RandomInt(5,10)`, with `PanicOptions { CommonLimit = 25 }` and `TankOptions { ShouldAllowSpecialsWithTank = true, SpecialRespawnInterval = 30 }`. The Parish rooftop: `PANIC 2, DELAY, TANK, DELAY, PANIC 2, DELAY, TANK, DELAY`, with `TankLimit = 1, WitchLimit = 0, CommonLimit = 20, HordeEscapeCommonLimit = 15`, and the delays 5 seconds in coop and 10 or 15 in versus. Sources: <https://github.com/Stabbath/L4D2-Decompiled/blob/master/Decompiled%20VScripts/c2m5_concert_finale.nut>, <https://github.com/Stabbath/L4D2-Decompiled/blob/master/Decompiled%20VScripts/c8m5_rooftop_finale.nut>. A finale is an authored row list with the director's reader switched off and its population limits set per row. That is the shape ADR 0006 described, used by the game ADR 0047 borrows from, at exactly the moments 0047 puts off limits.

The bridge gauntlet script reads two things the campaign director does not: `Director.GetFurthestSurvivorFlow()` scales the mob size from 5 to 20 across the bridge, and `Director.GetAveragedSurvivorSpeed()` adds up to 15 more when the team runs, with `CommonLimit = MobSpawnSize × 1.5` capped at 30. Source: <https://github.com/Stabbath/L4D2-Decompiled/blob/master/Decompiled%20VScripts/director_gauntlet.nut>. A set piece that wants speed to cost something reads speed directly, in its own script, rather than through the intensity value.

### How it was tuned (DOCUMENTED)

Booth, in the shipped commentary: "We started work in earnest on what was to become Left 4 Dead in early 2005 and in about a week we had a very rough, but very playable and fun prototype. We began our daily cycle of playtest/discuss/modify that continued for the next three years. Left 4 Dead is the result of this evolutionary design process." And on why a director was affordable: "we could theoretically create huge amounts of game experience with a small team and some clever programming... if we tweak some portion of how the game plays, we only have to do it in one piece of code which then automatically populates all 20 of our maps." Source: <https://left4deadwiki.com/wiki/Developer_Commentary_(Left_4_Dead)>.

Marc Nagel, same source, on the method: friends-and-family testers who have never played, observed while talking, then a Q&A asking "what the best and worst moments of the experience were for them and why." Charlie Burgin, Left 4 Dead 2 commentary: "Up until Left 4 Dead 2, all playtests conducted at Valve required an observer from the team to sit behind each tester... We recorded every playtest so we could go back and reference specific moments at will. This meant that most of the team could continue working during the playtest - which often takes several hours out of every day." Source: <https://left4deadwiki.com/wiki/Developer_Commentary_(Left_4_Dead_2)>.

Booth's deck on the bots: "Survivor Bots: Allowed us to assume 4 player Survivor team for game tuning and balance... Automated stress testing with 4 SurvivorBots and accelerated game time." Newell on the same: "We can play tens of thousands of games every night and then spend some time looking at graphs... It definitely moves us in a much more abstract and quantitative way of thinking about big chunks of gameplay while moving a lot of the responsibility out of the hands of level designers and into the hands of programmers." And the guard: "The automatic testing gives us a security blanket but we still want to watch people and make sure that the things that we create quantitative metrics for actually have real world applicability."

What playtesting cut, from the commentary: Left 4 Dead 2's park "started out having several paths that were dynamically changed for added replayability. However, playtesters found the paths confusing. When we removed the dynamic objects that forced survivors to go in certain directions, we found that having all paths open led to a better experience" (Dario Casali). The original city maps were "giant nonlinear city sections" cut to "a representative and fairly linear route" because "the teams quickly found their favorite route." A director does not survive a confusing space; the space was made legible first.

### Why it exists (DOCUMENTED)

Booth's stated goal is replayability against memorization, not difficulty: "Static placement of enemies and loot hinders replayability. Players are good at memorizing all static locations. Removes suspense of not knowing what will happen next. Results in 'optimal strategy' that works every time... Even multiple sets of manually placed triggers/scripts fails. Players learn all of them, and if they don't see A, they prepare for B or C, etc." Spoken: "We had many randomized sets where there'd be 3, 4 or 5 possible sets every single run. But players are smart; they figure that stuff out! What happened just totally killed replayability."

The pacing half is drama, in Counter-Strike's shape: "Natural pacing of CS is 'spiky', with periods of quiet tension punctuated by unpredictable moments of intense combat. Constant, unchanging combat is fatiguing. Long periods of inactivity are boring." Newell: "a difficulty level is like a flat line response as opposed to a wave... They want peaks and valleys and really big reactions to the choices that they make."

Two things follow that matter for a single five-minute stage. First, the director's reason for being is a map played hundreds of times; Booth's target problem is the one Hungry Grave's one stage will have. Second, the pacing wave it generates has a period: Build Up to a 0.9 peak, 3 to 5 seconds of sustain, a fade, and 30 to 45 seconds of relax, with mobs on a 90 to 180 second clock. INFERRED: a full cycle is on the order of one to three minutes, which is the length of Hungry Grave's entire scroll before the miniboss.

Chapter length, for the same comparison: "Each campaign typically lasts between 20 and 75 minutes depending on the difficulty level," five chapters per campaign, so four to fifteen minutes per chapter. COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_4_Dead>. The two-player record for all four original campaigns is 38:08, under two minutes per chapter at speedrun pace. COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <https://www.speedrun.com/l4d/runs/zqe6po8z>.

## 2. Risk of Rain 2: the director that refuses to read the player

### What the directors read (DOCUMENTED, decompiled C#)

The difficulty coefficient is the time-keyed formula already in progression-tuning-precedent.md; the decompiled `Run.RecalculateDifficultyCoefficentInternal` matches it, with `timeFactor = 0.0506 × difficultyValue × playerCount^0.2` (0.046 in the 2019 early-access build, raised 10% in Content Update 5 with the note "This is a bit of a sanity check, and shouldn't dramatically change the difficulty"). Sources: <https://github.com/WarmBuns/DecompiledPile/blob/c406b061fb3e9b225919653569bd23c9693f1e96/RoR2_diff_20_05_2024-main/DecompilationOutput/RoR2/Run.cs>, <https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Difficulty>.

Credits accrue per director at `creditsPerSecond = creditMultiplier × (1 + 0.4 × coeff) × (playerCount + 1) / 2`, with the two standard directors at 0.75 and the teleporter director at 2.0. The complete list of reads, confirmed in `CombatDirector.cs`: the run stopwatch, the difficulty's scaling value, participating player count, stages cleared, living player count (teleporter boss HP only), and the monster team headcount against a cap of 40. A grep for health, item count, kill or damage finds nothing beyond the elite health coefficient. Two narrow item reads live outside the standard directors: Mountain Shrine stacks in the boss budget, and Defiant Gouge's own stack count. Source: <https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Directors> (transcription, checked against the decompile by the researcher).

### What they change and how (DOCUMENTED)

Spawn cards carry a weight, a credit cost and a minimum stage (Beetle 8, Lemurian 11, Jellyfish 10, Void Devastator 800). Selection is a two-level weighted lottery. Tier 1 elites cost 6× base, Tier 2 (from stage 6) 36×; the director takes the highest tier it can afford at wave start. On a successful spawn the same card repeats after 0.1 to 1 seconds; a wave stops at 6 monsters (4 in 2019). On failure the card is discarded and the director waits 4.5 to 9 seconds (Fast), 22.5 to 30 (Slow), or 2 to 4 (Teleporter). A card is skipped as too cheap if `cost × 6 < credits` and it is not the most expensive card in the deck; no credits are spent on a failure. There is no credit ceiling; a deactivating director hands 40% of its remainder to a random surviving director.

Off limits: the standard directors never spawn the teleporter boss, which has its own instantaneous director with a burst of `600 × sqrt(coeff) × (1 + Mountain Shrine stacks)`; interactables come only from the Scene Director's separate budget, `map base × (0.5 + 0.5 × players)`, which the coefficient never touches (it raises their price instead, `cost × coeff^1.25`). There is no suspension during the boss: the Teleporter Director keeps spawning trash at 2.0 credits per second multiplier through the fight, and once the teleporter is charged no director runs at all.

### Why time and not the player (DOCUMENTED)

The Rock Paper Shotgun quotes are in reward-delivery-models.md ("it'd make every round feel the same"; "Monsters would scale to you, so it wouldn't be that rewarding"). Hopoo in 2019: "The player never really feels safe, or like they can take a deep breath... There is always a balancing act to take time gathering more items to power up or racing against the timer to stay ahead of the difficulty curve that the player is always aware of." Source: <https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-moving-from-2d-to-3d-shaped-the-design-of-i-risk-of-rain-2-i->. The teleporter bosses are the deliberate exception, hitting for a percentage of health "So they feel kind of scary no matter how strong you are." The Artifacts dev note names the tuning unit: "We aim for monsters to have, depending on their role, an approximate 'health per director point' ratio." Source: <https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2984138620263150954>.

### Risk of Rain (2013), for the team-size question (DOCUMENTED, COMMUNITY-MEASURED)

### Reading rank as a director (INFERRED)

Rank is a director with one number. It reads survival time (a proxy for how little pressure the player has been under) and power; it lowers on the one unambiguous pressure event, a death or a hit; it is capped; and it changes bullet amplitude, enemy HP and a few named spawn counts. It lives inside a two to four minute stage because it is a scalar applied to authored rows, not a scheduler with a cycle. Its documented failure is the coupling to power (Garegga's suicide loop, the sibling), and its documented fix is to cap it, hide nothing (SDOJ, Rev.2016), and keep death as the release. Where it reads only time and drops on hit, as EoSD does, the community found it "almost negligible" in the next game and ZUN let it lapse; where it reads power and never releases, as Pink Sweets does, the wiki says "suiciding for rank control is not advised" because there is no release.

### Halls of Torment: the six weeks that killed the decay term (DOCUMENTED, Steam patch notes, appid 2218750)

The first two steps are in reward-delivery-models.md. The rest, verbatim:

| Date | Change |
|---|---|
| 2023-08-31 | "Each killed enemy increases agony, while monsters that are alive reduce agony over time." |
| 2023-10-30 | "In some cases this led to some overly dramatic rubber-banding. In order to gain more control over the Agony dynamic, the score is increased over time independent of how many kills you make. However, the Agony decay is still in place." | 2023-11-24 | "Increasing the time before enemies reduce agony by 25%," "Increasing the amount by which enemies reduce agony by 400%," "Agony does not rubberband anymore." Rationale in the same Q&A: "Increasing monster counts also means impact to the performance." |

## 5. Other shipped directors, and what they cost

### Vermintide 2 (DOCUMENTED, decompiled Lua; DOCUMENTED, interviews)

Per-player intensity, 0 to 100: `intensity_add_per_percent_dmg_taken = 1`, `intensity_add_knockdown = 50`, `intensity_add_pounced_down = 10`, `intensity_add_nearby_kill = 1` scaled by 1/distance; decay 2 per second after a 3 second hold. Per-difficulty overrides invert the sensitivity as difficulty rises: Cataclysm uses 0.2 per percent damage and decays at 6 per second, so the director backs off far less on hard modes. The pacing reads the mean of alive players' intensity. Sources: <https://github.com/Aussiemon/Vermintide-2-Source-Code/blob/master/scripts/settings/conflict_settings.lua>, <https://github.com/Aussiemon/Vermintide-2-Source-Code/blob/master/scripts/managers/conflict_director/pacing.lua>.

A second signal, threat value, sums a per-breed `threat_value` over activated enemies and gates spawning: `delay_horde_threat_value` 40 on Normal and 80 to 100 on Cataclysm, `delay_specials_threat_value` 40 on Normal and 1000 on Cataclysm (never delayed). Source: <https://github.com/Aussiemon/Vermintide-2-Source-Code/blob/master/scripts/managers/conflict_director/conflict_director.lua>.

States: `pacing_build_up` (advance when mean intensity exceeds `peak_intensity_threshold`, 45 on Normal, 50 to 90 by difficulty), `pacing_sustain_peak` (3 to 5 seconds), `pacing_peak_fade` (advance when intensity is under 32.5), `pacing_relax` (50 to 70 seconds, or early exit on zero intensity or rushing). Sustain and fade set threat, specials and hordes to 0; relax keeps roamers and stops specials and hordes; leaving relax delays specials 5 to 10 seconds. Hordes run on their own timer (`horde_frequency` 70 to 150 seconds, `max_delay_until_next_horde` 160 to 200 seconds) and are only delayed by threat, never generated by intensity.

Off limits: bosses, patrols and terror events are level-placed or flow-triggered, and `check_pacing_event_delay` forces all three delay flags true during an event. The mini-patrol spawns only while intensity is between 1 and 15. Players see the same from outside: scripted finales have fixed waves, only the specials random. COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/ai-directors-problems-spikes-of-difficulty-during-specific-final-events/39054>.

Statements: Martin Wahlund on Vermintide 1, "we iterated and prototyped on our so called conflict director system. It reads how well the players do and presents threats to them to keep the game engaging, but not tiresome." Source: <https://80.lv/articles/warhammer-vermintide-stingray-interview>. The postmortem's stated failure was under-resourced QA, fixed by "everyone at the company played the game daily," with "up to 50 people working in the same files." Source: <https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-fatshark-s-i-warhammer-end-times---vermintide-i->. Mats Andersson on Vermintide 2: "The biggest problem we have right now is getting the mix right from the get go because the amount of combinations that we can throw at the players is staggering." Source: <https://www.pcgamesn.com/warhammer-vermintide-2/vermintide-2-enemy-guide>. Failure on record: specials spawning behind players with no cue, contrasted by players with Left 4 Dead's spawn-out-of-sight rule. COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/please-stop-letting-the-director-spawn-specials-right-behind-you-with-no-warning/19844>. No playtest count or tuning duration.

### Darktide (DOCUMENTED, decompiled Lua)

A rewrite, not Vermintide's code. One team-wide `tension` (max 100) with per-player shadows. Inputs by difficulty tier: `knocked_down` 120/80/60/60/60/20, `died` 60, `pounced` 60 down to 20, `netted` 15, `killed_by_daemonhost` 120 down, per-hit `damaged` 0.3 to 0.05, `absorbed_damage` 0.05 to 0.01. Six states: `build_up_tension_low` (to the next at 10), `build_up_tension` (at 60, back at 5), `build_up_tension_high` (at 90), `sustain_tension_peak` (12 to 15 seconds), `tension_peak_fade` (30 to 35 seconds or tension under 60), `relax` (65 to 100 seconds or tension under 5). Hordes, specials, roamers, trickle hordes and terror events are all off during peak and fade; monsters are allowed in every state. A `challenge_rating` per spawn type delays spawns, and a `ramp_up` (1200 seconds on the lowest tier down to 200) scales frequency over mission time. A `max_out_tension` table by alive-player count forces tension to infinity, an instant peak, when the team is reduced. Sources: <https://github.com/Aussiemon/Darktide-Source-Code/blob/master/scripts/managers/pacing/pacing_manager.lua>, <https://github.com/Aussiemon/Darktide-Source-Code/blob/master/scripts/managers/pacing/templates/default_pacing_template.lua>, <https://github.com/Aussiemon/Darktide-Source-Code/blob/master/scripts/settings/difficulty/minion_difficulty_settings.lua>. Failure on record after a 2024 patch: no cooldown between hordes, specials over-represented. COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/new-ai-director-changes/96692>.

### Barotrauma: the one small-team director with readable code (DOCUMENTED, open source)

Every 5 seconds, `target = ((1 - avgCrewHealth) + (1 - hullIntegrity) + flooding) / 3 + fire × 0.5 + enemyDanger`, with enemy danger from combat strength (0.02 per Crawler outside the hull, 0.2 inside). The value rises toward the target over 25 seconds and falls over 400 seconds. Events fire only below an `eventThreshold` that, in the code's own comment, "gradually increases with time... even if the players are trying to abuse the system by intentionally keeping the intensity high by causing breaches, damaging themselves or such." Event sets carry `MinIntensity`, `MaxIntensity`, cooldowns and `DelayWhenCrewAway`. Sources: <https://github.com/FakeFishGames/Barotrauma/blob/master/Barotrauma/BarotraumaShared/SharedSource/Events/EventManager.cs>, <https://regalis11.github.io/BaroModDoc/ContentTypes/RandomEvents.html>. No developer statement on cost or tuning was found.

### Back 4 Blood (DOCUMENTED, interviews and a developer video)

### Alien: Isolation (DOCUMENTED, talk write-up and interviews)

The macro director "always knows your location" and manages a menace gauge that rises when the alien is within short walking distance, when the player has line of sight on it, and when it is close on the motion tracker and can reach the player quickly. At peak "the director backs off and sends the alien elsewhere." Behaviour-tree branches unlock over the campaign, and none of the unlock conditions come from player deaths, "otherwise the AI may develop an unfair advantage." Source: <https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation>. Corroborated: the director "models the stress level of the player." Source: <https://mcvuk.com/business-news/a-tiger-in-the-office-how-alien-isolations-xenomorph-took-shape/>. By the time it was perfect, it was like, 'Oh, wow, everything takes a lot longer!'" and content could not be cut late because it "would kind of upend everything." Source: <https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/alien-isolation-is-too-long-because-the-alien-is-too-clever-says-former-creative-assembly-writer>. Kotaku's complaint: "sometimes the game stretches a bit too much and it starts to feel fake." COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <https://kotaku.com/alien-isolations-artificial-intelligence-was-good-too-1714227179>.

### Resident Evil 4, 2005 (COMMUNITY-MEASURED, debug build)

Rank 1 to 10 over a 1000 to 10999 bar; Normal starts at 5500, Easy caps at 6, Professional pins 10. Kill +75, critical headshot +127, dodge +50; damage taken -400 or -500; game over -800 at the last checkpoint; missed shots -1 to -50 by weapon. Affects enemy damage, health, crit chance, hitboxes and some rank-gated spawns. Source: <https://www.speedrun.com/re4console/guides/3sahj>. Capcom never stated it; the 2005 interview says only that Mikami fine-tuned the difficulty. Sources: <https://www.polygon.com/2015/6/2/8711329/resident-evil-4-mastered-in-game-difficulty-by-shutting-up-about-it/>, <https://shmuplations.com/residentevil4/>. The guide is itself the exploitation manual: runners keep the rank low by taking hits where they are cheap. RE4 is the one shipped director that reads kills as a reason to get harder, and it is the one whose players learned to fail on purpose.

COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <http://www.wiki.killingfloor2.com/index.php?title=ZED_Spawning>. Devil Daggers: "completely scripted," a spawnset file with community editors. COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <https://devildaggers.fandom.com/wiki/Enemy_Spawn_Times>. No More Room in Hell: mapper-placed spawn brushes with `spawn_density`. DOCUMENTED: <https://wiki.nomoreroominhell.com/Zombie_Spawning_Setup>.

Net: among small teams, Barotrauma and Risk of Rain are the shipped directors, one reading pressure and one reading only the clock; GTFO, Devil Daggers, Killing Floor and Deep Rock Galactic chose authored or timer-keyed tables. No small-team developer wrote down what building a director cost them.

## 6. Failure modes on record

**Rubber-banding.** Halls of Torment, four patches in six weeks, then removal (section 4). DOCUMENTED.

**Sameness.** Risk of Rain's cut player-state scaling: "it'd make every round feel the same." DOCUMENTED (sibling). Newell's contrasting claim is that a difficulty level, not a director, is the flat line; the two are describing different readers, since Hopoo's would have read kill speed (power) and Valve's reads damage taken (pressure).

**The suicide loop and its cousins.** Garegga (sibling); Pink Sweets, where the loop is closed off and the wiki's verdict is that rank "cannot reasonably be lowered"; RE4, where runners take cheap hits to hold rank down. COMMUNITY-MEASURED.

**Gaming the relax.** Booth's "Let's run!" and the flow-travel exit; Vermintide's rush and speedrunner handlers; Barotrauma's rising threshold; L4D speedrunners incapacitating bots to trigger generosity ("always incap the bots in the saferoom"). DOCUMENTED and COMMUNITY-MEASURED: <https://l4ds.forumotion.net/t54-the-science-of-l4d-speedruns>, <https://github.com/ImAciidz/L4D-Speedrunning/wiki/Fullgame-Routing>. The AiGameDev commenters in 2009 predicted the shape before the runners found it: "Is it possible for the player to control the difficulty of the game by faking his/her 'maximum intensity'?" Source: <https://www.cs.drexel.edu/~santi/teaching/2012/CS680/papers/> (the 11 Secrets PDF).

**Stalling.** Risk of Rain 2's too-cheap rule left late stages empty; patched three times and then given a "nothing spawned for 60 seconds" escape in Simulacrum. DOCUMENTED (section 2).

**Unreadable set pieces.** Vermintide players report difficulty spikes at scripted finales from the random specials layered on the fixed waves; Darktide players report hordes with no cooldown after a director change; Alien: Isolation's writer says the adaptive antagonist inflated every authored area. DOCUMENTED and COMMUNITY-MEASURED (section 5). Booth's own guard is the quieting rule at crescendos, finales and Tanks.

**A director hiding a bad stage.** Searched for and not found as a statement. The nearest evidence runs the other way: Valve cut the confusing dynamic paths and the nonlinear city rather than letting the director cover them, and Booth's sentence "even the 'worst possible' game is still pretty compelling" is a claim about the baseline population, which is authored per map as wanderer densities and possible item groups.

## The questions, answered directly

**What does a shipped director read, and is pressure a signal any shipped game computes?** Yes, under other names: intensity (Valve, Fatshark), tension (Darktide), stress level (Babbar, Creative Assembly), menace (Alien), agony's decay term (Chasing Carrots). The inputs are damage taken as a fraction of health (L4D categories at 20% and 50%; Vermintide 1 point per 1%; Darktide 0.3 per hit), being downed (L4D saturates; Vermintide 50; Darktide 120), a nearby enemy death weighted by distance (L4D and Vermintide both), and enemy proximity (Alien's gauge, Barotrauma's inside-hull weighting). Every one is additive events on a per-player float with a hold and a decay (L4D 5 s hold then 30 s to zero; Vermintide 3 s then 2 per second; Barotrauma 25 s up and 400 s down). No shipped director reads what the player owns.

**What does it change, and what is always off limits?** It changes frequency of the between-beat population: wanderers, mobs, specials (L4D); roamers, hordes, specials (Fatshark); event firing (Barotrauma); credit spend rate and card choice (RoR2). Rank changes amplitude instead: HP at spawn, bullet count and speed, fire frequency, a few named spawn counts. Off limits everywhere: bosses (L4D dealt by card, Vermintide level-placed, Darktide monsters allowed in every state but not directed by it, RoR2's own budget), set pieces (L4D quiets at crescendos and finales; Vermintide forces delays during terror events), and item placement (L4D chooses among designer spots and converts pills ahead only; RoR2 keeps interactables on a separate budget the coefficient never touches).

**How was each tuned, and against how much?** Valve: daily playtests for three years, hours per day, every session recorded by the sequel, bots playing tens of thousands of games a night, a three-state machine that became four when watching showed the relax being spent mid-fight. Fatshark: fifty people on Vermintide 1 and "everyone at the company played the game daily." Chasing Carrots: four patches in six weeks by reading player reports, then removal. Hopoo: by feel and by reports, three stall patches over two years. Nobody publishes a count. The tuning record is uniformly "watch a lot of people play, often," and the smallest team that shipped a pressure reader (FakeFish) left no tuning record at all.

**Is there any shipped precedent for a director in a five-minute single stage?** No pressure-reading director lives in a unit shorter than a Left 4 Dead chapter (four to fifteen minutes), and that chapter's director has a cycle period of one to three minutes. The only adaptive difficulty inside a two to four minute stage is arcade rank, a capped scalar over authored rows that reads time and power and drops on a hit or death. Every game at Hungry Grave's time scale that could be checked (Devil Daggers, GTFO waves, the survivors-likes, the Cave and Raizing stages in the sibling) shipped an authored table.

**What does an authored stage with real knobs look like as data, and how was it tuned?** Vampire Survivors: per-minute records of `minimum`, `frequency`, `enemies`, plus `bosses`, `treasure` and `events`, under a header of stage-wide multipliers. Brotato: per-wave groups with `spawn_timing`, `repeating_interval` reducing to `min_repeating_interval`, `min_number`, `max_number`, `spawn_chance`, gated by `min_wave`, `max_wave`, `min_difficulty`. 20 Minutes Till Dawn: sessions of enemy, start, end, HP, cap, cooldown, count. Left 4 Dead 2's own finales: a list of PANIC, TANK, DELAY stages with per-stage `CommonLimit` and respawn intervals. Tuned by editing the row named in a player report: "changed enemies in Mad Forest minutes 25-30," "Enemy spawn rate slightly reduced at the 8-10 minutes mark."

## What this implies for Hungry Grave

Stated as implications, not decisions. INFERRED throughout. The designer decides.

**On the signal, first.** Pressure is industry standard's signal and the formulas are all the same shape: size lost per hit as a fraction of size, a hit at the size floor as the incapacitation event that saturates, a swallow or kill within a short radius of the grave weighted by distance, and no decay while mob fire is in contact. One thing to settle before any of that is built: Left 4 Dead counts a nearby kill as intensity, because for Booth intensity is drama and a kill up close is exciting. For Hungry Grave a kill up close is food, and a grave in the storm at saturation would read as maximally pressed under that rule, so a director briefed to ease under pressure would thin the storm at exactly the moment the design wants it thickest. Reading only damage and floor events, and not kills, is the version that means "pressed" rather than "excited"; it is also the version Vermintide's Cataclysm tuning moves toward by shrinking the damage weight. Whichever is chosen, the record says the estimate can be crude and still work, and that the reader is exploited within weeks by anyone who learns what it reads.

**On the wavelength.** Every shipped cycle is one to three minutes long, and every shipped relax is 30 to 100 seconds. Hungry Grave's scroll before the miniboss is about two minutes and its whole stage is five, with three moments already off limits. A director with Left 4 Dead's constants gets one or two peaks and one relax between the first row and the drain-out, and its relax would be the same length as an authored phase. Either the constants shrink to the stage (a 10 second relax, a peak threshold reached in 20 seconds), which no shipped game has tuned, or the director's beats and the authored beats are the same size and will collide. That collision is the thing Booth's quieting rule and Fatshark's event delays exist to prevent, and both games prevent it by turning the director off, which for a five-minute stage with a miniboss, a Wall, a drain-out and a boss leaves it on for perhaps two and a half minutes.

**Option A: a director inside V1, Left 4 Dead's shape, ADR 0047's origin.** Reads pressure as above, over the rows as a floor, both raising and lowering by withholding, silent at the boss, the drain-out and the Wall, dice from its own seeded stream. What the precedent says it costs: Valve tuned it for three years of daily play with bots overnight and a recording rig; Fatshark with a company playing daily; Chasing Carrots spent six weeks and gave up on the one term that read the field. Newell names the other cost, the rows stop being the designer's knob and become a floor under a reader he has to trust. What the precedent says breaks: the relax gets gamed (run through it), the reader gets gamed (take a cheap hit at large size to be eased), and the cycle length fights the authored beats. What is in its favour, and it is real: Booth built the director for exactly Hungry Grave's situation, one map played hundreds of times until memorized, and the per-run roster and drop choice that 0047 cites are the "3, 4 or 5 possible sets" Booth says players learned anyway. The replay constraint has no precedent at all; every shipped director reads a wall clock.

**Option B: an authored stage with per-phase density knobs in data, Vampire Survivors' `Stage.json` and Brotato's wave groups.** Reads nothing. Changes nothing at runtime; the rows carry `count`, an interval, and an acceleration inside the phase (Brotato's `repeating_interval` down to a `min_repeating_interval`), with a header of stage-wide scalars the way `Stage.json` carries `EnemyHealthMultiplier` and 20 Minutes Till Dawn carries a per-session cap. Tuned the way every survivors-like tuned it: read the tape, edit the row. This is ADR 0006's shape, and it is also what Left 4 Dead 2 does inside its finales, which is where the game Hungry Grave borrows from puts its own authored beats. What it costs: nothing to build beyond the row schema, and every tuning pass is a data edit that the replay reproduces exactly. What breaks: the case that opened this question, a levelled player clearing the back half unpressed, is not answered by the rows, and 0006 named that trigger ("hand-authoring the full stage proving too slow, or replayability demanding cross-run variety"). What the precedent adds: the per-run roster already gives the rows a different cast each run, and the survivors-likes never needed more than a table to keep a fixed 20 minute schedule replayable.

**Option C: one capped rank-style number over the rows, the arcade shape with EoSD's reader.** Reads only survival time since the last hit and drops on a hit or a floor event; never power, which the ruling already forbids and which is the Garegga failure. Changes one thing: a multiplier on row `count` and interval, capped, with the boss, the drain-out and the Wall pinned at 1 the way Garegga's Super Easy pins boss HP. What it costs: a scalar and a cap, applied by the row loader, deterministic from the tape. What the precedent says breaks: it becomes negligible if the cap is low (PCB, Mountain of Faith), it invites cheap hits if a hit is cheap (RE4, where runners take damage on purpose), and it has no valley, only a slope with a reset, so it does not make Booth's wave. What it gives that A does not: it lives inside a three minute stage on record, and Chasing Carrots ended at almost exactly this shape after six weeks of trying the other one.

**Recommendation.** Build Option B for V1 and put Option C's scalar in the row schema as data with a cap, keyed only to time since the last hit, so the back-half question can be measured on tapes with the scalar at 1 and then at a value, without any code that reads the field. Hold Option A until there are recorded tapes from people other than the designer to tune it against, because the only thing every shipped director has in common is that it was tuned by watching a great deal of play, and the two teams that tried to read the field with less than that (Chasing Carrots, and Hopoo before release) both stepped back to a clock. This rests on: the cycle-length arithmetic against a five-minute stage with three off-limits moments; Booth's own statement that the director changes frequency and not difficulty and was tuned daily for three years; Left 4 Dead 2's finales being authored row lists with the reader switched off; the Halls of Torment patch sequence; Hopoo's "every round feel the same" and "things just... stop spawning"; the arcade record that a capped scalar over authored rows is the one adaptive shape that has shipped at stage length; and the absence of any replayable director to copy. The designer decides.
