# First Dig, The Hungry Grave

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Verdict:** Pursue

## Idea in the User's Words

> I want to create a game that might be something that could land in a Halloween game fun side project kind of way. Not sure how to do it but I think it could be cool to have the enemies be undead/ghouls/etc.
>
> The game should be a modern power-up-driven arcade shmup that blends bullet-heaven progression with restrained bullet-hell combat. The core fantasy is becoming an increasingly overpowered weapons platform: lots of satisfying projectiles, weapon combinations, and escalating firepower. Upgrades should primarily come from RNG drops collected during play, in the tradition of classic arcade shmups, rather than repeatedly pausing to choose upgrades from a menu.
>
> Normal combat does not need enormous enemy swarms or overwhelming enemy bullet density. When fewer or tougher enemies, minibosses, and bosses appear, the game should introduce more bullet-hell-style patterns and dodging. The balance should generally favor the player creating the bullet heaven, with enemies providing occasional, deliberate doses of bullet hell.

Later in the dig, his own words sharpened two things. On saturation: "there's just so many bullets flying at the end. It's just tons and tons of bullets flying, so many things spinning, so many explosions happening, bombs falling. It's just all over the place and that's what I want in this kind of game." On the protagonist, after rejecting vehicle-only and character-only options, he chose the Hungry Grave and pushed the hole.io mashup deeper himself, including corpse-eating as a deliberate movement incentive and the worry that lazy minimal dodging would otherwise dominate.

## Mechanics-Level Capture

### Fantasy

You are the Hungry Grave: a moving open grave, a hole in the ground, ascending a Halloween world bottom-to-top. Everything that dies falls to the field, and everything on the field goes in the hole. You start as a modest pit with a couple of weapon lines and end as a screen-filling storm of your own projectiles.

### Core Loop

Weapon lines auto-fire upward at descending undead. Kills leave corpses that scroll down the field; you physically drive the hole over them to swallow them. Swallowed corpses fuel dramatic burst weapons on top of an always-on floor of free lines. Kills also drop RNG upgrades, collected the same way, which add and level weapon lines. Eating grows the hole; enemy hits shrink it; size is health. The whole game is one collection verb: everything goes in the hole.

### Minute-to-Minute Play

Two alternating textures. Most of the time you are mowing down waves, roaming to hoover fresh corpses and drops under light return fire, watching your storm thicken. On a phase boundary the trash drains out and a miniboss or boss arrives alone; the direction of fire flips and you dodge authored bullet-hell patterns, banking swallowed reserves for the belch. Then it dies, drops a feast, and you are the storm again. Controls: steering, autofire, and one button (the belch, a stored-reservoir screen-clearing eruption).

## Interrogation

### Mechanical Ending

Kill the final boss of an authored stage run. The v1 done-line is one stage plus the final boss (product lens adjustment, accepted); the design target remains three stages of roughly 25 minutes, with stages two and three as stretch. Survival-timer and endless-score modes are recorded as future iterations, not v1 scope.

### Ugly Slice

One to two weeks. One five-minute stage, colored shapes only, one trash enemy type, three to four weapon lines with a few levels each, one miniboss and one boss with one or two patterns each, death, victory, restart. Playable in a desktop browser. It must include the three systems that carry the bet: the swallow-to-burst loop, size-as-health, and the belch. It must apply the readability rule (enemy bullets top layer, reserved high-contrast palette, player fire dimmer) even with rectangles. Instruments: time spent off the bottom edge, belch fire rate versus time at full reservoir, and whether a playtester can say out loud when they got hit. The test it must pass: grabbing the fourth upgrade feels different from the first, the boss feels like a different texture than wave-clearing, and steering into where danger just was (to eat) is delicious rather than miserable.

### Numeric Foundations

| Claim | Calculation or test | Result | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous snowball needs a steady drop cadence | 1 upgrade per ~20s over a 5-min stage | ~15 drops per stage, ~45 per 3-stage run | Drop economy sized before content |
| Restrained density still feeds that cadence | Kill every 2-3s = 100-150 kills/stage; drop rate 1 in 8-10 | ~15 drops/stage, 3-6 enemies on screen early | No tension between restraint and RNG progression |
| Content vs drop volume | 4 lines x 5 levels = 20 steps vs ~45 drops | Maxed early in stage 2 | Overflow rule required: maxed-line drops are eaten for radius/score/bomb |
| Endgame saturation target | ~300 projectiles at ~1.5s lifetime needs ~200 shots/s | Strict 1-corpse-1-bullet needs ~200 kills/s: impossible | Hybrid economy: always-on free lines plus per-corpse burst events |
| Burst cadence under the hybrid | Kill every 2-3s, most corpses caught | A burst every ~3-4s on top of the floor | Swallowing stays theatrical without carrying saturation |
| Render load | ~600-700 display objects worst case vs PixiJS v8 measured 200k sprites at 60fps | Three orders of magnitude of headroom | No engine risk |
| Collision load | ~10k circle-distance tests per frame worst case | Tens of microseconds in plain JS | No spatial partition or physics engine needed |

### Remaining Risks

- Open (the central bet, slice-answerable): shmup grammar teaches dodging away from danger; the swallow economy demands steering into where danger just was. Whether that tension is delicious or miserable is what the slice tests.
- Open (slice-answerable): after a bad hit, does the shrinking hitbox (comeback force) or the smaller mouth (spiral force) dominate? The size floor caps the worst case; the slice checks the seconds after a hit.
- Open (decision pending): which conveyor cure, corpse decay timers, freshness value, or both.
- Recorded with cure: belch hoarding; cure is a capped reservoir with visible overflow waste so belching is the greedy play.
- Recorded with cure: bosses starve the swallow economy; cure is bosses shedding edible pieces (armor, adds, feast chunks at phase breaks).
- Recorded as rule: readability is day-one (enemy bullets top layer, reserved palette); the documented bullet-heaven failure is player effects drowning threats.
- Recorded as rule: satellite mini-graves, if ever built, must not automate eating.
- Noted: stage 1 density is sparse for a "bullet heaven" first impression; eat-chime and burst juice must carry the early minutes.
- Noted: corpses and upgrade drops share one verb and must be perceptually unmistakable.
- Noted: size-as-health is proven mostly at jam scale (plus Osmos); treated as slice-must-include.

## Lens Pass

Three review gates ran 2026-08-17 (game design, product/vision, tech feasibility), plus a follow-up game design gate on the playfield-frame fork. Marker receipts are in the session scratchpad, owed to the map issue once it exists.

### Game Design

**Evidence**

- Devil Daggers: gems dropped by enemies power the weapon, vanish on a ~10s timer, and spiders steal uncollected ones; proof that fuel-you-must-touch with a deadline produces an attack-versus-scoop rhythm (IGN, GameSpot, community guides).
- Vampire Survivors: XP gems require touch (absent magnets) and uncollected gems consolidate to pull players off camping spots; free-roam gems otherwise persist forever, so urgency comes from enemies, not pickups (VS wiki, IGN).
- Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor: reviewers specifically credit the spatial mining objective with fixing Vampire Survivors' aimless kiting (Polygon, RPS, TechRadar).
- DoDonPachi bees and Raiden-lineage medals: collect-under-fire with placement-based value is native shmup grammar (shmups.wiki).
- Osmos: mass as both health and power shipped and read as immediately intuitive (IGN, GamesRadar).
- Katamari scale analyses and postmortem: size-gated ingestion plus camera zoom-out makes growth visceral and legible (Game Developer).
- Bomb hoarding: documented loss-aversion pattern; players die with bombs banked (Paper Dino essays, Game Developer "How to Stop Making Hoarders").
- Readability: bullet-heaven endgame failure is player effects drowning dodge-critical threats; enemy bullets belong on top in a reserved palette (Game Developer "Anatomy of a Shmup", Boghog's bullet hell 101).
- Frame fork: panning-wide vertical shmups (Raiden, Toaplan, Battle Garegga) use width as repositioning margin, not roaming room, and camera motion taxes bullet reading (shmups.system11.org, Boghog); both shipped shmup-survivors hybrids (Gunlocked, Gunfield) chose the fixed vertical screen and reviews praise the constraint.

**Finding**

Sound, better-precedented than assumed; six adjustments, all tuning with known cures, none structural: (1) fix the conveyor (decay or freshness) so bottom-edge strafing stops being fed; (2) bosses shed edible pieces so the signature verb fires at the climax; (3) cap the belch reservoir with visible overflow waste; (4) readability rules from day one; (5) hits never zoom the camera back mid-stage and shrink stays small relative to tier width; (6) satellites never eat at full value and are never independently steered. Follow-up gate on the frame: keep the pure vertical scroller (the scroll IS the corpse deadline, and the authored stage and phase bosses depend on the fixed frame); do not build a panning-wide playfield; free-roam arena is the right skin for the deferred survival mode, designed arena-native there; two frames as separate modes is sane sequencing provided v1 systems are built scroller-only.

### Product / Vision

**Evidence**

- Donut County was criticized precisely for the hole fantasy lacking challenge; hole.io wraps it in hypercasual shallowness and its solo mode is called shallow without rivals; nobody has shipped the hole fantasy inside a game with real pressure (reviews).
- Vampire Survivors entered early access with a single stage and broke out; its appeal per Galante is escalation plus slot-machine sensory feedback on every pickup (The Verge interview).
- Gunlocked and Gunfield show the vertical shmup-survivors hybrid ships and works but is noted for limited longevity; neither has anything like the hole.
- Size-as-health with shrink-comeback exists almost only at jam scale (Don't Exceed, Enemeal, Alice in Gunnerland): workable at this scale, unproven as load-bearer.
- Playtesting drifts later by default (CHI PLAY 2024 finding, cited in housewarming's 2026-08-08 product brief).

**Finding**

Coherent, distinct identity: the grave is one object doing four jobs (health bar, collection loop, ammo economy, comeback mechanic), and every future system gets one identity test, does it feed the hole. Strip either half of the mashup and you rebuild an existing game; the cross is the game. Adjustments, accepted: v1 done-line is one stage plus final boss (three stages stay the target, not the done-line); defer zoom tiers until a second stage exists; defer satellite mini-graves with the trigger "maxed lines undershoot the saturation target in practice"; the slice must include swallow-to-burst, belch, and size-as-health or it tests the part that was never in doubt; the slice milestone carries a named playtester and date; count content as 4 perceptually distinct lines whose 5 levels look different, never as 20 weapons.

### Technical Game Architecture

No feasibility veto. Worst-case entity count (~600-700 display objects plus particles) sits three orders of magnitude under measured PixiJS v8 throughput (200k sprites in a plain Container at 60fps, 1M in ParticleContainer, per the official ParticleContainer v8 announcement); collision is ~10k circle tests per frame, plain JS territory; stepped zoom is a container scale change; the workspace catalog pins pixi.js 8.19.0 and `apps/*` globbing means `apps/hungry-grave` slots in with zero workspace surgery. Non-blocking notes for later: housewarming's render-on-demand seam will not transfer (a shmup needs a continuous ticker); share GraphicsContexts or bake to textures so shapes batch; corpses need eventual despawn.

## Verdict

**Pursue**

All three lenses returned clean on their own terms: no feasibility veto, no structural design doubt, a distinct identity no simpler concept delivers, and a clear aggressive cut that still reaches a finish line. Every open risk is answerable by a two-week rectangle slice, which is exactly what a Park would sit waiting for. The strongest argument against: the ten weeks to Halloween are the same weeks housewarming's map calls a soft target, so pursuing this is also a scheduling decision about housewarming.

### User Response

Accepted Pursue explicitly, and resolved the scheduling argument himself: housewarming goes on the shelf for now and this game gets the focus. He also confirmed the frame decision (pure vertical scroller for v1, free-roam arena recorded as the deferred survival mode's native frame) after asking for and receiving the follow-up gate review.
