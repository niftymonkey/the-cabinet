# The Hungry Grave

A Halloween vertical shmup crossed with hole.io. You are a moving open grave ascending a scrolling world, swallowing what you kill to fuel an ever-thickening storm of your own projectiles, with deliberate, phase-separated doses of bullet hell from bosses.

## What it is, in a paragraph

Weapon lines auto-fire upward at descending undead. Kills leave corpses that scroll down the field, and you pass the grave under them so they fall in; swallowed corpses erupt back out as dramatic burst weapons on top of an always-on floor of free lines. Particular authored mobs carry upgrades, collected with the same verb, that add and level weapon lines. Swallowing grows the hole, mob fire shrinks it, and death is being filled in and sealed shut: size is health. The player makes the bullet heaven; bosses arrive alone, as the last sparse row falls away, and provide the doses of bullet hell.

## The core loop

Shoot, kill, and the field fills with two kinds of grave food: corpses (fuel, common) and upgrade drops (permanent power, carried by particular authored mobs so the stage's own schedule decides how much power a run can hold). Both are swallowed the same way: the grave passes under them and they fall in. Every corpse runs one freshness meter, about 10 seconds from kill to gone: freshness multiplies all three payouts (growth, burst, belch charge) down to a floor of about a quarter, the fill dims as the meter drains, flickers as a last-chance warning near empty (Devil Daggers precedent), and at empty the dirt sucks the corpse under. The seconds are derived and the coupling is the rule: a mid-screen kill must reach the bottom edge as a dim, nearly empty scrap, so a scroll-speed retune retunes the meter with it. Greed has a deadline, the level never feeds a bottom-edge camper, and scraps are never worthless. Upgrade drops never decay; the scroll is their only deadline, and a steady-bright drop beside fading corpses is a free legibility cue between the two.

## Size is health

There is no health bar. Hits shrink the hole; swallowing grows it; being sealed shut is death. The grave is a rounded rectangle, taller than wide, because you are a hole in the ground in the shape of a grave (ticket #30); one scalar is the size and the width follows it at a fixed aspect. Live mobs are never food: contact with one shrinks you the same as mob fire, and only kills leave corpses, so a big grave can never just slip under the wave for free. Shrinking also shrinks your hitbox, which is the built-in comeback. A hard size floor keeps the recovery path from ever physically closing, and size never gates a swallow at all: whatever the grave passes under goes in, whatever its size and whatever the grave's (Hungry Grave ADR 0003); damage at the floor runs a ladder, score first, then weapon levels down to the birthright loadout, then sealed shut when nothing is left to bleed. A hard size ceiling caps growth with the grave standing about a quarter of the field's width tall, because the slice has no zoom tiers to absorb unbounded growth and the Undertaker's gap rule scales with grave width; growth past the ceiling converts to score, so a big meal at full size is never worthless (ticket #30). A hit's shrink stays small relative to a size tier, and the camera never zooms back in mid-stage.

## The economy

Hybrid, sized by arithmetic (see the First Dig record's numbers table). The four v1 weapon lines, from ticket #28 and reshaped by the first-pool ruling of 2026-08-27 (#76): each line owns how it fires. The skull stream is always on, the bell and Territory each act on their own clocks, and the will-o-wisps fire on each swallow, so swallowing defends the dive that earned it, one corpse in, one theatrical volley out. A rotten corpse buys a smaller burst, and each on-swallow line scales the thing it pays in: the wisps by count with a floor of one soul, the stream's surge by duration (Hungry Grave ADR 0058).

The skull stream is the saturation workhorse: skulls pour straight up out of the grave's mouth, from one thin column at level 1 to five rigid fanned columns at level 5, surging for a beat after each swallow, and never homing. Territory is autonomous controlling ground: on its own clock it claims the densest knot of mobs ahead of the grave, inside a window anchored to the grave's own position, the patch drifts down with the world while the player keeps moving, and after a short opening beat grasping hands pull in, slow and repeatedly wound whatever body is over the ground for as long as it stays. Levels buy area and control strength, so a higher level tears open visibly bigger ground that pulls harder, slows more and pulses faster, and the player aims the line with position alone (ADR 0044). The will-o-wisps fire on each swallow and carry the run's homing: each swallowed corpse's soul tears loose and hunts, from one lazy wisp to a converging flight of eleven. The bell is the funeral toll on its own clock, an always-on pulse that throws expanding cones rather than a circle: one cone forward at level 1, more of them at each level, wrapping around toward the sides as they multiply until the top of the line has the whole surround back at field scale, with reach and push growing to pay for the narrow early levels (Hungry Grave ADR 0036). The four motions never blur: straight columns, claimed ground drifting with the field, curving trails, expanding cones. Read by where and when each line acts, the pool is my lane, ahead of me, anywhere, around me at field scale.

The run starts with the birthright at level 1, the skull stream alone and nothing else (Hungry Grave ADR 0045), and every other weapon line only ever arrives through swallowing (ticket #32). Power is metered by carriers rather than priced in kills: particular authored mobs carry the offer, killing one drops it where it died, and the stage's carrier rows decide how much power a run can hold (ADR 0002). A carrier the player never kills pays nothing, and the schedule holds more carriers than a full build needs so that a miss costs a step and not the run (ADR 0048). What falls is an offer of three option bodies side by side, drifting with the field; the grave passes under one and the other two vanish, and a second carrier killed while an offer stands banks toward the next one (ADR 0034).

Every swallow makes the baseline swallow chime and juice regardless of loadout, so a run that has not met a carrier yet is never silent. Four weapon lines at five levels each in v1, with the pool deliberately open beyond them, and the five levels of a weapon line must look different on screen. A maxed line is never offered, so the offer shrinks as lines max and a drop paid when nothing is offerable converts to overflow, bonus radius, score and belch charge (ADR 0034). Everything goes in the hole; nothing on the ground is ever worthless.

## The belch

The one button. Swallowing charges a capped reservoir; the belch vomits it all at once: gas that smothers every mob-fire shot on the whole field, and a burst that kills the mobs within a local radius of the grave (ADR 0008). The cap is load-bearing: corpses swallowed at a full reservoir visibly splash and waste, so belching is the greedy play, not the cowardly one. This is the documented cure for shmup bomb hoarding.

## The stage

The one stage runs eight to ten minutes in three named sections, nine minutes nominal: a short opening section to the Banshee, the longest section in the middle to a swarm set piece, and a shorter last section to the Undertaker, which is Ikaruga's short-longest-short shape (Hungry Grave ADR 0049 and ADR 0050). The clock marks are nominal design intent rather than one absolute run clock, because a shootable boss dies when killed. Before each boss the field thins instead of falling silent: a slow, sparse last row keeps arriving and the boss enters as the last body leaves it, so both bosses still arrive alone on an empty field and the emptiness is a beat rather than a pause (ADR 0051). Nothing thins before the set piece, which is a swarm and arrives into trash the way the Wall does.

Each section owns one property no other section has, which is what keeps nine minutes of three mob types from blending: the first owns emptiness, a ceiling of one live template on what the director may add rather than a law over the field, because a ceiling written as a law would fire at a player who kills slowly (ADR 0047, ADR 0023); the second owns overlap, never fewer than two; the third owns scarcity, a revenant-heavy roster that pays less food per second, though a revenant corpse pays double a shambler's per kill (`mobs.ts:94`), which is why the measure is food per second and never corpses per second. The boundaries announce themselves with a music change on the boundary event and a background that drifts continuously between them, with one real tint departure in the last section. There is no section card, and if a tester cannot say which section they were in, a card is one text draw away.

The set piece is the waking. A dormant eye rides the ground layer down from the top edge on a slower parallax, so it is on screen several times longer than a mob, opens around mid-field, and pours trash out of its one point while the source drags across and drifts down. It fires nothing and it is shootable, so killing it ends the moment early, and it leaves off the bottom edge when its budget is spent. It is a heaven moment and not a second dose of hell: the storm shreds the swarm, the corpses are the payout, and there is no second feast. A cooldown row follows it before the last section climbs.

**Amended 2026-09-08, Mark's ruling after ground adjustment 1:** the eye rides the ground at the field's own scroll rather than on a slower parallax, and it opens a quarter of the way down rather than around mid-field, so it is on screen about one crossing of the field instead of several times a mob's stay. What stood: the pour, its budget and its rate, the tick the eye opens on the Crowd's clock, the silence, the shootability, and the property that committing to the trail pays over waiting. What changed: how long it is there and how deep it opens. What the paragraph could not have known: the ground itself ran at half the field's scroll when this was written, so riding the ground and drifting on a slower parallax were the same sentence; ground adjustment 1 put the ground at the field's own scroll and split them, and the eye then slid upward against its own rock.

**Amended 2026-09-08, Mark's ruling on #104:** killing it removes the source's body, and the pour finishes anyway. The remaining budget keeps pouring from the pour point on the same clock until it is spent, so the hand that commits up the trail and fights the source down never ends its own food early. What stood: the shootability, the silence, the pour and its one point, and the leaving when the budget is spent. What changed: "killing it ends the moment early", which was a forfeit of the rest of the trail; the kill now costs the source's body and the rest of its stay, and nothing else. What the sentence could not have known: it described the shootable answer before the property was measured, and as a forfeit it paid the hand that held back over the hand that committed.

Trash arrives on an authored timeline that the director adds to and never removes from: data rows of phase-local time, template, count, and mob type over a named template library, with directed density filling between the rows over that floor (Hungry Grave ADR 0047 and ADR 0056). A seed pins the dice and not the stage, so two runs on one seed roll the same draws and meet the density their own play asked for (ADR 0012). Row time is phase-local: the stage is phases chained by boundary events, the opening section, the Banshee, the middle section, the set piece, the last section, the Undertaker, because a shootable boss dies when killed and fight length varies per player, so the printed clock marks are nominal design intent, not one absolute run clock. Count lives on the row, not the template, so density tuning and future difficulty modes turn a knob without editing the named, playtest-proven shapes. Every die in the stage (the offer dice, any scatter placement, the director's own) draws from its own named seeded stream, Slay the Spire's pattern, so a pinned seed rolls the same draws in the same order and a directed fill never shifts the authored placements downstream of it. The library starts at six shapes, all entering from the top edge, and a template is a placement rather than a cast: whichever mob type flies it supplies the motion and the firing (Hungry Grave ADR 0016). Drips are lone teaching kills. The File is a single-file lane whose corpses land in a trail that teaches the dive. The V is a spreading chevron that makes you pick a side. The Pincer angles two files in from opposite corners, forcing you across the middle. The Rain is a loose full-width scatter, the density filler a section turns up when its property asks for it. The Wall is the one-shot edge-to-edge curtain.

The rows inside a section are authored to that section's own property, and a section's length is its last row's time plus the fall of the last body in its sparse row. Exact rows, counts and spacings are stage data, sized so a section feeds the grave at the rate its property implies; power is not among the things they fund, because that is the carrier rows' job.

The Wall is the feast wave, and its anchor is split: the Banshee's death starts the Wall clock, with the edge-to-edge curtain at several times the opening section's densest wave entering about two seconds after she dies, and only the swallow of her corpse slams the reservoir full and fires the pulsing glow, so the belch stays earned through the mouth and the stage never waits on a pickup (no scrolling shmup does; DoDonPachi, Gradius and Touhou all roll on past a missed midboss drop). Her corpse drop is choreographed so a committed dive completes before the curtain closes; the dive window is slice tuning, watched by the belch-on-wave instrument. The loaded belch has one unmissable job at the Wall, clearing the air of the curtain's fire and bursting a lane through it, and the corpses of that lane rain down as the reward, while the rest of the width is handed back to the storm (ADR 0008; the set-piece property is re-read in play); the middle section then climbs back toward the Wall's figure without reaching it, so the feast stays the run's outlier. The no-belch Wall is not special-cased: its mobs are the ordinary trash mob with the ordinary contact shrink, no scripted damage, no scripted death, and no protective cap, so crossing it unloaded means carving a lane and weaving on skill, Vampire Survivors' swarm model, and a bad enough crossing can end the run by physics alone. If playtest deaths cluster there, the knob is the Wall's count on its row, which doubles as the later difficulty lever; the belch-on-wave instrument records the no-belch outcome as its third case.

## Bosses

Minibosses and bosses arrive on a phase boundary, alone on a field the sparse last row has emptied, and bring authored, readable bullet-hell patterns. They shed edible pieces throughout the fight (knocked-off armor, summoned adds, a feast chunk at each phase break) so the swallow economy never goes dark at the climax, and the player can earn a belch inside the fight. A dead miniboss is a feast: a corpse big enough to jump your size.

Bosses are always shootable (ticket #29). The health bar splits into chunks, one authored pattern per chunk, with a short invincible flash at each chunk break during which player shots do nothing; there are no pure-dodge survival phases anywhere in v1, because the player's storm must always matter. So the will-o-wisps need no boss-side rule: homing is plain damage against bosses. Bosses and minibosses take full bell damage but are immune to bell pushback, so authored patterns never smear; their summoned adds are ordinary live mobs and get pushed normally.

The belch works in boss fights too, in two scopes (ADR 0008): its gas smothers every mob-fire shot on the field, boss patterns included, and its burst kills the mobs within its radius and deals a big chunk of boss damage only when the boss is inside that radius; it never pushes a boss, and the pattern resumes emitting immediately, so a press buys a breath and a repositioning, not a skip. It fires only at a full reservoir, never as a partial bomb: one big earned moment, and hoarding past full still splashes and wastes. Each wipe is re-earned by diving for shed pieces; boss-fight belch taming (damage per belch, wipe re-earn cadence) is tracked tuning work on the map, watched by a slice instrument.

Shed armor pieces and add corpses run the normal freshness meter; the feast chunk dropped at each chunk break never decays, exactly like an upgrade drop, so the reward beat is calm and steady-bright keeps meaning treasure.

The slice miniboss is the Banshee. Chunk one: slow expanding tear-rings, each with one clean gap. Chunk two: a second offset ring source, so the gaps stop lining up. Her death drops a feast corpse that never decays, treasure class like the chunk-break feasts, so only the bottom edge can take it away; swallowing it pays a growth jump worth roughly 8 to 10 fresh trash corpses and slams the belch reservoir to full with a pulsing on-screen glow. Her death also launches the Wall, deliberately oversized and already arriving as the dive completes, so the loaded belch always has a stage to clear and is never reflexed into empty sky.

The stage's boss is the Undertaker: the man who seals graves, come to seal you. He runs about a minute and a half to two minutes across three chunks, and the length is bought in chunks rather than in health per chunk, because a boss past a minute needs somewhere to go (Hungry Grave ADR 0052). Chunk one, the burial: shovelfuls of dirt fall from the top as slow clod-curtains with one moving gap. The gap always fits (gap width = current grave width plus a fixed margin) and clods are ordinary bullets, one small shrink on touch and never a wall, so size earned before the fight is never punished. Chunk two, the exhumation: a slow one-arm shovel spiral plus summoned digger zombies, which are base trash respawned by the boss (no new mob budget), whose corpses land inside the fight and keep the swallow economy and the bell alive at the climax. Chunk three, the locked overlap: the clods keep falling while the shovel spirals, the curtain's gap sits where the arm has just swept, and the curtain thins to pay for the overlap while the diggers keep coming. His death is the ending: he topples into the grave and the swallow is the victory animation, no payout, the grave swallows the gravedigger.

Mob-fire pattern grammar stays exclusive: the Banshee owns expanding rings, the Undertaker owns falling curtains and slow spirals, and the third chunk's locked overlap is his two shapes at once rather than a new one. The ring is hers alone now that the bell throws cones, so the layer and palette separation stops carrying a shared shape.

## The storm, and reading through it

Endgame saturation is measured rather than targeted: the field is tuned until it is fun and the airborne-projectile instrument reads what it holds, and that figure becomes the density the readability check runs at (raised by Mark 2026-08-19; the old ~300 figure was an unsourced assumption). The skull stream's density (column count, skull spacing, speed) is the free knob. Readability at that density is a day-one rule, not polish, and its render stack, its grayscale value band, and its verification live in Hungry Grave ADR 0014.

The storm is also the ladder. Each line's level is carried by that line's own projectiles, so the player reads what they own by looking at what they are already looking at, and a slim strip carries the score and a row of level pips per line as the second channel. A rung lost shows twice, as that line's projectiles blowing up and as a pip going dark, because a single rung subtracted from a dense storm is invisible on its own (Hungry Grave ADR 0054). A rung stripped at the size floor tumbles off the grave as a body and drifts down with the field, catchable by a dive and taken away by the scroll (ADR 0055). Swallowing that body restores the same line's level rather than a generic one, and one body falls per rung stripped: Salamander is the one shipped case where the thing recovered is the thing lost, and a generic rung would let a hit re-roll a build in the worst second of the run, while the quantity rule comes from Battle Garegga's dropped power-up. Craft call, open to Mark's overrule, from `docs/research/section-feel-consent-and-rung-restore.md`.

## Deferred, with triggers

- Sharing a replay as a link is no longer deferred by the absence of a store: every run lands in one, byte-exact beside its parsed header (Hungry Grave ADR 0057), so a run is kept by uploading it and opened by its id. What is still deferred is the sharing surface itself, the page a link opens onto.
- Stepped zoom tiers with graduating mob sizes: deferred until a second stage exists. The one-tier game keeps the full identity.
- Satellite mini-graves: deferred; trigger is maxed weapon lines undershooting the saturation target in practice. If built, they shoot but do not swallow at full value, and are never independently steered.
- Survival-timer mode: future iteration, and free-roam arena is its native frame, designed arena-native (collection pins you under swarm pressure, short decay so doubling back really loses corpses, no v1-style phase bosses).
- Endless score mode: future iteration.
- A wave director is no longer deferred: it is ruled in, reading the pressure the run is putting on the player, adding over the authored rows as a floor and never removing them, and spending a finite budget per phase (Hungry Grave ADR 0047 and ADR 0056). The Battle Garegga caution that deferred it is answered rather than dropped: the signal reads harm and never power, and never a kill near the grave, because a kill up close is food here.
- The Sweep weapon line: parked, not rejected. Its returning-solid motion is the motion class the four-line pool intentionally retired when Territory replaced the headstones. Trigger: a pool change that reopens that motion class.
- The Detonation weapon line: parked, not rejected. It overlaps Territory's up-field battlefield job. Trigger: considering a replacement for Repel, or any material change to the pool's battlefield jobs.

## Explicitly not doing

- A horizontally panning playfield wider than the screen. Shipped shmups use width as repositioning margin, not roaming room; camera motion taxes bullet readability; corpse deadlines would expire offscreen.
- Genericizing v1's corpse, camera, or threat systems to pre-serve the future arena mode. v1 is built scroller-only.
- Pause-menu upgrade choices. Progression is physical and in-run.
- Mouse-cursor aiming, or any second aim axis. Position is the whole skill; aiming grammar belongs to the deferred free-roam arena mode.
- Cross-line homing upgrades. At most one weapon line homes at a time and a homing line is never always-on, so every other weapon line's shots fly dumb and straight.
- A rate-based spawn faucet. The director adds over authored rows and is silent through the sparse rows the phase-boundary boss rule requires; a bare faucet has no teaching rhythm and cannot hold a boss boundary open, and even Vampire Survivors runs authored per-minute wave tables underneath, randomizing only position.

## Open questions

- The central bet: steering into where danger just was (to swallow) versus the shmup instinct to dodge away. Delicious or miserable? The ugly slice answers it with rectangles.
- After a bad hit, does the comeback force (smaller hitbox) or the spiral force (smaller mouth) dominate? Freshness sharpens this: healing off older corpses pays quarter value, so the slice must watch recovery speed on decayed corpses specifically (product gate on #27).
- Slice instruments to build in: time off the bottom edge, belch rate versus full-reservoir time, drop count, inter-drop spacing, and drops swallowed versus scrolled off per run against the carrier schedule's own count (gates on #32: a tuning promise gets a watcher, and a missed-carrier number separates "unfair" from "missed three"), whether a playtester can say when they got hit, average freshness at swallow once the bell is leveled (its knockback may push kills up-screen into staler corpses), and whether the four weapon line motions stay tellable at full density. From ticket #29's gates: whether the post-Banshee loaded belch lands on the oversized wave or gets spent on empty sky, whether a tester can tell the Banshee's tear-rings from the bell's cones (the two shapes no longer collide, so this is a watch rather than a risk), and how often the belch fires inside each boss fight, with whether a pattern chunk ever ends before finishing one full emit. The human read is a person playing on desktop and on phone; who plays and when is Mark's.
