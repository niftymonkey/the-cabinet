# The Hungry Grave

A Halloween vertical shmup crossed with hole.io. You are a moving open grave ascending a scrolling world, swallowing what you kill to fuel an ever-thickening storm of your own projectiles, with deliberate, phase-separated doses of bullet hell from bosses.

## What it is, in a paragraph

Weapon lines auto-fire upward at descending undead. Kills leave corpses that scroll down the field, and you pass the grave under them so they fall in; swallowed corpses erupt back out as dramatic burst weapons on top of an always-on floor of free lines. Kills also drop RNG upgrades, collected with the same verb, that add and level weapon lines. Swallowing grows the hole, mob fire shrinks it, and death is being filled in and sealed shut: size is health. The player makes the bullet heaven; bosses arrive alone, after the trash drains out, and provide the bullet hell.

## The box

- v1 done-line: one authored stage plus a final boss, playable start to finish in a desktop browser and in a phone browser (ruled by Mark 2026-08-19; the viewport and layout work therefore lands inside v1, not after it). Three stages of ~25 minutes stay the design target, as stretch, not the done-line.
- Replay is in v1 (ruled by Mark 2026-08-23). Every run is recorded as a tape: its dice, its starting size, and the exact steering the grave was moved by. A tape replays the run exactly as it happened, and a tape that can no longer prove it is that run refuses to show a frame or report a number rather than showing an approximation. A replay is watched and a challenge is played, and they are different features: a challenge shares a seed, so the dice repeat and the hands are the friend's, while a replay shares a tape, so the run repeats whole. The recording core and the reading surfaces are dispatches of the instrument ticket; saving a tape you can keep and opening one somebody sent you is the player-facing half and has its own ticket (#49), because it was promised here before any dispatch owned it. Handing a tape over as a file is in v1. Handing it over as a link is deferred below.
- Frame: pure vertical scroller, bottom to top. The scroll is the corpse deadline and the authored stage and phase bosses depend on the fixed frame.
- Stack: PixiJS v8 on the create-pixi `creation-web` template + Vite + Vitest in the existing pnpm workspace, as apps/hungry-grave beside housewarming (ticket #30; React removed, UI is in-canvas via the template's screens and @pixi/ui). Continuous ticker; housewarming's render-on-demand seam does not transfer.
- The base app is a blank scaffold, and everything built to learn from is a prototype: a self-contained folder under `src/prototypes/<name>` behind a `#/prototypes/<name>` route, listed by the base app, removable by deleting the folder and its registry entry. No future iteration builds on prototype code; the real game starts from the blank scaffold once the prototypes have answered their questions (decision-log entry 11).
- Timeline: Halloween 2026 is the soft target (about ten weeks from the 2026-08-17 verdict). Fun side project; housewarming is shelved for now by Mark's explicit call.
- Controls: steering, hold-to-focus (slow, precise movement while held), autofire, one button (the belch). Nothing else.
- Units: the sim runs in one fixed logical field of 540x760 units and the renderer scales it to any screen at fixed aspect; no number in the design or the tuning is a device pixel (ticket #30).

## The core loop

Shoot, kill, and the field fills with two kinds of grave food: corpses (fuel, common) and upgrade drops (permanent power, priced in kills on a rising curve so the sparse opening still pays out fast and the late storm cannot flood the field). Both are swallowed the same way: the grave passes under them and they fall in. Every corpse runs one freshness meter, about 10 seconds from kill to gone: freshness multiplies all three payouts (growth, burst, belch charge) down to a floor of about a quarter, the fill dims as the meter drains, flickers as a last-chance warning near empty (Devil Daggers precedent), and at empty the dirt sucks the corpse under. The seconds are derived and the coupling is the rule: a mid-screen kill must reach the bottom edge as a dim, nearly empty scrap, so a scroll-speed retune retunes the meter with it. Greed has a deadline, the level never feeds a bottom-edge camper, and scraps are never worthless. Upgrade drops never decay; the scroll is their only deadline, and a steady-bright drop beside fading corpses is a free legibility cue between the two.

## Size is health

There is no health bar. Hits shrink the hole; swallowing grows it; being sealed shut is death. The grave is a rounded rectangle, taller than wide, because you are a hole in the ground in the shape of a grave (ticket #30); one scalar is the size and the width follows it at a fixed aspect. Live mobs are never food: contact with one shrinks you the same as mob fire, and only kills leave corpses, so a big grave can never just slip under the wave for free. Shrinking also shrinks your hitbox, which is the built-in comeback. A hard size floor keeps the recovery path from ever physically closing, and size never gates a swallow at all: whatever the grave passes under goes in, whatever its size and whatever the grave's (Hungry Grave ADR 0003); damage at the floor runs a ladder, score first, then weapon levels down to the birthright loadout, then sealed shut when nothing is left to bleed. A hard size ceiling caps growth with the grave standing about a quarter of the field's width tall, because the slice has no zoom tiers to absorb unbounded growth and the Undertaker's gap rule scales with grave width; growth past the ceiling converts to score, so a big meal at full size is never worthless (ticket #30). A hit's shrink stays small relative to a size tier, and the camera never zooms back in mid-stage.

## The economy

Hybrid, sized by arithmetic (see the First Dig record's numbers table). The four v1 weapon lines, from ticket #28 and reshaped by the first-pool ruling of 2026-08-27 (#76): each line owns how it fires. The soul stream is always on, the bell and Territory each act on their own clocks, and the will-o-wisps fire on each swallow, so swallowing defends the dive that earned it, one corpse in, one theatrical volley out.

The soul stream is the saturation workhorse: skulls pour straight up out of the grave's mouth, from one thin column at level 1 to five rigid fanned columns at level 5, surging for a beat after each swallow, and never homing. Territory is autonomous controlling ground: on its own clock it claims the densest knot of mobs ahead of the grave, inside a window anchored to the grave's own position, the patch drifts down with the world while the player keeps moving, and after a short opening beat grasping hands pull in, slow and repeatedly wound whatever body is over the ground for as long as it stays. Levels buy area and control strength, so a higher level tears open visibly bigger ground that pulls harder, slows more and pulses faster, and the player aims the line with position alone (ADR 0044). The will-o-wisps fire on each swallow and carry the run's homing: each swallowed corpse's soul tears loose and hunts, from one lazy wisp to a converging flight of eleven. The bell shockwaves are the funeral toll on their own clock, an always-on expanding damage ring reaching a short way at level 1 and nearly across the field at level 5, with pushback arriving as a higher-level property rather than from the first toll. The four motions never blur: straight columns, claimed ground drifting with the field, curving trails, expanding rings. Read by where and when each line acts, the pool is my lane, ahead of me, anywhere, around me at field scale.

The run starts with the birthright at level 1, one thin skull column and the smallest patch of claimed ground on Territory's own clock, and every other weapon line only ever arrives through swallowing (ticket #32). Drops are priced in kills on a rising curve, Vampire Survivors' leveling model with kills as the currency: the first drop costs about five kills, each next drop costs more, tuned to pay out ten to twelve drops across the run, and the dice only pick which weapon line a drop levels. Early on this reads as the founding one-in-eight-to-ten rhythm; late, when the storm kills fifty in a breath, a rising price keeps the same calm cadence. (Corrected 2026-08-22: this sentence said "prices in the hundreds", written before the row tables existed. The authored stage spawns 268 trash mobs, so prices in the hundreds pay about three drops in a whole run. The derivation and the shipped price table are in `dispatch-5-weapons.md` section 3.)

Every swallow makes the baseline swallow chime and juice regardless of loadout, so RNG drops can never leave the early minutes silent. Four weapon lines at five levels each in v1, with the pool deliberately open beyond them, and the five levels of a weapon line must look different on screen. Drops for maxed weapon lines are still swallowed: bonus radius, score, belch charge. Everything goes in the hole; nothing on the ground is ever worthless.

## The belch

The one button. Swallowing charges a capped reservoir; the belch vomits it all as a screen-clearing eruption. The cap is load-bearing: corpses swallowed at a full reservoir visibly splash and waste, so belching is the greedy play, not the cowardly one. This is the documented cure for shmup bomb hoarding.

## The stage

The slice's one stage is five minutes end to end: about three and a half minutes of scrolling trash, thirty seconds of Banshee at the 2:00 midpoint, and a minute of Undertaker from 4:00, with a deliberate drain-out silence before each fight (ticket #32; midpoint minibosses and pre-boss lulls are the genre's standard stage construction). The drain-out is 20 seconds, not the ten this document first said: a mob spends up to a spawn margin above the top edge, then its arriving beat, then falls the field's height plus its own half-height, and for the slowest type that is a little over eighteen seconds. It is a tuning number that belongs to whoever plays it, and it is a weaponless artifact, so dispatch 5 should re-derive it once the storm kills trash in a second or two (dispatch-4 plan section 4.7).

Trash arrives on a fixed authored timeline: data rows of phase-local time, template, count, and mob type over a named template library, so a pinned run is identical and the playtest instruments compare cleanly across testers (fresh runs roll fresh seeds, Hungry Grave ADR 0012). Row time is phase-local: the stage is phases (opening ramp, Banshee, feast and back half, Undertaker) chained by boundary events, because a shootable boss dies when killed and fight length varies per player, so the printed clock marks are nominal design intent, not one absolute run clock. Count lives on the row, not the template, so density tuning and future difficulty modes turn a knob without editing the named, playtest-proven shapes. Every die in the slice (the drop dice, any scatter placement) draws from its own named seeded stream, Slay the Spire's pattern, so every tester on a pinned seed gets the identical run and build sequence. The library starts at six shapes, all entering from the top edge, and a template is a placement rather than a cast: whichever mob type flies it supplies the motion and the firing (Hungry Grave ADR 0016). Drips are lone teaching kills. The File is a single-file lane whose corpses land in a trail that teaches the dive. The V is a spreading chevron that makes you pick a side. The Pincer angles two files in from opposite corners, forcing you across the middle. The Rain is a loose full-width scatter, the density filler the back half turns up. The Wall is the one-shot edge-to-edge curtain.

The ramp: the first 45 seconds are Drips and one File; Files, Vs and Pincers then overlap two at a time, Rain joining thin, until the drain-out that begins after the ramp's last row at 1:45; after the feast the back half climbs Rain plus Pincers plus Vs, overlapping two and three at a time, to a sustained peak just under Wall density, then drains out after its own last row at 1:08 into the phase. The clock marks are nominal design intent and the phases chain on boundary events, because a shootable boss dies when killed (ADR 0006): a phase's length is its last row's time plus the drain-out, which makes the ramp 2:05 and the back half 1:28. Exact counts per row are slice tuning, sized so the run's total kills fund the drop price curve.

The Wall is the feast wave, and its anchor is split: the Banshee's death starts the Wall clock, with the edge-to-edge curtain at 2.5 to 3 times the front half's densest wave entering about two seconds after she dies, and only the swallow of her corpse slams the reservoir full and fires the pulsing glow, so the belch stays earned through the mouth and the stage never waits on a pickup (no scrolling shmup does; DoDonPachi, Gradius and Touhou all roll on past a missed midboss drop). Her corpse drop is choreographed so a committed dive completes before the curtain closes; the dive window is slice tuning, watched by the belch-on-wave instrument. The loaded belch has one unmissable job, the run's first full-screen wipe, and the corpses of the whole width rain down as the reward; the back half then climbs back toward the Wall's figure without reaching it, so the feast stays the run's outlier. The no-belch Wall is not special-cased: its mobs are the ordinary trash mob with the ordinary contact shrink, no scripted damage, no scripted death, and no protective cap, so crossing it unloaded means carving a lane and weaving on skill, Vampire Survivors' swarm model, and a bad enough crossing can end the run by physics alone. If playtest deaths cluster there, the knob is the Wall's count on its row, which doubles as the later difficulty lever; the belch-on-wave instrument records the no-belch outcome as its third case.

## Bosses

Minibosses and bosses arrive on a phase boundary, after the trash wave drains out, and bring authored, readable bullet-hell patterns. They shed edible pieces throughout the fight (knocked-off armor, summoned adds, a feast chunk at each phase break) so the swallow economy never goes dark at the climax, and the player can earn a belch inside the fight. A dead miniboss is a feast: a corpse big enough to jump your size.

Bosses are always shootable (ticket #29). The health bar splits into chunks, one authored pattern per chunk, with a short invincible flash at each chunk break during which player shots do nothing; there are no pure-dodge survival phases anywhere in v1, because the player's storm must always matter. So the will-o-wisps need no boss-side rule: homing is plain damage against bosses. Bosses and minibosses take full bell damage but are immune to bell pushback, so authored patterns never smear; their summoned adds are ordinary live mobs and get pushed normally.

The belch is the bomb everywhere, boss fights included: it kills every mob on screen, cancels every mob-fire shot on screen (boss patterns too), deals a big chunk of boss damage, never pushes a boss, and the pattern resumes emitting immediately, so a wipe buys a breath and a repositioning, not a skip. It fires only at a full reservoir, never as a partial bomb: one big earned moment, and hoarding past full still splashes and wastes. Each wipe is re-earned by diving for shed pieces; boss-fight belch taming (damage per belch, wipe re-earn cadence) is tracked tuning work on the map, watched by a slice instrument.

Shed armor pieces and add corpses run the normal freshness meter; the feast chunk dropped at each chunk break never decays, exactly like an upgrade drop, so the reward beat is calm and steady-bright keeps meaning treasure.

The slice miniboss is the Banshee. Chunk one: slow expanding tear-rings, each with one clean gap. Chunk two: a second offset ring source, so the gaps stop lining up. Her death drops a feast corpse that never decays, treasure class like the chunk-break feasts, so only the bottom edge can take it away; swallowing it pays a growth jump worth roughly 8 to 10 fresh trash corpses and slams the belch reservoir to full with a pulsing on-screen glow. Her death also launches the Wall, deliberately oversized and already arriving as the dive completes, so the loaded belch always has a stage to clear and is never reflexed into empty sky.

The slice boss is the Undertaker: the man who seals graves, come to seal you. Chunk one, the burial: shovelfuls of dirt fall from the top as slow clod-curtains with one moving gap. The gap always fits (gap width = current grave width plus a fixed margin) and clods are ordinary bullets, one small shrink on touch and never a wall, so size earned before the fight is never punished. Chunk two, the exhumation: a slow one-arm shovel spiral plus summoned digger zombies, which are base trash respawned by the boss (no new mob budget), whose corpses land inside the fight and keep the swallow economy and the bell alive at the climax. His death is the ending: he topples into the grave and the swallow is the victory animation, no payout, the grave swallows the gravedigger.

Mob-fire pattern grammar stays exclusive: the Banshee owns expanding rings, the Undertaker owns falling curtains and slow spirals. Her ring deliberately echoes the player's bell ring, one shape with two owners, separated by layer and palette.

## The storm, and reading through it

Endgame saturation is measured rather than targeted: the field is tuned until it is fun and the airborne-projectile instrument reads what it holds, and that figure becomes the density the readability check runs at (raised by Mark 2026-08-19; the old ~300 figure was an unsourced assumption). The soul stream's density (column count, skull spacing, speed) is the free knob. Readability at that density is a day-one rule, not polish, and its render stack, its grayscale value band, and its verification live in Hungry Grave ADR 0014.

## Deferred, with triggers

- Sharing a replay as a link rather than as a file: deferred until a run has to reach somebody who cannot be handed a file, which in practice is the first time a run wants posting publicly rather than sending to one person. A link needs a blob store, a blob store needs a token-minting route, and this game is a pure static site that deploys `--prebuilt` because a server-side install breaks on the pnpm catalog. Handing over a file needs none of that and is in v1.
- Stepped zoom tiers with graduating mob sizes: deferred until a second stage exists. The one-tier game keeps the full identity.
- Satellite mini-graves: deferred; trigger is maxed weapon lines undershooting the saturation target in practice. If built, they shoot but do not swallow at full value, and are never independently steered.
- Survival-timer mode: future iteration, and free-roam arena is its native frame, designed arena-native (collection pins you under swarm pressure, short decay so doubling back really loses corpses, no v1-style phase bosses).
- Endless score mode: future iteration.
- A wave director for the full-length stage: deferred until hand-authoring the 20-25 minute stage proves too slow in practice, or the replayability question demands cross-run variety. The slice's playtest-proven wave templates are its vocabulary if it ever gets built. Standing caution: a director that reads player performance invites Battle Garegga's failure, where adaptive rank made deliberate suicide the optimal line.
- The Sweep weapon line: parked, not rejected. Its returning-solid motion is the motion class the four-line pool intentionally retired when Territory replaced the headstones. Trigger: a pool change that reopens that motion class.
- The Detonation weapon line: parked, not rejected. It overlaps Territory's up-field battlefield job. Trigger: considering a replacement for Repel, or any material change to the pool's battlefield jobs.

## Explicitly not doing

- A horizontally panning playfield wider than the screen. Shipped shmups use width as repositioning margin, not roaming room; camera motion taxes bullet readability; corpse deadlines would expire offscreen.
- Genericizing v1's corpse, camera, or threat systems to pre-serve the future arena mode. v1 is built scroller-only.
- Pause-menu upgrade choices. Progression is physical and in-run.
- Mouse-cursor aiming, or any second aim axis. Position is the whole skill; aiming grammar belongs to the deferred free-roam arena mode.
- Cross-line homing upgrades. At most one weapon line homes at a time and a homing line is never always-on, so every other weapon line's shots fly dumb and straight.
- A rate-based spawn faucet. It cannot guarantee the drain-outs the phase-boundary boss rule requires, it has no teaching rhythm, and even Vampire Survivors runs authored per-minute wave tables underneath, randomizing only position.

## Open questions

- The central bet: steering into where danger just was (to swallow) versus the shmup instinct to dodge away. Delicious or miserable? The ugly slice answers it with rectangles.
- After a bad hit, does the comeback force (smaller hitbox) or the spiral force (smaller mouth) dominate? Freshness sharpens this: healing off older corpses pays quarter value, so the slice must watch recovery speed on decayed corpses specifically (product gate on #27).
- Slice instruments to build in: time off the bottom edge, belch rate versus full-reservoir time, drop count, inter-drop spacing, and drops swallowed versus scrolled off per run against the ten-to-twelve target (gates on #32: a tuning promise gets a watcher, and a missed-drops number separates "unfair" from "rolled badly and missed three"), whether a playtester can say when they got hit, average freshness at swallow once the bell is leveled (its knockback may push kills up-screen into staler corpses), and whether the four weapon line motions stay tellable at full density. From ticket #29's gates: whether the post-Banshee loaded belch lands on the oversized wave or gets spent on empty sky, whether a tester can tell the Banshee's tear-rings from their own bell rings (trigger: shape-break the tears into teardrops before touching the pattern), and how often the belch fires inside each boss fight, with whether a pattern chunk ever ends before finishing one full emit. The slice milestone carries a named playtester and a date.
