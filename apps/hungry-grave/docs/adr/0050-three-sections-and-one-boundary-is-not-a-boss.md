# Three sections, and one boundary is not a boss

The stage runs in three sections. The Banshee ends the first, a swarm set piece ends the second, and the Undertaker ends the third and the stage. The opening section is the short one, the middle section is the longest, and the last is shorter again, Ikaruga's shape; where each boundary falls on the clock is stage data.

The middle boundary is deliberately not a boss. A set piece can punctuate a stage without a fight: Left 4 Dead's crescendo event is a scripted swarm at a place the level designer chose, and both Vampire Survivors and Halls of Torment schedule a swarm or a champion at a known minute ([../research/stage-length-with-a-director.md](../research/stage-length-with-a-director.md)). Choosing a swarm here rather than a second miniboss keeps the game's promise in its own order: bullet heaven with doses of bullet hell from bosses. A second boss at the middle boundary would spend a second dose at the point in the run where the player should be most fully the storm. The set piece is a heaven moment instead, a big authored swarm the storm shreds, and its corpses are the payout.

Two things it does not do, taken eyes-open. It is not a second feast, because the Wall stays the run's outlier and its reservoir gift is the one that is choreographed. And it is not a second hell dose, so it fires no pattern the player has to dodge as a boss pattern.

The consequences are named. This is the first phase boundary in the game that is not a boss arriving alone, so the north star's boundary wording is a boundary event rather than a boss. It is a set piece, so ADR 0042 binds it: whatever it turns out to be, it names the property it must keep before it is authored, and the property is what the tests hold, never the cast. And it is a fourth authored moment the director does not touch (ADR 0047).

Ruled by Mark 2026-09-07 in the V1 grill, over three sections with two minibosses and over a two-section stage.
