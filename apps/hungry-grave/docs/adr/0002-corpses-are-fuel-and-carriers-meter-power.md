# Corpses are fuel and carriers meter power

Kills leave two kinds of food swallowed the same way: corpses (fuel, common) and drops (permanent power). Always-on weapon lines carry saturation while each swallow erupts as a burst, because strict corpse-per-bullet was killed by arithmetic: it needs about 200 kills per second. Power is metered by carriers: specific authored mobs carry the offer, and killing one drops it where it died, so the stage's own schedule decides how much power a run can hold. Everything goes in the hole: a maxed weapon line's drop or growth past the size ceiling converts to overflow, so nothing swallowed is ever worthless.

Metering by carrier is what takes power out of the director's reach. The director adds mobs and never carriers, so the chain that ran density to kills to drops to power to pressure and back to density is cut on the power side, and directed density can no longer buy a build. Kills keep every other job they had: corpses are fuel, growth and belch charge come from swallowing, score is kills, and killing buys room to live. The carrier schedule is authored so a run that kills every carrier reaches the storm well before the final boss, and where and when carriers arrive is stage data, read off a tape as the time of each drop.

**Superseded in place by Mark's ruling of 2026-09-02.** What it replaced: drops priced in kills on a rising curve, Vampire Survivors' leveling model cited for the rising price alone, tuned to pay ten to twelve drops per run, with the rise as the throttle that stopped a storm killing fifty in a breath from flooding the field. What stood: the two kinds of food and the one verb that takes them, saturation plus per-swallow bursts, the overflow promise, and kills paying for everything except power. What the kill-priced curve could not have known: no director existed when it was written, so nothing could move the price's own input, and a rising curve read as a throttle rather than as the one place a density system could reach in and hand out a build. The precedent under the replacement is the genre's own supply authoring: DoDonPachi's four carriers, Battle Garegga's tanks, and Yuge's account of the practice, "what weapon would be good for a certain part of the game, then make sure there were enough power-ups" ([../research/reward-delivery-models.md](../research/reward-delivery-models.md)).

Named consequence: the kill-price table the old ruling required retires with it, along with the credit-per-kill that fed it. That is not only a table. `killsSinceDrop` and `dropsPaid` are folded state in the witness (`src/game/witness.ts`), so retiring the price table moves the witness version (ADR 0019) as its own boundary under ADR 0043, and the golden digest and the bot test regenerate with the change, the regeneration commit first.

Which weapon line a drop levels is its own decision (Hungry Grave ADR 0034), and a carrier the player never kills is Hungry Grave ADR 0048.
