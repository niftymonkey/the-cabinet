# Corpses are fuel and carriers meter power

Power is metered by carriers: specific authored mobs carry the offer, and killing one drops it where it died, so the stage's own schedule decides how much power a run can hold. The carrier schedule is authored, so a run that kills every carrier reaches the storm well before the final boss, and where and when carriers arrive is stage data, read off a tape as the time of each drop.

Metering by carrier is what takes power out of the director's reach. The director adds mobs and never carriers, so the chain that ran density to kills to drops to power to pressure and back to density is cut on the power side, and directed density can no longer buy a build. Kills keep every other job they had: corpses are fuel, growth and belch charge come from swallowing, a kill pays the score, and killing buys room to live.

The precedent is the genre's own supply authoring: DoDonPachi's four carriers, Battle Garegga's tanks, and Yuge's account of the practice, "what weapon would be good for a certain part of the game, then make sure there were enough power-ups" ([../research/reward-delivery-models.md](../research/reward-delivery-models.md)).

**Absorbed ADR 0048 on 2026-09-17 on Mark's ruling: a missed carrier is missed, and the schedule carries the slack.** A carrier the player never kills pays nothing, and nothing in the game reaches after the player to make up for it. What absorbs a miss instead is the schedule: it holds more carriers than a full build needs, so missing one costs a step rather than the run, and how much surplus it holds is a number in the stage data. Nothing in the game reads the player's power to place a carrier, move an offer or make up a miss. That is the same cut as the one above, which is why the two records are one: a catch-up carrier placed after a strip, Nemesis's recovery wave or DoDonPachi's Max Power on the last life, needs to read how much power the player is holding in order to decide where to put a carrier. An offer that drifts toward the grave, or holds still until it is taken, is rejected for a second reason, Raiden's lingering icon: it stops making the reach for a drop a piece of play. Slack in the supply is the shipped shape at both ends of the genre, Touhou's Embodiment of Scarlet Devil paying roughly two stages of supply against about five hundred kills to max and DoDonPachi's four carriers being the tight end of the same idea, and the cost is taken eyes-open: a sloppy run meets the boss under-built, which is the genre's norm and is already what ADR 0003's floor ladder does on a hit.

Which weapon line a drop levels is its own decision (ADR 0034).
