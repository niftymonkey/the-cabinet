# The bell is a timed pulse of cones

The bell fires on its own clock rather than on each swallow (ruled by Mark 2026-08-19). This supersedes the earlier bell, which fired on every swallow and therefore at whatever rate the player collected, so a late run swallowing roughly once a second cleared the field as a side effect of collecting. On a timer the bell's damage rate is its damage over its period, fixed and tunable, and it gives the player a rhythm to position against. Its damage also falls off with distance from the grave, so the far edge tickles rather than kills (ruled by Mark 2026-08-19): the falloff keeps an always-on line at field scale from becoming the free clear the timer exists to prevent, and the curve itself is tuning data rather than part of this record.

What a toll puts on the field is cones, not a circle. Level one throws one cone forward. Higher levels throw more of them, wrapping around toward the sides as they multiply, so the top of the line earns back the whole surround at field scale and the line's job stays repel: pushing mobs off the grave and out of the space it wants. The cones never become the line that owns the ground ahead of the grave, which is Territory's (ADR 0044); a bell at any level is answering what is already near, and reach is what carries it.

The cost of a cone is knowingly taken at level one: the sides and the rear are open, where the old circle covered everything from the first toll. What pays for it is concentration. The same damage and the same push occupy less of the field, so a toll is felt where it lands instead of tickling everywhere, and the push is the half that has to be felt, because a repel line the player cannot see repelling is not a repel line. Reach grows with level to widen the answer while the cone count is still low. Angles, reach, damage and push per level are tuning rows, never numbers this record carries.

**The circle was superseded in place by Mark's ruling of 2026-08-31, designed out on the same date and confirmed for the build on 2026-09-07.** What it replaced: the ring wording, an always-on expanding circle at every level, restated as recently as the 2026-08-27 pool ruling in [../design/weapon-pool-review.md](../design/weapon-pool-review.md) as "a field-scale ring on a fixed clock". What stood: the timer, the distance falloff, and the not-close-defence rule that same pool ruling wrote down, that the bell is field-scale repel and is never redefined as short-range defence. What those rulings could not have known: the push that makes the line repel had never been on the field to read. `BELL_PUSH_BY_LEVEL` is zero below the top levels and the bell reached level 2 on the only tape they had, and the first reading of the line with push in it came from #79, whose runs read 42, 51 and 0 field units of total pushback and 4, 3 and 2 bell kills against roughly 267 mobs. That is evidence of a line that was never felt, not a ruled number, and the tuning rows are what answer it.

Two consequences are accepted with the decision and recorded so neither is mistaken for a bug. A maxed bell kills at the top of its reach, those corpses scroll the whole way down and arrive nearly empty under Hungry Grave ADR 0004's coupling, and pushback shoves mobs further away before they die, so the strongest bell starves the fuel that made it strong; concentrating the push amplifies that, and the trade is that the mobs pushed back are pushed into the skull stream's fire, so the storm eats what the bell declined. That is the bell's price, the way Vampire Survivors prices its repeating screen clear by having it destroy its own loot, and the freshness-at-swallow instrument is its watcher. And the Banshee's expanding tear-rings no longer echo a player shape, so the boss grammar's ring belongs to her alone.
