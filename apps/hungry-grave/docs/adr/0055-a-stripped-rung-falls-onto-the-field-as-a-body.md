# A stripped rung falls onto the field as a body

When a hit at the floor takes a weapon level, the rung tumbles off the grave as a body and drifts down with the world like everything else on the field. The dive can catch it and the scroll can take it away. Where it lands, how fast it drifts and whether it decays are stage and tuning data.

This is what makes the loss land twice. It announces as a departure rather than as a decrement, which is the field channel the ladder needs for a loss (Hungry Grave ADR 0054), and the way back is the verb the whole game is built on, so recovery is play rather than a rule. The teeth are the scroll deadline and the position the player is in when it happens: at the size floor, under the fire that just took the rung, deciding whether to chase.

The alternatives fail at opposite ends. A rung that is simply gone, Cave Story's hit-stripped experience, gives the loss nothing to look at. A rung that lingers beside the grave before drifting, Salamander's reclaimable Options, makes the loss barely land at all and leaves the floor ladder toothless. Battle Garegga's dropped power-up, which falls into the field and is on the clock, is the shape chosen.

This is new ground and it is noted rather than pushed against: every shipped catch-back in the record recovers from a death, and nothing on record strips power on a hit and then lets the player chase it ([../research/visible-ladder-precedent.md](../research/visible-ladder-precedent.md)). Two costs are taken eyes-open. The floor ladder has fewer teeth against a bold player. And a body at the bottom of the field is something the base harness policy will always walk to, so the harness's take rate on stripped rungs is a number to read rather than a bug to fix (ADR 0053).

Whether a swallowed rung restores its own line's level or a generic one is a craft call made against Salamander's precedent and recorded in [../research/section-feel-consent-and-rung-restore.md](../research/section-feel-consent-and-rung-restore.md), not ruled here.
