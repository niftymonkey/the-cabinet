# The director spends a finite budget per phase

**Amended 2026-09-09, by the dispatching session under the one-push rule, and Mark's to overrule on the branch before merge.** The budget is pinned to the build and never travels in the tape header, which closes this record's own open question and makes #98's format bump one field rather than two. The rule that decides it is ADR 0027's scope: that record governs starting values, "the value the run actually started from", and its worked cases are a size a `?size=` pin resolved to and the levels a `?levels=` pin resolved to. The budget is not a value a run resolves. It is authored stage content, no URL pins it and no seed draws it. Recording it would also be a false promise, because a tape's replay already depends on every authored row in the stage and none of those rows is in the header. Where a retune changes what the run reproduced, the witness refuses the tape, since the fold reads live run state and an open set piece's budget is inside it; where the run never reached the retuned row, the replay is identical and there was nothing to catch. Neither case is helped by the header naming one stage row while a hundred sibling rows stay compiled, which is the rebuild it would be promising and could not deliver (ADR 0019 is the fidelity gate, ADR 0043 says the recorded identity answers interpretation). What stood: the whole ruling, the purse, the cards, the quiet interval, the pressure signal that reads harm and never a kill near the player, and three of the four items left open as design work. What changed: the fourth of those items, header or build, which is answered here rather than left standing. What this record could not have known: that ADR 0053 would ask for the bump to be taken once and that the only other candidate field, the policy that steered a run, would be ready in the same step, so the answer decides whether #98 pays one version or two; and that the budget would still have no column on `Phase` when the question was answered, so the ruling rests on this record's own wording that the budget is authored, and on the shape step 4 will author, rather than on a column anybody has read. **The trigger that reopens it, named rather than left implicit: the day the budget becomes something a run resolves**, drawn from the seed or pinned by URL for a tuning experiment, it is a starting value, ADR 0027 pulls it into the header, and a second bump is taken. That bump still lands before ADR 0057's store starts filling, which is the moment ADR 0043 says a bump is cheapest.

The director is bounded by a purse rather than by a ceiling. Each phase gives it a finite budget, it spends from that budget on mobs drawn as cards over the run's roster (ADR 0046), it spends only while the pressure signal reads low, and after every add it goes quiet for an interval before it may spend again. When the purse is empty the phase runs at its authored floor for whatever is left of it.

Risk of Rain 2's director works this way, spending credits it accrues on cards with costs and weights, and it is the shipped case closest to this game's shape. The alternative, a live-count gate of the kind Left 4 Dead's common limit and Vermintide's threat gating implement, refills the moment the player clears, which is a treadmill unless a quiet interval is stacked on top of it, and the quiet interval is the part that does the work: Darktide ships exactly that as a named wait for the ramp to clear, and the record's own summary is that the fix for a director that will not let up is a gate rather than a smaller number ([../research/director-knobs-and-signal.md](../research/director-knobs-and-signal.md)).

The record's own recommendation for the off-limits moments rides along as a consequence rather than as a second ruling: a per-phase permission row saying whether the director may spend at all, so ADR 0047's four off-limits moments read as cells in the phase's data rather than as conditions in code, which is Darktide's shipped permission matrix ([../research/director-knobs-and-signal.md](../research/director-knobs-and-signal.md)).

The signal the budget is spent against reads harm and floor events, and never a kill near the player. Vermintide 2 adds intensity for every enemy death near a player, weighted by inverse distance and indifferent to who killed it, so a player mowing a horde at contact range raises their own measured intensity as fast as they clear it. Darktide, the same studio's rewrite, deleted the kill term outright: its inputs are five harm events and two damage keys, with no enemy-death input at all. Here a kill up close is not pressure but food, the whole loop of the game, so a near-kill term would read a player doing exactly what the design asks and answer it by holding back.

Two things this closes. A finite purse makes the storm's payoff visible, because a clear rate that outruns a finite fill empties the phase and the player sees the phase won. And the engine's caps go back to being bug detectors rather than tuning knobs: a phase's worst case is its floor plus its budget, which is a number in data, so the mob cap is re-derived above that, and the corpse cap is sized from scroll physics so that it cannot bind in normal play, never evicts food, and raises a fault if it ever binds. That is the code's own rule about caps applied (`caps.ts`), not a new one.

The cost is taken eyes-open: a budget burned early leaves the rest of its phase at the floor.

Left open as design work: whether the budget is counted in credits or in mobs, the bounds of the quiet interval, and the cost and weight of each card. Whether the budget travels in the tape header or is pinned to the build was the fourth item and it is answered in the amendment above: pinned to the build. It was ADR 0043's question, since a new header field is a change of wire layout and ADR 0043 is where header-or-build is decided; ADR 0027 rules only the form a header value takes once it is there, a resolved value and never an absence, and ADR 0019's witness stays the fidelity gate either way.

Ruled by Mark 2026-09-07 in the V1 grill.
