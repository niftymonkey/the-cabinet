# Size is health

There is no health bar: swallowing grows the grave, hits shrink it, and sealed shut is death. One scalar, the half-height, is the size; the grave is a rounded rectangle taller than wide, its width derived at a fixed aspect.

The sim runs in one fixed 540 by 760 unit field that the renderer scales to any screen, and no number anywhere is a device pixel; base speed makes crossing the field's width take about two seconds. A hard size floor keeps the recovery path open, and size never gates a swallow at all: whatever the grave passes under goes in, whatever its size and whatever the grave's (ruled by Mark 2026-08-19). Gating a swallow behind size would put the Banshee's feast out of reach of exactly the player in the spiral it exists to rescue, and overflow already answers a swallow that cannot pay its normal way. A hard size ceiling, the grave standing about a quarter of the field's width tall, converts further growth to score, because there are no zoom tiers to absorb unbounded growth and the Undertaker's gap rule scales with grave width.

At the floor, damage runs a ladder (ruled by Mark 2026-08-19, matching the slice's implementation): hits bleed a capped slice of the score first, then weapon levels down to the birthright loadout, and only when nothing is left to bleed does the next hit seal the grave shut. The floor is never immortality and death is never abrupt. This supersedes entry 1.6's framing of weapon loss as a death penalty; the stripping happens on the way to death, not after it.

**Amended on the score's amount, in place, by Mark's ruling of 2026-09-16.** What stood: the ladder's order, score before weapon levels and weapon levels before the seal; the floor never being immortality; and death never being abrupt. What changed: the amount alone. A hit at the floor bleeds the lesser of the standing score and a flat cap, and the remainder stays, so the first rung costs a bounded number of kills rather than the run. His words are the ruling: "Cap the bleed." What this record could not have known: nothing in the game paid score for a kill when it was written, so a run that never reached the size ceiling carried a score of exactly zero and a whole bleed had nothing to take. Once a kill pays (ADR 0002 as amended the same day, design record [../design/show-what-you-have.md](../design/show-what-you-have.md) R4), slice M1's own batch measured the ladder taking 11,900 to 54,201 points from runs that ended holding 0 to 22,611, which is not a tax on a run but the run itself, and a friends' high score would have been decided first by whether a late hit landed. The cap's shape is the lesser of what is held and a flat amount, on the evidence in [../research/score-loss-on-a-hit-precedent.md](../research/score-loss-on-a-hit-precedent.md): nothing shipped takes a fraction of a banked score at all, every genuine arcade score subtraction is flat, and the lesser-of rule ships in Mario Kart's three coins and Shadow the Hedgehog's ten rings. The amount itself is a tuning row stated as a multiple of what a trash kill pays, never a magnitude this record carries. This amendment is taken by the dispatching session under the one-push rule, and it is Mark's to overrule on the branch before merge.
