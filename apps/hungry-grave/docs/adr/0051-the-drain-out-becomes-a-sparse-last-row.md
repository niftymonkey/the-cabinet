# The drain-out becomes a sparse last row

The spawn silence before a boss is replaced by a slow, sparse last row. Mobs keep arriving, thinly and further apart, and the boss arrives as the last of them leaves the field. What the old drain-out promised still holds: the Banshee and the Undertaker arrive alone on an empty field, and the emptiness is a beat rather than a pause. The row itself, how many, which type, how far apart, is stage data.

The silence was never a designed length. It was measured off the timeline as the time the slowest mob takes to fall the field, written down when the player had almost no weapons, and the game concept already asked for it to be re-derived once the storm kills trash in a second or two. Nothing in the shipped record supports a pause of that size: Guwange's pre-boss breather is described as very brief, and Cave's own shape is the boss arriving on the heels of the last wave. What ships instead, and what the record supports, is Carotenuto's advice to go slower rather than to go empty.

Trash continuing into the fight was rejected, though Cave does it with midbosses, because it breaks the alternation the game is built on: the field drains and a boss arrives alone, heaven then a dose of hell, one texture at a time.

Two consequences. The sparse row is the authored valley the director relaxes into before each boss, so the off-limits moment shrinks from a silence to that row plus the fall of its last mob (ADR 0047). And there is no drain-out before the set piece, which is a swarm and arrives into trash the way the Wall arrives seconds after the Banshee dies; only the two boss boundaries need the field empty. The cost is taken eyes-open: the breath before a fight is short.
