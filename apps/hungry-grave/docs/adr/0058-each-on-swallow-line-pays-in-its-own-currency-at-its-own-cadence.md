# Each on-swallow line pays in its own currency, at its own cadence

**Amended 2026-09-09, by the dispatching session under the one-push rule, and Mark's to overrule on the branch before merge.** Every line that pays on a swallow also owns a cadence floor, the fewest ticks between two of its own payments whatever the swallow rate, and the two lines floor in opposite shapes. The wisps floor the interval between volleys, and a swallow inside the interval pays nothing later rather than banking a volley, because a banked volley would pay a stale corpse's freshness on a fresh corpse's tick. The skull stream extends instead: one running surge runs longer toward a cap rather than a second starting beside it, because the run's own rule is already that a swallow chain overwrites an unspent surge rather than banking a queue, and extending is what overwriting becomes once a chain is the normal case rather than the corner. What stood: the whole currency ruling, ADR 0004's freshness curve and the floor it falls to, the wisps paying in souls floored at one soul, the skull stream paying in volleys and never in columns, and a rotten corpse never making a top-level stream look like a low-level one. What it replaced: the rule this record left implicit by carrying no cadence limit of any kind, that a swallow pays its line's burst whenever it happens, however fast the swallows come. What this record could not have known: a swallow was a rare event on a thin field when it was written, and the mow makes swallows continuous (ADR 0059), at which the auto-targeting lines do the mowing and the player does not, which is the opposite of what the mow exists for; each line's own cap would bind under that rate, and a bound cap raises a fault rather than throttling (ADR 0056). The intervals and the cap themselves are tuning rows, exactly as the currencies are.

A rotten corpse buys a smaller burst, on ADR 0004's freshness curve and down to the same floor that growth and reservoir charge use, and each line that pays on a swallow scales the thing it actually pays in. The wisps pay in souls, so freshness scales the count, floored at one soul, because a bare proportional count pays nothing at level one where the flight is a single wisp and a swallow that fires nothing reads as a bug. The skull stream pays as a surge, a run of extra volleys at a shortened interval, and freshness scales how many volleys the surge pays rather than how wide it fires, because the column count is exactly what draws the line's five levels and a rotten corpse must never make a level-five stream look like a level-two one.

ADR 0004 already ruled that freshness scales every payout; what this record adds is the axis per line, without which the rule cannot be enforced or tested. A test that fails if a burst is ever paid without freshness applied is the point of naming the axis.

Ruled by Mark 2026-08-25 as the starting point, with the tuning left to the tuning pass.
