# Homing is capped at one

Homing is capped at one weapon line at a time, and a homing line is never always-on. The cap keeps the protected property, that homing must not solve the field, while making homing a slot the player fills and a real loadout decision. The never-always-on half carries the other side: homing has to be bought with an act the player performs, because an always-on homing line is a turret that finds targets while the player does something else, and positioning is this game's one skill.

This supersedes the earlier rule that homing was the wisps' identity alone. What stood: the property being protected. What it replaced: protection by naming a specific line, which forbade anyone from ever designing a second homing thing, and which missed that the wisps were safe because homing was earned by diving into where danger just was, not because of their name.

The cap is vacuous while the wisps are the only homing line; the trigger to revisit is a second homing line entering the pool, at which point the dice need a stated rule for offering one to a player who already owns the other.
