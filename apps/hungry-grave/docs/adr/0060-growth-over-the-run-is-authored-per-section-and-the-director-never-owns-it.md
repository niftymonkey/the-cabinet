# Growth over the run is authored per section, and the director never owns it

**Re-ruled 2026-09-09, by the dispatching session under the one-push rule, on the survey of shipped stage tables in [../research/naming-the-authored-growth-rate.md](../research/naming-the-authored-growth-rate.md), and Mark's to overrule on the branch before merge.** Growth is authored as stepped values and never as a ramp between two figures, and a repeating row is an ordinary row with its repeat fields set rather than a second kind of row. What stood: growth authored per section and keyed to the clock, the answer keyed to pressure and owned by the director (ADR 0047), the rows staying the floor, the repeating interval that shrinks to a minimum, and the Vigil declaring none. What it replaced: a rate that lerps between a start figure and an end figure across the section's authored span, and the standing row as a second kind of row beside the one-shot rows. What this record could not have known: the survey of shipped stage tables did not exist when it was filed, so it could not see that four survivors-likes with visible structure author discrete stepped figures, that Vampire Survivors' own per-minute `frequency` column is deliberately non-monotonic, or that no shipped format found makes the continuous case a separate kind of entry.

Growth is keyed to time and authored; the answer to how a run is going is keyed to pressure and belongs to the director (ADR 0047). Those are two mechanisms and this record rules the split, so a player who is not being hurt meets a floor that has been rising all run plus whatever the phase can still buy over it, and the game answers their power without ever reading it.

The authored half is stepped, never a ramp. A section's growth is a run of standing rows, each holding a fixed rate for as long as it stands, one following the next, and a dip is authored where the section wants one rather than being an accident of a curve. That is what the genre ships: Vampire Survivors' Mad Forest holds one figure per minute in a `frequency` column that is milliseconds between spawn ticks, so a larger figure is a thinner minute, and it reads 1000, 1000, 500, 250, 500, 1000, 500, 500, 1500, 500, 500, 100 across its first twelve minutes, which is stepped and deliberately not monotonic, its thinnest minutes falling at 5 and 8. 20 Minutes Till Dawn, HoloCure and Halls of Torment each hold a constant figure for as long as one authored entry stands too. A lerp between two figures exists in shipped work, in GTFO's population ramp, and no survivors-like points one at its spawn schedule.

It is one construct and one list, not a second kind of row and never a second timeline. A row gains repeat fields: an interval it fires again on, a step that shrinks that interval, and the minimum the interval shrinks to, which is Brotato's `repeating`, `repeating_interval`, `reduce_repeating_interval` and `min_repeating_interval` on the same `WaveGroupData` that carries its one-shot groups. A row with those fields unset fires once, exactly as every row does today. No shipped format found does this any other way: Vampire Survivors puts the continuous `frequency` and the one-shot `bosses` and `events` on one per-minute object, and Taisei writes both as the same task with a count in one and a duration in the other. The authored stage stays one list read in one place, and what widens is what a row may be.

The glossary word for a row whose repeat fields are set is **standing row**, and it is a naming call settled by research rather than by preference. Every genre noun for this thing is spent or points both ways: "wave" names the continuous schedule in Vampire Survivors and the one-shot group in the shmup lineage this game is half descended from, and it is banned four times over in the glossary; "population" names a level a system maintains rather than an entry a designer wrote; "trickle" is the one uncollided genre word and it is wrong on magnitude; "floor stream" collides with the seeded stream names and with the skull stream weapon line. "Standing" is the ordinary English qualifier for a thing maintained rather than fired, and the noun stays "row" because the timeline's own unit is what this is.

That is why this is a record beside ADR 0047 rather than an amendment to it. ADR 0047 rules that the authored rows are the floor the director adds over, and that ruling stays true here word for word; what it never ruled is that the floor itself may grow over the run.

The rate holds at zero until the section's teaching rows have fired and steps up from behind them. A repeating row running from a section's first second would bury the run's first swallow and its first mob fire under arrivals, and ADR 0016 asks that a type be readable before it acts.

A standing row names the phase-local time it starts at, exactly as every other row does, and it ends at the next row of any kind rather than at the end of its phase. So a section that ends on `rowsSpentAndFieldClear` closes its own list with a standing row authored at zero, at the time of its sparse last row, and the field is free to read clear so the boss arrives on that event instead of into arrivals a rate is still producing (ADR 0051). Nothing measures a section's length, because a phase ends on an event and never on a clock (ADR 0049), so there is no length to measure against and no span to lerp across.

A section may declare no standing row, and the Vigil declares none, because it owns scarcity and a standing row there would buy exactly the growth that property forbids.

The two shipped cases decide the growth-versus-answer split rather than a preference. Risk of Rain 2 raises a difficulty coefficient on the clock and runs a credit-spending director on top of it, so growth and answer are separate mechanisms in the game closest to this one's shape. Halls of Torment moved its growth off kills and onto a clock, and the reason generalises: growth keyed to what the player did hands a strong build a harder game and a weak one an easier one, which is the loop ADR 0047 already refused when it ruled that the signal reads the pressure the player is under and not the power the player holds. Leaving growth to the director instead would put both jobs on the pressure signal, and a signal doing two jobs cannot be tuned for either.

A reader who remembers the caution against a rate-based spawn faucet should know it retired with ADR 0006 and that the bound is what keeps it retired. Here the bound is that the standing rows are authored per section, step on the clock alone, read nothing about the player, and add to a stage whose beats are still placed by hand.

What a rung buys is not this record's either: the per-rung climb of a line is that line's own data, set against shipped precedent, and ADR 0005 already rules that a line owns how its five levels grow.

Rates, intervals, troughs, weapon growth per rung and which type each section stands are stage data and are not this record's. They live in [../design/mow-ladder-director.md](../design/mow-ladder-director.md).

Decided by the dispatching session 2026-09-09 under the one-push rule, on Mark's mow ruling of the same date, and his to overrule on the branch before merge. The game design gate accepted the growth-versus-pressure split on the two shipped cases above.
