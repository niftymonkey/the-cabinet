# Growth over the run is authored per section, and the director never owns it

Growth is keyed to time and authored; the answer to how a run is going is keyed to pressure and belongs to the director (ADR 0047). Those are two mechanisms and this record rules the split, so a player who is not being hurt meets a floor that has been rising all run plus whatever the phase can still buy over it, and the game answers their power without ever reading it.

The authored half is a standing row: a row that stands for its section rather than firing once. It names a mob type, a template and a rate of bodies a second that ramps between two figures across the section's authored span, repeating down to a minimum interval. It is stateless within its section, so what it puts on the field is a function of the clock and of nothing the player did, which is what makes it growth rather than an answer. The shaped one-shot rows keep their times on top of it, as beats. A section may declare no standing row, and the Vigil declares none, because it owns scarcity and a standing row there would buy exactly the growth that property forbids.

The two shipped cases decide the split rather than a preference. Risk of Rain 2 raises a difficulty coefficient on the clock and runs a credit-spending director on top of it, so growth and answer are separate mechanisms in the game closest to this one's shape. Halls of Torment moved its growth off kills and onto a clock, and the reason generalises: growth keyed to what the player did hands a strong build a harder game and a weak one an easier one, which is the loop ADR 0047 already refused when it ruled that the signal reads the pressure the player is under and not the power the player holds. Leaving growth to the director instead would put both jobs on the pressure signal, and a signal doing two jobs cannot be tuned for either.

The ramp is measured against the section's authored span, the time of its last authored row, and never against a section length, because a phase ends on an event and never on a clock (ADR 0049), so there is no length to ramp against.

The rate holds at zero until the section's teaching rows have fired and ramps up from behind them. A standing row running from a section's first second would bury the run's first swallow and its first mob fire under arrivals, and ADR 0016 asks that a type be readable before it acts.

It is a second kind of row and never a second timeline. The authored stage stays one list read in one place, and what widens is what a row may be. That is why this is a record beside ADR 0047 rather than an amendment to it: 0047 rules that the authored rows are the floor the director adds over, and it never ruled that the floor itself may grow over the run.

A reader who remembers the caution against a rate-based spawn faucet should know it retired with ADR 0006 and that the bound is what keeps it retired. Here the bound is that the rate is authored per section, ramps on the clock alone, reads nothing about the player, and adds to a stage whose beats are still placed by hand.

Rates, ramps, troughs and which type each section stands are stage data and are not this record's.

Decided by the dispatching session 2026-09-09 under the one-push rule, on Mark's mow ruling of the same date, and his to overrule on the branch before merge. The game design gate accepted the growth-versus-pressure split on the two shipped cases above.
