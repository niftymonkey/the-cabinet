# The Undertaker's length is bought in chunks

The stage's final boss runs about a minute and a half to two minutes, and it gets there across three chunks rather than across a bigger health bar. The third chunk is a new authored pattern inside the Undertaker's own grammar of falling curtains and slow spirals, so the grammar stays exclusive to him. Health per chunk is tuning data, and what reads the fight is the harness's time-to-kill by style.

The rule under the number is that a boss past a minute needs progression. Cave's stage-ending bosses sit at roughly a minute to a minute and a half with three chunks, and the genre's own account of a longer fight is that it has to keep changing or it becomes a health bar ([../research/shmup-stage-design.md](../research/shmup-stage-design.md)). A minute and two chunks was written for a five-minute stage and is now too small an ending for it. Three minutes and more, the Touhou or Ikaruga finale, was rejected for two reasons that are the same reason twice: the hell stops being a dose, and the boss is the one stretch of the stage the director never paces, so its length is the length of the game's longest unpaced moment.

The cost is one more pattern to author, and one thing to watch: the belch has to be re-earnable at the fight's own cadence across the longer fight, which the boss-fight belch instrument is already pointed at.
