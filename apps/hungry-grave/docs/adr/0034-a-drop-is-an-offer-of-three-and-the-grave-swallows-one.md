# A drop is an offer of three and the grave swallows one

A drop no longer levels a line the dice picked: it spawns an offer, three option bodies falling side by side and apart from each other, and the grave gets exactly the one it passes under, the other two vanishing. The bodies hold their places relative to each other and drift with the field like any other food, so the choice is a place to be rather than a moment to hit. The offer is random and the take is chosen: options draw from the lines still unowned plus level-ups of owned, un-maxed lines, and a maxed line is never offered. The offer shrinks below three as lines max, and when nothing is offerable a paid drop converts to overflow, keeping ADR 0002's nothing-swallowed-is-worthless promise. The first offer of a run is fixed in shape, the skull stream and two unowned lines, so deepening the main gun and opening something new are both on the table from the first swallow.

Exactly one offer is live at a time. A second drop paid while an offer stands banks toward the next one, and the bank shows on the live offer so a burst of paying kills still reads as paid. Several offers at once was rejected as the chained menu this game has kept out since Vampire Survivors, whose menus are the influence's one unfun part; one bigger offer per burst has no precedent. Gradius's stored capsules and Brotato's bag are the shipped shape of a reward that waits its turn. How often a burst crosses more than one payment is unknowable until density is directed, so the bank is written as the rule for that corner and never tuned for, and the playing harness (Hungry Grave ADR 0053) exercises the crossing path.

The evidence is the 2026-08-31 tape reading: the belch landed 35% and 46% of all kills in the baseline runs and fell to 13% in the one run whose drops built the storm early, so the game already contains the curve that removes the belch lean, and under a uniform roll reaching it was seed luck. Choice makes the good curve a strategy. Vampire Survivors' level-up choice is the precedent, made diegetic.

**The spin was superseded in place by Mark's ruling of 2026-09-01.** What it replaced: three options spinning around each other, the grave taking whichever one it touched. What stood: the offer of three itself, random contents with a chosen take, the take as a movement input already on the tape so replay still runs from the seed plus inputs alone, and the economy under it. What the spinning ruling could not have known: the delivery-model research had not been done, so the failure mode had no name. It has one now, Iuchi's, that an item taken under fire is mis-taken, which is exactly what a rotating target makes of a choice, and Machiguchi's argument for a choice over a take-or-skip is what survives of the offer ([../research/reward-delivery-models.md](../research/reward-delivery-models.md)). One drop cycling its contents on a timer (Twin Cobra, Raiden) is Iuchi's failure stated plainly, and choice deferred to beats alone (Brotato, Nuclear Throne, ZeroRanger) pays a stage two choices.

An earlier ruling this record already carried: the seed-then-uniform roll of 2026-08-22 was itself a same-day refinement of full seeding, and what it could not have known is that no choice mechanic existed, so line access could only be priced in miss percentages under luck and a run's build was dice rather than play. With maxed lines never offered, ADR 0002's overflow branch for a maxed line's drop goes dormant; overflow itself stands for growth past the ceiling.

A choice-sphere wildcard, one offer slot that pauses the run and offers everything currently offerable, is recorded as a later candidate and deliberately not ruled here: it strictly dominates the ordinary slots, and its menu pick needs either a tape format bump or a frozen-world sim mode, so it waits until the offer itself has tape (gate reviews, 2026-08-31).

This ruling changes sim behavior, so the golden digest and bot test regenerate with it (regeneration commit first, ADR 0019).
