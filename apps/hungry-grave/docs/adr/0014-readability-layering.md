# Readability layering

Readability is a day-one rule, not a polish pass. The field draws as one fixed stack, top to bottom: mob fire, the grave's rim, treasure, mob bodies, corpses, the storm, the bell's ring, the belch's eruption, the grave's mouth, the ground. Every player effect sits in one band beneath the food, the storm highest of the three because it is the densest and most informative, and the bell's ring lands opposite the Banshee's tear-rings, which are mob fire: one shape with two owners, separated by layer as well as by palette. Mob fire owns a reserved value band that nothing else on the field may enter, background and mob bodies and food and player effects alike, so the rule is checkable in grayscale rather than by hue (value contrast is the channel that survives colour vision deficiency, which is why WCAG's own contrast formula carries no hue term; the Ikaruga polarity citation this once carried was fan analysis rather than any Treasure statement, and was retired 2026-08-20). No player effect may occlude mob fire, the belch's eruption included, because Hungry Grave ADR 0008 resumes the boss pattern immediately and grants no invulnerability, where Cave bombs and Crimzon Clover's Break both hold invulnerability for the whole blast.

The discriminator between the storm and mob fire is size and shape before brightness: the storm is small, regular and self-similar, mob fire is large, slow and irregular (Cave's grammar, and the reason log entry 4.3 chose the Banshee's rings). Dimming the storm per sprite is a dont-build: additive stacking makes the densest storm the brightest region of the screen, which is where the grave is, and a storm turned down until it is safe stops reading as the reward for swallowing. The requirement is the property, that mob fire wins wherever it overlaps at any density the game can produce, verified at the density the tuned field actually measures, recorded with the tuning revision that produced it and taken as the peak across the autopilot's runs and a human's. The 300-projectile figure this once named was an assumption introduced to run an arithmetic check, never an observation, so the bar is produced by the build rather than asserted ahead of it (raised by Mark 2026-08-19). Additive blending on the storm is a dont-build for the same reason, and it has to be forbidden rather than measured, because neither instrument can see compositing: the palette check reads declared colours and the grayscale screenshot reads one instant. A storm-opacity player setting is deferred (trigger: a tester asks, or the density check fails on a real screen); Halls of Torment ships one.

The band is pinned as numbers so a unit test can hold it, and the numbers are these. Value is measured as Rec.709 luma on gamma-encoded sRGB scaled 0 to 100, which is exactly what `filter: grayscale(1)` produces, so the declared-colour test and the grayscale screenshot cannot disagree about what they are looking at; relative luminance and CIE L* each give a different band and a different list of violators, so neither is permitted here. The band sits at the top of the range rather than the middle: a mob-fire core colour sits at luma 88 or above, every other declared colour sits at 68 or below, and the gap between the two stays at least 20. Mob fire is therefore three colours per sprite, a near-white core carrying the band, a saturated body below the ceiling carrying the hue and the size-and-shape grammar above, and a near-black outline so the sprite holds light against dark internally and still reads on a background the palette never planned for. The guarantee runs one way only: anything at or above the floor is mob fire, while mob fire itself is not required to lie entirely inside the band. The stricter reading, every mob-fire pixel inside the band, was priced and rejected, because reaching luma 74 forces the fire colours from saturation 0.76 down to 0.35 and turns them pale salmon, discarding the red-means-danger hue signal that this ADR's own Cave grammar leans on, and it drops the food into a mid-grey where corpses and treasure stop differing in brightness at all. Mob fire draws at alpha 1.0 with no blend mode, forbidden rather than measured for the same reason additive blending on the storm is, since a core at luma 90 drawn at alpha 0.90 over the night ground composites to 81.7 and falls out of the band. The 20-point margin is the thinnest number here and is stated as a floor rather than a derived figure: no standard covers telling two foreground objects apart, and its only empirical anchor is one shipped game (settled 2026-08-20; the sources and the arithmetic are in `apps/hungry-grave/docs/research/readability-value-band.md`).

The three food-and-threat classes stay tellable by silhouette first, brightness second: corpses hold constant size and fade, treasure stays steady-bright and never decays, mob fire carries a core in its reserved band. The grave is the health bar, since Hungry Grave ADR 0003 has no bar, so its rim reads above the food layer even under a pile while the mouth's interior stays beneath it and food still visibly falls in; a hit announces on at least two channels, one of them not the shrink. Verification is a grayscale screenshot from Hungry Grave ADR 0013's autopilot at target density, with the feel call staying the human's.
