# Readability layering

Readability is a day-one rule, not a polish pass. The field draws as one fixed stack with mob fire on top, every player effect in one band beneath the food, the storm highest of the three because it is the densest and most informative, and the bell's ring layered opposite the Banshee's tear-rings, one shape with two owners, separated by layer as well as by palette. No player effect may occlude mob fire, the belch's eruption included, because Hungry Grave ADR 0008 resumes the boss pattern immediately and grants no invulnerability, where Cave bombs and Crimzon Clover's Break both hold invulnerability for the whole blast. The full stack is pinned by the layering test.

Mob fire owns a reserved value band that nothing else on the field may enter, checkable in grayscale rather than by hue, because value contrast is the channel that survives colour vision deficiency, and WCAG's own contrast formula carries no hue term (the Ikaruga polarity citation this once carried was fan analysis, retired 2026-08-20). The guarantee runs one way only: anything at or above the floor is mob fire, while mob fire itself is not required to lie entirely inside the band, because the stricter reading was priced and rejected, discarding the red-means-danger hue signal the grammar below leans on. Value is measured as Rec.709 luma on gamma-encoded sRGB, exactly what `filter: grayscale(1)` produces, so the declared-colour test and the grayscale screenshot cannot disagree about what they are looking at; relative luminance and CIE L* each give a different band and a different list of violators, and neither is permitted. The band's numbers are pinned by the palette test and derived in [../research/readability-value-band.md](../research/readability-value-band.md), not here.

The storm and mob fire separate by size and shape before brightness: the storm small, regular and self-similar, mob fire large, slow and irregular (Cave's grammar). The three food-and-threat classes stay tellable by silhouette first, brightness second: corpses hold constant size and fade, treasure stays steady-bright and never decays, mob fire carries its core. Two dont-builds guard the grammar: dimming the storm per sprite, because additive stacking makes the densest storm the brightest region of the screen, which is where the grave is, and a storm turned down until it is safe stops reading as the reward for swallowing; and any blend mode on the storm or on mob fire, forbidden rather than measured, because neither instrument can see compositing. The mob-fire property is verified at the density the tuned field actually measures, never at an asserted figure. A storm-opacity player setting is deferred with a trigger: a tester asks, or the density check fails on a real screen.

The band's ceiling binds every colour drawn while the field is live, the readouts on top of it included; colours that only ever draw when the field is not live are declared apart and exempt, and the exemption is held shut by the source scan. Verification is a grayscale screenshot from Hungry Grave ADR 0013's autopilot at target density, with the feel call staying the human's.

The field boundary's grading is Hungry Grave ADR 0039 and the hit announcement is ADR 0040, both extracted from this file on 2026-08-26. Decision log entries 1.8, 2.3, 2.4, 4.3, and 4.5.
