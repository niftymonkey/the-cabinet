# Weapon lines are a pool, and homing is capped at one

A weapon line owns its own properties rather than belonging to a category: how it fires (always-on, on each swallow, or on a timer), whether the run starts with it, and how its five levels grow. The earlier split into two floor lines and two burst lines is removed (ruled by Mark 2026-08-19). It was a club with fixed membership, which made a fifth line surgery instead of data, and the timed bell below is a line it could not describe at all. Always-on survives as a property a line can have; floor lines and burst lines as categories do not. Vampire Survivors is the precedent for the shape: each weapon carries its own profile, including which modifiers it ignores, which is how that game ships new weapons without rebalancing everything.

The pool is open by design. The intent is a small always-on core plus a growing set of lines drawn by the dice, with player choice over the draw as a later possibility. v1 commits to four lines at five levels each: the soul stream (always-on, straight fanned columns, the saturation workhorse), the headstones (always-on, counter-rotating orbits, last-ditch close defense), the wisps (on swallow, the run's homing, each swallowed corpse's soul tearing loose to hunt), and the bell (timed pulses, below). More than four in the tracer build is a stretch, never a commitment. Three rules govern anything added later, and they are generative rather than restrictive: a new line claims a motion no line owns yet, because the motions must never blur; nothing it puts on screen enters the value band Hungry Grave ADR 0014 reserves for mob fire; and it obeys the homing cap.

Homing is capped at one line at a time, and a homing line is never always-on. This supersedes the earlier rule that homing was the wisps' identity alone, which protected positioning by naming a specific line and therefore forbade anyone from ever designing a second homing thing (ruled by Mark 2026-08-19). The cap keeps the property the old rule was really protecting, that homing must not solve the field, while making homing a slot the player fills and a real build decision. The never-always-on clause carries the other half: the wisps were safe because homing had to be earned by diving into where danger just was, not because of their name, and an always-on homing line is a turret that finds targets while the player does something else. Positioning is this game's one skill and every homing line still has to be bought with an act the player performs.

The bell is a timed pulse rather than a per-swallow burst (ruled by Mark 2026-08-19): always-on, an expanding damage ring on its own clock, its radius growing from a short reach at level 1 to nearly the full field at level 5, with pushback arriving as a later-level property rather than from the first level. This supersedes the earlier bell, which fired on every swallow and therefore fired at whatever rate the player collected, so that a late run swallowing roughly once a second cleared the field as a side effect of collecting, the same defect the wisps' ordnance arithmetic exists to prevent. On a timer the bell's damage rate is its damage over its period, fixed and tunable, and it gives the player a rhythm to position against. Its damage falls off with distance from the grave, so the far edge of a big ring tickles rather than kills (ruled by Mark 2026-08-19), which keeps the toll's power concentrated where the player is standing and leaves the whole curve as a tuning knob. One consequence is deliberate and is recorded here so it is never mistaken for a bug: a maxed bell kills at the top of the field, those corpses scroll the whole way down and arrive nearly empty under Hungry Grave ADR 0004's coupling invariant, and higher-level pushback shoves mobs further up before they die, so the strongest bell starves the fuel that made it strong. That is the bell's price, the way Vampire Survivors prices its repeating screen clear by having it destroy its own loot, and the freshness-at-swallow instrument is its watcher. Bosses take its damage but never its push (Hungry Grave ADR 0007). The consequence for the chime is that the two decouple: the swallow chime stays the baseline sound and juice of every swallow, firing from the very first one regardless of loadout so RNG drops can never leave the early minutes silent, and it is no longer the thing the bell line upgrades.

The homing cap is vacuous while the wisps are the only homing line, and the trigger to revisit it is a second homing line entering the pool, at which point the dice need a stated rule for offering one to a player who already owns the other.

Live mobs are never food: contact shrinks the grave and only kills leave corpses, so a big grave can never pass under a wave for free. Grasping hands were cut because their jobs were spoken for, and mouse aiming was declined because position is the whole skill.
