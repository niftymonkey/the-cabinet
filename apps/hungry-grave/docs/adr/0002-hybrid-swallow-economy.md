# Hybrid swallow economy

Kills leave two kinds of food swallowed the same way: corpses (fuel, common) and drops (permanent power). Always-on weapon lines carry saturation while each swallow erupts as a burst, because strict corpse-per-bullet was killed by arithmetic: it needs about 200 kills per second.

Drops are priced in kills on a rising curve (Vampire Survivors' leveling model, cited for the rising price alone), tuned to pay out ten to twelve drops per run, and the dice only pick which weapon line levels; a flat every-Nth-kill cadence floods once the storm kills fifty in a breath. Everything goes in the hole: a maxed weapon line's drop or growth past the size ceiling converts to overflow, so nothing swallowed is ever worthless.

How the dice pick was left open here and has now been ruled twice, and the second ruling is the one in force.

The first ruling (Mark, 2026-08-22, in the weapon-lines dispatch) seeded before it rolled: while any line was still unowned the roll picked only among the unowned ones, and once every line was owned it was uniform over all four. Its evidence was simulation. Over 200,000 simulated runs at eleven drops, runs missing a line entirely are 8.4% under a uniform roll, 1.25% under a roll weighted toward what the player does not own and 0.000% under seeding; the chance any line reaches level five is 0.725, 0.440 and 0.626; and the modal build's share of runs is 0.17, 0.33 and 0.23. The precedent ran the same way and none of it argues for anti-concentration weighting: Vampire Survivors weights toward what you own and requires a maxed item to evolve it, Slay the Spire has no ownership bias at all, Risk of Rain 2 has none and stacking is the build engine, and Hades concentrates deliberately by showing no new gods after four.

Play overruled it the same day (Mark, 2026-08-22, after playing). Seeding on every unowned line sent the first three drops of a run to the three lines the run does not start with, so no line ever gained depth: the soul stream could not reach level two until every other line was open, and the player felt no weapon get stronger across the minutes that matter most. The rule in force is that the run's first drop seeds among the lines still at level zero and every drop after it is uniform over all four. One seeded drop still opens a line the birthright does not carry, and everything after it can go deep. The price is the very defect the seeding existed to remove, paid deliberately and only in part: with one seeded drop and ten uniform ones, a run misses a line when the other initially unopened line is never drawn again, at 0.75 to the tenth or 5.6%, against the uniform roll's 8.4% and full seeding's 0.000%. The rule is still a pure function of the levels and the drop's own ordinal, so it stays testable without running a stage, and a maxed line stays reachable so the overflow rule above stays live.
