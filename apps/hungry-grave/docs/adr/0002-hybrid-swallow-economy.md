# Hybrid swallow economy

Kills leave two kinds of food swallowed the same way: corpses (fuel, common) and drops (permanent power). Always-on weapon lines carry saturation while each swallow erupts as a burst, because strict corpse-per-bullet was killed by arithmetic: it needs about 200 kills per second.

Drops are priced in kills on a rising curve (Vampire Survivors' leveling model, cited for the rising price alone), tuned to pay out ten to twelve drops per run, and the dice only pick which weapon line levels; a flat every-Nth-kill cadence floods once the storm kills fifty in a breath. Everything goes in the hole: a maxed weapon line's drop or growth past the size ceiling converts to overflow, so nothing swallowed is ever worthless.

How the dice pick was left open here and is now ruled (by Mark 2026-08-22, in the weapon-lines dispatch). They seed before they roll: while any line is still unowned the roll picks only among the unowned ones, and once every line is owned it is uniform over all four. The alternative considered was a roll weighted toward what the player does not own, and seeding beats it on every measure at once. Over 200,000 simulated runs at eleven drops, runs missing a line entirely are 8.4% under a uniform roll, 1.25% under the weighting and 0.000% under seeding; the chance any line reaches level five is 0.725, 0.440 and 0.626; and the modal build's share of runs is 0.17, 0.33 and 0.23. So seeding removes the missing-line defect outright while costing less of the ceiling and less of the run-to-run variety than a weighting does. The precedent runs the same way and none of it argues for anti-concentration weighting: Vampire Survivors weights toward what you own and requires a maxed item to evolve it, Slay the Spire has no ownership bias at all, Risk of Rain 2 has none and stacking is the build engine, and Hades concentrates deliberately by showing no new gods after four. The rule is a pure function of the levels, so it is testable without running a stage, and a maxed line stays reachable so the overflow rule above stays live.
