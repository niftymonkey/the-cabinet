# A missed carrier is missed, and the schedule carries the slack

A carrier the player never kills pays nothing, and nothing in the game reaches after the player to make up for it. What absorbs a miss instead is the schedule: it holds more carriers than a full build needs, so missing one costs a step rather than the run, and how much surplus it holds is a number in the stage data.

Slack in the supply is the shipped shape at both ends of the genre. Touhou's Embodiment of Scarlet Devil pays far more power over a cycle than a maxed shot needs, roughly two stages of supply against about five hundred kills to max, and DoDonPachi's four carriers are the tight end of the same idea ([../research/reward-delivery-models.md](../research/reward-delivery-models.md)). The cost is taken eyes-open: a sloppy run meets the boss under-built, which is the genre's norm and is already what ADR 0003's floor ladder does on a hit.

The rejected alternatives are rejected for one shared reason. An offer that drifts toward the grave, or holds still until it is taken, is Raiden's lingering icon, and it stops making the reach for a drop a piece of play. A catch-up carrier placed after a strip, Nemesis's recovery wave or DoDonPachi's Max Power on the last life, needs something that reads how much power the player is holding in order to decide where to put a carrier, which is a small version of the loop Hungry Grave ADR 0002 cut on purpose.
