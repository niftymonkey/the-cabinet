# The tape format

A tape carries a format version integer and three separable sections, from the first tape ever written. The body holds the run's dice, its resolved starting size and the exact steering the simulation consumed, tick by tick. The second section holds the witness, which Hungry Grave ADR 0019 defines. The third holds observations the witness cannot cover. All three land at once because a format that has to be versioned later invalidates every tape recorded before it, and the tapes recorded this month have to still be readable next month.

**The open pool has a counterweight, and it is the standing constraint on every decision in this ADR** (stated by Mark 2026-08-23). Keep the instrumentation needed to produce trustworthy, repeatable evidence for tuning the game by feel. Do not cut something merely because it is instrumentation, and do not expand the instrument beyond what is needed to help Hungry Grave play better. Between two designs, prefer the smallest that preserves trustworthy measurement and gets to usable feedback. The pool below says a kind of evidence nobody has named yet is welcome; this says the test for admitting it is whether it helps the game play better, and the two are the same rule read from either end. A technically complete instrument that still cannot say anything about how the game plays has missed the reason the work is being done.

**The instrument's purposes are an open pool, by the same design idiom as weapon lines and mob types (Hungry Grave ADRs 0005 and 0016).** Two are in hand. Gameplay and tuning evidence is one: mobs and density, waves and phases, weapon lines and upgrades, damage contribution, reservoir behaviour, range and coverage, kills, deaths and drops. Performance evidence is the other, and it is first-class rather than a sidecar: which gameplay situations create frame debt, at what mob and projectile and effect counts a phone starts to struggle, whether a bad frame is dominated by simulation, invariant checking, synchronisation or rendering, whether an optimisation measurably improved representative runs, and how all of that differs between a desktop and a phone. Naming two would turn a partial list into a definition and a third kind of evidence would then read as out of scope, which is exactly the failure the pool idiom exists to prevent. The architectural test is therefore not "does the format serve these two" but "does it admit a kind of evidence nobody has named yet" (ruled by Mark 2026-08-23, correcting an earlier framing of this as two axes).

**That test has a precise answer, and the answer is the whole design. Deterministic and recomputable evidence may be derived later from replay; runtime and measured evidence must be captured during the original run, because replay cannot recreate it** (ruled by Mark 2026-08-23). Anything recomputable from deterministic state is open for free, forever: a tape replays exactly, so replay rebuilds the authoritative `RunState` at every tick and a question invented next year gets asked of a tape recorded today, with nothing anticipated and no shadow simulation. Anything measured outside the simulation exists only if it was written down at record time. Wall clock, renderer, device, and whatever arrives later are all in that class, none of it replays, and what was not captured is lost from that run permanently. So the format needs a general place to put non-replayable observations rather than a special case built for timings. That is what the third section is, and timings are simply its first inhabitants. Runtime errors and warnings, whether audio clips dropped, and a run's pauses and tab-switches, which `takeElapsed` swallows on the way back from a backgrounded tab and which are invisible in a body holding only executed ticks, are offered to show the shape of the class rather than to extend a list.

**Damage is attributed in both directions, and that is a rule about the record rather than a detail of an event type** (ruled by Mark 2026-08-23). A `SimEvent` says a mob was damaged and by which weapon line, and it says the grave was hit and by what: mob fire, naming the type that fired it, or a body landing on the player. Attributing only the damage the player deals would let the record say which line carried a run and never say what ended it, while every parked read waiting on this instrument is harm-side. The two failures have opposite fixes, so one undifferentiated hit count describes neither. This is recorded as architecture rather than as a ticket line because the event vocabulary is one of the things later work builds on top of, and because a run recorded without the attribution has lost it for good by the record-time rule above. It cannot move the golden digest, since the digest folds state and never events.

**The third section carries only what cannot be recomputed, and that rule is what keeps the pool open**, because a new kind of evidence justifies itself against replay rather than against a list. Mob count, projectile count, corpse count, phase, loadout and weapon state at an expensive frame are all reconstructable by replaying the tape to that tick, so none of them are written down. The section carries timings plus the tick index the frame started at, and every gameplay-load correlation then comes free from the join.

**The frame row is a minimum timing row and never a speculative telemetry schema** (ruled by Mark 2026-08-23). It records only what is required to answer the performance questions actually in hand, which today are the confirming play's. The open pool above says a new kind of evidence is welcome; it does not license writing down fields nobody has a question for, and the row is permanent from the first tape, so a speculative field is a permanent one. The standing constraint applies here as sharply as anywhere: prefer the smallest design that preserves trustworthy measurement.

**A frame row records what was actually observed at the frame seam, and unavailable context stays unavailable** (ruled by Mark 2026-08-23). That is the governing rule and it settles the skipped-frame case, which matters because skipped frames must be marked rather than omitted. **The seam lives inside a run's lifetime and the nullable field is the tick index** (ruled by Mark 2026-08-23). `GameScreen.update` at `GameScreen.ts:540` returns early on four conditions in a single guard, `ending`, `menuPaused`, `backgrounded` and a null `run`, and only the last of them leaves a frame with no run to attribute a row to. The hook therefore sits after a `this.run` check and before the other three, so every frame of a live run is recorded and no row is ever orphaned from the tape it belongs in. A paused, backgrounded, ending or countdown frame bought no ticks, so **its tick index is absent rather than fabricated**, nullable in the format by construction, and a reader that finds it missing learns something true instead of reading a tick index somebody invented to fill the column. **The predicate is the tick purchase and never the pause, so an absent index is not a pause marker**: at a 120Hz refresh against a 60Hz tick rate roughly half of ordinary live frames buy no tick either, and their row is byte-identical to a paused frame's, so the four cases listed here are examples of a frame that bought nothing rather than the set of them. **This refines the ruling of 2026-08-23 that the run attribution is the thing that goes absent.** That ruling was right that an absence gets recorded rather than invented, and that half stands unchanged. What it could not have known is that a run-less frame has no tape to be written into at all, because a tape is one run's recording and `Execution`'s lifetime is the run's, so recording those frames would have required a sink outliving a run that nothing in this dispatch specifies. That is what keeps the section small, and it is why one artifact serves every purpose instead of one artifact per purpose. The series is per frame rather than per run: `clock.debtTicks` in the header is one number for a whole run and cannot answer which gameplay situations created the debt.

What can be measured splits by who owns the signal, and two named signals must not be promised. From PixiJS and the runtime, cheap and already exposed by the version in use: the raw frame interval from `Ticker.elapsedMS`, FPS, `deltaMS` and `deltaTime` where they explain frame behaviour, renderer backend and resolution. `elapsedMS` is the interval between one frame and the last and never the work done inside a frame, so it reports that a frame was late and never what made it late; frame work time is instrumented by the game rather than read off the ticker, and an earlier draft of this paragraph offered both off the same field. Instrumented by the game itself: simulation tick duration, invariant-check duration, ticks executed per rendered frame, catch-up and tick-debt and clamp activity, render and sync and announce costs, entity counts by meaningful type, and `SimEvent` volume. **That is a list of what the game is able to measure and never a list of what gets written down**, and the two must not be read as the same list. The paragraph below is what decides: anything a replay can recompute is not written, so entity counts and `SimEvent` volume are measurable and are deliberately absent from the record, recovered by replaying to the frame's tick instead. The two to keep out of any promise until they are verified against pixi 8.19.0 are memory and GC evidence, where `performance.memory` is non-standard and Chrome-only so there is no portable reading, and batch breaks, where pixi exposes no cheap counter so "effects that break batching" is a derived inference rather than a measurement.

**Observation produces structured, persistable evidence that can be analysed later without the player interpreting or transcribing telemetry** (stated by Mark 2026-08-23). It governs gameplay and tuning evidence exactly as it governs performance evidence, and the shape is one shape for both: gameplay, then structured observations, then a persisted or exportable run record, then agent analysis. What it rules out is the only way a performance number can be read off this game today, where a person watches the `DEBT` label in a corner of the screen and types it back. A figure that reaches an agent through somebody's eyes and fingers arrives with no tick index, no device and no run identity, so there is nothing to join it to, nothing to compare it against and no way to ask a second question of it later, which is the same loss the record-time rule above describes arriving by a different route. Evidence eventually flows to a server or a database, so runs from a phone, from a desktop and eventually from other players' devices can be queried and compared against one another; before that backend exists the same shape holds with a local or exportable sink. **The future backend replaces the sink, never the observation schema and never the principle.** That is why the format is settled now while the store that writes it is not.

Sections are separable at the byte level, which means length prefixes or an offset table, so a reader can skip a section it does not understand rather than merely being told the sections are separate. Byte order is stated in the format and never left to the platform. And the decoder bounds-checks every section length against the buffer before it allocates, because the instrument route feeds arbitrary bytes from an arbitrary URL into it and a replay file from a stranger is the classic vector.

**Nothing in the format may require a value known only at run end.** No total tick count in the header, no trailing checkpoint index, and a decoder that accepts a body stopping mid-stream and verifies to its last complete checkpoint. The store that writes during a run is later work, but being writable incrementally is a property of the format and the format is settled now. MP4 writes its index last, so an interrupted recording is unplayable, which is the whole reason OBS ships a hybrid MP4; Doom's LMP appends tics sequentially but relies on an end-of-file marker, so a crashed recording lacks its termination signal. The design half of the argument outweighs the engineering half: one of the two shapes "too easy" takes is not dying but losing interest and closing the tab, so a format that only yields tapes for finished runs is structurally blind to the failure the instrument was built to find.

The header is written before the first tick, carries the run's identity, and sits entirely outside the witness. The seed, and the **resolved** starting size as a number rather than a nullable "pinned or not", because a run with no `?size=` starts at the compiled `SIZE_START` and recording the absence means a later tune of that default silently changes what every old tape replays as. The tick rate, because `TICK_HZ` is what makes "one command per tick" mean anything and the third section's wall-clock join depends on it. The checkpoint spacing and the witness version. The commit hash as human-readable metadata, plus a reserved field for a resolvable build identity whose machinery is deliberately not built. Identity: author, input device, keyboard speed multiplier, and the per-run runtime context, meaning renderer backend and renderer resolution, since header shape is one-way once tapes exist and those are constants for a run rather than a per-frame series. Without identity a folder of tapes holds Mark's runs, bot runs, keyboard runs and phone runs with nothing telling them apart, which is what Touhou's `.rpy` and StarCraft II's `replay.attributes.events` both carry it for. And when the run was recorded, in wall clock, because Mark is the most practiced player of this game alive and this instrument exists to ask whether the build got easier, so an unordered folder of his tapes measures him improving at the same time as it measures the build easing and the two trends point the same way. The header holds nothing beyond that, because a run's conditions and its outcome are known only when it stops and the paragraph above forbids those here.

**Run-end values live in a trailer rather than in the header, and a missing trailer is itself the reading.** The rule above forbids the header from carrying anything known only when a run stops, and a run's conditions and its outcome are all of that kind, so they are written once at the stop. `clock.debtTicks` is one of them, because discarded ticks are invisible in a body that holds only executed ticks, so a 12,000-tick run recorded over 400 stuttering seconds is byte-identical to one recorded over 200 smooth ones and slowdown makes a shmup easier, which is why Touhou's score rules cap it at roughly 5%. The outcome is two orthogonal fields rather than one status enum. `ending` is how the run **ended**, meaning sealed or victory or absent, and Hungry Grave ADR 0017 keeps a fault out of it because sealed is this game's word for death. `stop` is how the run **stopped**, meaning finished or quit or faulted or unknown. `integrity` is clean, faulted or unchecked, and says whether any invariant fired at all, fatal or not. **The third value is Mark's ruling of 2026-08-23.** It exists because the instrumentation build carries a checks-off control for the length of this dispatch, so a tape recorded with the checks off would otherwise read clean and be indistinguishable from a genuinely sound run. `unchecked` puts that fact where a reader already looks rather than in a header field, and once the control is deleted at the end of the dispatch the value simply stops appearing. It does not reopen the rejection of a single three-valued status below, which is about merging orthogonal outcome fields into one enum: `unchecked` describes the fault list rather than contradicting it, because with the checks off the list is empty for a reason the reader can now see. A single three-valued status was rejected because it can contradict the fault list it summarises, while two orthogonal fields cannot: a clean run has a clean integrity, faulted-but-continued is a faulted integrity with a `stop` that is not faulted, and faulted-and-aborted is a `stop` of faulted. A reader that finds no trailer reads `stop` as unknown, which is exactly the case the rule above exists for, since one of the two shapes "too easy" takes is a player losing interest and closing the tab, and that reading is the one the instrument most needs. The fault records themselves are third-section observations rather than trailer fields, because they are per-tick and that section already names runtime errors and warnings among its inhabitants; the trailer carries only the summary a reader needs before deciding whether to trust the run at all.

**The checkpoint spacing is data in the tape header, not a constant in the reader.** A reader obeys whatever the tape says, so a later measurement can change the spacing without versioning the format and without invalidating a single existing tape. Sixty is what the recorder writes on day one and it is a starting value rather than a rule: the figure it replaces was arithmetic, and this work exists partly to stop numbers like that from being written down as rules.

Steering is stored as two `float32` per axis, through the same single-precision rounding `math.ts` already applies inside the simulation under Hungry Grave ADR 0015. The recorded working figure of `int16` at a scale of 1/256 fails on range rather than on resolution: it tops out at 128 base-speeds, while a touch command is a position error over `BASE_SPEED` and a full-height flick after a 250ms catch-up stall reaches 168.9. Making it fit needs a clamp in the input path, which is the shape Hungry Grave ADR 0011 has already been burned by: its dont-build is capping touch drag at keyboard speed, and that cap was the input lag felt on device. The name is different and the mechanism is the same, so the objection is stated as a precedent rather than as a citation, since 0011 does not forbid a clamp in these words. The error a covering scale costs is derived here rather than asserted, because a number written down as a rule without its working is exactly what this work exists to stop. `int16` reaches 32767, so covering 168.9 base-speeds allows **at most** 194 steps per base-speed, and the finest power-of-two scale under that ceiling is 1/128, reaching 255.99 base-speeds; 1/256 would want 256 steps, exceeds the ceiling and tops out at 128.00 base-speeds. At 1/128 a unit diagonal component is 0.7071, which is 90.51 steps, so it rounds to 91 and the resulting vector is 0.54% long. Swept across ADR 0011's sixteen keyboard speed settings from 0.75 to 1.5 the diagonal error runs from **-0.56% to +0.54%** and never lands on zero, while the axis-aligned error reaches 0.39%, so the two axes disagree by a different amount at every setting. **Focus doubles it.** ADR 0011's `FOCUS_FACTOR` of 0.5 halves the command before it is quantised, and the same sweep focused runs **-1.21% to +1.02%** diagonal with the axis error reaching 0.74%, so the honest figure for the mode ADR 0011 exists to protect is roughly twice the unfocused one. That asymmetry is permanent and baked into every tape ever written. `float32` has no scale to derive, no range limit and no clamp, and costs 8 bytes a tick against 4. Exact `float64` was rejected on size at about 200KB packed and 500KB as JSON per run.

The quantisation happens inside `executeTick` rather than at `combineSteer`'s call site, and rounding inside the tape was presented in order to be rejected. `combineSteer` has exactly two call sites, so quantising there covers only the live input path: the bot policies, the golden scenario's script and every test helper produce commands that never pass through it, and a tape of a bot run would hold something the simulation did not consume. Rounding after the simulation has seen the true value is worse still, because replay then feeds a different number in and the run drifts, which is precisely the divergence Hungry Grave ADR 0015 and the golden digest exist to catch. Mark stated the invariant: the tape always contains exactly the commands the deterministic simulation consumed, never rounded or transformed after that boundary. Recording at the one execution authority makes that structural rather than a rule to remember. The price was measured by patching it, running the full suite and reverting: `GOLDEN` did not move, the bot bands survived, and one assertion failed, a `toBeCloseTo` at precision 9 on a deliberately wrong control, which loosens to precision 4 without losing its point.

The encoder produces bytes and never a JSON string, and `src/engine/utils/storage.ts` is not the home. **The frame rows dominate the tape, and the working figure that said otherwise was the body's alone.** A 12,000-tick run encodes to 110KiB of header, body, witness and trailer, but the observations section is 24 bytes per **rendered frame** and a 12,000-tick run is 12,000 frames at a 60Hz refresh and 24,000 at 120Hz, so the whole tape measures 391KiB at 60Hz and 672KiB at 120Hz and the rows outweigh the body by two and a half to five times. Base64 and UTF-16 multiply that by eight thirds, so one run costs roughly 1.0MiB of `localStorage` quota at 60Hz and 1.75MiB at 120Hz and a 5MiB origin holds four runs, or two from a 120Hz phone, against the nineteen the body-only figure promised. Every conclusion here survives the correction and one of them hardens: IndexedDB stores an `ArrayBuffer` with no inflation, and four runs an origin is not a store, it is one evening's play before the oldest tape has to be thrown away, so the choice between the two is now overwhelming rather than marginal. And 672KiB is still a file somebody can hold and send, which is what the paragraph exists to protect: a full stage's tape has to stay that, or sharing a run becomes a hosting problem.
