**What the boss machine does and does not do in this slice.** `spawnBoss` puts a boss on the field and nothing calls it from the stage: a boss phase still ends on its rows running out, which is slice 3's stand-in, and wiring the arrival here would stall every run in the Banshee phase with no pattern to kill and no ending to reach. **Slices 7 and 8 wire it**, and spec test 23 is pinned at the seam instead, driving each boss phase's own column and asserting the field it arrives on is empty. `advanceBoss` runs the flash down and the pattern clock up, and the pattern itself is delegated to `banshee.ts` and `undertaker.ts` when they exist. `bossArrived` and the three set-piece events are declared with no producer in this slice, each naming the module that will fire it, which is the cited-future form: the boss ones are slices 7 and 8's and the set piece's are slice 10's.

**`RunState.setPiece` is declared and folded here and filled at slice 10, which is the plan's own ruling, and it needed a type.** `stage/setPiece.ts` exists holding the `SetPiece` record and nothing else; slice 10 fills the module. The record is folded whole, and its invariant (`set piece budget not negative`) is live code over a field that exists rather than a check waiting for one.

**The fold appends and never reshuffles.** `foldBoss` and `foldSetPiece` run after `foldOffer`, each folding its own absent sentinel when nothing stands there, which is what moved `GOLDEN`'s checksum on a scenario that meets neither. `boss.kind` folds through `BOSS_KIND_CODES`, append-only and read by name like every other union in that file. `boss.id` is excluded as spawn identity, `mobFire[].kind` as a field written once at spawn and never mutated.

**The two new fault identities take wire codes 19 and 20, which is what slice 5's note predicted.** `boss chunk only increases` and `set piece budget not negative`, both recoverable: a chunk that came back replays a pattern the player has already beaten and a budget below zero pours nothing, and neither poisons a number anything downstream reads. The chunk check needs memory across ticks, so `StageWatch` gained `seenBoss`, which holds the id as well as the chunk: two bosses run in one run, and a memory of the chunk alone would read the second one's arrival at chunk zero as the index falling.

**The tests were proved to bite rather than assumed to.** Eight mutations, each reverted. A pushable boss (the seam's flag test alone, which is the finding above). A belch-killable boss (the flag test and the belch guard). The boss dropped from the seam's list (seven tests across both new files, spec 25 among them, which is the storm-must-always-matter property). The flash guard removed (three). The chunk-break feast removed (two). A directed shot re-aimed at the grave (two). The box left behind by a push (one, and nothing else in the tree saw it). A walk planted in `skullStream.ts` (fence 124's roster sweep).

**`FieldRenderer` and `mobFireSprite` needed no edit, and one of them is stated rather than a surprise.** `mobFireSprite.ts` still names `MOB_FIRE.trash` for both the shot and its scatter, so **boss fire draws in the trash body colour until slice 14**, which the plan puts there because that is the slice that draws bosses at all. The three sprites `palette.ts` has been carrying since before any boss existed are still unused.

## 14. Slice 7, the Banshee

**She arrives because the phase says so, and her phase ends because she is gone.** `enterNextPhase` reads `Phase.boss` and calls `spawnBoss`, announcing `bossArrived` with the chunk count `bossChunks` reads off her own health row; `phaseEnded` answers a `bossKilled` column with `state.boss === null`. Those two lines are the whole wiring, and together they are what makes the Wall's clock her death: the Crowd's phase-local zero is the tick after she falls, and its own first row is the curtain at t=2.

**What her rings are, as data.** `RING_ROWS` is one row per chunk: a period, a spoke count, how many adjacent spokes the opening replaces, how far the opening turns from one ring to the next, a jitter, and the sources it fires from. Chunk one is one source at her centre; chunk two is two, offset seventy units either side with their openings half a turn apart and the drift reversed. Initial rows every one of them, and what is not tuning is the shape: one clean opening per source, an opening that walks rather than stands still, and two sources whose openings never point the same way.

**The jitter is the one place chance enters her pattern, and it is one draw per ring.** All the sources of a tick share it, so the offset between two openings is exactly the authored one rather than two independent rolls that could quietly close the gap between them. It is drawn from the fire stream, because a ring is fire and because a sixth stream name would widen the witness fold and `WITNESS_VERSION` moved once already, at slice 6.

**The delegation table is how a boss's grammar reaches the machine.** `BOSS_GRAMMARS` in `chunks.ts` holds `{ advance, died }` per kind, `advanceBoss` runs the pattern on the clock it can read and then moves it, and `killBoss` announces the death before it asks the boss's own module what the death sheds. The Undertaker is absent from the table, which is what keeps the stage crossable until slice 8; section 4's item 26 is the whole of that argument.

**What the Banshee costs the headless birthright runs, measured rather than assumed.** This is the biggest thing in the slice that is not code. A policy that only dodges has nothing to kill a boss with, so a fresh run now ends inside her phase or just past it rather than crossing the stage:

- Fresh, five seeds: none reaches victory, none seals, all five run to the budget. Three of them (101, 303, 404) do empty her on the birthright storm alone, in ten to fifteen thousand ticks, and then run out of budget inside the Crowd; 202 and 505 are still in her phase at the end. Kills fall from 20 to 112 down to 11 to 32, and offers from 1 to 4 down to 0 to 4.
- From the size ceiling, five seeds: not one empties her, so every run is still in her phase when the budget ends, ending neither way.
- Maxed, five seeds: all five still reach victory, which is why that set exists, but they take 22000 to 48000 ticks against the 27000 the rows alone bound. `MAXED_RUN_TICKS` is three times the authored ceiling for exactly that reason.

**That last spread is the harness's input at step 4 and not a defect here.** A maxed dodger spends one to seven minutes on a boss authored for forty-five seconds, because it never parks under her and its storm reaches her only when its dodging happens to; a parked still hand on the birthright alone empties her in about 4900 ticks. Her health per chunk is the design record's derived 1100 and is tuning data by ADR 0052, so nothing here was retuned to make a bot's number nicer.

**Where the coverage went.** `reaches both endings across the five seeds` used to read its sealed half off a dodger that got ground down before the Vigil, and no dodging birthright run ends at all now. It reads the maxed set for victory and the hit-taking policy for sealed, which is the policy written to reach it; `sealedRun` caches those five runs because two suites now read them. **The honest summary is that between this slice and the tuning pass, the birthright loadouts no longer cross the stage headlessly, and what covers the whole stage is the pinned maxed build the plan's verification step 7 already names.**

**Two rig budgets grew and each says why on itself.** `STAGE_TICKS` in `stage.test.ts` adds a `BOSS_FIGHT_TICKS` allowance per boss phase, because a fight has no authored length and the still hand's own 4900 ticks left the old ceiling with six per cent of slack; and `bounds the tail after a section's last row` took a stated timeout, because it plays two whole stages nothing else has warmed and a stage is a fight longer than it was. The bot file's birthright budget deliberately did **not** grow: those runs do not empty a boss, so a bigger budget buys minutes and nothing else.

**The tests were proved to bite rather than assumed to.** Eleven mutations, each reverted, and every one turned at least one of this slice's tests red: firing every spoke (the one-clean-gap test), giving chunk two one source and lining the two openings up (the offset test, twice), dropping the stream draw and moving it to the spawns stream (the stream test, twice), drawing a tear in the trash read (three tests, including the gas one), shedding nothing on her death (all four death and Wall tests), ending a boss phase on its rows again (spec 7, the stubbed-phase test, the Wall test and all five ceiling seeds), never arriving at all (nineteen), skipping a chunked target in the burst and applying the kill rule to it (the two belch tests). Two of the mutations also moved bot seed outcomes, which is the path effect that file has documented four times: a different fight is a different run from there on.

**One thing this slice does not do, stated so it is not read later as a bug.** Her tears carry the `tear` fire kind and `mobFireSprite` still draws every shot in `MOB_FIRE.trash`, so boss fire is drawn in the trash colour until slice 14, exactly as slice 6's note said. Nothing draws her body at all yet either; `BossRenderer` is slice 14's.

## 15. Slice 8, the Undertaker

**The arrival gate is gone, and its going is the whole of the stage change.** `BOSS_GRAMMARS` is a total `Record<BossKind, BossGrammar>` now rather than a `Partial`, `bossIsAuthored` is deleted with its caller, and `arriveBoss` reads `phase.boss` and nothing else. What the totality buys is the thing the gate was standing in for: a kind added to `BossKind` with no module to fight with is a compile error rather than a boss who never arrives and a phase nothing ever leaves.

**What his three chunks are, as data.** `CURTAIN_ROWS` and `SPIRAL_ROWS` are each three long and each carries a null where its chunk does not run that shape: the burial throws curtains, the exhumation turns the arm, the last chunk does both. A curtain row is a period and a clod count; a spiral row is the arm's revolution, its shot interval and its digger cadence. Every number is an initial row and what is not tuning is the shape.

**How a curtain is built, and why there is always a way through.** The clods are spread evenly across everything the opening does not take, so the count is the row's whatever the grave's size is: a bigger grave buys a wider way through and never a thinner curtain, and the opening exists by construction rather than by arithmetic that happens to work out. The opening is held far enough from either edge that all of it is on the field, because half an opening against a wall is not the width the gap rule promised.

**Where the opening sits is the one branch in the module, and it is the locked overlap.** A chunk with no arm turning walks its opening across the field on its own; a chunk with one opens it where the arm has just swept, a quarter of a revolution behind. Both read the one pattern clock, which is what makes "in phase for the whole chunk" true by construction rather than by a second clock that has to be kept in step.

**The arm is the line the spiral's shots leave along, and the curtain reads where that line reaches.** That is what `ARM_REACH` is for and why it is a row rather than a drawing decision: at 180 of the field's 540 the arm sweeps between x 90 and 450 and never needs the edge clamp, so the opening tracks it exactly. The diggers come up at the same place, which is what makes them an exhumation rather than a spawn.

**The lattice is why the phase tests carry a tolerance, and the tolerance is derived.** A curtain's clods stand on a lattice of their own spacing, so an opening measured between two of them can only land within half a step of where the pattern aimed it. Both overlap tests compute that half step from the row and the current gap rather than writing a number down, and the follows-rather-than-leads half asserts the arm's current position is further off than the tolerance, so a lag of zero fails.

**What the storm floor is computed from, and never pinned.** Spec test 44 reads `COLUMNS_BY_LEVEL`, `SKULL_DAMAGE` and `STREAM_INTERVAL` off the skull stream and `BELL_DAMAGE_NEAR` and `BELL_PERIOD` off the bell, at their top level, and derives about 147 points a second. Territory and the wisps are left out exactly as the design record leaves them out, so the figure is a floor rather than a prediction. The chunk-health property is then that each chunk's row clears that floor across one emit of its own longest cycle, which at 240 and 300 ticks is 588 and 735 against rows of 1700.

**A boss test cannot switch the storm off, so the rig holds the chunk instead.** Zeroing `skullStream` raises the fatal `levels in range` invariant, because the birthright is what a run is born holding. The pattern rig restores the live chunk's health after each tick and holds the grave immortal at whatever size the test set, and it stands the run in his own phase, which authors no rows, so everything that arrives on the field in these tests is his.

**What he costs the headless runs, measured rather than assumed.** All five maxed seeds still reach victory and the set did not move, but victory now means his death rather than a phase a stub fell through. The runs take 26000 to 52000 ticks and his fight is 3600 to 4800 of them on every one of the five, against the Banshee's 1280 to 27230 on the same runs. That tightness is worth reading: he is fought where the Vigil has left the field nearly empty, so a dodger stands in front of him and its storm lands, where her fight is spent dodging across a field she shares with nothing. Sixty seconds against ADR 0052's ninety to a hundred and twenty is what a maxed build buys, and it is the harness's input at step 4 rather than a defect here.

**One rig budget grew and it says why on itself.** `BOSS_FIGHT_TICKS` in `stage.test.ts` is 19500 against 7500, because the still hand's own measurement moved: a parked grave on the birthright alone empties the Banshee in about 4900 ticks and the Undertaker in about 13000, his three chunks against her two. It is a ceiling and not a prediction, and every run in that file still ends on victory long inside it.

**One test beyond the plan's list, and it is what a mutation asked for.** Spec 38 holds that a curtain is never a wall, and the first mutation, clods spread across the whole field with no opening cut, left an opening against the right-hand edge wide enough to pass it. That opening is real, so the measurement is right and the test list was short: nothing held that the way through **moves**. `walks the way through rather than opening it in the same place twice` is the missing half of game-concept.md:70's "one moving gap", and it holds both sides, that the opening moves at all and that it never moves further than the row says it walks.

**The tests were proved to bite rather than assumed to.** Ten mutations, each reverted, and every one turned at least one of this slice's tests red: a curtain with no opening cut (the walk test and both overlap tests, which is the finding above); a gap rule reading a fixed size (the gap-rule test and both grave-size tests); the overlap reading the arm's current tick instead of the lag (both overlap tests); the last chunk's curtain at chunk one's clod count (the thinner-curtain test); the last chunk's digger cadence removed (the keeps-digging test); a whole curtain fired from one point (six tests, the no-ring one among them); a chunk's health dropped under its emit floor (spec 44); a digger cadence over `BOSS_ADD_ALLOWANCE` (module 121 in `rows.test.ts`); the burial turning an arm as well (the one-shape-at-a-time test and the walk test); and the arrival gate put back for the Undertaker alone (both stage tests this slice touched).

**Two things this slice does not do, stated so neither is read later as a bug.** His clods and spiral shots carry the `clod` and `spiral` fire kinds and `mobFireSprite` still draws every shot in `MOB_FIRE.trash`, so his patterns draw in the trash colour until slice 14, exactly as slices 6 and 7 said. And victory still fires on entering the `over` phase rather than on his death: the stub is slice 9's to retire, and what changed here is that reaching that phase now costs a fight.

## 16. Slice 9, both endings

**Victory is announced from its own seam, and the last boundary rides with it.** `winStage(state, tickEvents)` in `stage.ts` reads the tick's own `bossKilled`, checks it against the boss the table's last fight carries, crosses into `over` and fires `victory`, all on the tick he falls. `step.ts` calls it straight after `resolveDeaths`, which is the last phase of a tick that can empty a boss. The stub in `enterNextPhase` is gone and that function now only announces a phase and stands up whatever boss it carries. Section 4's item 33 is the whole of why the crossing moved in here rather than being left to the next tick's `advanceStage`.

**Which boss ends the stage is read off the phases and never written down.** `FINAL_BOSS` folds `PHASES` for the last phase carrying a boss, so a stage that gains a section ends on that section's boss with no edit in this module. The second half of the check is the current phase's own boss column, which is what keeps a boss stood up outside his phase by a rig from ending anything: `chunks.test.ts` spawns bosses wherever it likes and none of it moved.

**What the ladder needed was the run and not the policy.** `hitTakingPolicy` steers into the nearest threat and is unchanged. Standing it in the Undertaker's phase on a run pinned at level two with score on it, ADR 0003's rungs fall in order: the whole score bled on the first floor hit, then two strips down to the birthright, then sealed, with the boss still standing at 1331 of his last chunk's 1700. Level two rather than five is deliberate and stated on the row: a full build empties a chunk faster than his pattern grinds a grave to the floor, and a fight that ends in victory cannot also seal.

**`STRIPS_A_RUNG` was re-measured and is still empty**, which the equality in `bot.test.ts` asserts on every run. Nothing in this slice touches the Procession, where that policy seals at tick 1132 before the first carrier at t=21, so its second rung is still out of a birthright run's reach there. The constant's comment now points at `endings.test.ts` as where the whole ladder is walked instead of at a slice that had not happened yet.

**A warning for slice 10, which owns module test 104.** That test asks the bot to reach sealed "through the whole ladder" on at least one seed, and `sealedRun` plays `hitTakingPolicy` from the ceiling on a birthright loadout in the Procession: it bleeds score once and seals, and there is no rung between them. Reaching the whole ladder from that file needs the same two pins this slice used, a build above the birthright through `createRun`'s third parameter and a fight to stand in, or the test measures the policy rather than the ladder.

**The lifecycle test's victory rig had to become a real fight.** `screenLifecycle.test.ts`'s "complete run in both directions" stood the run in the last boss phase with nobody in it and let the crossing win it, which is exactly the route this slice retired. It now spawns him on his last point of health and plays until the birthright storm reaches him, which is 79 ticks from a parked grave on every seed measured. It is the only test outside the new file that the ruling moved, and the rest of the suite was green on the first run after the change.

**The tests were proved to bite rather than assumed to.** Six mutations, each reverted. The stub put back in `enterNextPhase` (three tests, two of them `endings.test.ts`'s and one the stage's). Any boss's death ending the run (seventeen, the Banshee's half of the payout test among them, because a stage that ends at the Banshee never reaches the Vigil). The ending without the crossing behind it (six, including the seven-phase chain and both boundary-tick tests, which is the measurement behind item 33). His death shedding a feast like hers (three, spread across `endings.test.ts` and `undertaker.test.ts`). The ladder reordered to strip before it bleeds (two, this file's and `grave.test.ts`'s own). The already-ended guard dropped from `winStage` (one, the same-tick test, which is the only thing that holds it).
