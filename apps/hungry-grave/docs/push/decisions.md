The loop as it stands: density makes kills, kills buy drops, drops move power, power moves pressure, the director reads pressure and moves density. Shipped ways out, from reward-delivery-models.md and progression-tuning-precedent.md: price drops in time or beats rather than kills (Risk of Rain 2 keys difficulty to the clock; Halls of Torment moved Agony to a clock after "rubber-banding"; Brotato pays at wave end), or keep kill pricing and cap what the director can add above the authored floor (the arcade rank games couple on purpose, capped, and death lowers it). 10. **Power is metered by carriers, not by kill count.** Mark chose this (2026-09-02). Specific authored mobs carry the offer; kill one and the offer falls where it died. The director adds mobs but never carriers, so the director cannot touch power, which cuts the loop (density makes kills, kills buy drops, drops move power, power moves pressure, the director reads pressure) on the power side. Rejected: kill count (today's rising price table; the loop stays), and the clock (Risk of Rain 2, Brotato wave end; skill buys nothing on the power side). Precedent: DoDonPachi's four carriers, Garegga's tanks, Yuge's "what weapon would be good for a certain part of the game, then make sure there were enough power-ups." What stays: kills feed the grave (corpses are fuel, growth and belch charge come from swallowing), score is kills, killing buys room to live. This supersedes ADR 0002's kill-priced rising curve and retires drops.ts's price table when the ADR step runs; the offer of three (decisions 8 and 9) rides on the carrier's drop unchanged, and one live offer at a time still holds.

Mark's condition, his words: carrier authoring must be a knob too, because "if the director can't spawn carriers then we need to ensure we're still giving the player enough power to maximize fun." His stated fun goal, which can change but is "an absolute for sure goal": the player gets to the point where "there's just so many projectiles flying out of you," the bullet-heaven storm, killing mobs. Eating corpses and what it buys adds fun, and score on top of that (highest score) may add more. So the carrier schedule is authored so a player who kills every carrier reaches the storm well before the boss; where and when carriers arrive, per stage, is data, read as time-of-each-drop on the tape (Hopoo's "progression rate") and power reached by minute.

Open, for later in the grill or the design: the missed-carrier case (Gradius syndrome). Shipped catch-ups: Nemesis's recovery wave after a death, DoDonPachi's Max Power item after the last life, Raiden's Fairy releasing power-ups on death. Not asked yet.
