## STEP 4 BRIEF: the mow, the ladder, the director

**What step 4 is.** Re-author everything authored so the game starts from a better place, then put the director on top. Mark's rulings that bind it, all 2026-09-09: (1) **the mow**: bullet heaven is mowing and mowing, getting more power and seeing more and harder enemies as you get it; density is bought with weak bodies, never tough ones. (2) **The ladder**: reaching the end with every line maxed should be rare and for the best players, but the ceiling stays high and visible; rarity and skill expression, never a lower cap. (3) The three changes (authored schedule, progression curve, director) ship in one pass, not sequenced. (4) A density ADR passes the game design gate before filing (pre-authorization 6). ADR 0047 (director reads pressure, never power; four off-limits moments; rows are the floor; own seeded streams) and ADR 0056 (finite purse per phase, quiet interval after an add, signal reads harm and never a nearby kill, caps re-derived and never evicting) stand and are Mark's. Gates never overrule rulings.

**Where the game is (measured, two human runs, build `ad4657276e`).** 1.13 arrivals a second, 458 bodies over 406 s (356 shambler, 55 ghoul, 47 revenant), mean 4.4 mobs on screen, peak 30. Enemy shots in the air peak 70 in every bot run, mean 5; the player's storm peaks 7 under the harness hand. All four lines at level 5 by the end, 35 hits, no strips. Mark's felt run: boring start, then dodging without the power to kill, then okay, then super easy near max. Vampire Survivors at comparable minutes runs 100 to 300 on screen at 1 to 3 HP, and buys every dense minute by making bodies weak again (minute 8: 100 at 1.0 HP; minute 11: 300 at 1.5 HP; minutes 5 and 12 are 10 to 20 at 15 to 18 HP). Every shipped survivors-like kills trash in 1 to 4 hits at minute zero and never carries difficulty on trash HP (DRG:S trash is 0.95x at max hazard).

**The economy as the code has it, which the old handoff got half wrong.** Weapon levels come only from carriers: `offer.ts:322` is the one increment, `grave.ts:165` the strip. 25 rows carry (`rows.ts`), a full build is 19 rungs, `CARRIER_SLACK` 1.3. Corpses pay size (`CORPSES_TO_CEILING` 80, payout 0.506 a corpse, start 27, ceiling 67.5, floor 18, 3 per hit), belch charge (`RESERVOIR_CAPACITY` = feast = 9 corpses), overflow to score, and the two on-swallow bursts (skull surge and wisp volley, ADR 0058, no cadence limit). So ten times the bodies is ten times the belch, ten times the swallow-driven storm and instant ceiling, and it does not move the ladder at all. The ladder's levers are separate: carrier count, the bank that never expires (`offer.ts`, ADR 0034), and how long an offer stands (bodies drift at the scroll, 38 units a second, a field crossing is 20 s, freshness is 10 s). Rows: shambler 40 HP, every third armed, 3 s interval, falls at half the scroll; revenant 64 HP, all armed, 2.5 s; ghoul 20 HP, never fires, chases. Damage: skull 8, wisp 10, bell 40 near and 5 far, territory 5 a pulse. Caps: `MOB_CAP` 160 (derived against a 51-mob peak), `MOB_FIRE_CAP` 400, `CORPSE_CAP` = 160 + peakArrivals(10 s) + 10. Sections: Procession 118 s, Crowd 138 s plus the 15 s pour, Vigil 69 s, Banshee about 30 s, Undertaker about 60 s, two 15 s drain-outs; about 400 s in all.

**The starting point, reasoned rather than swept. Every number below is a data row the first batch moves; state each as one thing against another, never a target (ADR 0053).**

1. *Fire lives on the few, not the many.* Ten times the shamblers at every-third-armed is 700 shots in the air against a cap of 400 and a bullet hell ten times worse. The mow body never fires: shambler `fire` becomes `NEVER_FIRES`. Fire stays on the revenant (all armed, few, tough) and the bosses, so enemy fire peaks near or below the revenant share of today's 70, and the "heaven with doses of hell" is the roster split itself. The Procession's "Drip of three, one armed" teaching row becomes a lone revenant Drip. Alternative rejected: a low armed share on the shambler; at 8 a second even one in ten is 48 shooters a minute, and it makes the mow body a shooter with worse odds rather than a different thing.
2. *Trash HP to one skull.* Shambler 40 to 8: one skull, one wisp, one near toll, two far tolls, two territory pulses. Ghoul stays 20 (three skulls; it is the body threat and must die fast but not free). Revenant stays 64 (eight skulls; the few-and-tough row at 8x trash, against VS's 15x). Integers stay; the invariant is hit count, not scale, and 8 gives exact one-skull kills without rewriting every damage row into floats. The #76 pass A touch counts (5, 4, 8, 1) are superseded by the mow ruling; the far-toll-tickles ruling in ADR 0005 survives as the ratio (far is an eighth of near).
3. *Growth over the run is authored as a per-section floor stream, keyed to time.* This is the VS minute table in our shape and the half of the split ADR 0047 does not own: a data row per section, `{type, bodiesPerSecond from, to, template}`, repeating (Brotato's `repeating_interval` down to a minimum). Starting rates: Procession 2 a second rising to 5 by its end; Crowd 8 rising to 12, with the authored trough at 3; Vigil no stream (it is the few-and-tough minute by ADR 0049's scarcity ruling, and that ruling now reads as VS's minute 5 rather than as a contradiction). The shaped rows (Files, Vs, Pincers, Wall, revenant rows, the 25 carriers) stay as the beats on top, with their shambler counts roughly doubled rather than tenfolded, because a File of 60 is 27 s of scroll and templates are shapes, not faucets. On-screen expectation at 4 s a body: Procession 8 to 20, Crowd 30 to 50 plus the beats, so peaks near 60 to 80. Alternative rejected: tenfold the row counts in place; the templates cannot hold it and the cadence between rows (4 to 9 s) is the emptiness Mark felt.
4. *The food economy is re-stated in units that survive the retune.* `CORPSES_TO_CEILING` 80 to about 400, so the ceiling lands late in the Procession rather than ten seconds in; the reservoir is stated in corpses of expected mowing, `RESERVOIR_CORPSES` about 300, so a belch is roughly every 40 s at Crowd rates rather than every 2 s; the feast still fills the reservoir in one swallow (decision 5.11) so `FEAST_PAYOUT` = reservoir capacity, which makes the feast about 30 size units of growth, nearly half the ceiling, a real moment. The on-swallow bursts get a cadence floor per line (a minimum interval between wisp volleys, about 30 ticks, and one running skull surge whose duration extends to a cap) because at ten swallows a second the level-5 wisps launch 110 a second into a cap of 64 and already land over half of all kills; without the floor the auto-targeting line does the mowing and the player does not, which is the opposite of the feel. ADR 0058's freshness axis is untouched; this is a new row beside it and wants its own ADR line or an in-place amendment.
5. *The ladder: expire the bank, keep the slack, measure before moving carriers.* A banked offer that is not opened within one field crossing (20 s) is lost, reported as its own event. This is Mark's "offers going away faster" applied to the one thing that today never goes away, and it keeps ADR 0034's Gradius and Brotato precedent (a burst still pays) while making a player who leaves offers standing pay for it. Carriers stay 25 and `CARRIER_SLACK` 1.3 for the first batch; if the sharp hand still maxes on most seeds, the second iteration moves slack toward 1.1 (ADR 0048 makes it data). Offer lifetime stays the scroll. Alternatives rejected for the first pass: removing the bank outright (needs its own argument against 0034's precedent), a shorter offer lifetime (Iuchi's mis-take under fire, the record's own caution). The skill expression is routing: carriers die to wisps far from the grave and the offer opens where they died, so reaching it costs position in a field that is now full.
6. *The director, on top, exactly as ruled.* Per phase: a purse counted in bodies (about a third of the phase's floor stream total), a permission cell (the `directed` column already on every row), cards over the mob types with a cost each (shambler 1, ghoul 3, revenant 4), spent only while the pressure signal reads low, then a quiet interval drawn from a min/max pair (4 to 8 s). Signal: harm and floor events only, held 5 s, linear decay over 30 s (the L4D constants), never a nearby kill. Its dice from its own named stream, chained, so `WITNESS_VERSION` goes 6 to 7 once. The research's strongest cross-game finding: build the instrument first (a replay graph from a tape: signal, mobs alive, each add with its reason) and a signal lock, then tune the gate and the population and never the signal's weights. The time-keyed growth is the floor stream's, so the director reads pressure and the player still feels the game answer their power, which is the resolution Mark said he liked; it goes through the design gate as a proposal.
7. *Caps become derivations of data, not constants.* `MOB_CAP` = max floor-stream peak plus beats plus purse, `MOB_FIRE_CAP` from the revenant peak, `CORPSE_CAP` from scroll physics as ADR 0056 says; a bound cap raises a fault. **Measure the frame budget first**, before any content: `src/dev/framePerformance.ts` exists; a synthetic field of 100 mobs and 250 corpses on desktop, then the phone at the first deploy.
