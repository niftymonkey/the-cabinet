# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

People Mark sends a link to: friends, family, playtesters. They arrive on a phone or a laptop, already knowing who built this, wanting to reach a game. Nobody needs to be sold, introduced, or taught.

## Product Purpose

The Cabinet is a multicade: one arcade machine with every game Mark has built inside it. The website is the machine. You stand in front of it, lean in to its monitor, and pick a game from the game select. Picking one sends you to that game's own address.

The name reads two ways, an arcade cabinet and a cabinet of curiosities. The first reading is what people see. The second is why any era or genre fits inside.

Success is someone tapping the link, seeing the machine, and playing a game a few seconds later, having felt the machine was worth walking up to.

## Brand Personality

A machine in a friend's basement. Cozy, personal, a little dim. Found, not bought. Three words: warm, homemade, patient. It does not shout for a coin. It hums and waits.

## Anti-references

- A portfolio. No bio, no resume, no about page. The games speak.
- An arcade floor: bright, loud, every machine shouting for your coin.
- A product shot: the machine floating clean and lit on nothing.
- A list of links wearing a picture of a machine.

## Design Principles

- The site is the machine. Nothing sits beside it: no page chrome, no navigation, no text around the canvas.
- Standing in front comes first. The whole cabinet, with room to move around it, is the first thing anyone sees.
- Leaning in is the only door. Every game is reached by leaning in to the monitor and picking it from the game select.
- A game keeps its own house. The cabinet points at a game's address and never owns or imports it.
- The machine teaches in its own idiom. Whatever a visitor needs to learn, how to move around it, how to lean in, the cabinet shows with its own motion or a hint on its monitor, never with text beside the canvas.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Phones and desktops get the same cabinet. Drag or mouse to move around it, tap or click to lean in, and the keyboard works for both.
- The cabinet's subtle motion, and whatever plays on the monitor, respect the visitor's reduced-motion setting.
