# Craft values for the standing-in-front scene: cool near-black darks with the warmth kept in the highlights, a home cabinet reads by its faux coin door and light-up marquee, idle drift is measured in tens of seconds, and the ticker stops when nothing moves

Research for The Cabinet, ticket [#93](https://github.com/niftymonkey/the-cabinet/issues/93). Labels: **DOCUMENTED** = an author's own statement, public source code, a manufacturer's or vendor's own page, official docs, or a measurement the source itself made. **COMMUNITY-MEASURED** = a third-party review, issue thread, or benchmark with a measured or reproduced result. **INFERRED** = my reading across sources, including my own measurements of publisher screenshots and my own inspection of code.

The brief is in [apps/cabinet/PRODUCT.md](../../PRODUCT.md): a machine in a friend's basement, cozy, personal, a little dim, found not bought, it hums and waits. The anti-references are an arcade floor and a product shot. The palette bans brown and purple. The four questions were palette, cabinet materials, motion timing, and frame loop policy.

Tooling note: the Exa tools were not available in this session, so real-world usage came through WebSearch and direct fetches, and docs came through Ref. Where a page could not be fetched (Medium, the Mancave Empire guide, the Apple HIG motion page, the North Coast kit page) the record says so and labels the item accordingly.

Measurement note for the palette question: I pulled each game's own Steam store screenshots through the store's `appdetails` API, scaled each to 192 by 108 with ffmpeg, and computed three numbers per shot: the mean colour, the mean of the darkest 30 percent of pixels by luminance ("dark30"), and the mean of the brightest 10 percent ("light10"), each with its hue, saturation and value. These are publisher-chosen, JPEG-compressed images, so hue on near-black pixels is noise and is only reported where saturation and value are both above noise. Every number from this is INFERRED. The raw stats are in `shots/stats.json` beside this file.

The strongest sources are code and measurements: Henry Heffernan's camera keyframes ([CameraKeyframes.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/Camera/CameraKeyframes.ts)), the decompiled Stardew Valley night-lighting path ([Game1.cs](https://github.com/WeDias/StardewValley/blob/master/Game1.cs)), PixiJS v8's own render-loop and ticker docs plus the `Ticker.ts` source, the WebKit power-usage post, the WebKit bug that throttles `requestAnimationFrame` to 30 fps in Low Power Mode, the Arcade1Up and AtGames product pages, and the X-Arcade build guide.

The gap: no shipped 2D game found ships a dim, warm-lit interior that avoids brown in its mid-tones; the sampled evidence shows that where amber light lands on a mid-value surface it measures brown, so the ban is a real constraint that the palette has to design around rather than a direction any precedent already walks. No canvas portfolio read here handles `prefers-reduced-motion` at all, and no source gives a measured battery number for a mostly-still canvas loop on a phone; the closest is a desktop powermetrics run.

## What is solid

### Palette

1. **The default cozy-interior palette in shipped games is brown, and the developers say so.** Toge Productions' own design write-up: "Coffee Talk uses a brownish color palette that emits the game's relaxing, warm, and casual atmosphere", with "thin outlines and grainy colors" after Cowboy Bebop and Evangelion ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/brewing-meaningful-ux-in-the-interactive-visual-novel-i-coffee-talk-i-), DOCUMENTED); the itch page pitches "90s anime inspired pixel art visual and chill vibes inducing color palette" and "the calm feelings of drinking warm drinks in the middle of a cold rainy night" ([itch.io](https://togeproductions.itch.io/coffee-talk), DOCUMENTED). Measured across its six store shots, Coffee Talk's mean colour sits at hue 0 to 30, saturation 14 to 29 percent, value 15 to 37 percent (for example `#433b38`, `#53493f`), which is brown by any reading, and its dark30 is near black (`#0d0d0d`, `#151011`) (INFERRED). The lesson is not "copy Coffee Talk"; it is that cozy-at-night has a gravitational pull toward brown and the cabinet's ban means designing against the default.

2. **Purple arrives the same way: a red-leaning warm light meeting a blue shadow at mid value.** VA-11 Hall-A's six store shots all measure dark30 at `#000000` and a mean at hue 262 to 305, saturation 25 to 33 percent, value 15 to 18 percent (`#271d2b`, `#2b212d`): a black bar whose only light is pink and cyan neon, and the mix is purple (INFERRED from shots such as [ss_2e423a](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/447530/ss_2e423af338f78a728112aefc4dbada0050715ae2.1920x1080.jpg)). Unpacking's bedroom shot does it too: mean `#7e778d` at hue 259, dark30 `#4c3d5e` at hue 267 ([ss_9b3585](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1135690/ss_9b358510285a4b70e446237de5996bc434415c01.1920x1080.jpg), INFERRED), where the same game's warm-lit living room measures dark30 `#454547` (neutral, saturation 3 percent) with light10 `#c0b6a3` (hue 39, saturation 15 percent) and a mean of `#706d67` at hue 40, saturation 8 percent: warm grey, not brown and not purple ([ss_e0be82](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1135690/ss_e0be82259f402c277973c5d0c4f2a22d01d9b1c2.1920x1080.jpg), INFERRED). Read together: keep the warm light yellow-leaning (hue 35 to 45) rather than red-leaning (hue 10 to 20), keep warm mid-tones under about 15 percent saturation, and the two banned colours do not appear (INFERRED).

3. **Direction A, slate or teal-blue darks under a warm lamp, has the most precedent.** Unpacking's darks measure `#2e2e44` (hue 240, saturation 32 percent), `#3a5865` (hue 198, saturation 43 percent) and `#3a3c3e` across four of six shots, with cool daylight highlights (`#b4c2d4`, `#c8d7e8`) (INFERRED). A Short Hike's two evening shots put dark30 at `#29566b` and `#26506f` (hue 199 to 205, saturation 62 to 66 percent) under gold highlights `#e8d886` and warm greys ([ss_a22817](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1055540/ss_a22817e259c4220cad0c25db315f417b6d3641b8.1920x1080.jpg), [ss_56e51b](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1055540/ss_56e51b6bc4604ca1231d5a8d534ac9b67449d353.1920x1080.jpg), INFERRED). Stardew Valley's night is built this way in code: `eveningColor` is `Color(255, 255, 0)` (pure yellow), `outdoorLight = eveningColor * num` ramps `num` from 0.30 toward a cap of 0.93 after "starting to get dark", the light map is filled with that colour, and it is drawn over the scene with a `BlendState` whose `ColorBlendFunction = BlendFunction.ReverseSubtract` ([Game1.cs](https://github.com/WeDias/StardewValley/blob/master/Game1.cs) lines 360, 525 to 530, 5072 to 5083, DOCUMENTED). Subtracting yellow leaves blue, and the store's night shot measures mean `#122c49` (hue 212, saturation 75 percent) with dark30 `#010740` ([ss_d836f0](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/413150/ss_d836f0a5b0447fb6a2bdb0a6ac5f954949d3c41e.1920x1080.jpg), INFERRED); the winter branch swaps in `Color(245, 225, 170)`, a cream, which still subtracts least from blue (line 7643, DOCUMENTED). The pixel-art rule behind all of this is Raymond Schlitter's: "positive hue shift usually results in more natural colors, warming as they become brighter", built with "20 degrees of positive hue shift between each swatch", saturation that "never fully goes to 100, or 0", and the warning that "colors with very low brightness can become overly rich and weighty with high saturation" ([Slynyrd Pixelblog 1](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/1/10/pixelblog-1-color-palettes), DOCUMENTED). Cool darks, warm lights, saturation tapering at both ends.

4. **Direction B, true near-black with the machine self-lit, is what every dim-interior game and both canvas portfolios actually do with their darkest 30 percent.** Coffee Talk, VA-11 Hall-A, Kentucky Route Zero and Stardew's night all measure dark30 at value 0 to 8 percent (INFERRED, items 1 to 3 and 5). Bruno Simon's folio clears to black: `this.renderer.setClearColor(0x000000, 1)`, with the earlier `0x414141` commented out ([Application.js](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2019/blob/master/src/javascript/Application.js) line 90, DOCUMENTED). Heffernan's UI layer is `background-color: #000` with `#fff` text and a 4 px white border ([UI/style.css](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/UI/style.css), DOCUMENTED). The colour in these scenes lives entirely in the lit objects (neon, screens, lamps), and the differences between them are in the mid-tones, which is where brown and purple crept in (INFERRED). For the cabinet this direction means a black or near-black room with a slight cool cast, the marquee and the screen carrying all the warmth, and nothing mid-value and warm large enough to read as brown.

5. **Direction C, teal-black with amber light, is Kentucky Route Zero, and it shows exactly where the brown line is.** Four of KRZ's eight store shots measure dark30 at hue 180 to 195, saturation 14 to 44 percent, value 5 to 7 percent (`#0c0e0e`, `#0c0f10`, `#0a1012`) with teal highlights `#79888a`, `#4d6e7b` ([ss_86f61b](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/231200/ss_86f61b9478c321fd10ab4b8a75c92111138da563.1920x1080.jpg), [ss_7646b7](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/231200/ss_7646b7187875432c8fe7e2ae0dc81a613f9d7d94.1920x1080.jpg), INFERRED). The two amber-lit shots, though, measure mean `#3d2717` (hue 25, saturation 62 percent) and `#61594e` (hue 35, saturation 20 percent), and their dark30 is `#160c05` (hue 25, saturation 77 percent): brown ([ss_48cec8](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/231200/ss_48cec89cdde5e5e1fbdf4838d3dd813246000fc7.1920x1080.jpg), INFERRED). Tamas Kemenczy on the intent: "We have spent years in darkness and a subterranean context", and on the small light moves that mattered, "during the mid-day, there's clouds and the light dims a little and then dims back up. That feeling, those little details, were important to get right", "It's such a small programmatic operation but I was really happy with the emotional effect it drew" ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/kentucky-route-zero-creators-on-finding-hope-and-the-burden-of-4k-gaming/), DOCUMENTED; his GDC 2014 talk is [Scenography of Kentucky Route Zero](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020596/Scenography-of-Kentucky-Route)). So amber over teal-black works only while the amber stays in the top 10 percent of values; the moment a wall or floor at mid value takes the amber, it is brown (INFERRED).

### Materials of a real home multicade

6. **Arcade1Up's own product language names the tells: "Light-Up Marquee", "front faux coin door", "Real-feel joystick and buttons", a 17-inch LCD, "integrated dual speakers", "WIFI leaderboards", $499.99.** ([Pac-Man Deluxe](https://arcade1up.com/collections/all/products/pac-man-deluxe-arcade-game-1), DOCUMENTED). The collection page puts the newer Supreme series on a "premium 19" BOE display" and sells the "Classic SE Arcade Riser" as a "14.25" Riser" ([collection](https://arcade1up.com/collections/all), DOCUMENTED). The owner's manual gives the cabinet without riser as 48.7 by 19.4 by 22.3 inches (123.6 by 48.3 by 56.6 cm) running from a "DC12V 3A 36.0W" adapter ([manual](https://manuals.plus/arcade1up/light-up-marquee-plus-riser-manual), DOCUMENTED). A retailer's measured spec for the earlier generation: 116.3 cm tall, 49 wide, 58 deep, 36.3 kg, "just shy of 147cm" on the riser; panels "13mm and under in thickness" against 18 mm on higher-spec cabinets; the screen is a "17" 1280x1024 LCD" behind a "1mm soft acrylic panel with the bezel artwork UV printed onto the back"; one "3Watt speaker mounted top right"; no coin mechanism, free play ([Electro Arcade](https://electroarcade.com.au/australian-arcade-1up-gaming-machine-cabinet-specs-and-reviews), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). A reviewer of the same maker's pinball: the "faux coin door is a nice touch and a good place to put the buttons you'll need to interact with menus" but "would be more convincing if it had the fake coin slots to go with the fake coin return"; construction is "a mixture of metal, MDF, and electronics" ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/118385/arcade1up-pinball-cabinet-review-a-great-start/), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). The faux door with menu buttons on it is the single most "home" detail found: a 1980s floor machine has a real door because it takes money.

7. **AtGames Legends Ultimate is the full-height multicade, and its panel is the multi-game giveaway.** AtGames: 29.53 by 21.65 by 66.44 inches; "two joysticks, six action buttons per player, a high-performance trackball, and two spinners" on an "Authentic arcade-style control panel"; "HDMI/USB connectors on the front left of the control panel"; about 10 minutes of assembly ([atgames.net](https://www.atgames.net/arcades/features/), DOCUMENTED). Wikipedia adds the "24-inch, 1080p resolution flat-panel display", that the "chassis is made from heavy MDF", and the two sizes, "46-inch, US$399 compact model and a 66-inch, US$599 full-sized model" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AtGames_Legends_Ultimate), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). No coin door is documented anywhere for it. A panel with two sticks, twelve buttons, a trackball and two spinners exists to play hundreds of different games; no single 1980s machine had that layout (INFERRED).

8. **A homebuilt cabinet is 3/4-inch MDF, T-molding on every exposed edge, vinyl side art from a print shop, and an LED strip behind translucent acrylic for the marquee.** The X-Arcade guide: "about one and a half sheets of 3/4-inch MDF" for a full-size upright of 66 by 25 by 30 inches (bartop 24 by 22 by 20, pedestal 38 by 28 by 24); control panel "8-12 degrees from horizontal for ergonomic play"; a coin door opening of "Standard 10.5" x 10""; "T-molding to all exposed MDF edges"; print "side art and marquee art at a local print shop on adhesive vinyl"; "an LED marquee header using a cheap LED strip behind translucent acrylic"; a "27-inch 1080p IPS monitor" ([X-Arcade](https://shop.xgaming.com/blogs/news/how-to-build-a-mame-arcade-cabinet-with-x-arcade-controllers-complete-2026-guide), DOCUMENTED). A kit maker's cabinet is "CNC precision cut black melamine panels" with "a pre-cut groove for installing t-molding", 28.5 by 41.5 by 73.8 inches, about 180 lb, for a 27-inch monitor ([North Coast Ultimate Arcade II](https://www.northcoastarcades.com/product-page/ultimate-arcade-ii-cabinet), DOCUMENTED, seen only through the search excerpt because the page itself returned 404). A custom builder's four-player MegaCade is 48 by 38 by 72 inches with "RGB rope lighting" as an upgrade ([Extreme Home Arcades](https://www.extremehomearcades.com/), DOCUMENTED). T-molding ships in 1/2 inch through 2 inch widths in "Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Purple, Pink, Black, Gray, White, Brown and Beige, Silver / Chrome, Brass / Gold, Woodgrain"; the vendor's displayed top sellers are 3/4-inch black, white, light blue, bright red, yellow and purple ([t-molding.com](https://www.t-molding.com/), DOCUMENTED). Wikipedia's anatomy for the drawing: the marquee is "a sign above the monitor displaying the game's title. They are often brightly colored and backlit", the bezel is "the border around the monitor. It may contain instructions or artwork", and cabinets are "usually made of wood and metal, about six feet or two meters tall" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_cabinet), COMMUNITY-MEASURED).

9. **What the drawing needs, pulled from items 6 to 8.** The home machine is shorter and narrower than the floor machine: Arcade1Up is about 46 inches bare and 58 on its riser at 19 inches wide, the Legends Ultimate is 66 inches at 29.5 wide, a homebuilt is 66 inches at 25 wide, and Wikipedia's floor machine is about 72 at 28 to 30 wide. Its tells, in rough order of how loudly they say "home": a faux coin door with menu buttons on it, or no door at all; a light-up marquee that names the machine or the brand ("Legends Ultimate", "Pac-Man Deluxe") rather than one title; a flat LCD in a printed acrylic bezel, not a curved CRT behind glass; a visible riser seam a foot above the floor, or a plain black plinth; a multi-game panel (two sticks, six buttons each, trackball, spinners) and a USB or HDMI port on the panel front; black melamine or black-printed MDF with printed side art; T-molding in black or one bright colour along every edge; and a single plug-in power adapter rather than a service panel. A floor machine says the opposite: one title on the marquee, a metal coin door with two mechs and a return cup, a CRT bezel, cigarette-burn wear, and a full-height 28-inch-wide box (INFERRED).

### Motion timing

10. **Heffernan's idle drift is two sines with periods of 79 seconds and 26 minutes, and the pointer lag is two per-frame lerps at 0.05 and 0.025.** The idle keyframe sets `position.x = Math.sin((elapsed + 19000) * 0.00008) * origin.x` and `position.y = Math.sin((elapsed + 1000) * 0.000004) * 4000 + origin.y - 3000`, where `origin` is `(-20000, 12000, 20000)` and `elapsed` is milliseconds since the loading screen finished ([CameraKeyframes.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/Camera/CameraKeyframes.ts), [Time.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/Utils/Time.ts), DOCUMENTED). Worked out: the x sweep has a period of 2π / 0.00008 ms, 78.5 seconds, and an amplitude of 20000 units at a camera radius of about 31000 units from the focal point, so the peak angular speed at the zero crossing is roughly 3 degrees of arc per second and the camera takes 39 seconds to cross from one side to the other; the y sweep has a period of 1571 seconds, 26.2 minutes, and 4000 units of amplitude, a peak of 16 units per second, invisible in practice (INFERRED arithmetic on the documented values). The desk keyframe lags the mouse: `targetFoc.x += (mouse.x - width / 2 - targetFoc.x) * 0.05` and `targetPos.x += (mouse.x - width / 2 - targetPos.x) * 0.025`, with the same factors on y, and neither is multiplied by frame time (DOCUMENTED). At 60 fps a 0.05 lerp reaches 63 percent of the way in 20 frames, a third of a second, and 0.025 in 40 frames, two thirds of a second; at 30 fps both take twice as long in wall-clock time (INFERRED). Tween durations: `transition()` defaults to 1000 ms with `TWEEN.Easing.Quintic.InOut` and is what a `mousedown` uses to toggle idle and desk; `enterMonitor` is 2000 ms with `BezierEasing(0.13, 0.99, 0, 1)`; `leftMonitor` returns to desk at the 1000 ms default; the loading screen hands off to idle over 2500 ms with `Exponential.Out`; entering free-cam is 750 ms and leaving it is 4000 ms `Exponential.Out` ([Camera.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/Camera/Camera.ts), DOCUMENTED). The monitor keyframe's distance is `origin.z + aspect * 1200 - additionalZoom` with `additionalZoom` 600 at 768 px and wider, 0 below; the desk's is `origin.z + aspect * 3000 - 1800` (DOCUMENTED).

11. **Other shipped lag factors sit in the same band: Bruno Simon at 0.1 per frame, a 2026 Codrops case study at 0.08.** Bruno's zoom and camera easing is 0.1 per frame ([Camera.js](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2019/blob/master/src/javascript/Camera.js), DOCUMENTED, read for the earlier [precedent record](whole-site-pixi-precedent.md)). The Codrops piece on 3D transitions: "The `easeFactor` of `0.08` controls how quickly the models catch up to the cursor", applied as `current += easeFactor * (target - current)` each frame ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/03/18/building-seamless-3d-transitions-with-webflow-gsap-and-three-js/), DOCUMENTED). Three shipped sites, three factors between 0.025 and 0.1 per frame, none corrected for frame time (INFERRED).

12. **Neither canvas portfolio handles `prefers-reduced-motion`; the one that does gates the whole animation on a media query.** GitHub code search for `prefers-reduced-motion` and `reducedMotion` returns zero hits in henryjeff/portfolio-website and brunosimon/folio-2019 (INFERRED from my own search). The Codrops study wraps its hover work in `gsap.matchMedia().add('(min-width: 992px) and (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)', ...)`, so under `reduce` "the callback never runs and the buttons behave normally" (Codrops, DOCUMENTED). Apple's own description of the switch: Reduce Motion "decreases motion in the UI like parallax and animations", and "People with motion sensitivity can opt to decrease the amount of movement in screen elements, which can affect animations like parallax, animated images, and screen transitions" ([Apple developer docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accessibility/testing-system-accessibility-features-in-your-app#Adjust-animations-to-reduce-onscreen-motion), DOCUMENTED). Both of the cabinet's planned motions, a camera that lags the pointer and a slow idle drift, are parallax by that definition (INFERRED).

13. **WCAG makes an auto-starting idle drift a Level A obligation: it must be pausable if it runs longer than five seconds beside other content.** Success Criterion 2.2.2: "For any moving, blinking or scrolling information that (1) starts automatically, (2) lasts more than five seconds, and (3) is presented in parallel with other content, there is a mechanism for the user to pause, stop, or hide it unless the movement, blinking, or scrolling is part of an activity where it is essential"; "Five seconds was chosen because it is long enough to get a user's attention, but not so long that a user cannot wait out the distraction if necessary to use the page"; essential means "if removed, would fundamentally change the information or functionality of the content" ([W3C Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html), DOCUMENTED). A reduced-motion preference honoured in code is the cheapest mechanism that satisfies it, and it costs nothing when the drift is already a function of time that can be frozen (INFERRED).

14. **How much is "subtle": degrees, not pixels, and seconds, not milliseconds.** Andy Clarke's ambient-animation article defines the category as "passive movements you might not notice at first. But, they make a design look alive in subtle ways", "they don't demand attention, aren't distracting, and don't interfere with what someone's trying to achieve", and his worked example rotates by three degrees over "6s ease-in-out infinite alternate" and "3s ease-in-out infinite alternate", wrapped so "they only run when they're welcome" under `prefers-reduced-motion` ([Smashing Magazine](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/09/ambient-animations-web-design-principles-implementation/), DOCUMENTED). Cinemachine's handheld guidance: "For frequency, a typical low range is 0.1-0.5 Hz, the mid range 0.8-1.5, and the high 3-4", "Handheld camera operators tend to shake more rotationally than they do positionally", and "Convincing noise profiles typically mix low, medium, and high frequencies together" ([Unity docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@2.10/manual/CinemachineNoiseProfiles.html), DOCUMENTED). Val Head on what hurts: "Animations that move an object across a large amount of space are most apt to trigger a negative response for someone suffering from a vestibular disorder", "Exaggerated parallax and scrolljacking animations are highly likely to be triggering", while "Animation that involves only non-moving properties, like opacity, color, and blurs, are unlikely to be problematic" ([A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/designing-safer-web-animation-for-motion-sensitivity/), DOCUMENTED). Carbon's checklist adds the test: "Is your motion frequently noticed by average users? If so, consider removing or minimizing it", and the warning "on mobile screens, subtle motions may be easily missed" ([Carbon motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/), DOCUMENTED). Heffernan's 79-second sweep is at 0.013 Hz, an order below Cinemachine's "low" band, which is why it reads as the room breathing rather than a handheld camera (INFERRED).

15. **Transition durations for the keyframe moves have a documented ceiling of about a second, and the shipped scenes use one to two.** Material's duration tokens run from 50 ms to 1000 ms with the rule "duration should increase as the area/traversal of an animation increases" ([Material Motion.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md), DOCUMENTED). Carbon's longest token is 700 ms for "Background dimming", its micro-interactions sit at 90 to 120 ms, and "the larger the change in distance (traveled) or size (scaling) of the element, the longer the animation takes" (Carbon, DOCUMENTED). Heffernan's 1000 ms idle-to-desk and 2000 ms desk-to-monitor are full-viewport camera moves and sit just above the design-system ceiling, with the slow-out Bezier `(0.13, 0.99, 0, 1)` front-loading the motion so the last second is settling (item 10, DOCUMENTED; the reading is INFERRED).

### Frame loop policy

16. **PixiJS v8 documents three loop controls and one manual pattern, and the docs themselves name the battery case.** The render-loop guide: "You can pause the loop by stopping the ticker, and resume it later" via `app.ticker.stop()` and `app.ticker.start()`; "To cap the frame rate (useful for reducing battery usage on mobile)" via `app.ticker.maxFPS = 30`; and "For manual rendering without a ticker at all" the pattern is `app.ticker.stop()` then a hand-rolled `requestAnimationFrame` loop calling `app.renderer.render(app.stage)` ([render-loop.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/__docs__/concepts/render-loop.md), DOCUMENTED). The ticker plugin doc: `app.stop()` and `app.start()` are "Useful for: Pausing a game or animation, Throttling on inactive tabs, Managing visibility events", and with `autoStart: false` the loop is `app.ticker.update(); app.render(); requestAnimationFrame(animate)` ([ticker-plugin.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/app/__docs__/ticker-plugin.md), DOCUMENTED). The official skill file restates it: "Use `autoStart: false` plus your own frame driver when you need to pause on tab blur, run a fixed-timestep loop, or render offscreen" ([pixijs-ticker SKILL.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-ticker/SKILL.md), DOCUMENTED). `maxFPS` defaults to 0 (unlimited) and `minFPS` to 10; setting one past the other clamps the other ([ticker.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/ticker/__docs__/ticker.md), DOCUMENTED).

17. **`maxFPS` skips work inside the frame; it does not stop the browser waking the page.** In `Ticker.update`, `maxFPS` becomes `_minElapsedMS = 1 / (fps / 1000)`, and each tick computes `delta = currentTime - _lastFrame` and does `if (delta < this._minElapsedMS) return;` before touching listeners or rendering ([Ticker.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/ticker/Ticker.ts) lines 246 to 251 and 676 to 688, DOCUMENTED). So a 30 fps cap halves GPU and listener work but the `requestAnimationFrame` callback still fires at display rate; only `stop()` cancels the frame request ("Pause and cancel the animation frame", ticker.md, DOCUMENTED). For a scene that is still, the saving from a cap is half of something that should be zero (INFERRED).

18. **The platform vendors say the same thing in their own words: do not touch the canvas when nothing changed.** WebKit: "When the user is not interacting with the page, try to make the page use as little power as possible"; "The GPU is also used for `<canvas>` rendering, both 2D canvas and WebGL/WebGPU"; "don't call into the `<canvas>` APIs if the canvas content isn't changing"; "Minimize the use of timers to avoid waking up the CPU"; and when a page goes inactive, "requestAnimationFrame is stopped" and "CSS and SVG Animations are suspended" ([WebKit blog](https://webkit.org/blog/8970/how-web-content-can-affect-power-usage/), DOCUMENTED). Chrome: "Chrome does not call `requestAnimationFrame()` when a page is in the background. This behavior has been in place since 2011", background timers get a budget after 10 seconds that regenerates at "0.01 seconds per second" ([Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/background_tabs), DOCUMENTED). three.js's manual: "rendering continuously is a waste of the devices power and if the user is on portable device it wastes the user's battery", and "The most obvious way to solve this is to render once at the start and then render only when something changes. Changes include textures or models finally loading, data arriving from some external source, the user adjusting a setting or the camera or giving other relevant input" ([three.js manual](https://threejs.org/manual/#en/rendering-on-demand), DOCUMENTED). Troika, a Three.js layer, ships this as policy: it re-renders only when "The scene is updated", "A facade animation or transition is running", "A facade changes properties" or "A facade indicates that it requires a re-render", because continuous loops cause "unnecessarily high CPU/GPU usage and battery drain for scenes that are largely static" ([Troika docs](https://protectwise.github.io/troika/troika-3d/performance/), DOCUMENTED).

19. **Phones already throttle the loop under you, which makes per-frame lerps a bug and delta-time the rule.** WebKit bug 168837, "[iOS] Throttle requestAnimationFrame to 30fps in low power mode", filed by Chris Dumez on 2017-02-24, "to save battery", resolved fixed 2017-02-28 in r213169 ([WebKit Bugzilla](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=168837), DOCUMENTED). Chrome's Energy Saver "conserves battery power by reducing the display refresh rate, affecting scrolling and animation fidelity and video frame rates", and "If your site uses `requestAnimationFrame()` loops and assumes that exactly 16.67 milliseconds will have elapsed between callbacks, your animations will run twice as slow when Energy Saver mode is enabled" ([Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/memory-and-energy-saver-mode), DOCUMENTED). Heffernan's 0.05 and 0.025 per-frame lerps (item 10) are exactly that assumption, so on a phone in Low Power Mode his desk parallax takes twice as long to settle; the drift sines are time-based and unaffected (INFERRED).

20. **The one measured power dataset found is desktop, and it says the idle baseline is two orders of magnitude below a running WebGL effect.** On an M1 MacBook with `powermetrics`, median of three 10-second runs: an idle empty page draws "roughly 25mW", CSS animations "100mW to 500mW", video "50mW to 150mW", and WebGL effects "200mW - 2000mW" depending on shader complexity ([Torchbox](https://torchbox.com/wagtail-cms-services/blog/how-much-power-do-web-assets-use/), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). A 2026 cross-device benchmark of web animation systems found that at 10 objects "all technologies maintained optimal performance across platforms with negligible CPU/GPU load", that WebGL "consistently delivers the best performance in all conditions", and that mobile browsers "lack reliable instrumentation" for CPU and GPU numbers ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12843483/), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). No phone battery figure for an idle canvas loop was found; the ordering (stopped loop at baseline, capped loop somewhere between, free-running WebGL at the top) is consistent across every source and the vendors' own advice (INFERRED).

21. **The PixiJS community has asked for "render only on change" as a first-class option and it has not shipped.** Issue #5620, "[Feature] Allow for manual rendering, without halting the 'Application' ticker", asks for an `autoRender` option because `autoStart: false` "requires calling the update/render event manually, defeating the purpose of using the ticker"; the workaround in the thread removes the plugin's auto-render and calls `app.renderer.render(app.stage)` conditionally from a ticker callback; the issue is marked stale ([pixijs #5620](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/issues/5620), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). The v8 docs' `autoStart: false` plus `app.render()` pattern (item 16) is the supported route, and the cabinet's own loop becomes: run the ticker only while something is animating (a tween, the drift when reduced-motion is off, a pointer lerp still settling), `app.stop()` when it all comes to rest and on `visibilitychange`, and a single `app.render()` on any input that changes the scene (INFERRED).

## What is thin

Direction D, an olive or moss dark, has only two sampled hints: Kentucky Route Zero's exterior shot at mean `#2f2f2c` (hue 60, saturation 6 percent) and A Short Hike's dark30 `#645f3a` (hue 53) under a pale green highlight. Nothing found makes it a whole scene's dark, and no author write-up mentions it.

The sampling is of store screenshots, not of frames I chose. Unpacking's six shots are mostly daylight, Stardew's twelve include one night exterior and no night interior, and no Stardew interior night tint was chased: interiors use a per-map `ambientLight` set from map data and lamps drawn into the same light map, which is a different path from the `eveningColor` subtraction in item 3.

Unpacking's developers said nothing about colour in any page fetched (the GamesRadar making-of truncated, the GDC design-pillars write-up is about subtractive design, the press kit and the ACMI talk page carry no art notes). Henry Heffernan's page colour is read from his UI stylesheet, not from a screenshot; the 3D scene's baked textures were not sampled.

## The gap

Nobody found has shipped the cozy night interior without brown. Every warm-lit precedent measured brown where the lamp light lands on a mid-value surface, and every cool-dark precedent that avoided brown did so by keeping the darks blue, teal or true black and the warmth in the top decile of values. The cabinet's palette will have to be built from that rule (cool darks at hue 180 to 240, warm light at hue 35 to 45, warm mids under 15 percent saturation) rather than lifted from a game, and then checked by measuring it the same way.

No canvas site read here honours `prefers-reduced-motion`, so there is no precedent for what the cabinet looks like with the drift and the pointer lag off; WCAG 2.2.2 and Apple's definition of Reduce Motion both say the drift and the parallax are the things to freeze, and the frame loop in item 21 already needs a "nothing is moving" state, so the two are one design.

There is no measured battery number for a mostly-still canvas on a phone. The vendor guidance, the iOS 30 fps throttle and the desktop powermetrics run all point the same way, but the actual cost of "ticker running, scene still, 30 fps cap" versus "ticker stopped" on a phone is unmeasured and is worth ten minutes with a phone's battery panel once the scene exists.
