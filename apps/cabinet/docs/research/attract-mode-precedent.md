# Attract mode precedent: the idle screen is a short, silent loop of the game playing itself, and every modern front hands off on a gesture

Research for The Cabinet, ticket #90. Labels: **DOCUMENTED** = a developer statement, an operator manual, official platform documentation, or shipped behaviour transcribed from code or markup. **COMMUNITY-MEASURED** = a wiki, a forum measurement, a press how-to, or a review. **INFERRED** = my reading across sources.

The question is what plays on the cabinet's screen while a visitor stands in front of it, on a site that draws one cabinet holding one game and must read on a phone. Two lineages answer it and they agree. The arcade lineage (Atari's operator manuals, the Space Invaders disassembly, three shmuplations interviews) shipped a continuous loop that starts at power-on, runs the game itself as a demonstration, prints a score table and a coin prompt, and is interrupted only by a coin or a start button. The modern lineage (Apple's App Preview documentation, the Steamworks trailer page, itch.io's creator docs, the markup of shipped store pages and official game sites) converged on a muted, looping, poster-backed video that starts by itself and a play action that needs a tap. Browser policy, read from Chrome, WebKit, Firefox and MDN, is why: muted autoplay is always allowed, sound needs a gesture, and iOS Low Power Mode blocks even silent video.

The strongest sources are Konami's Utata Kiyoshi in 1988 naming the standard loop as "title screen --> gameplay clip --> high score ranking" and calling it a mini-tutorial, the Space Invaders disassembly showing the demo is a live simulation fed by an eleven-byte command table with the sound off, a 2014 forum measurement of a full Galaga attract cycle at 1:31, Apple's statement that App Store previews "autoplay" and "play with the sound muted" by default, itch.io's statement that HTML5 games "require a click to start" so the page does not slow the viewer's browser, and Masahiro Sakurai's account of a title screen that "just appeared suddenly" being judged "pretty weak, in terms of grabbing people's attention."

The gap: no source in this pass measures whether a self-playing demo, a trailer, or a still with motion draws more starts, and no shipped precedent exists for an attract loop drawn inside a drawn cabinet on a phone that the visitor zooms into.

## What is solid

These are the claims I would build on.

1. **An arcade attract mode is a continuous state that begins at power-on and ends only on a coin or a start press.** Atari's Asteroids Deluxe manual (TW-165) states "The attract mode begins when power is applied to the game" and runs until a start button is pressed, and the Sky Raider manual describes the machine as three states, "attract (power applied), ready-to-play (coins accepted), and play (START button activated)." DOCUMENTED, [Asteroids Deluxe manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/3987701/Atari-Asteroids-Deluxe.html), [Asteroids manual TM-143](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/2500236/Atari-Asteroids.html), [Sky Raider manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/4192774/Atari-Sky-Raider.html). Attract is not a screen, it is the machine's resting state, and the coin moves it to a second resting state before play begins. The handoff has two steps.

2. **The 1978 Space Invaders loop is fully transcribed: a title message, a printed score table, a live demo with the sound off, a coin prompt, and a gag that fires every other pass.** The disassembly's comments show `OneSecDelay` ("Delay of 64 (tad over 1 sec)"), `DrawAdvTable` ("SCORE ADVANCE TABLE" printed with a "delay between letters"), `TwoSecDelay`, a splash animation, then "Play demo", then "INSERT COIN", then "Print credit information and do splash." The demo is a simulation: "In demo mode the player always fires, and when the player shot is removed from play a new demo command is read from an 11 byte table. The change of direction in the demo thus depends on the shot exploding, which is somewhat non periodic." The demo loop runs "without sound." The upside-down Y that an alien drags off and replaces, and the extra C in "INSERT CCOIN" that it shoots, "happen every-other pass through the attract mode and not on the first pass." DOCUMENTED (shipped code with the annotator's comments), [Computer Archeology, Space Invaders](https://www.computerarcheology.com/Arcade/SpaceInvaders/) and its [annotated code](https://www.computerarcheology.com/Arcade/SpaceInvaders/Code.html). The first attract mode was already a live game wrapped in one and two second holds, with its humour rationed for the second viewing.

3. **The demo in the classic lineage is the game running itself or replaying a developer's inputs, never a video.** Pac-Man's demo is the real game under the real difficulty setting: "You can determine the difficulty setting of a machine by observing which ghost kills Pac-Man during the attract mode demo game. In normal difficulty, Pac-Man gets captured by Inky in the lower-left area of the maze. If the difficulty jumper has been connected, however, he is captured by Clyde near the same location." DOCUMENTED (the Dossier transcribing shipped behaviour), [The Pac-Man Dossier](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-pac-man-dossier). Galaga's demo reads player input through a bug: "The arcade game Galaga had an attract mode that, due to a bug, you could actually control." COMMUNITY-MEASURED, [Museum of the Game forums](https://forums.arcade-museum.com/threads/galaga-attract-mode.303394/). Revenge of Shinobi replays recorded inputs: "the four demo playthroughs you see at the title screen? Those were all recorded by me." DOCUMENTED, Ohba, [shmuplations](https://shmuplations.com/shinobi/). Each is the engine on its own screen, which is why a demo can leak the difficulty jumper and a bug can make it playable.

4. **By 1988 the loop had standardised, and a Konami designer named it and called it a tutorial.** Utata Kiyoshi, on Super Contra: "if you look at the demo attract screens for most recent arcade games, almost all of them use the following loop: title screen --> gameplay clip --> high score ranking. It's essentially just a mini-tutorial for the game. Originally, however, arcade games were all about being flashy and grabbing the player's attention." Super Contra's "attract demo features various dramatic scenes from the game, more akin a movie trailer." DOCUMENTED, [shmuplations, Short Cuts](https://shmuplations.com/shortcuts/). Cave's Junya Inoue calls Air Gallet's "the massive opening attract demo (which was basically an indulgence)", locked in before the stages worked. DOCUMENTED, [shmuplations, Esprade](https://shmuplations.com/esprade/). Two arcade developers a decade apart treat the attract demo as a large discretionary production item.

5. **A full cycle runs about a minute and a half, and the holds inside it run one to two seconds.** The only whole-cycle measurement found is Galaga: "Attract mode running time 1:31" for the original board and "1:32" for the Class of 1981 re-release, each ending when the demo ship dies. COMMUNITY-MEASURED, swm3rd, 2014-02-14, [Classic Arcade Gaming forums](https://www.classicarcadegaming.com/forums/index.php?topic=5783.0). Space Invaders' per-screen delays are the constants in item 2 and its demo length is data dependent because it ends when the demo player is hit. DOCUMENTED. One stopwatch on one game: an order of magnitude, not a spec.

6. **Attract sound is an operator setting, and in the classic era it was usually absent or off.** MAME's core defines the standard DIP label `"Demo Sounds"` for every driver that exposes it (DOCUMENTED, [mame/src/emu/ioport.cpp](https://github.com/mamedev/mame/blob/master/src/emu/ioport.cpp)); the Neo Geo MVS service manual lists soft-DIPs "DEMO SOUND" and "HOW TO PLAY" (DOCUMENTED, [MV1FZ manual](https://archive.org/stream/arcademanual_neogeo_mv1fz_manual/neogeo_mv1fz_manual_djvu.txt)); Raw Thrills' Fast and Furious manual has "Attract Volume: Adjust the volume during the attract mode sequences (from 0 to 20)" and an "Attract Music" frequency (DOCUMENTED, [ManualsLib](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/2902844/Raw-Thrills-Fast-And-Furious-Arcade.html)); the Injustice Arcade manual offers attract sound as On, Off, or Sometimes (DOCUMENTED, read from the [Manualzz listing](https://manualzz.com/doc/73508724/raw-thrills-injustice-arcade-operator-s-manual) summary rather than the page). Collectors: "Donkey kong does not have attract mode sounds. Actually alot of games of that era did not have attract mode sounds," with Berzerk and Wizard of Wor the named 1980 exceptions. COMMUNITY-MEASURED, [Donkey Kong thread, 2007](https://forums.arcade-museum.com/threads/donkey-kong-attract-sounds-off.28719/), [Pac-Man thread, 2015](https://forums.arcade-museum.com/threads/why-doesnt-pacman-have-sound-in-attract-mode.350400/). The shipped norm is a silent loop with sound as an opt-in, which is what browsers permit (item 14).

7. **The handoff is a blinking coin prompt the loop returns to, and the coin does not start the game.** Space Invaders prints "INSERT COIN" after the demo and, once credited, prints the credit count and goes back into the splash loop (item 2). Atari puts ready-to-play between coin and play (item 1). The Neo Geo BIOS adds an optional "HOW TO PLAY" screen (item 6). TV Tropes: "Arcade games with an attract mode will almost always have blinking text on the screen telling the player to 'Insert Coin'." COMMUNITY-MEASURED, [TV Tropes, Attract Mode](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AttractMode). Two beats: an ambient prompt that never leaves, then a credited state where the loop keeps running and only the prompt changes.

8. **Console and handheld title screens inherited the loop and then dropped it, and one designer said why.** Sakurai on Kirby: Nightmare in Dream Land: "During development the title screen just appeared suddenly, and everyone thought that was pretty weak, in terms of grabbing people's attention." And: "it also didn't make much sense to do a traditional gameplay demo, like the kind you would make to attract customers at an arcade. GBA games aren't often displayed in storefronts either. But still, we wanted something with some impact, and something that wouldn't use much memory." The answer was the Kirby rush, a sprite flood. DOCUMENTED, [shmuplations, Kirby](https://shmuplations.com/kirbynightmare/). TV Tropes records the retail arc: attract modes "were also common in console games for a few decades, to persuade customers in retail stores," and now "demo stations at stores will opt to just play the trailers for these games instead, while the few games that still have attract modes usually just play the opening cutscene if left idle." COMMUNITY-MEASURED, same page. Once the screen leaves the shop floor the demo loses its reason and a trailer or cutscene takes its place.

9. **A modern mobile title screen that keeps the arcade idea runs the game's world under the menu and fades the menu out.** Alto's Odyssey: "Team Alto has also made the title screen into its own zen background scene, fading away the on-screen menu and the game's title to give a better view of the landscape, accompanied by the music, animal chirps, and the occasional period of rain." DOCUMENTED (a reviewer transcribing shipped behaviour), [AppleInsider, 2018-02-26](https://appleinsider.com/articles/18/02/26/review-altos-odyssey-for-ios-heads-down-the-mountain-as-an-excellent-sequel-to-altos-adventure). Alto's site uses the web equivalent, a `<video muted autoplay loop poster="...">` hero. DOCUMENTED (markup fetched 2026-09-07), [altosadventure.com](https://altosadventure.com/). This is the closest shipped attract mode on a phone found in this pass: the live scene is the idle state, the UI recedes, a swipe starts play.

10. **Every console store front for a single title is a muted autoplaying video over key art, with a system switch that turns it back into a still.** Apple: "App previews display and autoplay on your product page and within search results," "By default, app previews play with the sound muted, so consider using copy to add additional context to your footage," up to three previews of up to 30 seconds, and "If someone has turned off video autoplay in their App Store settings, a poster frame will display." DOCUMENTED, [Apple, App Previews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/). The iPhone switch is Settings > Accessibility > Motion: "Auto-Play Video Previews: Turn off. (When on, it allows apps such as the App Store to automatically play video previews.)" DOCUMENTED, [iPhone User Guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/iphone/iph0b691d3ed/ios). On Apple TV, resting the remote on the Arcade app plays "a video for a game from the Apple Arcade app." COMMUNITY-MEASURED, [iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2019/10/03/disable-auto-play-video-previews-apple-tv/). Xbox One store pages autoplay the game's promotional video, and with "Play videos automatically" off the game's artwork sits in the background instead. COMMUNITY-MEASURED (press how-tos, not fetchable in full), [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/xbox-one-microsoft-store-autoplay-disable), [TekRevue](https://www.tekrevue.com/tip/xbox-store-autoplay-videos/). Switch 2 eShop, 2025: "the autoplay trailer includes the sound. I nearly jumped off my couch the first time I left my cursor on a game," listed as a con, "Autoplaying of trailers WITH SOUND happens way too fast." COMMUNITY-MEASURED, [Nintendo World Report, 2025-06-09](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/71350/nintendo-eshop-switch-2-review). PS5: Sony documents per-game hubs with Activities "displayed via on-screen cards" (DOCUMENTED, [PlayStation Blog, 2020-10-15](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/10/15/first-look-playstation-5s-next-generation-user-experience/)); that highlighting a tile swaps the whole background to that game's art is press-described only (COMMUNITY-MEASURED, [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/features/ps5s-welcome-hub-everything-you-need-to-know)). The one front that autoplays with sound is the one whose reviewer flinched.

11. **Steam's guidance assumes a viewer with no audio and under ten seconds of attention, and its page markup is the muted loop with a poster.** Steamworks: "The first two valid trailers will be shown to players before any screenshots," "you may have less than 10 seconds to make an impression," "the customer may be watching your trailer without any audio," "the first trailer you list on your store page is primarily gameplay, showing the player what they will be doing in the game," plus an auto-generated "poster image of 600px x 380px that displays in the video player until the video has loaded enough to start playing." DOCUMENTED, [Steamworks, Trailers](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/trailer). Balatro's page carries six description videos as `<video class="bb_img" autoplay muted loop playsinline poster="...">`. DOCUMENTED (markup fetched 2026-09-07), [Balatro on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2379780/Balatro/); the main trailer player is script-rendered and unreadable from static HTML. Community threads describe an "Autoplay Videos" toggle under the player's cog and say "the volume is auto-muted whenever Auto-Play is enabled." COMMUNITY-MEASURED, [Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/SteamClientBeta/discussions/0/573770913622995278/).

12. **itch.io documents why a browser game should not start by itself, and forces click-to-launch on phones.** "By default your project will require a click to start. This will ensure your game doesn't slow the viewer's browser when the itch.io page initially loads." On mobile: "Click to launch in fullscreen mode regardless of how you've configured your embed for desktop computers." And: "Keep in mind audio may be muted on some browsers when using auto-start." DOCUMENTED, [itch.io, HTML5 games](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5). The 2018 note frames click-to-play as "Give players a chance to read about your game before they dive in." DOCUMENTED, [itch.io update, 2018-01-22](https://itch.io/updates/more-embed-options-for-html5-games). The cover "could be an animated GIF" (DOCUMENTED, [Designing your page](https://itch.io/docs/creators/design)) and cover GIFs play on hover, not by themselves (COMMUNITY-MEASURED, [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/8558324)). A still that moves on hover, a trailer on click, a game on click.

13. **Official single-game sites split two ways, and both are gesture-gated for sound.** Cuphead's hero is `<video id="trailer-video" autoplay muted playsinline loop data-video="...Cuphead_web.mp4">`; Alto's is the same shape (item 9). Balatro, Celeste and Hollow Knight: Silksong embed a YouTube player that plays on click. DOCUMENTED (markup fetched 2026-09-07), [cupheadgame.com](https://cupheadgame.com/), [playbalatro.com](https://www.playbalatro.com/), [celestegame.com](https://www.celestegame.com/), [hollowknightsilksong.com](https://www.hollowknightsilksong.com/). None of the six sites checked runs the game, or a canvas simulation, as the hero.

14. **Browser autoplay policy is settled and identical in shape across engines: muted or silent media start by themselves, anything audible needs a gesture, and the app must read the play() promise.** Chrome, 2017: "Muted autoplay is always allowed"; sound only after "The user has interacted with the domain (click, tap, etc.)", after the desktop Media Engagement Index threshold, or once installed to the home screen; Web Audio contexts made before a gesture start suspended and need `resume()`. DOCUMENTED, [Chrome, Autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay). WebKit iOS 10, 2016: "<video muted> elements will also be allowed to autoplay without a user gesture," "<video autoplay> elements will only begin playing when visible on-screen," `playsinline` keeps playback out of fullscreen, and "If a <video> element gains an audio track or becomes un-muted without a user gesture, playback will pause." DOCUMENTED, [WebKit, New video policies for iOS](https://webkit.org/blog/6784/new-video-policies-for-ios/). Safari macOS, 2017, says the same and adds that a silent audio track does not count as muted. DOCUMENTED, [WebKit, macOS auto-play](https://webkit.org/blog/7734/auto-play-policy-changes-for-macos/). Firefox 66, 2019-03-19: "Firefox will block audible audio and video by default," "Muted autoplay is still allowed," and a blocked play() rejects with `NotAllowedError`. DOCUMENTED, [Mozilla Hacks](https://hacks.mozilla.org/2019/02/firefox-66-to-block-automatically-playing-audible-video-and-audio/). MDN's cross-browser summary and `navigator.getAutoplayPolicy()` example: [MDN, Autoplay guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay). DOCUMENTED.

15. **iOS Low Power Mode blocks even silent autoplay, by design.** WebKit bug 219889, "Unable to hide native video controls when iOS has 'Low Power Mode' enabled and video is set to autoplay," is RESOLVED WONTFIX; Eric Carlson's 2021-08-20 comment says autoplay is disabled in Low Power Mode to preserve battery and the play button is shown deliberately, citing the earlier "[iOS] Disable autoplay of silent videos in low power mode." DOCUMENTED, [bugs.webkit.org](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=219889). A muted `<video>` attract is not guaranteed on a phone.

16. **A canvas animation is outside autoplay policy but inside two other constraints: it pauses in background tabs and it falls under WCAG's five-second rule.** "requestAnimationFrame() calls are paused in most browsers when running in background tabs or hidden <iframe>s, in order to improve performance and battery life." DOCUMENTED, [MDN, requestAnimationFrame](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/requestAnimationFrame). WCAG 2.2.2: "For any moving, blinking or scrolling information that (1) starts automatically, (2) lasts more than five seconds, and (3) is presented in parallel with other content, there is a mechanism for the user to pause, stop, or hide it unless the movement, blinking, or scrolling is part of an activity where it is essential," with animation that is the only content on the page exempt. DOCUMENTED, [W3C, Understanding 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html). INFERRED: the iOS "Auto-Play Video Previews" switch (item 10) is an app-level preference a PixiJS scene never sees, so a canvas attract runs where a `<video>` attract would be stopped, and `prefers-reduced-motion` is the only signal it gets.

## What is thin

**No developer statement on cycle length or on why the loop is ordered title, demo, ranking.** Utata names the order and calls it a tutorial; nobody found says how long a pass should be. The Galaga 1:31 is one collector's stopwatch on one game.

**No Nishikado or Iwatani statement about attract mode was found.** The Space Invaders and Pac-Man evidence is shipped behaviour read from code and from the Dossier, strong for what the games do and silent on intent.

**The console fronts are mostly press-described.** Sony's 2020 post documents hubs and Activities cards, but the whole-background swap on highlight comes from Push Square, and the PS5 user guide URL that would settle it now redirects to a generic help page. That the original Switch HOME menu shows still icons is not stated in any Nintendo page read; treat it as INFERRED. Steam's client-side autoplay and auto-mute are community-described, and the Xbox behaviour is press how-to only, for the Xbox One generation.

**The Injustice Arcade "On / Off / Sometimes" setting is quoted from a manual listing summary**, not the manual page.

**Mobile title-screen precedent is one review of one game.** Nothing equivalent was found for Crossy Road, Downwell, or any shmup on a phone.

## The gap

Nothing found compares what the idle screen is made of against what it does. No operator, platform or developer measures whether a live demo, a recorded run, a trailer, or a still with motion draws more starts. Steam's "less than 10 seconds" and Apple's "30 seconds" are budgets, not results.

No source addresses an attract loop at phone size. The arcade lineage assumed a 19-inch monitor at eye level and a passer-by; the store lineage assumed a tile in a grid; the game-site lineage assumed a desktop hero. The cabinet's screen is a drawn rectangle inside a drawn cabinet on a phone, seen from a distance until the visitor zooms, and nothing shipped matches that.

No source describes a handoff where the coin is a zoom. Every precedent hands off on a coin, a button, a play glyph, or a swipe. The cabinet's framing has the visitor tap the cabinet to zoom into the screen and then interact with the screen to open the game, which is Atari's attract, ready-to-play, play sequence with a camera move as the first step. That mapping is plausible and, as far as this pass found, unprecedented.

Web Audio inside the attract loop is unresolved: the policies say a gesture is required and say nothing about whether the first tap on the cabinet is the right place to spend it.

## What this suggests for the cabinet

INFERRED throughout. Run the game, not a video: every classic attract is the engine on its own screen, the demo can be a live simulation or a recorded input stream, and a canvas loop dodges the autoplay and Low Power Mode blocks that stop a `<video>` on iOS.
Build the pass as Utata's loop with the arcade's holds: a title beat, a short self-playing demo that ends on the demo player's death, a score or how-to-play beat, and a persistent prompt, at roughly a minute to a minute and a half per pass with one to two second holds between beats.
Keep it silent by default and treat sound as the operator's "Demo Sounds" switch, earned by the first tap.
Treat the zoom as the coin: the loop keeps running after the tap and only the prompt changes from attract to start, matching Atari's ready-to-play state.
Ration the surprise the way Space Invaders did, with at least one thing that happens only on the second pass.
Honour reduced motion and the five-second rule with a way to hold the loop, and expect the screen to freeze in a background tab.
