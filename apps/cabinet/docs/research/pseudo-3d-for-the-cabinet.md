# Pseudo-3D for the cabinet: pre-render the body as a short yaw turntable, put the live monitor on a PerspectiveMesh whose corners ride along with the frame, keep the room as Z-multiplied parallax layers, and skip lighting libraries, because pixi3d and pixi-lights both stop at PixiJS v7 and every technique worth keeping renders on demand

Research for The Cabinet, following the standing-in-front craft record ([craft-values-standing-in-front.md](craft-values-standing-in-front.md)) and the whole-site precedent record ([whole-site-pixi-precedent.md](whole-site-pixi-precedent.md)). Labels: **DOCUMENTED** = an author's own statement, public source code, official docs, a vendor's own page, or a measurement the source itself made. **COMMUNITY-MEASURED** = a third-party review, issue thread, or benchmark with a measured or reproduced result. **INFERRED** = my reading across sources, including my own reading of code and my own arithmetic.

The brief is in [apps/cabinet/PRODUCT.md](../../PRODUCT.md): the site is the machine, standing in front comes first, leaning in is the only door, and the anti-reference is a product shot floating clean on nothing. Mark's steer: flat Pixi shapes may not be the right solution, he wants it "a little bit more 3D-ish", not photoreal, so it feels like a real entry to a game. The frame loop policy is already decided: run the ticker only while something animates, stop at rest and on `visibilitychange`, one render per input. Words here follow [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md): cabinet, monitor, marquee, standing in front, leaning in.

The installed engine is `pixi.js` 8.19.0 (`apps/cabinet/node_modules/pixi.js/package.json`, with `^8.8.1` in [package.json](../../package.json)). Every Pixi source claim below was checked against that installed copy as well as the `dev` branch docs.

Tooling note: Exa was not available. Docs came through Ref and direct fetches, GitHub issue threads through `gh`. CodePen returned a Cloudflare block on every route including its oEmbed endpoint, Medium blocked both fetch routes, and the freefrontend "Pure CSS Retro Arcade Machine" page returned 403; those items are labelled as unreached below. No Pixi-built precedent of a single stylised object as a 2.5D entry point was found at all; every shipped analogue is Three.js, CSS 3D, or an image sequence on a 2D canvas.

## Recommendation

The stack, in the order I would build it, each line with what it buys and what it costs on a phone.

1. **Room as flat layers with Z-as-multiplier parallax and a lagging camera.** Already the plan; nothing here changes it. Buys the depth of the room around the cabinet for the price of a handful of sprites in one batch. Costs nothing new. Every layer is a `Sprite`, and Pixi batches sprites with up to 16 textures per draw call (item 21).

2. **Cabinet body as a pre-rendered yaw turntable, rendered from a 3D model at small angular steps across the narrow range the camera can reach, and picked by frame index from the camera yaw.** Buys the one thing no in-engine 2D trick gives: the silhouette changes, the side panel comes into view, the marquee box and control panel show their thickness, and the ambient occlusion under the panel and behind the coin door is baked in by a real renderer (items 1 to 4, 17). Costs texture memory, which is the phone's real constraint: at 512 by 1024 device pixels a frame is 2 MiB as RGBA8, 13 frames at 2 degree steps are 26 MiB, 25 frames at 1 degree steps are 50 MiB, and KTX2 or Basis compression through Pixi's own loaders cuts that four to eight times (items 5, 6, 22). Ship a half-resolution variant for small screens through AssetPack's `resolutions` option (item 6). It also costs a one-off asset pipeline: a Blender model of the cabinet and a render script, which is the same work a "3D-ish" look would need under any technique.

3. **Monitor as a `PerspectiveMesh` whose four corners come from a table exported with the turntable, one row per frame.** Buys a live monitor at every yaw: the attract loop, the hint, and the game select are Pixi content drawn into a quad that sits exactly where the render put the monitor's glass. Costs one mesh; at the default 10 by 10 grid it has exactly 100 vertices and Pixi still batches it with the sprites around it, at 20 by 20 it becomes its own draw call (items 7 to 9). The same table can carry the marquee if the marquee is ever animated.

4. **Light spill and a floor reflection as sprites.** Marquee and monitor glow as `blendMode: 'add'` sprites grouped together so they cost one extra draw call rather than one per sprite, and the floor reflection as the current turntable frame drawn again with `scale.y` negative, low alpha, and a fade (items 16, 18, 21). Drop shadows and contact darkening live in the frames and in the room art, not in filters (item 19).

5. **Skip, with reasons.** Normal-map lighting: `pixi-lights` peers on PixiJS 7 and `@pixi/layers`, last published July 2023, no v8 work in its tracker; a custom `Shader.from` with a normal texture is feasible and cheap per pixel, but the marquee and monitor are fixed lights on a fixed cabinet, so the lighting bakes into the turntable frames for free and a normal map would have to exist per frame as well, doubling memory (items 10 to 13). `pixi3d`: v5 to v7 only, the maintainer has said three times he has no time for v8, and Pixi's own 3D is announced but not in 8.19.0 (items 14, 15). Depth-map parallax shaders: the one shipped example found runs per pixel every frame and its own write-up calls it "Highly GPU-intensive" (item 27). Mixing in Three.js for one object: supported by Pixi's guide, but it means a second renderer, separate texture memory, and no shared assets (item 15).

**Fallback if the 3D model pipeline is unwanted.** Build the cabinet from five or six `PerspectiveMesh` faces (front, one side visible per yaw, marquee box, control panel, plinth) re-projected each input from a fake pinhole camera. It is a few lines of arithmetic and renders on demand, but each face is a flat plane, relief within a face does not move, draw order is painter's-sorted by hand, and the doc is explicit that this is "not a full 3D mesh" (items 7 to 9). It reads as a cardboard cabinet; the turntable reads as a cabinet.

**Frame loop.** Everything in the stack is a pure function of the camera yaw and the pointer, so it renders once per input and stops. The only things that force a running loop are on the monitor: a video texture re-uploads every render and an `AnimatedSprite` drives itself from `Ticker.shared` unless told otherwise (items 24, 25). That is already the attract-mode question, not a new one.

## What is solid

### Pre-rendered turntable frames

1. **Frame counts in shipped spin viewers cluster at 24, 36 and 72 for a full 360 degrees, which is 15, 10 and 5 degrees per frame.** Sirv: "In general, we recommend 36 images. The rotation feels smooth and the images download at an acceptable speed", with 24 images at 336 KB, 36 at 505 KB, 72 at 1.1 MB in their example, and "some Sirv users create ultra-smooth spins from 3D renderings with 200+ images" ([Sirv help](https://sirv.com/help/articles/images-for-a-360-spin/), DOCUMENTED). Their 3D-model page: "Once your 3D model has been constructed in 3D modelling software (such as Blender, SketchUp, DAZ 3D or Rhinoceros), a set of JPEG or PNG images can be exported and uploaded", "A Sirv animation can have up to 500 frames", and keep it "below 100 frames so that users don't wait too long" ([Sirv 3D demo](https://sirv.com/demos/360-spin-3d-model/), DOCUMENTED). CloudPano's Spin API: "The current default is 96 frames, with supported values from 12 through 120", and sprite sheets exist so "the browser can download a much smaller number of mosaic files containing those images arranged in a grid" and "A low-resolution mosaic can help make a spin usable before all detailed assets are loaded" ([CloudPano](https://www.cloudpano.com/blog/how-the-spin-api-delivers-360-spins-with-sprite-sheets-and-frame-arrays), DOCUMENTED). Naver's egjs SpinViewer takes a "Sprite image of (n x m) Grid style" with `rowCount: 24` in its example ([egjs-view360](https://naver.github.io/egjs-view360/v3/spinviewer.html), DOCUMENTED). The cabinet needs a 24 degree arc, not 360, so the same per-frame smoothness (10 degrees per frame reads as smooth to Sirv's customers) costs 3 frames, and 1 degree per frame, far finer than any of these products ship, costs 25 (INFERRED).

2. **Apple's product pages are the largest shipped image-sequence-on-canvas precedent, and they drop to a single image on slow connections.** CSS-Tricks' teardown of the AirPods Pro page: 148 frames drawn with `context.drawImage(img, 0, 0)` from a preloaded `Image` array, frames around 31 KB each, and on a throttled connection Apple loads "8 out of 111 requests", "loading a single fallback image instead of the entire image sequence" ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/lets-make-one-of-those-fancy-scrolling-animations-used-on-apple-product-pages/), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). The lesson for the cabinet is the fallback, not the count: the first paint is one frame, and the rest of the arc arrives behind it (INFERRED).

3. **Pre-rendering is the oldest trick for making a low-power machine show a high-end model, and its named cost is the fixed viewpoint.** Wikipedia: "pre-rendering or offline rendering is the technique of rendering images before playing them back at a later time"; its advantage is the "ability to use graphic models that are more complex and computationally intensive than those that can be rendered in real-time"; its disadvantage is "a generally lower level of interactivity" and "a game with pre-rendered backgrounds is forced to use fixed camera angles"; Donkey Kong Country (1994) and Myst (1993) are the canonical examples ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-rendering), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). The cabinet's camera is already constrained to a narrow arc by design, so the disadvantage is one the product has already accepted (INFERRED).

4. **Baking is a first-class renderer feature, and the Blender manual names the exact passes the cabinet wants.** Cycles baking exists for "Baking ambient occlusion or procedural textures" and "Creating light maps to provide global illumination or speed up rendering in games"; the bake types include "Combined: Bakes all materials, textures, and lighting except specularity", "Ambient Occlusion: Bakes ambient occlusion as specified in the World panels. Ignores all lights in the scene", "Shadow: Bakes shadows and lighting", and "Emit: Bakes Emission, or the Glow color of a material"; and "Use Render Bake in intensive light/shadow solutions, such as AO or soft shadows from area lights" ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/baking.html), DOCUMENTED). For a turntable the bake is not even needed as a texture: the frames are the render, so the AO, the soft shadow of the panel overhang and the marquee's emissive glow on the cabinet top all come out in the pixels (INFERRED). Ambient occlusion itself: "a shading and rendering technique used to calculate how exposed each point in a scene is to ambient lighting", producing "a diffuse, non-directional shading effect that casts no clear shadows, but that darkens enclosed and sheltered areas" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_occlusion), COMMUNITY-MEASURED); it is the single cue that says "this box has thickness" and it is what flat Pixi shapes lack (INFERRED).

5. **Texture memory is the phone cost, and the arithmetic is unforgiving before compression.** An RGBA8 texture is four bytes per pixel; a 512 by 1024 frame is 2 MiB, 13 frames are 26 MiB, 25 frames are 50 MiB, and a 4096 by 4096 sheet holding 32 such frames is 64 MiB (INFERRED arithmetic). MDN: "MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE: 4096" is the floor "effectively all systems support", "Your desktop may support 16k textures ... but most other systems don't", and "WebGL doesn't offer APIs to query the maximum amount of video memory on the system", so applications should keep a "per-pixel VRAM budget" ([MDN WebGL best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices), DOCUMENTED). webgl2fundamentals: "99% of devices support 4096 but only 50% support > 4096" as of 2020 ([webgl2fundamentals](https://webgl2fundamentals.org/webgl/lessons/webgl-cross-platform-issues.html), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). Pixi's own warning on the same number: a container "too large (e.g., over 4096x4096 pixels)" may fail to cache as a texture "depending on GPU limitations" ([cache-as-texture](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/docs/guides/components/scene-objects/container/cache-as-texture.md), DOCUMENTED). On iOS specifically, the "Total canvas memory use exceeds the maximum limit (224 MB)" error arrived with iOS 12 for canvases ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/112218), COMMUNITY-MEASURED), and WebKit bug 219780 records a page killed when WebGL memory leaked from under 300 MB to 1.25 GB, fixed in iOS 14.3 ([WebKit Bugzilla](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=219780), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). 50 MiB of frames is inside those numbers but is a real share of them, which is why the next item matters (INFERRED).

6. **Pixi v8 ships compressed-texture loaders, and AssetPack builds the variants; that is the lever that makes a turntable affordable on a phone.** "Compressed textures significantly reduce memory usage and GPU upload time, especially on mobile devices or lower-end hardware"; the formats are DDS, KTX, KTX2 ("Modern container supporting Basis Universal and Supercompressed formats") and Basis; the loaders are opt-in imports (`import 'pixi.js/ktx2'`) that "must run before any call to `Assets.load`"; and a manifest entry may list `["assets/bg.ktx2", "assets/bg.basis", "assets/bg.png"]` so Pixi loads the best supported variant ([compressed textures guide](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/docs/guides/components/assets/compressed-textures.md), DOCUMENTED). The installed package has `compressed-textures/{basis,dds,ktx,ktx2}` (inspection of `node_modules/pixi.js/lib`, DOCUMENTED). MDN on why: "GPU compressed texture formats are smaller on in GPU memory, and are faster to sample from. (This reduces texture memory bandwidth, which is precious on mobile)", every system has one of S3TC, ETC or PVRTC, WebGL 2 gets "universal support by combining" S3TC and ETC, and Basis Universal "offers a way to support all common compressed texture formats with a single compressed texture file" (MDN, DOCUMENTED). AssetPack's texture packer: "The maximum size a sprite sheet can be before its split out. Defaults to `4096`", and `resolutions` "Defaults to `{ default: 1, low: 0.5 }`" ([AssetPack texture-packer](https://pixijs.io/assetpack/docs/guide/pipes/texture-packer), DOCUMENTED); Pixi's performance tips add "Add the extension `@0.5x.png` to the 50% scale-down spritesheet so PixiJS will visually-double them automatically" ([performance tips](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/__docs__/concepts/performance-tips.md), DOCUMENTED). The cabinet's own `assets` script already runs AssetPack ([package.json](../../package.json), DOCUMENTED). Jesse Zhou's ramen shop ships its baked textures exactly this way: `ramenShopBaked1024.ktx2`, `machinesBaked1024.ktx2`, `floorBaked1024.ktx2`, `miscBaked1024.ktx2`, `graphicsBaked512.ktx2` ([sources.js](https://github.com/enderh3art/Ramen-Shop/blob/main/src/Experience/sources.js), DOCUMENTED).

### PerspectiveMesh

7. **`PerspectiveMesh` is a subdivided plane whose grid vertices are moved to their projected positions by a 3 by 3 homography; the doc says it is "not a full 3D mesh".** The class doc: "A perspective mesh that allows you to draw a 2d plane with perspective. Where ever you move the corners the texture will be projected to look like it is in 3d space", "The calculations is done at the uv level. This means that the more vertices you have the more smooth the perspective will be. If you have a low amount of vertices you may see the texture stretch", and "[!IMPORTANT] This is not a full 3D mesh, it is a 2D mesh with a perspective projection applied to it" ([PerspectiveMesh.d.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/scene/mesh-perspective/PerspectiveMesh.ts), read from the installed 8.19.0, DOCUMENTED). The skill reference: "There is no Z axis, no depth buffer, and no camera. For real 3D, use a full WebGL/WebGPU library on top of PixiJS, or drive vertex positions through a manual transform", "Corners must be listed clockwise from the top-left", and non-convex corners "produce visual artifacts" ([mesh-perspective.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-scene-mesh/references/mesh-perspective.md), DOCUMENTED). The guide page: "A special subclass of `MeshPlane` that applies perspective correction by transforming the UVs" ([mesh guide](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/docs/guides/components/scene-objects/mesh/index.md), DOCUMENTED). Reading the installed code: `setCorners` stores eight numbers and calls `updateProjection`, which runs `compute2DProjection` (unit square to the four corners, a 3 by 3 matrix via adjugates) and then `applyProjectiveTransformationToPlane`, which walks every grid vertex, computes `newX = a00*x + a01*y + a02`, `newY = ...`, `w = a20*x + a21*y + a22`, writes `newX / w, newY / w` into `geometry.buffers[0]` and calls `buffer.update()` ([PerspectivePlaneGeometry.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/scene/mesh-perspective/PerspectivePlaneGeometry.ts), [applyProjectiveTransformationToPlane.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/scene/mesh-perspective/utils/applyProjectiveTransformationToPlane.ts), DOCUMENTED). So the UVs stay on a regular grid and it is the vertex positions that move; within each small quad the texture is still interpolated affinely, which is why more vertices means less stretch, and why the doc's phrase "at the uv level" is loose (INFERRED from the code).

8. **A `PerspectiveMesh` at its default 10 by 10 grid batches with sprites; at 20 by 20 it does not.** Defaults: `verticesX: 10, verticesY: 10`, a 100 by 100 white quad ([PerspectiveMesh.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/scene/mesh-perspective/PerspectiveMesh.ts) `defaultOptions`, DOCUMENTED). `PlaneGeometry` allocates `total = verticesX * verticesY` vertices ([PlaneGeometry.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/scene/mesh-plane/PlaneGeometry.ts), DOCUMENTED). `Mesh.batched` returns false with a custom shader or with depth test or culling in the state, and otherwise, for `batchMode === 'auto'`, returns `this._geometry.positions.length / 2 <= 100` ([Mesh.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/scene/mesh/shared/Mesh.ts), DOCUMENTED). The skill reference states the same rule: batched only with `MeshGeometry`, no custom `shader`, no depth test or culling, and "The `'auto'` rule: geometry has 100 or fewer vertices"; "Custom shaders always render independently" ([mesh.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-scene-mesh/references/mesh.md), DOCUMENTED). 10 by 10 is exactly 100, so the default mesh rides in the sprite batch; the reference's recommended "20×20 is a good default" is 400 vertices and becomes its own draw call (INFERRED arithmetic on the documented rule). For one monitor the difference is one draw call either way; for a six-face cabinet it is one draw call versus six, still trivial (INFERRED).

9. **A cabinet from four to six faces re-projected from a fake camera is straightforward arithmetic, and its limits are the limits of flat faces.** The reference already sells the pattern as "angled cards, fake 3D layouts", a "Fake floor", and "Call `setCorners` each frame to animate the warp" ([mesh-perspective.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-scene-mesh/references/mesh-perspective.md), DOCUMENTED). The per-input cost is `compute2DProjection` plus one loop over 100 to 400 vertices and one buffer upload per face, which is negligible against a single frame's fixed cost (INFERRED from the code in item 7). What it cannot do: relief inside a face (T-molding, the coin-door lip, the recess of the monitor behind its bezel) is painted on and does not shift with yaw; there is no depth buffer, so the side panel and the front must be ordered by hand for the current yaw, and the panel that is turning away has to be dropped when its quad would go non-convex; and self-shadowing does not change because nothing is lit (INFERRED from items 7 and 8). Pixi's own precedent for depth without a library is Héctor Monerris' helper where "the Z position acted like a multiplier for X,Y position and scale", which the earlier record already adopted for the room ([whole-site-pixi-precedent.md](whole-site-pixi-precedent.md) item 10 citing his [making-of](https://www.awwwards.com/one-last-beat-the-making-of.html), DOCUMENTED).

### Normal-map lighting

10. **`pixi-lights` (`@pixi/lights`) is a PixiJS 7 plugin and has had no v8 work.** README: "This modules _requires_ v7.0.0+ of PixiJS and v2.0.1+ of @pixi/layers" ([pixijs-userland/lights](https://github.com/pixijs-userland/lights), DOCUMENTED). `package.json` peers: `@pixi/core ^7.0.0`, `@pixi/display ^7.0.0`, `@pixi/layers ^2.0.1`, `@pixi/mesh ^7.0.0` (read through `gh api`, DOCUMENTED). npm: `@pixi/lights` latest 4.1.0 published 2023-07-12, and the older `pixi-lights` name last published 3.0.0 on 2021-07-06 (npm registry, DOCUMENTED). The repo's last push was 2024-12-20 and it is not archived, but the only issue that matches "v8" is #57 "Examples are broken" (2024-03-11, closed), and the tracker's newest open issue dates from January 2020 ([issues](https://github.com/pixijs-userland/lights/issues), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). Its architecture is deferred shading through `@pixi/layers`: "You have to create three layers: one for sprites, one for their normals and one for lights. Sprites and normals are rendered to temporary RenderTexture, and lights have those two textures as an input" (README, DOCUMENTED), and `@pixi/layers` is itself a v7-era package superseded by v8's built-in `RenderLayer` ([Pixi layers API](https://api.pixijs.io/@pixi/layers.html), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). Plainly: there is no normal-map lighting library for PixiJS v8.

11. **A custom v8 shader with a normal texture is feasible and the pattern is on record, but the docs are thin and the community says so.** Discussion #11040 (asked 2024-11-07, answered 2024-11-19) shows the working shape: `Shader.from` with `resources: { uTexture: texture.source, uOverlayTexture: Texture.WHITE.source, uNormalTexture: normal?.source ?? Texture.EMPTY.source }` and matching `uniform sampler2D` declarations in the fragment shader ([pixijs discussion #11040](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/discussions/11040), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). Issue #11769 (2025-11-25) asks for "V8 Shaded Mesh examples / documentation" after the author "spent two hours discovering that uniform resources require wrapping in a named group object", with no maintainer reply at fetch time ([pixijs #11769](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/issues/11769), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). The filter route is documented: `Filter.from({ gl: { fragment }, resources })`, GLSL ES 3.0 (`in`/`out`, `texture()`, `out vec4 finalColor`), uniforms in named groups, and "For dual-renderer support (WebGL + WebGPU), include both a `glProgram` and a `gpuProgram`" ([filters.md custom filters](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/filters/__docs__/filters.md), DOCUMENTED). The v5-era normal-map answer from Ivan Popelyshev on html5gamedevs was also "a custom shader on a Mesh", not a library ([html5gamedevs](https://www.html5gamedevs.com/topic/45723-running-normal-maps-on-pixijs/), COMMUNITY-MEASURED); his CodePen demo could not be fetched (Cloudflare block).

12. **The runtime cost of two point lights is small; the cost that matters is the second texture and the lost batch.** Per pixel, a normal-mapped point light is one extra texture sample plus a normalize, a dot product and a falloff, well under the "post-processing effect" budget of any built-in filter (INFERRED). The structural costs are documented: a custom shader means the mesh "always render[s] independently" (item 8), and the normal map is a second RGBA texture the same size as the diffuse (INFERRED). MDN adds that compressed formats "are generally only acceptable for colors (not e.g., normals or coordinates)", so the normal map cannot take the compression discount of item 6 (MDN, DOCUMENTED). Combined with a turntable, every frame would need its own normal frame, doubling the memory that item 5 already calls the constraint (INFERRED).

13. **For this scene the lights do not move, so the lighting bakes for free and a live normal map buys nothing the frames do not already hold.** The marquee and monitor are fixed to the cabinet; their light on the cabinet's own surfaces is identical at every yaw and comes out of the render (item 4). The only light that changes is what plays on the monitor, and the cheaper answer to that is a baked "monitor spill" sprite over the control panel whose tint and alpha follow the attract loop, which is one sprite in the additive group of item 18 rather than a shader (INFERRED). The one shipped v7 system that does full normal mapping, shadow casting and SSAO in Pixi is `dobrado76/pixi-lights-and-shadows`, which targets "PIXI.js 7.x with @pixi/react", offers "4 point lights + 4 spotlights + 2 directional lights per pass" before it goes multi-pass, and publishes no mobile frame numbers ([README](https://github.com/dobrado76/pixi-lights-and-shadows), COMMUNITY-MEASURED); it is the scale of machinery a lit 2D scene needs, and the cabinet does not need it.

### pixi3d

14. **pixi3d is PixiJS v5 to v7 only, and its maintainer has said three times that v8 is not coming from him.** README: "Compatible with PixiJS v5, v6 and v7" ([jnsmalm/pixi3d](https://github.com/jnsmalm/pixi3d), DOCUMENTED). npm latest is 2.5.0, published 2023-12-17, with `pixi.js ^6.5.1` in its devDependencies and no peer dependency declared (npm registry, DOCUMENTED); GitHub releases match, v2.5.0 on 2023-12-17 after v2.4.0 in May 2023 (`gh release list`, DOCUMENTED); the repo's last push was 2024-05-30 (`gh api`, DOCUMENTED). Issue #208 "Pixi v8 support" (2024-08-27) was closed the next day by the owner with "Yes, it would. I don't have time to do it though." ([#208](https://github.com/jnsmalm/pixi3d/issues/208), DOCUMENTED). Issue #209 "Feature Request: PixiJS v8 Support" (2024-09-02) is open; the owner on 2024-09-08: "I have too much other stuff going on to do that I'm afraid. Also, I don't really see the need of PixiJS 8 I general, why would I go with that instead of v7?"; on 2024-09-11: "I'll stick with v7 for a while more for the production apps"; on 2025-10-20: "Same status as previously. I have so much other stuff going on that I don't have any time left for a project of this magnitude. From what I understand though, PixiJS guys is working on adding 3D support to PixiJS v8" ([#209](https://github.com/jnsmalm/pixi3d/issues/209), DOCUMENTED). Its glTF loader is therefore moot for this app: it does not load in 8.19.0 at all.

15. **PixiJS itself has announced 3D but has not shipped it, and the supported way to mix in real 3D today is a second renderer on a shared context.** The 8.16.0 release post: "We're actively building 3D support into the engine, designed to work alongside the 2D renderer", framed as "While not part of this release, we want to give you a look at where PixiJS is headed" ([PixiJS blog 8.16.0](https://pixijs.com/blog/8.16.0), DOCUMENTED). The installed 8.19.0 `lib/` has no 3D module (inspection, DOCUMENTED). The official guide for mixing Three.js and Pixi shares one WebGL context, calls `resetState()` on each renderer before its `render`, needs `stencil: true` on the Three renderer for Pixi masks and `clearBeforeRender: false` on Pixi, and warns "resources like textures are not shared between PixiJS and Three.js" ([mixing guide](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/third-party/mixing-three-and-pixi), DOCUMENTED). For one object it is a whole second engine, a second copy of every texture it touches, and two loops to keep in step with the frame loop policy (INFERRED).

### Cheap depth cues that need no 3D

16. **Every cue on the list maps to a documented Pixi v8 primitive.** Parallax: `Container.position` and `scale` driven from a Z multiplier, the transforms table ("position", "scale ... can be set independently for the x and y axis", "skew ... similar to the CSS skew() function, and is specified in radians", "pivot") ([scene objects transforms](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/docs/guides/components/scene-objects/index.md), DOCUMENTED). Skew on a side panel as the view moves: `Container.skew`, same table, with the caveat that skew is affine and only a `PerspectiveMesh` gives converging edges (INFERRED). Scale-with-depth: `scale` per layer from the same Z, which is Monerris' rule (item 9). Glow spill: `sprite.blendMode = 'add'`, "Adds the colors of the source and destination pixels", alongside `normal`, `multiply`, `screen` ([blend modes](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/docs/guides/components/scene-objects/index.md), DOCUMENTED); the advanced set (`overlay`, `soft-light`, `color-dodge` and the rest) needs `import 'pixi.js/advanced-blend-modes'` and, on WebGL, `useBackBuffer: true` because they "sample the existing render target" ([filters.md advanced blend modes](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/filters/__docs__/filters.md), [application options](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-application/references/application-options.md), DOCUMENTED). Floor reflection: a second `Sprite` on the same texture with `scale.y` negative and a lower `alpha`, faded by a vertical gradient mask or a pre-faded copy of the frame; `alpha` is "Opacity from 0.0 (fully transparent) to 1.0 (fully opaque), inherited by children" (transforms table, DOCUMENTED). Baked shadows and AO: item 4, in the art.

17. **The baked route is what the two most-cited 3D portfolios actually ship: unlit meshes wearing baked JPEGs, with only screens and lamps left live.** Heffernan's `BakedModel` wraps every mesh in a `THREE.MeshBasicMaterial({ map: this.texture })` with `flipY = false` and sRGB encoding, and his asset list is `computer_setup.glb` plus `baked_computer.jpg`, `environment.glb` plus `baked_environment.jpg`, `decor.glb` plus `baked_decor_modified.jpg`, then `smudges.jpg` and `shadow-compressed.png` layers for the monitor ([BakedModel.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/Utils/BakedModel.ts), [sources.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/sources.ts), DOCUMENTED). Jesse Zhou's ramen shop uses `MeshBasicMaterial` with flat colours for every neon sign and LED, five baked KTX2 textures for everything else, and a `littleTVScreen.mp4` video texture for its one live screen ([Materials.js](https://github.com/enderh3art/Ramen-Shop/blob/main/src/Experience/World/Materials.js), [sources.js](https://github.com/enderh3art/Ramen-Shop/blob/main/src/Experience/sources.js), DOCUMENTED). `MeshBasicMaterial` is Three's unlit material, so neither scene computes a single light at runtime; all of the "3D-ish" warmth people react to in those sites is baked in Blender and the geometry only supplies parallax (INFERRED). A turntable is the same idea one step further: bake the geometry's parallax into the frames too, because the camera only needs 24 degrees (INFERRED).

18. **Glow spill is the cue that carries the palette, and the earlier record already fixes where the warmth may land.** The craft record found that every warm-lit precedent turned brown where lamp light hit a mid-value surface, and that the working rule is cool darks with the warmth in the top decile of values ([craft-values-standing-in-front.md](craft-values-standing-in-front.md) items 3 to 5 and "The gap", INFERRED there). Additive sprites are the right tool for exactly that: `add` only ever brightens, so a spill sprite painted in the marquee's hue over near-black stays in the highlights and cannot produce a brown mid-tone on its own; a `multiply` or `normal` tinted overlay could (INFERRED from the blend mode definitions in item 16). Stardew's night is built the same way in reverse, subtracting yellow to leave blue (craft record item 3, DOCUMENTED there).

19. **Filters are the wrong tool for these cues, by Pixi's own advice.** "Filters are expensive, using too many will start to slow things down!" and, under filter performance, "Prefer sprite sheets: For static effects, bake them into textures instead of using runtime filters" ([performance tips](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/__docs__/concepts/performance-tips.md), [filters.md performance](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/filters/__docs__/filters.md), DOCUMENTED). `BlurFilter` for a soft glow is the classic misuse: a pre-blurred PNG costs one sprite in the batch, the filter costs an offscreen render of the subtree plus `quality` passes (filters.md, DOCUMENTED; the comparison is INFERRED). `cacheAsTexture` is the middle road if a filter must exist somewhere: render the filtered container once, then "Subsequent renders reuse this texture", at the cost of "GPU memory" and the 4096 ceiling ([cache-as-texture](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/docs/guides/components/scene-objects/container/cache-as-texture.md), DOCUMENTED).

### Phone cost and batching

20. **A phone at `resolution: devicePixelRatio` renders three to nine times the pixels of the same canvas at 1, and Pixi's own mobile advice is to spend that budget deliberately.** Application options: `resolution` defaults to `1` ("Set to `window.devicePixelRatio` for HiDPI"), `autoDensity` defaults to `false`, `antialias` to `false`, `backgroundAlpha` to `1` and "Cannot be changed after init" ([application options](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-application/references/application-options.md), DOCUMENTED). The performance skill: "`resolution: 2` quadruples the pixel count. On mobile, this can halve frame rate. Profile to find the right balance", and "Mobile-friendly: lower resolution, no antialias", `backgroundAlpha: 1` because "opaque background is faster" ([pixijs-performance SKILL.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-performance/SKILL.md), DOCUMENTED). MDN: "A common (and easy) way to trade off quality for speed is rendering into a smaller back buffer, and upscaling the result", and beware "moire artifacts with non-integer values of `devicePixelRatio`" (MDN, DOCUMENTED). The dark room is a gift here: near-black has nothing to alias, so a lower canvas resolution with a full-resolution monitor texture is a plausible split, to be measured rather than assumed (INFERRED).

21. **The batching rules that these techniques touch, from the docs and the installed code.** "Sprites can be batched with up to 16 different textures (dependent on hardware)"; "Group similar object types together ... `sprite / sprite / graphic / graphic` produces 2 draw calls, while `sprite / graphic / sprite / graphic` produces 4"; "Different blend modes break batches ... `Screen / Normal / Screen / Normal` = 4 draw calls, `Screen / Screen / Normal / Normal` = 2 draw calls"; "Graphics objects are batched when under a certain size (100 points or smaller)" ([performance tips](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/__docs__/concepts/performance-tips.md), DOCUMENTED). The skill lists the four break causes: "Object type change", "Texture source change (beyond the per-batch texture limit, typically 16)", "Blend mode change", "Topology change" ([pixijs-performance SKILL.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-performance/SKILL.md), DOCUMENTED). In the installed batcher the break test is `blendMode !== adjustedBlendMode || topology !== element.topology` plus the texture-slot count ([Batcher.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/rendering/batcher/shared/Batcher.ts), DOCUMENTED), and on WebGL the per-batch texture limit is `gl.MAX_TEXTURE_IMAGE_UNITS` reduced by `checkMaxIfStatementsInShader` ([GlLimitsSystem.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/rendering/renderers/gl/GlLimitsSystem.ts), DOCUMENTED), where MDN's floor is `MAX_TEXTURE_IMAGE_UNITS: 8` (MDN, DOCUMENTED). Meshes join the batch under the 100-vertex rule (item 8); a custom shader never does; a filter renders its subtree offscreen (item 19). For the recommended stack the whole standing-in-front scene is two or three draw calls: the `normal` batch (room layers, cabinet frame, monitor mesh, reflection), the `add` batch (spill sprites), and nothing else (INFERRED).

22. **The over-the-wire cost of a turntable is small next to its GPU cost, because a dark cabinet compresses well.** Sirv's photographic spins: 24 frames at 336 KB, 36 at 505 KB, 72 at 1.1 MB after their compression, from 40.4 MB of originals (item 1, DOCUMENTED). Apple's frames average about 31 KB (item 2, COMMUNITY-MEASURED). A 512 by 1024 frame that is mostly near-black with a lit marquee and monitor should sit in that range as PNG or as a KTX2 with supercompression, so 25 frames are on the order of a megabyte down the wire while occupying 50 MiB uncompressed or 6 to 12 MiB compressed on the GPU (INFERRED arithmetic; the ratio is the point, the exact numbers are to be measured on the real render).

### Reduced motion and the frame loop

23. **Every technique in the recommended stack is a pure function of camera yaw and pointer, so it renders once per input and stops.** Turntable frame selection is an index into a texture list; `PerspectiveMesh.setCorners` is a synchronous geometry update; parallax is a transform; additive and flipped sprites are static. None of them needs a `Ticker` callback to exist (INFERRED from the APIs in items 7, 8 and 16). Pixi's supported pattern is `autoStart: false` with `app.render()` or `renderer.render(stage)` when something changed, and `app.stop()` / `app.start()` around animations ([application options ticker](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-application/references/application-options.md); the craft record's items 16 to 21 already cite the render-loop guide, ticker source and the community request for a first-class "render on change", DOCUMENTED there).

24. **The things that force a continuous loop all live on the monitor.** A video texture: "The `Sprite` reflects the current video frame automatically because `VideoSource` re-uploads on every render", `updateFPS` "`0` re-uploads every render tick (smoothest; highest cost)" and a cap like `15` "saves bandwidth on static-ish video backgrounds" ([video textures](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-assets/references/video.md), DOCUMENTED). An `AnimatedSprite`: `autoUpdate` defaults to `true` and "uses `Ticker.shared`. Set `false` and call `update(ticker)` manually to drive playback from a custom ticker or pause it with game logic" ([animated-sprite.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-scene-sprite/references/animated-sprite.md), DOCUMENTED). A shader with a `uTime` uniform advanced from the ticker, as in the docs' wave filter example (item 11's source, DOCUMENTED). None of these is a turntable or a mesh; they are attract-mode choices, and the attract record owns them ([attract-mode-precedent.md](attract-mode-precedent.md)). The turntable itself should not be an `AnimatedSprite` with `autoUpdate` on; it should be a plain `Sprite` whose `texture` is set from the yaw, or an `AnimatedSprite` with `autoUpdate: false` driven only by `gotoAndStop(frame)` (INFERRED from the doc).

25. **Under reduced motion the stack degrades to a still frame without losing the 3D read, which is the CSS precedent's finding too.** web.dev's 3D game menu keeps `perspective`, `preserve-3d` and the rotated faces under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` and removes only the transitions and the floating animation: "The 3D perspective was still really neat for the `reduced` motion preference. The top and bottom elements show the effect in a nice subtle way" ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/building/a-3d-game-menu-component), DOCUMENTED). For the cabinet that means: freeze the idle drift (the craft record's WCAG 2.2.2 and Apple Reduce Motion findings, items 12 and 13 there), leave the cabinet on its resting frame with its baked depth intact, and treat a deliberate drag as user-initiated yaw rather than parallax the visitor did not ask for (INFERRED). A pre-rendered frame set has one more property here: the stepped yaw is discrete, so a visitor who drags sees the cabinet turn without any easing tween running afterwards if the app chooses none (INFERRED).

### Precedents

26. **Shipped single-object 2.5D entries exist in three technologies, and none is Pixi.** Three.js with baked textures: Heffernan's desk and Zhou's ramen shop (item 17), Bruno Simon's folio (whole-site record, DOCUMENTED there). Three.js with real-time post: manthrax's "Thralaga", an arcade cabinet with a playable Galaga-like on its screen, using "bloom, gtao, and some hand rolled crt style +chromatic abberation shaders", "Only tested on desktop + 1080gtx" ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/threejs-simulated-arcade-game-thralaga/64050), live at [manthrax.github.io/mcade](https://manthrax.github.io/mcade/index.html), DOCUMENTED); it is the closest object match found and the clearest warning about phone cost, since its author never tried one. CSS 3D: web.dev's game menu with `perspective: 40vw`, `transform-style: preserve-3d`, `rotateY`/`rotateX` and `translateZ` layers (item 25, DOCUMENTED). Image sequence on a 2D canvas: Apple's product pages (item 2). Pure-CSS arcade cabinets exist on CodePen ("The CSS Arcade Machine" by pmk, "Arcade Cabinet Pure CSS" by gilsjhonny, and a 2026 "Pure CSS Retro Arcade Machine" featured by freefrontend) and were surfaced only as search titles; CodePen and freefrontend blocked every fetch, so nothing about their construction is claimed here (unreached). Nothing built in Pixi that presents one object as the door into a game was found; the only Pixi-built drag-around site on record remains Chris Simmons' portfolio from the whole-site record (DOCUMENTED there).

27. **The one shipped depth-map parallax found is a Three.js shader, and its own write-up prices it.** Karim Maaloul's "Halloween 2.5D Parallax Shader" turns "a flat 2D image into an interactive, volumetric scene with dynamic lighting, fog, and depth distortion" from "four separate, perfectly aligned textures: the base image, a depth map, a normal map, and a noise texture"; freefrontend's spec sheet: "Performance: Highly GPU-intensive. The entire visual effect is calculated per-pixel inside the fragment shader sixty times a second", weight about 650 KB with Three.js, and "Fails completely without JavaScript or WebGL support" ([freefrontend](https://freefrontend.com/code/halloween-2-5d-parallax-shader-2026-05-07/), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). It is the technique that would let a single painted cabinet appear to turn, and it is exactly the continuous per-pixel loop the frame loop policy forbids; under the on-demand policy it would run once per input, which is fine for cost but still only warps one image and cannot reveal the side panel a turntable shows (INFERRED).

## What is thin

No phone measurement exists for any of this yet. The texture memory numbers in items 5 and 22 are arithmetic on assumed frame sizes, and the compression ratios are the format's nominal bits per pixel, not a measurement of the cabinet's own frames. The first real frame set should be weighed on a phone with the renderer's texture count and the browser's memory panel before the frame count is chosen.

The PerspectiveMesh reading in item 7 (positions move, UVs stay regular) is from the installed code and contradicts the doc's phrase "at the uv level"; both are cited so a later reader can check. It changes nothing about the recommendation.

The batching claim in item 8 that a 10 by 10 `PerspectiveMesh` batches with sprites rests on the `<= 100` comparison in `Mesh.batched` and the `verticesX * verticesY` allocation in `PlaneGeometry`; I did not render and count draw calls.

pixi-lights' tracker was read through GitHub's issue list and a `gh` search for "v8"; the repo restricts issue creation and I did not read closed issues one by one, so a v8 discussion in a pull request or a fork would have been missed. The npm dates and peer dependencies are conclusive on their own.

Pixi's own 3D is quoted from the 8.16.0 post only. The pixi3d thread's February 2026 comment pointed to that same post and nothing newer was found; whether a later 8.x post gives a date was not chased, and 8.19.0 on disk has no 3D module.

The iOS 224 MB figure is a forum-reported error string for canvas memory, not a WebKit document, and it is not clear that it governs WebGL textures. The WebKit bug's 1.25 GB kill point is a single report on iOS 14.2.

Heffernan's monitor is not read here beyond the smudge and shadow layers in his asset list; the whole-site record covers his screen interaction. Jesse Zhou's own case study was blocked twice (Medium via fetch and via Ref), so his intent is not quoted; his repo's asset list is.

Sirv's and CloudPano's frame counts are for 360 degree product photography, not a 24 degree arc. The proportional reading in item 1 is mine.

## The gap

Nobody found has shipped a pre-rendered turntable of a single object as the entry to a game, on a phone, in Pixi. The pieces each have precedent (Sirv and CloudPano for the frames, Apple for the canvas sequence and the slow-connection fallback, Heffernan and Zhou for baked-not-lit, Pixi's docs for the mesh and the compressed loaders), but the combination is new and its two open questions are measurable rather than arguable: how many frames the 24 degree arc needs before the step is invisible on a phone, and how many MiB of compressed frames a mid phone carries before Safari reloads the page.

The monitor-corner table, one quad per frame exported alongside the render, is the join between the turntable and the live monitor, and no source describes it because no source needed it. It should come out of the render script, not be eyeballed, and its correctness is testable: the projected corners for frame 0 must match the monitor's glass in frame 0 to the pixel.
