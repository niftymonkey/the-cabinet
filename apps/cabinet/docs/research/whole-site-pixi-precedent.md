# Whole-site canvas precedent: the shipped sites you move around in are camera keyframes plus one free axis, and the PixiJS showcase does not contain one

Research for The Cabinet, ticket [#89](https://github.com/niftymonkey/the-cabinet/issues/89). Labels: **DOCUMENTED** = an author's own statement, the site's public source code, or official PixiJS docs and source. **COMMUNITY-MEASURED** = a third-party write-up, an issue thread, or a comment thread with a measured or reproduced result. **INFERRED** = my reading across sources, including my own inspection of a live site's markup.

The question was which shipped sites are one PixiJS (or comparable canvas) application you move around in, and how they handle moving with mouse and keys, zooming into a focal object, touch drag on a phone, and staying readable at phone size. The framing is in [docs/design/cabinet.md](../../../../docs/design/cabinet.md).

The strongest sources are public source code: Henry Heffernan's desk portfolio (Three.js, [henryjeff/portfolio-website](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website)), which is the closest shipped analogue to "stand in front of a machine, click its screen, zoom into it"; Bruno Simon's 2019 folio (Three.js, [brunosimon/folio-2019](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2019)) with his Awwwards case study on first-contact instructions; Chris Simmons' legacy Pixi portfolio ([endigo9740/endigo-design-pixi](https://github.com/endigo9740/endigo-design-pixi)), the one genuinely Pixi-built drag-around site found; the PixiJS v8 events guide, EventSystem source and the official pixi-viewport plugin; and Héctor Monerris' making-of for One Last Beat on faking depth in Pixi. The showcase itself turned out to be the weakest source.

The gap: no shipped site found combines all four of the cabinet's needs, nobody has published how visitors discover that a canvas can be dragged, and the PixiJS docs say nothing about pinch or keyboard.

---

## What is solid

1. **The PixiJS showcase does not currently contain a whole-site-you-move-around-in entry.** The showcase data file lists 28 entries ([showcase.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/src/data/showcase.ts), DOCUMENTED): twenty games, four tools or SDKs, and three that read as sites and are, on inspection, scroll pages. Benoît Wimart's [jaiunsite.com](https://jaiunsite.com) drives its current effect from `window.scrollY` in inline script and loads no Pixi bundle, Kadir Inan's [uix.me](https://www.uix.me/) is a scrolling portfolio, and [emberdesign.net](https://emberdesign.net/) is a scrolling landing page (INFERRED from fetching each page; the Wimart showcase image is named `jaiunsite-pixijs.webp`, so an earlier version may have been Pixi). The older `pixijs.io/gallery` address returns 404. The model Mark remembers is real, but its shipped exemplars live outside the showcase, and two of the three best ones are Three.js.

2. **The one Pixi-built drag-around site found is a top-down world with a clamped camera, drag only, and its author was asked for arrow keys within a day of launch.** Chris Simmons' 2022 portfolio offered "game mode" (a Hyper Light Drifter-styled Pixi world) beside a traditional site. Its camera is a container translated by pointer delta and clamped to world bounds: `pointerdown` records the last position, `pointermove` adds the delta, `pointerup` and `pointerout` end the drag, and `adjustForWorldBounds` clamps to `container.width - app.screen.width` ([Camera.ts](https://github.com/endigo9740/endigo-design-pixi/blob/main/src/lib/game/Camera.ts), DOCUMENTED). There is no keyboard path in the source. On the [dev.to launch post](https://dev.to/endigo9740/my-new-portfolio-3ke6), two separate commenters said they instinctively reached for the arrow keys ("Shows how old I am that I tried to navigate with the cursor keys", "Instinctively wanted to use arrow keys to move Chris (or the map)"), and the author replied "That actually sounds like a good quality of life improvement, plus keyboard a11y is always nice too" (DOCUMENTED, comment thread). Note also that this camera listens to `pointermove` on the world container and ends the drag on `pointerout`; under PixiJS v8 that pattern only keeps working because the container fills the screen (INFERRED, see item 3).

3. **PixiJS v8 documents drag as one specific pattern, and the v7 pattern silently breaks.** The events guide states that in v8 `pointermove`, `mousemove` and `touchmove` "are fired only when the pointer is over a display object", and that `globalpointermove` "fired on every pointer move, regardless of whether any display object is hit" exists to keep the old behaviour ([events guide](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/docs/guides/components/events.md), DOCUMENTED). The official skill file's drag pattern is `eventMode = 'static'`, `pointerdown` sets a flag, `globalpointermove` applies the position, and both `pointerup` and `pointerupoutside` clear the flag; it lists "Move events only fire over the object in v8" as a HIGH-severity common mistake ([pixijs-events SKILL.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-events/SKILL.md), DOCUMENTED). `FederatedPointerEvent` carries `global`, `client`, `pointerType` (`'mouse' | 'pen' | 'touch'`), `pointerId` and `isPrimary`, which is enough to tell touch from mouse and to track two fingers by hand. `eventFeatures.globalMove` is described as "Expensive; turn off if you don't need it" ([ApplicationOptions reference](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-application/references/application-options.md), DOCUMENTED).

4. **Pixi takes over the canvas's touch behaviour by default, and its wheel listener is passive.** `EventSystem.setTargetElement` sets `style.touchAction = 'none'` on the canvas when pointer events are supported, `autoPreventDefault` defaults to `true` and calls `preventDefault()` on cancelable normalized pointer events, and the wheel listener is registered with `{ passive: true, capture: true }` ([EventSystem.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/src/events/EventSystem.ts), DOCUMENTED). The consequence for a full-viewport canvas: the browser will not scroll or pinch-zoom the page from the canvas, so a touch drag is entirely the app's to interpret, and a `wheel` handler cannot stop page scroll, which is harmless only because a full-screen page has nothing to scroll (INFERRED).

5. **Pixi itself has neither pinch nor keyboard, and its own tutorial writes keyboard handling on `document`.** A search of the pixijs.com docs and the pixijs source docs for "pinch" finds only a marketing features list; the architecture page describes Events as "pointer-based interaction" and Accessibility as tab-focus DOM overlays ([architecture](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/docs/guides/concepts/architecture.md), [accessibility](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/docs/guides/components/accessibility.md), DOCUMENTED). The official SpineBoy tutorial's controller is a plain class on `keydown` and `keyup` with a key map, "W, A, S and D keys as directional input on the key map so they will behave like the arrow keys", a 300 ms double-tap window, and this line: "Now tap on the preview screen to make sure the canvas is focused, then try tapping away the right button" ([step 3](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/src/tutorials/v8.0.0/spineBoyAdventure/step3/step3-content.md), DOCUMENTED). Focus is the keyboard's first-contact problem and Pixi's own docs trip on it.

6. **pixi-viewport is the ecosystem's answer for drag, pinch, wheel and clamping, it is on v8, and it has no keyboard movement.** Its README lists "dragging, pinch-to-zoom, mouse wheel zooming, decelerated dragging, follow target, animate, snap to point, snap to zoom, clamping, bouncing on edges, and move on mouse edges", with v6.0.0 moving to pixi.js v8 and construction requiring `events: app.renderer.events` ([README](https://github.com/pixijs-userland/pixi-viewport/blob/master/README.md), DOCUMENTED). The plugin surface is `drag` (with `direction`, `clampWheel`, `mouseButtons`, `keyToPress`), `pinch`, `wheel` (`smooth`, `percent`, `interrupt`), `decelerate`, `clamp`, `clampZoom` (`minScale`, `maxScale`, `minWidth`), `snap`, `snapZoom`, `animate`, `mouseEdges` ([jsdoc](https://viewport.pixijs.io/jsdoc/Viewport.html), DOCUMENTED). The only keyboard option is `keyToPress`, "codes of keys that can be pressed for the drag to be triggered", a gate on mouse drag rather than movement.

7. **pixi-viewport's issue tracker is the measured record of where pointer gestures hurt.** Trackpad two-finger pan versus pinch: the maintainer found "on Chrome you cannot use two-finger movement on a trackpad. Once you start a two-finger movement, chrome locks you in the horizontal or vertical direction" and closed the issue in 2019; a user's 2022 working setup is `.drag({ mouseButtons: 'middle-right' }).pinch().wheel({ smooth: 3, trackpadPinch: true, wheelZoom: false })` ([#143](https://github.com/pixijs-userland/pixi-viewport/issues/143), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). Pinch on Firefox mobile and Android Chrome "spazzes out between the two touch points" after the first pinch, traced by one user to `pointerId` reuse in the Pixi v6 interaction package; still open ([#364](https://github.com/pixijs-userland/pixi-viewport/issues/364), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). Cmd+wheel on Mac scrolls the page instead of zooming ([#492](https://github.com/pixijs-userland/pixi-viewport/issues/492), COMMUNITY-MEASURED).

8. **Zooming into a focal object ships as a tween between named camera keyframes, not as free zoom.** Henry Heffernan's camera has keyframes `idle`, `desk`, `monitor`, `loading` and `orbitControlsStart`; a `mousedown` anywhere toggles `idle` and `desk` over 1000 ms; the mouse entering the monitor's iframe fires `enterMonitor`, which tweens to `monitor` over 2000 ms with `BezierEasing(0.13, 0.99, 0, 1)`, and leaving returns to `desk` ([Camera.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/Camera/Camera.ts), [MonitorScreen.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/World/MonitorScreen.ts), DOCUMENTED). The `monitor` distance is recomputed from viewport aspect each frame, `origin.z + aspect * 1200 - additionalZoom`, with `additionalZoom` 600 at 768 px and wider and 0 below ([CameraKeyframes.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/Camera/CameraKeyframes.ts), DOCUMENTED). Bruno Simon's zoom is a scalar clamped to 0..1, moved by `wheel` and by two-finger distance ratio on `touchmove`, eased at 0.1 per frame ([Camera.js](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2019/blob/master/src/javascript/Camera.js), DOCUMENTED), and he added a keyframe-style move for the cabinet's own reason: "Many testers told me that the projects section wasn't readable enough... I simply tweened the position of the camera, above the car and looking downward, when the user is entering the section", plus "a zoom in and out feature using the wheel to improve readability" ([case study](https://medium.com/@bruno_simon/bruno-simon-portfolio-case-study-960402cc259b), DOCUMENTED).

9. **The one author who tried "zoom into the computer screen while the character stays visible" abandoned it.** Matt Park's plan was "when user presses space in front of the computer, make the zoom into the screen (while it also shows my character), change the screen to the torus display where it would have all my works", and his verdict: "the UX of this actually sucks. I needed to have a minimized screen to be able to have all the computer model, the robot and the displays all fit in one screen: and the user really cant view all the works comfortably". He moved the works to a separate page ([How I made my 3D portfolio](https://medium.com/@mattparkarchive/how-i-made-my-3d-portfolio-website-50b413a75541), DOCUMENTED). Heffernan's monitor keyframe, by contrast, fills the viewport with the screen and nothing else, and the Galaxy Portfolio changes the journey's endpoint by device: "monitor on desktop, phone on mobile" ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/galaxy-portfolio-an-interactive-zoom-journey-from-milky-way-to-desk/82229), DOCUMENTED).

10. **Depth in-canvas is done three ways, and all three are cheap.** Héctor Monerris (One Last Beat, Pixi) wrote "a helper class to alter the position and scale of regular PIXI Sprites to fake perspective", where "the Z position acted like a multiplier for X,Y position and scale. Extremely close to a parallax effect", with blurred PNGs standing in for motion blur ([making-of](https://www.awwwards.com/one-last-beat-the-making-of.html), DOCUMENTED). Heffernan's `desk` keyframe lags the mouse: focal point eases toward the mouse at 0.05 per frame and camera position at 0.025, so the desk parallaxes under the cursor without any explicit layer system ([CameraKeyframes.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/Camera/CameraKeyframes.ts), DOCUMENTED). PixiJS v8 ships `PerspectiveMesh`, a textured quad with perspective-correct UVs driven by four corners via `setCorners`, meant for "angled cards, fake 3D layouts", with the explicit caveat "There is no Z axis, no depth buffer, and no camera" and a recommended 20x20 vertex grid ([mesh-perspective reference](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-scene-mesh/references/mesh-perspective.md), DOCUMENTED).

11. **First-contact affordance, where it is documented, is put inside the scene and demonstrated by motion before the visitor touches anything.** Bruno Simon: "The experience starts with the universe popping up from the ground, and the car falling down from above with a little bounce, so that the user notices the physics"; "The only thing written are the instructions, directly on the ground, inviting the user to use his arrow keys to move"; the car's start position was chosen so "bumping the title 'BRUNO SIMON' at the beginning... would maximise understandability of elements being moveable"; tiles act "like a path, to help user understand easily that there is more to see in that direction"; and after launch "I decided to add more instructions (but not visible directly in the intro to prevent information flood)" ([case study](https://medium.com/@bruno_simon/bruno-simon-portfolio-case-study-960402cc259b), DOCUMENTED). The source swaps the ground label by input, `this.config.touch ? introInstructionsControlsTexture : introInstructionsArrowsTexture`, and skips the physical arrow-key objects on touch ([IntroSection.js](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2019/blob/master/src/javascript/World/Sections/IntroSection.js), DOCUMENTED). Heffernan types "Click anywhere to begin" into a DOM prompt, hides it on the first `mousedown` or on `enterMonitor`, and the `idle` camera drifts on sine curves so the scene is already moving ([HelpPrompt.tsx](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/UI/components/HelpPrompt.tsx), DOCUMENTED). A touch-first Three.js gallery author states the alternative: "There is no WASD, so please hit the info icon on the top left corner to learn how to look around, move and interact with the objects", because "a high percentage of my audience will view this page on mobile" ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/interactive-3d-portfolio-gallery-for-touch-input-and-mouse/22725), DOCUMENTED).

12. **Keyboard movement in these sites is always a DOM `keydown` listener with arrows and WASD, and every author found a key they had to explain.** Bruno's controls map `ArrowUp` and `KeyW` (and the other three pairs) on `document`, with the case study adding "it's not indicated anywhere, but you can actually use WQSD or ZQSD (french keyboard) controls" and that Shift for the jump was not obvious to non-gamers ([Controls.js](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2019/blob/master/src/javascript/World/Controls.js), case study, DOCUMENTED; the Shift remark is the case study's paraphrase as fetched). Matt Park's map is arrows plus WASD, Shift to run, Space to teleport, and he built an instruction box copied from Unrailed's UI to explain it ([Medium](https://medium.com/@mattparkarchive/how-i-made-my-3d-portfolio-website-50b413a75541), DOCUMENTED). The Pixi tutorial's controller is item 5.

13. **On a phone, the shipped answers are a DOM joystick, a warning, or a lower quality preset, and never a canvas-drawn control.** Bruno sets `config.touch = true` on the first `touchstart`, disables the blur passes on touch, and builds a fixed-position DOM joystick and boost button with `touchmove` registered `{ passive: false }` ([Application.js](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2019/blob/master/src/javascript/Application.js), [Controls.js](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2019/blob/master/src/javascript/World/Controls.js), DOCUMENTED); his own words: "I know I know, I said 'no interface' but I couldn't find any intuitive solution other than that for mobile" (case study, DOCUMENTED). Heffernan shows, below 768 px, "WARNING: This experience is best viewed on a desktop or laptop computer." on the loading screen, and nothing else mobile-specific was found ([LoadingScreen.tsx](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/UI/components/LoadingScreen.tsx), DOCUMENTED); his monitor entry is hover-driven, so the touch path into the screen is undocumented (INFERRED). Monerris kept One Last Beat "perfectly playable on mid end mobile devices", put the whole GUI in DOM "to maintain sharpness on high-DPI devices", and shows a landscape prompt while "nothing breaks up if they use portrait mode" (making-of, DOCUMENTED).

14. **The official PixiJS open games ship one readability pattern for phones: a minimum design size fit by scaling the renderer up and the CSS size down, at resolution `max(devicePixelRatio, 2)`.** Both Bubbo Bubbo and Puzzling Potions compute `scale = max(minWidth / windowWidth, minHeight / windowHeight, 1)`, resize the renderer to `window * scale`, set the canvas CSS size to the window, and call `window.scrollTo(0, 0)` "to avoid issues on mobile resize"; Puzzling Potions' minimum is 375 by 700 ([bubbo-bubbo main.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/open-games/blob/main/bubbo-bubbo/src/main.ts), [puzzling-potions main.ts](https://github.com/pixijs/open-games/blob/main/puzzling-potions/src/main.ts), DOCUMENTED). The documented alternative is `resizeTo: window` with `resolution: window.devicePixelRatio` and `autoDensity: true` ([resize plugin](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs.com/blob/main/docs/guides/components/application/resize-plugin.md), [ApplicationOptions](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-application/references/application-options.md), DOCUMENTED).

15. **When the focal object is a screen, the shipped precedent keeps the screen's contents as DOM, and Pixi v8 has a container for that.** Heffernan renders the computer screen as a `CSS3DObject` wrapping an iframe of a separate site (`os.henryheffernan.com`), with a GL plane copying its transform ([MonitorScreen.ts](https://github.com/henryjeff/portfolio-website/blob/master/src/Application/World/MonitorScreen.ts), DOCUMENTED). PixiJS v8's `DOMContainer` positions an HTML element (input, iframe, video) by the scene graph's transform, is marked EXPERIMENTAL, always renders above all canvas content in a root div at `z-index: 1000`, and ignores Pixi filters and masks ([pixijs-scene-dom-container SKILL.md](https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/blob/dev/skills/pixijs-scene-dom-container/SKILL.md), DOCUMENTED).

## What is thin

The Awwwards "PixiJS" list ([awwwards.com/websites/pixijs](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/pixijs/)) holds 31 sites, but on the ones fetched Pixi is a scroll-page effect layer, and the Site of the Year entry's own case study ("The Other Side of Truth") never mentions Pixi ([Design Under Shelling](https://www.awwwards.com/the-other-side-of-truth-design-under-shelling.html)). The award-winning Moooi and Max Mara entries are canvas games rather than sites you move around in and were not inspected.

Karim Maaloul's "Castles, responsive parallax landscape" is the one Pixi demo in the Codrops hub tagged parallax and depth ([Codrops hub](https://tympanus.net/codrops/hub/tag/pixi-js/)); its CodePen source could not be fetched. Liam Egan's Codrops underwater navigation is a full-page Pixi canvas built from a real HTML nav, hidden from assistive tech with `aria-hidden` and `tabindex=-1` and driven by focus events, but one commenter reported "Your demo stops my entire phone for a minute" and the author's reply was that device detection and disabling would be needed in production ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/04/24/how-to-build-an-underwater-style-navigation-using-pixijs/), DOCUMENTED for the mechanics, COMMUNITY-MEASURED for the phone report). It is a menu, not a scene.

Heffernan's site is read from source, not from a phone. Hover-to-enter-monitor has no touch equivalent in the code and the warning text is the only mobile handling found; whether a phone visitor can reach the screen at all is unverified.

Bruno Simon's Medium case study was read through a text proxy because Medium blocks direct fetches; the quotes match the [Awwwards mirror](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html) where both cover the same ground, and his Shift-key remark is labelled as paraphrase above.

pixi-viewport's mobile pinch bug (#364) was diagnosed against Pixi v6. No v8-era report was found either way.

The endigo camera is the only Pixi drag camera read in full, and it is v6-era API (`interactive = true`, `e.data.global`): a shape precedent, not a code precedent.

## The gap

No shipped site found does all four things the cabinet needs. Heffernan has click-to-zoom and mouse parallax but no keys, no drag, and a desktop warning. Bruno has keys, touch, wheel and pinch zoom but is a driving game with a following camera, not a scene you pan. Simmons has drag on a Pixi world but no keys and no zoom. Nobody has keys plus drag plus a zoom keyframe plus a phone path in one canvas.

No author in this pass measured or described how visitors discover that a canvas can be dragged. Bruno's instructions are for arrow keys and he documents the reasoning; the touch label swap exists in his code but he says nothing about whether phone visitors found the joystick. Simmons' two arrow-key comments are the only observed first-contact data, and they are about keys, not drag. "Drag to explore" as a hint pattern surfaced nothing beyond generic drag-and-drop UX articles.

No source says what a wide horizontal scene does on a portrait phone. Monerris says portrait "doesn't break", the open games fit a portrait minimum, Heffernan removes 600 units of zoom below 768 px. None of them describes a landscape scene squeezed into a portrait viewport, which is exactly the cabinet's case.

The PixiJS docs have nothing on pinch, nothing on keyboard beyond the tutorial's DIY controller, and nothing on wheel-to-zoom. Every one of those is either pixi-viewport or hand-written in every site examined.

No author states a regret about drag navigation itself. The regrets on record are Simmons on the missing keys, Park on the zoomed screen that could not hold the content, Bruno on needing a DOM joystick, and Monerris' mobile video autoplay hack.

## What this suggests for the cabinet

INFERRED throughout. Build the camera as named keyframes with tweens (Heffernan's idle, desk and monitor) and make horizontal position the only free axis, clamped to the cabinet's bounds the way Simmons clamps his world; a wheel or pinch scalar (Bruno) is a second free axis the cabinet has not asked for.

Use the v8 drag pattern verbatim (`static` plus `pointerdown`, `globalpointermove`, `pointerup` and `pointerupoutside`) on a stage-sized hit area, write the keyboard as a `document` keydown controller with arrows and WASD as the Pixi tutorial does, and give the canvas focus on load so the tutorial's "tap to focus" trap never fires; pixi-viewport buys pinch and deceleration the cabinet does not need and brings a mobile pinch history it does not want.

Put the first-contact hint in the scene rather than in a DOM overlay, swap its wording on the first `touchstart` (Bruno's label swap), and let the cabinet move before the visitor does (Heffernan's idle drift, Bruno's falling car), because motion is how both shipped sites demonstrate that the scene is not a picture.

Fake depth with Monerris' Z-as-multiplier layers for the room and Heffernan's camera-lags-mouse for the parallax, and use `PerspectiveMesh` only for the cabinet's angled faces; none of it needs a 3D library.

On the phone, ship the open games' minimum-size fit at `resolution: max(devicePixelRatio, 2)`, compute the zoomed-in keyframe from aspect as Heffernan does, and treat portrait as the primary case since no precedent covers it; never show a "best on desktop" warning.

Keep whatever is drawn on the cabinet's screen as DOM (Heffernan's iframe, Pixi's `DOMContainer`) if it must stay readable at phone size, and take Park's failure as the rule: the zoomed screen fills the viewport or the zoom is not worth having.
