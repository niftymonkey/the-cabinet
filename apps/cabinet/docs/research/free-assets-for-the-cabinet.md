# Free assets for the cabinet: Kenney's CC0 Mini Arcade is the safe 3D base to render from, ambientCG and Poly Haven cover every material the multicade needs for nothing, Google Fonts covers the type under the OFL, and the things to keep out of a public repo are unlicensed itch.io packs, Craftpix files, and anything under Sketchfab's Standard or Editorial terms

Research for The Cabinet. Labels follow [craft-values-standing-in-front.md](craft-values-standing-in-front.md): **DOCUMENTED** = the asset site's own license page, the pack's own page, the license text itself, official API docs, or a file I downloaded and read. **COMMUNITY-MEASURED** = a third-party article, forum thread or client library. **INFERRED** = my reading across sources, including my inspection of preview images and the API calls I ran myself on 2026-09-08.

The brief is in [apps/cabinet/PRODUCT.md](../../PRODUCT.md): the site is the machine, a multicade in a friend's basement, lit by its own marquee and monitor, not photoreal, and Mark wants it "a little bit more 3D-ish" than flat Pixi shapes. The repo is public on GitHub and deploys to Vercel, so every shipped file has to allow modification and redistribution in a source repo and use on a website. The palette bans brown and purple; the chosen look is a near-black cool room, warm marquee `#f6e094`, teal monitor glow `#8fd0c9`, cabinet body `#0b0d10`, side art `#1d3a42` with a `#4fa3a8` stripe, sticks and buttons red `#d94f4f`, yellow `#e6c65c`, teal `#4fa3a8`.

Tooling note: Exa was not available. Sketchfab's developer pages, its two 2024 and 2025 community posts, Poly Pizza's API docs (Cloudflare bot check), Kenney's asset page (JavaScript), Google Fonts' FAQ and specimen pages (JavaScript), and itch.io's browse pages all defeated the plain fetcher; Kenney and the Google Fonts FAQ were read through Ref's renderer, Sketchfab's model data came from its public API, Kenney's pack came from downloading the zip, and where only a search engine's excerpt of a page was available the record says so. Preview images of every model candidate were pulled and looked at; the "what it lacks" notes are read from those previews and are INFERRED.

## Recommendation

**Route 1, ranked first: take Kenney's Mini Arcade `arcade-machine.glb` (CC0), rebuild it in Blender into a home multicade, and render it to sprite layers for Pixi.** Kenney's pack is CC0 with a license file inside the zip that says "You can use this content for personal, educational, and commercial purposes" and "Support by crediting 'Kenney' or 'www.kenney.nl' (this is not a requirement)" (item 1). It ships GLB, FBX and OBJ with a texture folder, plus wall, floor and column pieces for the room (item 1). What it costs: the machine is a squat, chunky "Mini" series upright with a marquee and a screen but no faux coin door, no riser and a single-stick panel, so the multicade tells from the earlier record (faux coin door with menu buttons, light-up marquee, riser seam, two sticks and six buttons each) have to be modelled on, and its bright toy palette has to be repainted to the look. What it buys: nothing to attribute, nothing to audit, a file that can sit in the repo, and a base whose proportions already read as a home machine rather than a floor machine. Render the body, marquee, monitor bezel, control panel and coin door as separate layers from one fixed three-quarter camera, plus two or three extra camera angles for the parallax, and the scene becomes Pixi sprites with real shading baked in (INFERRED).

**Route 2, ranked second and combinable with route 1: draw the cabinet as flat Pixi art but light it with CC0 PBR maps.** ambientCG and Poly Haven are CC0 in their own words (items 12 and 13) and both ship normal, roughness and displacement maps at 1K to 8K. ambientCG's `Plastic006` is tagged "black, plastic, shiny, smooth" (melamine and T-molding), `Metal009` to `Metal012` are "brushed, bumpy, metal, scratches, silver, steel" (the coin door), `Carpet012` is "blue, carpet, dark, plain" and `Fabric022`/`Fabric023` are "blue, carpet, cloth, dark" (a cool basement floor that stays out of brown), `PaintedPlaster003` is a scratched painted wall, and Poly Haven's `laminate_floor_02`, `black_painted_planks` and `herringbone_parquet` cover the wood-floor alternative (items 12 and 13). What it costs: a small custom Pixi filter that reads a normal map and lights it from the marquee and monitor positions (the pixijs-custom-rendering skill covers `Filter.from` with GLSL), and trimming the downloads to 1K JPG (about 5 MB zipped per material). What it buys: the "lit by the machine" effect becomes real surface response rather than painted highlights, which is the cheapest honest way to get "more 3D-ish" without a 3D renderer in the page (INFERRED).

**Route 3, ranked third: a CC-BY cabinet from Sketchfab or Poly Pizza as a more detailed base.** The best fits found are joshtmc's "Arcade Cabinet" on Sketchfab (CC Attribution, 2,122 faces, marquee, CRT bezel, side art, coin door) and Chris Ross's "Arcade Cabinet" on Poly Pizza (CC BY 3.0, black body with blue edge trim, marquee, dark screen, one stick and two buttons, and a coin door carrying two buttons, which is exactly the Arcade1Up faux-door tell) (items 3 and 4). What it costs: CC BY 4.0 requires that we keep the creator's identification, a license notice and a link "in any reasonable manner", and "indicate if You modified the Licensed Material" (item 16), so a `CREDITS.md` in the repo and a credit reachable from the site are mandatory; Sketchfab downloads need an account, and the API download endpoint needs OAuth (item 24); and both models are single-title floor-style machines that still need the multicade tells added. What it buys: more geometry than Kenney and, in Chris Ross's case, a coin door with buttons already there.

**Not recommended for a public repo.** Anything Sketchfab labels Standard, Free Standard or Editorial: the license says "You may not use the 3D asset in a way that allows others to use or access the 3D asset as a stand-alone file", and Editorial adds no "commercial, promotional, endorsement, advertising or merchandising use" (item 18); a model file committed to a public repo is a stand-alone file (INFERRED). itch.io packs with no license text, such as AurynSky's arcade machines: itch.io's terms give users only a license to use content "as permitted through the functionality of the Service" and a moderator's reply on the asset-license thread is that no license text means "you don't know what the license is" (item 19). Craftpix free assets: the license forbids redistributing "the art or modified version of the art in a manner that would make some or all of the art files useable to another end user" (item 20). Branded models such as the Neo-Geo cabinet: CC0 and CC-BY do not touch trademarks, "No trademark or patent rights held by Affirmer are waived" (item 15).

**Fonts.** Bungee is OFL, designed by David Jonathan Ross, and the README says it "celebrates urban signage by wrangling the Latin alphabet to work vertically as well as horizontally", which is the marquee brief (item 14). For the coin-door menu labels, Barlow Condensed (OFL, Jeremy Tribby, nine weights with italics) is drawn after California road signage and plates (item 14). For the monitor hint text, Press Start 2P (OFL, CodeMan38) is "based on the font design from 1980s Namco arcade games" and "works best at sizes of 8px, 16px and other multiples of 8", with VT323 (OFL, Peter Hull, from the DEC VT320 terminal) as the CRT-terminal alternative (item 14). Self-host from the google/fonts repository with each family's `OFL.txt` beside it: the OFL FAQ says the license text may be omitted only "when a font is embedded in a document or bundled within a program", and Google's FAQ says the fonts may be included "within a product that is sold commercially" (item 17).

**The 2D sprite packs found are all pixel-scale (16 by 32 to 112 by 58 pixels) and cannot be the full-viewport cabinet; they are usable as pixel art on the monitor or as reference only** (items 5 to 8).

### Fonts

12. **Every font on the list is on Google Fonts under the OFL, from the repository's own metadata.** Bungee: designer "David Jonathan Ross", license "OFL", category "DISPLAY", file `Bungee-Regular.ttf` ([ofl/bungee/METADATA.pb](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/bungee/METADATA.pb), DOCUMENTED); the project README: "Bungee is a font family that celebrates urban signage by wrangling the Latin alphabet to work vertically as well as horizontally", with Regular and Hairline weights and "Inline, Outline, and Shade" layerings, "Bungee is released under the SIL Open Font License" ([github.com/djrrb/Bungee](https://github.com/djrrb/Bungee), DOCUMENTED); Bungee Shade and Bungee Tint (a colour font from the `Bungee_Color` source) are separate OFL families on Google Fonts ([bungeeshade/METADATA.pb](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/bungeeshade/METADATA.pb), [bungeetint/METADATA.pb](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/bungeetint/METADATA.pb), DOCUMENTED). ### Licensing rules that matter for a public repo plus a Vercel site

13. **CC0 waives copyright outright and asks nothing, but leaves trademarks alone.** "Affirmer hereby overtly, fully, permanently, irrevocably and unconditionally waives, abandons, and surrenders all of Affirmer's Copyright and Related Rights"; if the waiver fails, a fallback "royalty-free, non transferable, non sublicensable, non exclusive, irrevocable and unconditional license"; and "No trademark or patent rights held by Affirmer are waived, abandoned, surrendered, licensed or otherwise affected by this document" ([CC0 1.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode), DOCUMENTED).

14. **CC BY 4.0 requires a retained credit, a license notice, a link, and a note of modification, and lets a hyperlink to a credits page satisfy it.** The grant is "a worldwide, royalty-free, non-sublicensable, non-exclusive, irrevocable license" to "reproduce and Share the Licensed Material, in whole or in part; and produce, reproduce, and Share Adapted Material"; attribution must retain "identification of the creator(s)", "a copyright notice", "a notice that refers to this Public License", "a notice that refers to the disclaimer of warranties", "a URI or hyperlink to the Licensed Material to the extent reasonably practicable", and "indicate if You modified the Licensed Material and retain an indication of any previous modifications"; all of it "in any reasonable manner based on the medium, means, and context", and "it may be reasonable to satisfy the conditions by providing a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes the required information" ([CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode), DOCUMENTED). Sketchfab's own gloss for CC BY: "You may share, edit, and use the model, but they must give credit to the original model uploader for their work" ([Sketchfab license filters post](https://sketchfab.com/blogs/community/refine-downloadable-model-searches-with-new-license-filters/), DOCUMENTED). Poly Pizza's models are CC BY 3.0, whose attribution clause is the same shape (item 4). For this repo: a `CREDITS.md` at the repo root listing title, author, source URL, license URL and "modified" for each CC-BY asset, and a link to it from the site (the page's HTML `<head>` or a credits line the monitor can show), satisfies the "hyperlink to a resource" route (INFERRED).

15. **The OFL allows web embedding and bundling, forbids selling the font alone, and reserves the font name for modified versions.** "Permission is hereby granted, free of charge, to any person obtaining a copy of the Font Software, to use, study, copy, merge, embed, modify, redistribute, and sell modified and unmodified copies of the Font Software"; "Neither the Font Software nor any of its individual components, in Original or Modified Versions, may be sold by itself"; "No Modified Version of the Font Software may use the Reserved Font Name(s)"; bundling is allowed "provided that each copy contains the above copyright notice and this license" ([OFL text](https://openfontlicense.org/open-font-license-official-text/), DOCUMENTED). The FAQ: webpages "Yes! Go ahead!", loading "as webfonts through CSS @font-face declarations is a much better method" (2.1); WOFF conversion is allowed "but you need to be careful. A change in font format normally is considered modification, and Reserved Font Names (RFNs) cannot be used" (2.2); the license text may be dropped only "when a font is embedded in a document or bundled within a program" (1.10); acknowledgement "is not required" (1.1.2) ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/), DOCUMENTED). Google's FAQ: "all Google Fonts are open source and are without cost", "you can use them commercially, and even include them within a product that is sold commercially", "You can redistribute open source fonts according to those conditions", and Google "only adds fonts licensed under the SIL Open Font License" ([fonts.google.com/faq](https://fonts.google.com/faq), DOCUMENTED, read through Ref). Practical consequence: serve Google's own WOFF2 from `fonts.gstatic.com` or the unmodified TTF from the repo; converting to WOFF2 ourselves means renaming the family (INFERRED from 2.2).

16. **Kenney's terms are CC0 in the license file and on the support page, with a logo carve-out** (item 1 and [kenney.nl/support](https://kenney.nl/support), DOCUMENTED).

17. **Sketchfab's Standard and Editorial licenses forbid the stand-alone file, and Editorial forbids any promotional use.** "You may not use the 3D asset in a way that allows others to use or access the 3D asset as a stand-alone file"; "'Editorial Licensed Material' means any Licensed Material identified as 'Editorial' or with other similar designation on the Sketchfab Website", and "your License is further conditioned upon you not using... the Editorial Licensed Material for any commercial, promotional, endorsement, advertising or merchandising use"; the page does not mention Creative Commons at all ([sketchfab.com/licenses](https://sketchfab.com/licenses), DOCUMENTED). The API's license table lists `by`, `by-sa`, `by-nd`, `by-nc`, `by-nc-sa`, `by-nc-nd`, `cc0`, `free-st` ("Free Standard"), `st` ("Standard") and `ed` ("Editorial") ([api.sketchfab.com/v3/licenses](https://api.sketchfab.com/v3/licenses), ran on 2026-09-08). Sketchfab's status: Epic staff wrote on 2024-10-01 that "Sketchfab will remain open and continue hosting free-to-download content into 2025" ([Epic forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/what-will-happen-to-free-downloadable-sketchfab-assets/2039791), DOCUMENTED); new users "will use Epic Games accounts" from 2025-12-11 and "every AI-generated model will require the 'CreatedWithAI' label" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/sketchfab-to-require-mandatory-ai-disclosure-epic-games-accounts-for-users), COMMUNITY-MEASURED); on 2026-08-10 KitBash acquired Sketchfab and ArtStation from Epic and "Existing portfolios, libraries, and core workflows will continue operating as they currently do" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/artstation-and-sketchfab-have-been-acquired-by-kitbash), COMMUNITY-MEASURED; Epic's own post returned 403 and Sketchfab's returned empty). ## Programmatic access

Each subsection covers (a) the official API or CLI, (b) key or account, (c) a working example, (d) the license field, (e) rate limits and terms. Every example marked "ran" was executed from this machine on 2026-09-08 and returned the described result.

### Sketchfab

(a) The Data API v3 at `https://api.sketchfab.com/v3` has a public search endpoint and a download endpoint; the developer pages ([data-api/v3](https://sketchfab.com/developers/data-api/v3), [download-api](https://sketchfab.com/developers/download-api), [download-api/guidelines](https://sketchfab.com/developers/download-api/guidelines), [oauth](https://sketchfab.com/developers/oauth)) are a JavaScript app and returned nothing to either fetcher, so their wording below comes from search-engine excerpts of those pages (COMMUNITY-MEASURED). Per the excerpts: the Download API "requires end-user OAuth authentication", downloads are "glTF, GLB, and USDZ formats" and "not currently available in their source formats such as FBX and OBJ", the response holds temporary links with an "expires" field of 300 seconds and "you should not cache the URL", "Access tokens last 1 month", and the announcement scoped it to "content available under a Creative Common license" ([downloading-models](https://sketchfab.com/developers/download-api/downloading-models), [announcement](https://sketchfab.com/blogs/community/announcing-the-sketchfab-download-api-a-search-bar-for-the-3d-world/)). (b) Search needs no key. Download needs a Sketchfab login (an Epic Games account for accounts made after 2025-12-11, item 17) and an OAuth application registered at the OAuth page; `GET /v3/models/{uid}/download` without credentials returned HTTP 401 (ran). (c) `curl "https://api.sketchfab.com/v3/search?type=models&q=arcade%20cabinet&downloadable=true&license=by&count=10&sort_by=-likeCount"` returned ten CC Attribution cabinets with `name`, `uid`, `user.username`, `faceCount`, `vertexCount`, `license.label`, `thumbnails.images[]`, `viewerUrl` (ran); `curl https://api.sketchfab.com/v3/licenses` returned the ten slugs in item 17 (ran); `curl https://api.sketchfab.com/v3/models/{uid}` returns the full record including `license.slug` and `license.requirements` (ran). (d) `license` on each result carries `label` in search results and `label`, `slug`, `fullName`, `requirements`, `url` on the model endpoint; filter at query time with `license=cc0` or `license=by`, and re-check `license.slug` on the model record before use, because a description can claim CC0 while the field says `free-st` (item 3). (e) No rate limit statement was found. The guidelines excerpt says "Your app should clearly display the license and author attribution" and "The Creative Commons license and attribution must follow the asset everywhere it is used" ([guidelines](https://sketchfab.com/developers/download-api/guidelines), COMMUNITY-MEASURED). Practical shape: a script can search, filter and list candidates with thumbnails; a human downloads through the site or a one-time OAuth flow (INFERRED).

### Craftpix

No API; the freebies search is an HTML page ([craftpix.net/freebies/?s=arcade](https://craftpix.net/freebies/?s=arcade), ran); no cabinet exists there and the license bars repo redistribution (items 8 and 19). Manual only, and not for this project.

### Ranked: worth a small repo script, versus manual

1. **ambientCG v3**: JSON, no key, direct zip URLs with sizes, all CC0, `include=downloads` gives everything a fetch-and-cache script needs. Wrap it.
2. **Poly Haven**: JSON, no key, per-map per-resolution URLs with `md5`, all CC0, plus a semantic `/search`. Wrap it.
3. **Google Fonts via the google/fonts repository**: raw TTF plus `OFL.txt` plus `METADATA.pb` by predictable path, no key; the `license` field in `METADATA.pb` is the filter. Wrap it (a dozen lines).
4. **Sketchfab search**: JSON, no key, `license=` filter and thumbnails; downloads need OAuth, so the script lists and links and a person downloads. Wrap the search half only.
5. **itch.io RSS**: discovery of free packs by tag, no key; no license field, so every hit is a manual read. Wrap for discovery, never for download.
6. **Poly Pizza**: needs an account key and unreadable docs; three cabinets total. Manual.
7. **Kenney**: one grep per pack for the zip link; no listing endpoint. Manual, or a two-line helper when a pack is chosen.
8. **OpenGameArt**: HTML only and staff discourage scraping. Manual.
9. **Quaternius, Craftpix**: manual, and nothing there for this scene.

## What is thin

The "what it lacks" notes on every 3D model are read from one preview image each, not from opening the geometry; a coin door that looks like a flat decal in a thumbnail may be modelled, and the reverse.

Sketchfab's developer documentation, its 2024 "now that Fab's live" post and its 2025 generative-AI post could not be read by any fetcher available here; the Download API facts are search-engine excerpts of those pages and the "free models remain" statement rests on an Epic staff forum reply from October 2024 plus the August 2026 KitBash announcement as reported by 80.lv.

Poly Pizza's API documentation is behind a bot check; the endpoint, the 401 and the key location are verified, the header name and response fields are from third-party clients.

itch.io's free 3D Arcade listing returned 403 to the fetcher and was not read; the free Arcade sprite tag was read through RSS and holds no cabinet in its first 36 items, but deeper pages were not paged.

Kenney's `arcade-machine.glb` was not opened in a viewer; its description here is from the pack preview. The pack's `Overview.html` and `Previews/` folder were not read.

The Sketchfab "Restricting Generative AI Use of Free Models" post is the one place a no-AI clause might attach to CC-licensed Sketchfab downloads, and it was unreadable; the license texts themselves carry none.

The General Paid Asset License is a template written by an itch.io user and hosted on itch.io's blog, not itch.io policy; it matters only if a chosen pack adopts it.

No shipped flat-asset cabinet-as-entry was found after searches on the web, three.js's forum, Awwwards, GitHub topics and `gh search`; the two GitHub repos that describe themselves that way are unstarred student or personal projects with no live URL confirmed.

## The gap

Nothing found ships the exact thing: a stylised home multicade, drawn from flat assets, standing in a dark room as the only door to several games. The 3D precedents (Thralaga, Heffernan) wrap one game or one desk; the kiosk precedents (MakeCode) list games without drawing a machine; the flat pixel cabinets found are all under 200 pixels wide. So the cabinet's own art is the work, and the asset question reduces to what to build it from: a CC0 base model to render (Kenney), CC0 surface maps to light it with (ambientCG, Poly Haven), and OFL type (Bungee, Barlow Condensed, Press Start 2P).

No CC0 3D cabinet found has the home-machine tells from the earlier record (faux coin door with menu buttons, riser, two sticks and six buttons); the closest to a faux door is Chris Ross's CC BY 3.0 model on Poly Pizza, and the closest to the panel is BenjaTheMaker's CC0 pack on OGA. Whichever base is chosen, the tells get modelled on, and that modelling is where the "found, not bought" character will come from.

No black melamine or T-molding texture exists by that name on either CC0 site; `Plastic006` and `Plastic012A/B` on ambientCG are the stand-ins, and a wood floor that is not brown does not exist on either site, which is one more reason the floor should be `Carpet012` or `Fabric023` in dark blue, or a black-painted plank, and lit cool.
