# Research brief: product/vision lens (2026-08-08)

Sourced claims gathered to ground the product reviewer in the three-lens review of the same date. Subject-matter research, looked up at review time, not durable role-craft.

1. The Case of the Golden Idol was playtested 5-7 times per scenario starting at the prototype stage, using placeholder visuals and even Excel tables before production art existed; Housewarming's already-built headless kernel is exactly the artifact this practice says to put in front of humans now, before the PixiJS scene exists. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-the-case-of-the-golden-idol-i-used-frequent-testing-to-improve-its-mystery-solving

2. The Golden Idol team calibrated difficulty by silently watching testers for confusion and frustration, prioritizing qualitative observation over metrics; an automated solver measures solvability, not the "aha," so the solver cannot replace watching humans read a room. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-the-case-of-the-golden-idol-i-used-frequent-testing-to-improve-its-mystery-solving

3. Research on solver-graded puzzle difficulty finds solver metrics correlate with human difficulty only when the solver mimics human reasoning steps (constraint propagation, strategy switches), while crude measures like clue counts fail; the difficulty instrument should count human-accessible inference steps, not just confirm a unique solution exists. https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/sudoku-arxiv.pdf and https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:812097/FULLTEXT01.pdf

4. Shadows of Doubt, the highest-profile procedural deduction game, is criticized for mysteries that converge on repetitive patterns where mastering the formula means executing the same steps with different names; four procedurally rolled trait axes carry the same risk, and perceptual variety across a 30-50 night run needs explicit playtest attention, not just combinatorial math. https://www.polygon.com/23743064/shadows-of-doubt-proc-gen-detective-game-pc-steam-early-access/

5. Kate Compton's "10,000 bowls of oatmeal" problem states generated content can be mathematically unique yet perceptually identical; content-volume claims for Housewarming should be counted in perceptually distinct spirits and mornings, not in the trait-pool cross product. https://ems.andrew.cmu.edu/2018_60212f/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/kate-compton-oatmeal.pdf

6. Return of the Obra Dinn took an established developer 4.5 years for 60 authored fates, and its three-fates-lock-at-once rule exists specifically to block brute-forcing single entries; whole-name submission needs its own anti-brute-force answer (four axes with small pools is guessable), and authored Obra Dinn-scale content is not a realistic fallback if proc-gen disappoints. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-lucas-pope-s-i-return-of-the-obra-dinn-i-

7. Golden Idol's two-person team reportedly spent one to two months per scenario across 12 scenarios (this per-scenario figure came via an Exa-grounded answer citing thinkygames and I could not verify the exact number in the article text); the calibration point is that authored deduction content costs person-months per hour of play, which is the argument for proc-gen, but it means the folklore trait pools are the authored content and deserve authored-content time. https://thinkygames.com/features/how-the-case-of-the-golden-idol-developers-made-one-of-the-decades-best-detective-games-twice/

8. Solo and first-time postmortems (The Ramp, Captain Kaon, Yurivania 3, AstroClash) repeatedly name scope creep and playtesting-too-late as what ballooned or sank projects; Housewarming's explicit cuts (no prestige, no combat, no map) are the documented survival move and should be defended as such in review. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-i-the-ramp-i- and https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/indie-post-mortem-captain-kaon-part-one

9. Solo-dev postmortems on motivation (Prune, The Last Humble Bee) tie burnout to isolation and recommend external deadlines, enforced balance, and community contact as the mitigations; a year-plus horizon with AI agents as the only collaborators makes a public devlog or regular friend-playtest cadence a motivation structure, not just marketing. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-joel-mcdonald-s-i-prune-i- and https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-last-humble-bee-postmortem-staying-sane-in-solo-development

10. Derek Yu's finishing-games writing treats finishing as a learnable skill and says a tepidly received project should be finished faster and moved on from, not fixed through extended development; the "smallest start-to-finish version that a year can build on" framing matches this, provided the finish line is a real release, not a perpetual foundation. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-spelunky-i-designer-want-to-be-an-indie-then-learn-how-to-finish-games-reliably

11. Milestone guidance for solo projects converges on building an end-to-end vertical slice first, then defining milestones by falsifiable outcome criteria (e.g. "a player finishes a run in an hour unaided") with explicit exclusion lists; since the kernel already runs generator-through-save, the next milestone should be a playable start-to-finish run with placeholder visuals, not a polished room scene. https://www.newgameplus.guide/post/milestone-planning and https://mojolabs.nz/what-makes-a-small-game-actually-finishable/

12. Playtesting guidance for puzzle games says 3-5 testers per iteration captures most qualitative findings, observed silently and unhelped; many small rounds with friends beats one big late test, and this is cheap enough to schedule monthly. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-many-players-should-you-playtest-with- and https://www.getpostcurious.com/post/puzzle-testing-process

13. A CHI PLAY 2024 study of indie studios found developers know early playtesting matters but delay it anyway due to recruitment friction and logistics, defaulting to informal Discord/remote tests late in development; the review should ask for named testers and dates in the plan, because drift to "later" is the documented default. https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/216480/7/CHI_PLAY_24_Indie_Playtesting_final_.pdf

14. Remote playtest guides prescribe think-aloud over Discord/Zoom with screen recording, invisible telemetry for objective events, and a short post-test survey, triangulating the three; the existing Upstash playthrough logging is the right instrument and should capture per-night events (submissions, wrong names, warmth trajectory, time-to-first-naming). https://gamesuserresearch.com/remote-games-user-research/ and https://lobbi.tech/resources/guidance/guide-remote-playtesting

15. A Dark Room's free web version drew 630,000+ game starts and built the reputation, but revenue came from the later paid iOS port ($697,270 in two years); free web distribution reliably buys audience and credibility, not money, which fits a first game whose goal is a finished foundation. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/a-two-year-look-at-the-sales-of-chart-topping-ios-title-i-a-dark-room-i-

16. Universal Paperclips was intended as a weekend project, took nine months, then found hundreds of thousands of players in weeks as a free browser game via Hacker News and Twitter; the scope multiplier is the base rate to plan with, and a free web incremental can find its audience with no storefront at all. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Paperclips

17. The Roottrees are Dead, the closest structural comparable (solo deduction game), shipped free on itch after 11 months of post-jam expansion and only became a commercial Steam release when a collaborator later joined to rebuild it; ship-free-first-then-decide-commercial is a proven path for exactly this genre and team size. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roottrees_are_Dead

18. Developers report itch.io typically contributes 1-5% of PC revenue versus Steam's 80-95%; web/itch is coherent for Housewarming's stated destination, but any future commercial ambition means a Steam page and wishlists, which take months of lead time to matter. https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/05/12/benchmark-itch-io-traffic/

19. Obra Dinn's chapter/disaster structure exists to compartmentalize information so deductions arrive in a digestible order; the house-opening-one-axis-per-room pacing is Housewarming's analog, and the solver should verify each opening genuinely changes which inferences are newly possible rather than just adding rooms. https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/how-a-book-binds-the-return-of-the-obra-dinn

Unverified: I could not confirm Lucas Pope's original time estimate for Obra Dinn (sources agree on 4.5 years actual but give no crisp initial number), and claim 7's per-scenario cost figure could not be checked against the article's full text.
