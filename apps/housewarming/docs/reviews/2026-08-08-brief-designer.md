# Research brief: game designer lens (2026-08-08)

Sourced claims gathered to ground the designer reviewer in the three-lens review of the same date. Subject-matter research, looked up at review time, not durable role-craft.

**Expertise brief: Housewarming design review (sources looked up 2026-08-08)**

1. Lucas Pope added Obra Dinn's batch-of-three fate confirmation because immediate per-answer feedback "can be cheated... it's trivial for them to just try all the combinations," so he made three simultaneous correct fates cheaper to earn honestly than to brute-force (his own devlog words). Housewarming's whole-name rule inherits the same threat: with four axes drawn from small pools, the review should compute whether guessing a name is cheaper than deducing it, and whether any batch equivalent is needed. https://dukope.com/devlogs/obra-dinn/tig-30/

2. In the same devlog Pope standardized fates to Subject-Verb-Object partly so each death could accept multiple synonymous answers, "so hopefully players won't get stuck by entering something they thought was correct but wasn't flagged as so." Folklore-sourced trait pools must give each axis value one unambiguous in-scene reading, or players will be right in their heads and wrong in the book, the worst stuck state a deduction game has. https://dukope.com/devlogs/obra-dinn/tig-30/

3. Written analysis of Obra Dinn's confirmation system finds that once a player is confident in two of three fates they can and do farm the third by trial, held back only by soft warnings and combinatorial size. Housewarming's anti-spiral rule (drain shrinks per trait "ruled in") is a per-trait correctness oracle; the reviewer should probe whether warmth deltas let players confirm axes one at a time, dissolving the whole-name rule from the side. https://intermittentmechanism.blog/2024/05/21/confirmation-in-the-return-of-obra-dinn/

4. Golden Idol's designers found that whole-scroll right/wrong feedback "worked fine for a smaller, tutorial-like scenario" but frustrated players in complex ones, and adding sub-puzzles that reward partial progress "greatly improved the satisfaction." Over 30-50 nights, mornings that neither rule a trait in nor out are the dead turns the genre is known for; every night's experiment should be checkable for whether it can advance at least one axis. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol

5. The same team's "two or fewer slots are incorrect" indicator rewarded near-misses but measurably "reduced the difficulty and length of the game." Near-miss feedback is a difficulty dial with a known cost, relevant to how precisely a warmth delta should telegraph a nearly-correct name. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol

6. Golden Idol was tuned with a written "thought path" of insight steps, and clues letting testers bypass steps were removed as making parts of the game superfluous. An automated solver measures solvability and search depth, not the human insight chain; the review should ask what Housewarming's per-spirit thought path is and whether the solver models it. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-the-case-of-the-golden-idol-i-used-frequent-testing-to-improve-its-mystery-solving

7. Golden Idol abandoned Obra Dinn-style lived-in tangential detail because recall load "quickly proved to create a bad player experience," keeping only solution-necessary content and deliberate misdirection. Reading a room as a scene must cap simultaneous evidence tokens, and everything observed should persist somewhere consultable rather than taxing memory. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol

8. Golden Idol's team playtested from Excel-table prototypes onward with a standing group of four or five, relying on qualitative interviews rather than metrics, flagging "understands the mystery immediately then stuck thirty minutes on a technicality" as the failure signature. A solo-plus-agents project has no standing tester group; the solver cannot catch technicality-stuck, so the review should ask where human playtests enter the schedule. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-the-case-of-the-golden-idol-i-used-frequent-testing-to-improve-its-mystery-solving

9. Strange Horticulture punishes wrong identifications with a "Rising Dread" meter that, when filled, forces a recoverable puzzle rather than a fail state, alongside optional progressive hints that narrow candidates. This is the closest shipped analogue to warmth-as-pressure in a cozy-dark deduction game, and its lesson is recoverability: the review should test whether the Leave-action cold floor reads as a recoverable dread or an Adams-style grind-down. https://www.polygon.com/reviews/22935956/strange-horticulture-plant-shop-review-impressions/

10. Bad Viking admits clue-to-plant balance had no method, "a lot of trial and error... we tweaked, tweaked, and then tweaked some more," continuing past release. Procedural generation moves that hand-tuning burden into trait-pool curation and solver thresholds; expect the same volume of tweaking, just relocated. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/finding-right-plant-job-strange-horticulture

11. Cultist Simulator shipped without a tutorial only after an unplanned final month spent threading breadcrumb clues and UI nudges through the whole game, and the retrospective calls not budgeting for it earlier a mistake. Housewarming's no-prose scene-reading is the same deliberate obscurity, so first-contact coaching is a scheduled work item, not polish. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/cultist-simulator-the-retrospective

12. Alexis Kennedy concedes Cultist's optimal line degenerated into "work quietly, building up funds" before pursuing the interesting goals, compelling players "to do something quite boring for a while." A single resource (warmth) with safe income from named spirits invites the identical hoard-then-act loop; the review should check the optimal night is also the interesting night. https://medium.com/sex-lies-and-videogames/alexis-kennedy-on-cultist-simulator-the-way-through-the-wood-part-2-70c40826b3b0

13. Frostpunk's designers split societal feedback into hope and discontent because survival research showed hope, not comfort, is what keeps people alive. Warmth in Housewarming carries economy and mood in one meter; the review should ask whether one number can do both jobs, since 11 bit needed two. https://www.pcgamer.com/frostpunk-developers-on-hope-misery-and-the-ultimately-terrifying-book-of-laws/

14. Frostpunk's lead designer says they ended the game "the way a novel ends" because letting the sandbox continue after the storm felt anticlimactic against a story of sacrifice. This directly supports the one-sitting, closed 30-50-night arc with no prestige layer. https://www.pcgamer.com/frostpunk-developers-on-hope-misery-and-the-ultimately-terrifying-book-of-laws/

15. Ernest Adams's canonical column defines the downward spiral (losses that impair future performance) as "depressing... a slow grind down to failure isn't much fun" and prescribes exactly the mitigation Housewarming uses: don't let damage impair performance. The anti-spiral rule is textbook, so the review's question is calibration: whether the last-keeper death remains reachable enough to be a real threat once traits start ruling in. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-designer-s-notebook-preventing-the-downward-spiral

16. A Dark Room's creator reports the best-loved qualities were "the expanding scope" and treating the game's own mechanics as a mystery, while the most-hated element was an ending players felt "invalidated all of their work." House-opening-as-pacing matches the loved half; the freeze/Leave endings and the optional last-keeper entry must not read as invalidating the filled book. https://www.tumblr.com/interviewswithmakers/55486639138/michael-townsend-a-dark-room

17. Frank Lantz says Universal Paperclips' shape was fixed from the start so the ending is the mechanic performing the theme, the player throwing loyalty "under the bus... in pursuit of your one true goal." Endings in mechanics-first games land when enacted, not narrated: naming the last keeper should stay a mechanical act (a book entry) rather than acquiring prose. https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/interviews-and-opinion/66271/interview-paperclips-developer-frank-lantz/

18. Cryptid's designers generated clue sets programmatically so all clues intersect in exactly one hex (validated by exhaustively checking every hex), and their playtest-derived principles were: no barriers between player and puzzle, information permanently visible, and clues must feel fair, with roughly equal elimination power and no redundant clues. These are direct generator requirements for Housewarming: per-axis trait evidence needs comparable elimination power per night, and every past observation should stay visible in the book. https://boardgamegeek.com/blog/1/blogpost/81460/designer-diary-cryptid-or-decrypting-deduction-det

19. Cryptid also ships an optional meta-hint about the clue distribution ("there are no 'within one' clues") that groups can invoke mid-game to soften difficulty. A hint about the generator's distribution, rather than the answer, is a cheap, solver-compatible hint form for a procedural deduction game. https://boardgamegeek.com/blog/1/blogpost/81460/designer-diary-cryptid-or-decrypting-deduction-det

20. A 2024 AIIDE study generating logic-grid puzzles with constrained MAP-Elites found solver-loop counts significantly correlated with players' perceived difficulty but only borderline with their actual correctness. The solver-as-difficulty-instrument approach is empirically supported, yet solver metrics still need human calibration before trusting the generator's difficulty curve. https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/31873

21. The Search for Planet X's designers confined the app to two jobs, "randomize setup and give private answers to public questions," keeping all agency in the players' hands. It is a clean model for the headless kernel's role boundary: oracle and generator, never adviser. https://www.cardboardrepublic.com/articles/interviews/star-search-a-planet-x-qa

Unverified: I could not confirm Strange Horticulture's hint specifics from the developers themselves (claim 9 rests on reviews), and I found no primary Pope source on Obra Dinn difficulty tuning beyond the tig-30 devlog; claims 1-2 are his words, difficulty-curve statements elsewhere were not verified.
