# Housewarming: three-lens review, adjudicated

**Date: 2026-08-08**

Three reviewers (game architect, game designer, game product manager) read the full design record plus researched briefs, and every plan-changing finding went to an adversarial verifier who tried to break it against the record and against outside facts; fourteen product and designer findings got no verifier and are flagged as unverified below. I spot-checked the disputed claims myself against run.ts, rules.ts, game-concept.md, and issue #1 before adjudicating. The single most important finding, raised independently by two lenses and CONFIRMED twice: the ending you have designed cannot happen in the kernel you have built. settle() in src/kernel/run.ts ends the run the moment the roster is named, and her entry, which the decision log says is found the same way as every other spirit, needs nights that the state machine then refuses. It has no ticket and is absent from issue #1's fog list, so the spec can close "complete" without the thing that makes it complete.

## Where the lenses agree

Cross-lens agreement is the strongest signal in this material. Seven findings arrived from more than one direction:

**The ending has no mechanics and no ticket** (architect and designer, both independently CONFIRMED by verifiers). Details in Decide now.

**Sound's stale promise contradicts the night contract** (all three lenses). game-concept.md still says sound "carries information... a voice from a room you were not in", one day older than the settled rule that an unwatched room shows nothing, ever. All three converge on the same answer: sound presents scene data and never solely carries a signal. Adjudicated under Conflicts, landed under Adjustments.

**The 1296 Mastermind growth target is arithmetically dead** (designer and product, both unverified, but I checked the arithmetic myself against rules.ts and the doc). Two or three openings of one axis each, the first spent on rooms per #11, hour capped at one widening per #7: the space tops out near 4 x 6 x 4 x 7, roughly 670, and #11 already showed the economy is the difficulty dial anyway.

**The solver must model the player who guesses** (architect, CONFIRMED; designer, unverified). #12's honest solver produces identical output at any wrongName value, so it structurally cannot price the one number #9 handed it direct evidence about: the coin flip on the last trait. Sources both cite: https://dukope.com/devlogs/obra-dinn/tig-30/ and https://intermittentmechanism.blog/2024/05/21/confirmation-in-the-return-of-obra-dinn/

**The book needs an owner** (designer, WEAKENED to ADJUST; product, unverified). Both agree it is the largest unbuilt React surface, the observed teaching device, and load-bearing for whether #15's solver-tuned prices transfer to a human with imperfect memory, and it has no ticket.

**Solver numbers need human anchoring** (designer's calibration finding and product's #10-is-stale finding, both unverified, converge). The genuinely open question in #10 is not whether a solver can be written (deduction.ts and the forty-seed sweeps already answer that) but whether the automated keeper plays like a person, which the Upstash human logs in the same JSONL format exist to check.

**Nothing on the map produces a thing a person plays start to finish** (product, unverified, but I confirmed against issue #1: the destination is "a buildable spec" and no ticket yields a playable whole, while game-concept.md's own constraints box orders a crude playable build early). The architect's crash-reporting defer and the designer's calibration clause both quietly presuppose this build exists.

## Conflicts, and how I ruled

**Sound: decide before #12/#13 tune (architect) vs defer to the sound ticket (designer) vs declare atmosphere now (product).** The verifier's refutation is right: the boundary is already drawn. Issue #3's resolution makes the three morning scenes the complete signal inventory, the kernel implements exactly that, and the solver reads only the kernel, so audio cannot leak into the tuning no matter when the ticket is cut. What remains is record hygiene: the 2026-08-06 decision log line and the sound section are stale against the 2026-08-07 contract, and someone working the sound ticket later off the older text would rebuild the contradiction. Ruling: ADJUST now (fix the text, one paragraph), ear-only rule adopted at the sound ticket (Defer). No gate on #12/#13.

**Autosave storage: DECIDE-NOW (architect) vs DEFER (verifier).** The verifier wins on the record as it stood: the choice is entirely shell-side, the kernel seam passes a string, and there was no shell ticket to put the decision beside. But the playable-slice charter (below) creates that ticket, and the slice writes the first real save. Ruling: DEFER with a near trigger, and the failure states the architect named (persist() denial as a real state, private-mode detection before an hour is invested) go into the slice ticket by name so they cannot be skipped. The quota and Safari-ITP worries are overstated at tens of kilobytes and one-sitting play.

**The book: "who does the deducing" as an open crisis (designer) vs already settled (verifier).** The verifier's refutation holds: the core loop in game-concept.md step 4 already says the keeper marks traits ruled in and out by hand, #9's working-memory finding is recorded in the fog note, and #11 deliberately computed drain relief from evidence rather than marks precisely so the economy never trusts the book. The kernel comments in run.ts and deduction.ts do lean derived and should be reconciled, but no decision is drifting. Ruling: ADJUST, not DECIDE-NOW. Cut the book ticket now (both lenses agree on that), and the product lens's point survives inside it: #15's prices only transfer to humans if the book holds ruled-in/ruled-out state, so the book ticket is upstream of trusting #15 against people.

**Save ruleset drift: DECIDE-NOW (architect) vs standing constraint (verifier).** The mechanism is real (loadSave validates trait ids as bare strings, so a renamed pool value silently empties consistentNames and the run becomes unwinnable with no version gate tripped), but no save-writing UI exists yet, so nothing is lost by waiting. Ruling: ADJUST, with the same note as storage: the slice charter moves "the first deployed build that writes a save" much closer, so the constraint goes into the slice ticket, not into a someday list.

**The slice milestone vs the map's destination.** Product's charter finding conflicts with issue #1's stated destination ("a buildable spec"), not with another lens. The record argues against itself here: the constraints box in game-concept.md explicitly orders a crude playable build early because "the risk is that the interesting part arrives too late." Ruling: the constraints box wins, because the three riskiest open questions (the 30-50 morning freshness, the Leave beat, the flat middle) are only answerable by a played whole, and the hosting and logging pipeline for it is built and idle.

## Decide now

**1. How the ending plays.** Architect and designer, both verifiers CONFIRMED. The decision log settles that her entry becomes available after the win and that she is found the same way as every other spirit, but settle() in run.ts flips to won at named.length === spirits.length and assertPlayable() then throws on every playNight and submitName; she is not in the roster (generator stages sum to six) and no ticket owns the epilogue. Why now: #5 is on the frontier producing ending content with nowhere to land, #12's solver is about to code against the current won condition, and #15 will tune an economy whose final phase does not exist. Postponing means re-measuring #12/#15 and reworking the run state machine and save shape after they harden. As a moment of play: the last true name lands, the house warms, and then the player sets a candle for one more night to find the person who left them the house. My recommendation: add a fourth run phase (the house won, nights still playable, drain over, her trace now able to appear), and suspend the named-spirit warmth yield inside it so the remaining stock is a finite candle budget; that keeps the settled "winning ends the drain," keeps her entry a played thing under gentle pressure rather than a patience reward, and dodges the ending-resentment trap A Dark Room's maker reported (https://www.tumblr.com/interviewswithmakers/55486639138/michael-townsend-a-dark-room). Decide the phase now and ticket it; the exact pressure numbers belong to #15.

**2. Roster size, before #6.** Product, unverified, but the load-bearing fact is in issue #1's own text: rooms must outnumber the roster, so spirit count is the input #6's opening budget and #14's pool sizes are functions of, and the supply of clean attested trace kinds caps it near seven or eight from the other side. Why now: #6 is frontier today; settle it without the roster number and it reopens the day the roster is chosen. Recommendation: keep six, the number the kernel is already built and measured at, and record it; nothing in the record argues for more, and traces argue against.

**3. Which axes the openings widen, with elimination power in view.** Designer, DECIDE-NOW, **unverified**. The claim: haunt pins in one sighting and hour dates in one candle, but lure and aversion are one-value-per-spirit-per-night serial probes with no cross-elimination, so every value added to those pools buys guess-and-check nights, and those are exactly the discretionary widenings left after rooms and hour are spoken for (the kernel's stage table already widens lures). As play: mid-game mornings shrink to one bowl bit and one ward bit per spirit, which is where dead mornings come from. Why now: #6 is frontier, and baking this in means #6, #14, and #12 all reopen when late spirits measure as long probe tails. I could not fully verify the elimination arithmetic against the resolver in the time given, so treat the analysis as an input to #6 rather than settled fact, but the asymmetry is visible in the night contract itself. Recommendation: work #6 with this analysis on the table, preferring openings that change what an instrument can mean (per #7, noon and the eve leave the night) over more values on the probe axes. Cryptid's designers treat equal elimination power per clue as a fairness core: https://boardgamegeek.com/blog/1/blogpost/81460/designer-diary-cryptid-or-decrypting-deduction-det

**4. What leaving preserves.** Designer, DECIDE-NOW, **unverified**, but I checked the arithmetic in rules.ts myself: the Leave window is five warmth wide (offered at 8, withdrawn at 3) while a single late night can swing 15-20 (two loose spirits at drain 8 plus experiment costs), so many runs will jump the window between mornings and never see the offer; and in run.ts 'left' and 'lost' differ only by a status string, so a rational player at the threshold always gambles. As play: the flagship beat, the exit visibly withdrawing while you weigh one more night, currently cannot fire as a dilemma because leaving keeps nothing that freezing loses. Why now: #15 is about to place thresholds around a choice with no payoff. Decide what leaving preserves (a distinct ending scene, the book surviving into the fiction, the line of keepers acknowledged to continue), and adopt the structural rule that the window must be wider than the maximum nightly swing as a #15 tuning constraint. Recommendation: the book survives; it is the cheapest payoff, it is already the game's emotional object, and it makes leaving a sad ending rather than a reskinned loss.

**5. Charter the ugly end-to-end slice, and put names and a date on playtesting.** Product, **unverified**, confirmed against issue #1 by me as described above. The kernel runs generator through versioned save; the slice is a crude React book and setup over it plus a text morning, reusing morning-signals patterns. Why now: the longest human run to date is 8 nights by you on toy pools, and the untested assumption underneath everything is that one room-scene grammar stays worth reading 30-50 times in an hour (the oatmeal problem: https://ems.andrew.cmu.edu/2018_60212f/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/kate-compton-oatmeal.pdf). Postponing repeats the best-documented solo-dev failure, playtesting arriving after the presentation hardens. The logging pipeline is built and idle and the jam Discord is a ready tester pool, so name three testers and a first date tied to the slice, and watch two of them read a morning silently; the solver can never measure whether naming feels like naming.

## Adjustments

Each of these touches something decided; the decided thing is named.

**#12's deliverable list** (architect CONFIRMED; designer additions unverified): alongside the honest solver's nights-to-crack, the harness reports the warmth cost of the best guess-and-check policy per house, a zero-information-night count, and a minimum-warmth-margin distribution. Reason: an economy tuned only against a player who never guesses is invalid, and added before #12 is built this is one more policy over the same kernel.

**game-concept.md's growth target** (the decision-log pair "four values per axis toward 1296" and "each opening widens exactly one axis" cannot both hold): rewrite the ramp as economy, stagger, and parallel loose spirits with modest pool growth, before #6 and #14 settle anything. Unverified by the adversarial pass but the arithmetic is checkable and I checked it.

**game-concept.md's sound section and the 2026-08-06 sound log entry** (WEAKENED from a DECIDE-NOW): reword to match the night contract, and record the rule that sound presents scene data and never adds to it, next to "no paragraphs of prose." Reason: the kernel already enforces this; only the text can mislead a future ticket.

**save.ts's "the rules are code" posture** (WEAKENED from a DECIDE-NOW): a ruleset identifier (version or content hash) enters the envelope before the first deployed build that writes a save, with the loader refusing or migrating on mismatch. Reason: #14/#15 exist to churn THE_HOUSE's tables under continuous deploy, and a data edit never trips SAVE_VERSION; write the constraint into the slice ticket so it cannot slip through.

**api/log.ts's read gate** (WEAKENED: Vercel deployment protection currently covers it, verified by the adversarial pass with an actual curl): still make it fail closed when LOG_READ_TOKEN is absent and set the token on preview and development. Reason: #8 decided reads are gated, and the current guarantee rests on a platform default nobody decided; the fix is three lines.

**The book gets a ticket** (designer WEAKENED to this; product unverified): cut it now, covering the working-memory marks (which become saved player state), the teaching job of her unfinished entry, the composed-name templating, and the N-vs-N+1 page-count question from the ending. Also reconcile the run.ts/deduction.ts comments with the recorded manual-marks lean, and backfill #11's kernel-embedded design calls into the decision log, where the map's own rules say they belong (the log ends 2026-08-07; #11 closed after).

**#10's written scope** (unverified): rewrite from "build a throwaway solver against the toy" to "does the kernel's automated keeper play like a person," answered by comparing its traces to logged human runs on shared seeds, and add a calibration clause between #12 and #14: human nights-to-crack distributions against solver distributions before #14's dials are accepted, plus a standing note that technicality-stuck reports come only from people. Reason: the writability half of #10 is already demonstrated in deduction.ts and the forty-seed sweeps, and solver difficulty only predicts human difficulty when anchored (https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/31873).

## Safe to defer

- **Where the autosave lives** (localStorage vs IndexedDB, persist() denial as a state, private-mode detection): trigger is the slice ticket, which writes the first real save; the named failure states go in it explicitly.
- **The kernel-to-room seam and render-on-demand** (RoomHandle has only destroy(); the placeholder ticker never rests): trigger is the first #17 build session; define the scene-in/events-out contract and autoStart: false together while the room is one file.
- **Crash reporting through the existing log pipe** (window.onerror and unhandledrejection posting into the Upstash envelope with the run id): trigger is the first outside tester on a real build; decide on data whether Sentry ever earns its place.
- **Migration fixtures**: trigger is the commit that first bumps SAVE_VERSION; that commit lands a canonical version-1 save as a fixture loaded through the full chain, and the ritual repeats every bump.
- **The ear-never-sole-carrier rule**: trigger is the sound ticket; anything heard in the night also lands in the book in the keeper's hand, because evidence must stay consultable (and muted tabs and deaf players must stay in the game).
- **Which generator rules her notes disclose** (one spirit per room, roster size, traces as pure identity): trigger is the book ticket; the paper prototype showed learning the room rule mid-run changed play, so withholding is a real difficulty choice to make on purpose.
- **Distribution**: free web where it already lives, itch mirror at release; the one item with real lead time is a Steam page, so the trigger is the moment any commercial ambition appears, because wishlists are measured in months.

## Explicitly not building

- **Save obfuscation or local anti-cheat**: client-side secrecy is unwinnable, wrong names already cost warmth, and a readable plaintext save is a debugging and fixture feature; write it down as a decision so it is never re-argued.
- **Cold degrading morning legibility (#16)**: the economy already throttles information through what a cold keeper can afford, so this would tax the losing player twice through the same resource; the atmosphere survives whole as vignette, palette, frost, and sound. This resolves #16 in the cheap direction and unblocks #17's art scope.
- **Per-value morning art**: the night contract made scene states generic (mark heights, bowl taken or not, trace stopped or crossed), so the only art that scales is one trace kind per spirit and the setup screen's lures and wards, both bounded; drawing per-lure states would re-couple art cost to pool width.

## Hidden dependencies between decided pieces

- Roster size fixes how many openings go to rooms (#11's invariant), which fixes #6's budget, which fixes #14's pools; the trace supply caps the roster from the other side. The fog item is the first domino.
- The kernel already answered #6's "do pools widen under a puzzle in progress" (Spirit.stage plus namesStillOpen); reversing that is a save migration and a deduction change, not a design edit.
- Every #14 pool edit changes anti-spiral dynamics through drain relief, not just puzzle difficulty, so #14 and #15 must always be re-measured together, which raises the stakes on #12 being fast.
- Drain relief is computed from evidence, not the keeper's marks, so warmth deltas can announce a settled axis before the player has concluded it: a hint channel nobody has chosen to have. The book ticket should face it.
- The whole-name rule's integrity is priced by wrongName: submissions cost no night and are unlimited per morning, so unless a wrong name costs more than roughly two testing nights (or costs a night), the endgame coin flip dissolves the rule economically.
- Leave thresholds can only stage a dilemma that has a payoff, and the window must be wider than the maximum nightly warmth swing or the offer is never even seen.
- The book's page count either shows N and grows to N+1 at the win (a reveal to stage) or shows N+1 from the start (a spoiler); the book ticket and the ending decision must agree.
- The only honest hour widenings (noon, the eve) leave the night, but the candle is a night instrument, so a daytime hour value has no experiment unless a day instrument is invented; this breaks the axis-pairs-with-a-control rule and #7 flagged only half of it.
- Determinism currently holds because all randomness happens in startRun; any future mid-run draw (arrival staggering is unspecified) makes RNG state saveable, which is a version bump.
- One playthrough being one sitting is itself the mitigation for Safari's 7-day storage deletion; the storage decision should say so rather than promise around it.
- The hour-long sitting implies roughly 70-90 seconds per night including the morning read, which quietly caps how much inspection #17's scene may demand; #17 is currently sized by legibility alone.

## What does not apply to this game

Merged from all three lenses so nobody re-raises them: camera systems (one fixed interior scene), collision and physics (nothing moves at runtime), a game loop or tick model (turn-based pure functions), performance and scale engineering (one room, a 672-name space, a free-tier workload measured with room to spare), input abstraction (forms and pointer hits), animation tooling (tween-and-filter presentation), multiplayer and social systems, monetization and pricing, combat and enemy balance (decided out, soundly), map and traversal design (no map screen by decision), prestige and meta-progression (deliberately cut with sourced support), save management UX (single versioned slot is decided and fits one-sitting play), platform ports, localization (near-zero text by rule), live-ops and backend scaling, engine licensing, team scaling, and ratings or compliance.

## What this review could not verify

The entire product lens and five designer findings received no adversarial verifier; every one is flagged unverified above, and their external claims (the CHI PLAY playtesting-drift study, Golden Idol's tester cadence, the oatmeal essay, Shadows of Doubt criticism, milestone-planning guidance) were taken on the reviewers' citations without an independent check. I spot-checked the mechanical claims myself where I could: run.ts's settle/assertPlayable behavior, the Leave window against the drain schedule in rules.ts, the 1296 arithmetic against the stage table and the doc, and issue #1's fog list and destination all read as the findings describe. What I could not check from here: the designer's serial-probe elimination arithmetic against the resolver, the actual Vercel environment configuration beyond what the verifier's curl established, anything about how the middle of a 30-50 night run feels (no instrument existing or planned can measure that; only the slice in front of friends can), and the review's date, which the material does not carry.
